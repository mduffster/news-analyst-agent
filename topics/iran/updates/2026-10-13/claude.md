## Key Developments (Past 24 Hours)

### **SAUDI COALITION STRIKES SANAA OIL FACILITIES** [VERIFIED]

A coalition of forces in Yemen led by Saudi Arabia carried out new airstrikes Tuesday on the Houthi rebel-held Yemeni capital of Sanaa. Saudi-led coalition forces launched airstrikes near Yemen's capital, Sanaa, on Tuesday, October 13, targeting an oil storage facility after warning civilians to evacuate the surrounding area.

Residents told Xinhua loud explosions were heard across Sanaa, causing panic in parts of the city and long queues at fuel stations over fears of supply disruptions. The channel, citing a company source, said the facility was empty of petroleum products at the time of the strikes and had "no military value."

**[ANALYSIS]** This marks the first coalition strike on the Houthi capital since the airport attacks escalated. The pre-announced evacuation warning suggests Saudi Arabia is trying to calibrate its response while avoiding civilian casualties.

### **HOUTHIS THREATEN ARAMCO, NAME SPECIFIC TARGETS** [OFFICIAL]

The Iran-backed Houthis issued an evacuation warning Tuesday to civilians near all facilities belonging to Saudi Arabia's state-run energy giant Saudi Aramco.

The group urged experts, engineers and workers to avoid Saudi oil facilities that could be targeted, and renewed warnings to airlines, passengers and airport employees over the use of designated Saudi airports and airspace. The Houthis named King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh, Aramco's Riyadh refinery and an Aramco facility in the eastern city of Dammam as potential targets.

**[ANALYSIS]** By naming specific Aramco facilities, the Houthis are signaling a potential shift from airport disruption to energy infrastructure attacks—a significant escalation threshold.

### **TRUMP: HOUTHIS TRYING TO HURT MIDTERMS, "I WILL NEVER FORGET"** [OFFICIAL]

President Donald Trump on Tuesday accused Yemen's Iran-backed Houthi militia of deliberately trying to drive up oil prices ahead of the US midterm elections and warned that he would "never forget" their attacks on Saudi Arabia.

Trump also made clear that his warning would extend beyond the November 3 midterm elections, saying, "I'll be there after the elections, and I will never forget!"

When asked if the US has made a decision to strike Houthi targets, Trump avoided a direct response when speaking to reporters before departing on a campaign trip to Alaska.

On Day 206 he decided against striking the Houthis despite Saudi pleas; an American has since been killed in Riyadh. No decision was announced.

**[ANALYSIS]** Trump's framing shifts blame to Houthis (and implicitly Iran) for high gas prices ahead of midterms, but his threat of post-election action suggests he continues to defer the strike decision until after November 3.

### **STRAIT OF HORMUZ: ATTACK SURGE CONTINUES** [VERIFIED]

At least 12 tankers were attacked in and around the Strait of Hormuz in a single week, targeting oil, LNG and LPG vessels.

Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps took credit for the attack, saying it was the third ship it had struck in two days. Since the start of October, there have been at least 18 attacks on ships in or around the Strait of Hormuz.

The video showed the apparently laden Liberian-registered VLCC Al Maqam with a fire blackened funnel and a tug attempting to extinguish a fire on the vessel. The Al Maqam is owned and managed by ADNOC Logistics and Services.

Iran appears to be targeting commercial vessels conducting ship-to-ship oil transfers in and near the Strait of Hormuz in order to try to undermine vessels' ability to move energy through the strait, the Institute for the Study of War said Tuesday.

**[ANALYSIS]** Iran is actively escalating in the strait, now averaging 2-3 attacks per day. The ADNOC VLCC strike is notable as UAE is a key US partner. The shift to targeting ship-to-ship transfers suggests Iran is adapting tactics to counter workarounds.

### **OIL PRICES: EASING ON RUSSIAN DIESEL DEAL** [VERIFIED]

Crude oil prices declined on Tuesday, with Brent crude futures falling 1% to $105.6 a barrel and US West Texas Intermediate (WTI) crude futures dropping 1% to $92.8 a barrel.

US President Donald Trump said Russia had agreed to supply diesel to global markets, while his administration temporarily lifted sanctions on Russian fuel.

However, US lawmakers argued that President Trump's agreement with Russian President Vladimir Putin appeared to violate US law prohibiting Russian energy imports until Moscow ends its aggression against Ukraine.

**[ANALYSIS]** The Russian diesel deal provides short-term price relief but creates new political complications. Oil remains elevated at ~$105-106 Brent despite the pullback.
