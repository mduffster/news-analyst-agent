# US-Israel-Iran Conflict: Saudi Coalition Strikes Yemen Oil Facilities as Houthis Threaten Aramco

## October 13, 2026 — Day 228

**Assessment:** The Saudi-Houthi confrontation entered a new phase of infrastructure-targeting escalation today as the Saudi-led coalition struck multiple oil facilities across Houthi-held Yemen—including sites in Sanaa, Hodeidah, and Dhamar—following evacuation warnings. The Houthis responded by designating specific Saudi Aramco facilities as "legitimate targets," warning civilians to evacuate Aramco headquarters in Dhahran, Riyadh refinery operations, and King Khalid International Airport ahead of imminent strikes. This represents the most significant mutual threat to energy infrastructure since the conflict widened in October. President Trump denounced the Houthis as "misbehaving very badly" but avoided committing to strikes, explicitly signaling that any major response would come after November 3 midterms. Meanwhile, Strait of Hormuz attacks reached 18 incidents in the first 13 days of October, including confirmed strikes on an ADNOC-owned VLCC, as Iran's IRGC Navy commander threatened to sink US warships approaching the strait. Oil prices softened to ~$105-106 on Russian diesel deal news, while the Iranian rial continued its remarkable week-long recovery, strengthening another 0.8% to 2,605,000/USD.

## Saudi Coalition Expands Target Set to Houthi Energy Infrastructure

The Saudi-led coalition crossed a significant threshold on October 13 by executing pre-announced strikes on multiple Houthi-controlled oil facilities—the first such coordinated infrastructure campaign since the conflict escalated.

The war between the Saudi-led coalition and Yemen's Iran-backed Houthi rebels escalated further on Tuesday, as both sides traded threats against oil installations. The coalition announced that it had carried out an operation against energy sites in Houthi-held areas of Yemen, including Al Sabahah station in the capital Sanaa and piers used for loading fuel at the port of Hodeidah. [VERIFIED]

The Saudi-led coalition said military operations were conducted against "vital assets" used by the Houthis to wage war in Yemen. In a statement posted on X, coalition spokesperson Major General Turki Al-Maliki says the targets included the Al-Sabaha central station in the capital Sanaa, underground fuel reserves and fuel tanks, and energy terminals in Hodeidah, Sanaa and Dhamar. [OFFICIAL]

Yemen's Houthi-run health ministry said two people were killed when an oil company in the Red Sea province of Hodeidah was attacked, blaming Saudi Arabia for the strike. [OFFICIAL - Houthi claim]

The YPC sought to reassure the public, saying it had taken all necessary precautions in the event of an "emergency" to "meet people's needs." "Al-Sabaha facilities currently do not contain any stock of petroleum products, and therefore, targeting them will not affect fuel stock or supply chains," it said in a statement. [REPORTED]

**Why this matters:** The strikes represent a deliberate shift from reactive defense to offensive degradation of Houthi logistics capacity. The pre-announced evacuation warning—unprecedented in this phase of the conflict—suggests Saudi Arabia is attempting to calibrate military pressure while managing civilian casualty concerns and potential international criticism. However, the operational effect may be limited if facilities were indeed emptied of petroleum products as the Houthis claim.

## Houthis Designate Specific Aramco Facilities as Attack Targets

The Houthis issued their most detailed targeting threat to date, naming specific Saudi oil infrastructure slated for strikes.

Yemen's Houthis have warned all employees and civilians near Saudi Arabia's oil facilities to evacuate, calling the sites "a legitimate target." The warning on Tuesday from the group's spokesman, Yahya Saree, was prompted by an evacuation order from the Saudi-led coalition urging civilians to evacuate Yemeni Oil Company's al-Sabah central station in the Houthi-controlled capital Sanaa. [OFFICIAL - Houthi statement]

Yemen's Houthi movement has issued an evacuation warning for Riyadh's airport and two Aramco facilities in Saudi Arabia on Wednesday, shortly after a massive strike targeted a Yemeni oil facility near Sanaa. It said it will target the Aramco oil facilities in Dhahran, east of the kingdom, and the capital Riyadh, as well as the King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh. [OFFICIAL - Houthi statement]

Earlier in the day, the coalition had urged civilians to leave Al Sabahah, owned by the Yemen Petroleum Company, before what it described as a "special operation." In response to the strikes, the Houthis announced that oil installations in Saudi Arabia would be considered legitimate targets and that any civilians working at those sites should stay away. [VERIFIED]

**Key Assessment:** The Houthi threat to strike Aramco facilities represents the highest-stakes development of the day. A successful attack on Aramco's Dhahran headquarters or Riyadh refinery operations would mark a fundamental escalation from aviation disruption (reputational/economic damage) to direct attacks on global energy supply infrastructure. The explicit naming of targets suggests this is not merely rhetorical deterrence but operational signaling. Whether the Houthis possess the precision capability to strike specific industrial facilities—versus airports—remains uncertain, but their demonstrated reach to Riyadh and Dammam indicates the threat is credible.

## Saudi Air Defenses Intercept New Attacks; Airport Closure Extends

Saudi defenses successfully intercepted new Houthi missiles and drones on October 13, though the aviation disruption continues to spread.

Saudi Arabia intercepted more missiles and drones launched by Yemen's Houthis on Tuesday, as fighting with the Iran-backed militant group showed few signs of subsiding. Three drones north of Riyadh were intercepted and two ballistic missiles fired toward Khamis Mushait near the Yemeni border were shot down, a spokesperson for the Saudi-led coalition in Yemen said Tuesday. [OFFICIAL]

Saudi Arabia has launched an investigation into why its air defenses failed to intercept a barrage of Houthi missiles and drones that struck Riyadh's international airport, killing at least 13 people and injuring more than 300, The Associated Press reported Monday. [REPORTED]

Etihad Airways has cancelled all flights between Abu Dhabi and Riyadh until Monday, October 19, extending disruption to services between the UAE and the Saudi capital into next week. Flydubai has also cancelled Riyadh flights until Wednesday, October 14, while Emirates and Air Arabia services have been affected. [VERIFIED]

The disruption continues beyond Riyadh, with flights to and from other Saudi destinations, including Dammam, Jeddah and Abha, also cancelled. Aviation analytics company Cirium said 46.4 per cent of scheduled flights across Saudi Arabia had been cancelled as of 4pm UAE time on Monday. [VERIFIED]

Qatar Airways: Riyadh and Dammam temporarily suspended; the airline's 12 October notice gives no restart date. KLM: Riyadh and Dammam suspended through 31 October. Its corrected notice separately places Dubai suspensions through 24 October. [REPORTED]

**Significance:** The extension of airline cancellations—now affecting major carriers through at least October 19, with KLM suspended through October 31—indicates the crisis has moved from emergency disruption to institutionalized operational change. The 46% cancellation rate across all Saudi aviation represents sustained economic damage beyond Riyadh.

## Trump Signals Post-Election Response, Declines Immediate Action

President Trump condemned the Houthis but explicitly deferred any US military response until after the November 3 midterm elections.

United States President Donald Trump has said that the Houthi rebel group in Yemen are stepping up attacks on infrastructure in Saudi Arabia in a bid to increase energy prices in the run-up to the US midterm elections. Trump said on Tuesday that the Houthis were behaving "very badly" and repeated his threat that the US could step up military attacks across the Middle East after the November elections. [OFFICIAL]

President Donald Trump has not confirmed whether he will authorize strikes on Iran-backed Houthi rebels following recent attacks in Saudi Arabia. Speaking to reporters outside the White House, Trump described the Houthis as "behaving very badly" but did not elaborate on any military plans. [OFFICIAL]

Trump mentioned he might request Turkish President Tayyip Erdogan to support Saudi interests, indicating potential international collaboration. [REPORTED]

"If the US becomes involved in the aggression against Yemen, we will not deliberate over the decision to respond, rather, there will be a swift and painful response targeting the US and all its interests in the region," Houthi political bureau member Mohammed Al Bukhaiti told the European news agency. [OFFICIAL - Houthi statement]

**Analysis:** Trump's framing—accusing Houthis of deliberately driving up oil prices to harm Republicans in the midterms—serves dual purposes: shifting blame for high fuel costs while preserving the option for post-election military action. The reference to Turkish involvement is new and may reflect exploration of alternative regional partnerships, though Turkey's practical capacity to project force into Yemen is limited. The Houthi counter-threat of "swift and painful" response to US involvement establishes deterrent messaging but is unlikely to prevent post-midterm US action if Trump commits to it.

## Strait of Hormuz: 18 Attacks in October as IRGC Threatens US Warships

Iran's maritime pressure campaign intensified with confirmed strikes on high-value targets and escalating rhetoric from IRGC commanders.

The UKMTO has reported four more attacks on commercial ships in the Strait of Hormuz on 11 October, while Iran claims to have hit an ADNOC Logistics & Services VLCC. The UK naval organisation issued four "late-time reports" of attacks that took place in the Strait of Hormuz on 11 October involving three tankers and a container ship. [VERIFIED]

The video showed the apparently laden Liberian-registered VLCC Al Maqam with a fire blackened funnel and a tug attempting to extinguish a fire on the vessel. The Al Maqam is owned and managed by ADNOC Logistics and Services according to Pole Star Global. [VERIFIED]

The UKMTO has issued 18 reports of attacks on vessels in the Strait of Hormuz and the surrounding region during October as Iran has ramped up its targeting of commercial shipping which it claims are transiting the waterway without its permission as it continues to try and exert control over the Strait. [VERIFIED]

A tanker and a container ship were struck by unknown projectiles in the Strait of Hormuz on Tuesday, the United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations said in separate notices citing verified sources. Authorities are investigating both incidents, whose exact times have yet to be confirmed. The notices did not provide details of damage or casualties. [VERIFIED]

IRGC Navy commander Ali Azmaei said Tuesday Iran was waiting for US warships to approach the Strait of Hormuz so they could be sunk, warning that if Washington believed the waterway was open, its forces should "come closer and see who controls" it. "The US military's seven fleets have turned into fuel smugglers. They have moved away from the Strait of Hormuz because they are vulnerable," he said. [OFFICIAL - IRGC statement]

US Secretary of State Marco Rubio rejected that assessment last week, saying commercial oil flows had largely recovered despite Iran's efforts to disrupt shipping. "The Strait of Hormuz is open. There's almost as much oil flowing out now as there was before this conflict began," he said. "So they've lost complete control of the strait." [OFFICIAL]

**Assessment:** The strike on an ADNOC-owned VLCC is particularly significant as it targets a vessel belonging to a major US regional partner. The 18 attacks in 13 days represents a near-daily tempo that is unsustainable for commercial operations absent military escort. The competing narratives from IRGC commanders and Secretary Rubio reflect genuinely contested control—oil is flowing, but at high risk and cost. The IRGC commander's threats against US warships suggest Iran is testing whether Trump's pre-midterm restraint extends to defensive operations.

## Oil Markets and Currency

Crude oil prices declined on Tuesday, with Brent crude futures falling 1% to $105.6 a barrel and US West Texas Intermediate (WTI) crude futures dropping 1% to $92.8 a barrel. Prospects of additional Russian diesel supplies eased some concerns over fuel availability, although escalating geopolitical tensions in the Middle East continued to threaten global energy flows. [VERIFIED]

Brent fell to 105.91 USD/Bbl on October 13, 2026, down 0.70% from the previous day. Over the past month, Brent's price has risen 0.22%, and is up 69.75% compared to the same time last year, according to trading on a contract for difference (CFD) that tracks the benchmark market for this commodity. [VERIFIED]

On Tuesday 13 October 2026, the price of US Dollar in the market was 2,605,000 Iranian Rials. This is a 22,000 IRR (0.84%) decrease compared to yesterday. The price of US Dollar in Iranian Rials continued its downward trend. It fell by 30,000 Rials yesterday to 2,627,000, and today (Tuesday 13 October 2026), it declined by another 22,000 Rials (0.84%) to 2,605,000 Rials. [VERIFIED]

Compared to a week ago, the price has seen a 3.41% decrease. [VERIFIED]

**Rial Analysis:** The Iranian rial's continued strengthening—now 3.4% stronger over the past week—is a notable counter-indicator to military escalation. Potential explanations include: Central Bank intervention to stabilize expectations, market anticipation of eventual negotiations, or reduced panic selling as the conflict enters a more stable phase. The recovery from ~2,718,000 to 2,605,000/USD over seven days suggests Iranian domestic markets may be pricing in a contained conflict rather than regime collapse.
