## Critical Developments Since May 26

### 1. Trump Cabinet Meeting: "Finish the Job" Warning

**[OFFICIAL]** President Donald Trump held a Cabinet meeting at the White House, canceling a plan for a session at Camp David due to weather concerns.

**[OFFICIAL]** Trump said Iran is "negotiating on fumes" and "they want very much to make a deal." "So far, they haven't gotten there, we're not satisfied with it, but we will be," Trump said. "We will be. Either that or we'll have to just finish the job. Their navy is gone, as I've said a thousand times, their navy is gone, their air force is gone, everything's gone."

**[OFFICIAL]** Trump vowed he wouldn't be pressured by political timelines: "'We'll out wait him. He's got the midterms.' I don't care about the midterms. Look what happened last night. That was the prelude to the midterms... They know that, very simple, Iran cannot have a nuclear weapon."

**[REPORTED]** In a separate interview with PBS News, Trump said Tehran would receive no concessions or sanctions relief in exchange for handing over its highly enriched uranium. He said any such arrangement would not include the lifting of sanctions.

**Assessment:** The shift from Camp David symbolism to a combative White House posture signals Trump is playing hardball—either as negotiating theater or genuine frustration with Iran's position. The "finish the job" threat is the clearest escalation warning since the ceasefire began. However, his explicit acknowledgment that Iran "wants very much to make a deal" suggests both sides remain engaged.

### 2. Iranian State TV Publishes Draft Deal — White House Calls It "Fabrication"

**[REPORTED]** Iranian state broadcaster IRIB said on Wednesday that Tehran had obtained a draft of an initial, unofficial framework for a memorandum of understanding with the United States. Under the reported framework, Iran would restore commercial shipping through the Strait of Hormuz to prewar levels within a month, while the United States would withdraw its military forces from the vicinity of Iran and lift a naval blockade.

**[OFFICIAL]** The White House on Wednesday pushed back on Iranian state media reports about a draft of the memorandum of understanding, calling it a "complete fabrication." "This report from Iranian controlled media is not true and the MOU they 'released' is a complete fabrication. Nobody should believe what Iranian state media is putting out."

**[REPORTED]** Some of the details are similar to how US officials have described the emerging deal. American officials have said President Donald Trump would be willing to lift the blockade as long as Iran allows commercial vessels to transit the Strait of Hormuz.

**[REPORTED]** IRIB is regarded as led by hardline elements in the Iranian regime. The report added that the framework of the deal is not yet finalized, and no step will be taken by Tehran without "tangible verification." Negotiations were "currently moving back and forth, with both sides refining the wording of the draft text."

**[REPORTED]** IRGC-affiliated Fars News Agency reported, citing informed sources, that President Donald Trump may unilaterally announce in the coming hours that an agreement between the United States and the Islamic Republic has been finalized.

**Assessment:** This is a classic pattern in high-stakes negotiations—one side leaks favorable terms, the other denies them. The fact that markets moved sharply on the Iranian report (despite the US denial) suggests traders believe the broad framework is real, even if specific details remain contested. The 30-day Hormuz normalization timeline is consistent with previously reported terms.

### 3. Oil Prices Plunge Despite Mixed Signals — Markets Betting on Deal

**[VERIFIED]** At 9 a.m. Eastern Time today, the price of oil sits at $96.28 per barrel, using Brent as the benchmark. That's a decrease of $3.92 since yesterday morning and roughly $32 more than at this time last year.

**[VERIFIED]** For context: On May 20, 2026, the price of oil was $110.34 per barrel—meaning Brent has fallen **~13%** in one week.

**[VERIFIED]** Brent crude futures, the international benchmark, gained more than 3% to close at $99.58 a barrel on Tuesday compared to its Monday settlement. West Texas Intermediate futures fell nearly 3% to settle at $93.89 per barrel compared with Friday.

**[VERIFIED]** Swiss multinational investment bank UBS said Friday the global oil market was showing mounting signs of strain as inventories continue to fall amid continued disruptions to shipments via the Strait of Hormuz. Observed global oil inventories dropped by a combined 246 million barrels in March and April, while cumulative production losses could exceed 1 billion barrels by the end of May. The sharp inventory drawdowns suggest the market remains "strongly undersupplied."

**Assessment:** Markets are decisively betting on a deal. The ~$14 drop from May 20's $110 to today's $96 is the most significant price correction of the crisis. However, the UBS warning about being "strongly undersupplied" suggests vulnerability to "violent repricing" if talks fail.

### 4. Israel Escalates Lebanon Campaign — Deadliest Day Since Ceasefire

**[VERIFIED]** Israel's military clashed with the Iran-backed militant Hezbollah group on Tuesday along a strategic river in southern Lebanon as Israeli troops pushed farther north, as Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu vowed Israel will expand its operations in Lebanon. At least 31 people were killed in the latest attacks, including several children, according to Lebanon's health ministry.

**[VERIFIED]** Two sources said on Tuesday that the Israeli military had expanded its ground operations in southern Lebanon past the security zone but gave no further details on the extent of the advance beyond the so-called Yellow Line. Israel pounded Lebanon with more than 120 air strikes on Tuesday in one of the heaviest days of bombing in weeks.

**[VERIFIED]** Netanyahu said the Israeli military "is operating with large forces in the field and capturing and controlling areas." "We are fortifying the security strip to protect the northern communities," he said, referring to a self-declared security zone occupied by Israeli troops several kilometers inside southern Lebanon. Two sources said on Tuesday that the Israeli military had expanded its ground operations in southern Lebanon past the security zone.

**[VERIFIED]** The escalation leaves the US-brokered ceasefire in the Israel-Hezbollah conflict nominal by the day, complicating efforts at a broader peace in the Iran war, as Tehran wants an agreement to include an end to the fighting in Lebanon. The Litani River has been a de facto boundary in Lebanon, with large areas to the south under Israeli military control.

**[VERIFIED]** Lebanon's health ministry says the cumulative toll from the Israeli offensive since March 2 had reached 3,213 dead and 9,737 wounded as of May 26. The Israeli military said that 10 of its soldiers had been killed since the April 16 ceasefire, six of them by Hezbollah's explosive drones. The World Health Organization has said at least 608 people in Lebanon have been killed in Israeli attacks since the truce.

**Assessment:** Israel appears to be deliberately escalating ahead of the May 29 Pentagon talks—likely to establish facts on the ground and ensure any US-Iran deal doesn't constrain Israeli operations in Lebanon. This creates a structural contradiction: Iran asserted that Lebanon must be included as part of a ceasefire deal with the United States and Israel, but Israel is actively expanding beyond ceasefire lines.

### 5. Iran Internet Restored After 88-Day Blackout — With Heavier Restrictions

**[VERIFIED]** On 25 May, President Masoud Pezeshkian gave an order to end the internet blackout. On 26 May, Iran partially restored internet.

**[VERIFIED]** Iran restored global Internet access to some of its people Tuesday ending a blackout that has lasted 88 days. NetBlocks posted on X: "Confirmed: Live metrics show a partial restoration to Internet connectivity in Iran on day 88, after 2,093 hours of near-total isolation from international networks, the longest nationwide Internet shutdown in modern history. It is unclear if the restoration will be sustained."

**[VERIFIED]** Iranian authorities have eased a monthslong internet shutdown that they cast as a wartime necessity, but that has cost the country's economy an estimated $30 million to $40 million a day.

**[REPORTED]** NetBlocks research director said: "Access is not universally back to its original state, with some regional variation." She added that there were signs of "more extensive filtering" than prior to January—"including additional restrictions to messaging apps like WhatsApp."

**[VERIFIED]** "Historically, each time Internet access has been restored after an Internet shutdown in Iran, it has come back with heavier restrictions and tighter controls." Internet analysis company Kentik said that Iran's access was still less than 10% of pre-shutdown levels.

**Assessment:** The internet restoration is significant for two reasons: (1) it signals the regime is trying to reduce domestic economic pressure, possibly to create space for a deal, and (2) it will allow outside observers to monitor internal conditions for the first time in three months. However, the restoration is partial and comes with heavier controls.

### 6. Deal Framework: Key Sticking Points Remain

**[REPORTED]** Iran and the United States have signaled they are closing in on an agreement to turn the existing ceasefire that ended weeks of conflict into a more long-lasting settlement. Both sides are talking of a "memorandum of understanding" that will set out a roadmap for resolving all outstanding issues.

**[REPORTED]** Iran appears to be signaling that while it may allow commercial traffic to return to pre-war volumes, it still intends to maintain a greater degree of control over passage through the strait than existed before the conflict. "Military ships are not included in this commitment" to reopen the Strait, IRIB said Wednesday.

**[OFFICIAL]** A senior US administration official told CNN on Sunday that the unfreezing of Iranian assets will occur only once the Strait of Hormuz has reopened. The US has given no commitment on how these assets, which are held in several foreign banks, will be returned to Iran.

**[VERIFIED]** On May 27, Trump said both sides were close to finalizing an agreement involving strong inspections. Araghchi stated he was unsure whether a deal was imminent, while Khamenei's advisor, Ali Shamkhani, dismissed Trump's desired control over the Iranian nuclear program as a "fantasy".

**Assessment:** The sequencing dispute remains the core obstacle: US wants Hormuz open before sanctions relief; Iran wants assets before Hormuz. The leaked draft suggests both sides are close on structure but far apart on implementation timing.
