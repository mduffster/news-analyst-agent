### Critical Developments (May 26-27)

#### 1. Cabinet Meeting: Trump Takes Harder Line, Camp David Canceled

**[OFFICIAL]** President Donald Trump held a Cabinet meeting at the White House, canceling a plan for a session at Camp David due to weather concerns.

**[OFFICIAL]** Trump's rhetoric at the Cabinet meeting was notably more aggressive than recent days: Trump said: "So far, they haven't gotten there, we're not satisfied with it, but we will be... Either that or we'll have to just finish the job. Their navy is gone, as I've said a thousand times, their navy is gone, their air force is gone, everything's gone."

**[OFFICIAL]** Trump vowed to strike a favorable deal, warning that Iran's efforts to outlast him won't work because "I don't care about the midterms." He said: "They thought they were going to outwait me, you know? 'We'll outwait him, he's got the midterms.' I don't care about the midterms. Look what happened last night, that was a prelude to the midterms"—referring to a Texas GOP primary win.

**Assessment:** The shift from Camp David symbolism to a combative White House posture signals Trump is playing hardball—either as negotiating theater or genuine frustration with Iran's position. The "finish the job" threat is the clearest escalation warning since the ceasefire began.

#### 2. Iranian Draft Deal Published, White House Denies

**[REPORTED]** An unofficial draft of a US-Iran interim peace deal says maritime traffic through the Strait of Hormuz can return to normal within a month of the agreement being finalized, according to Iranian state television. It's unclear how recent the draft, reported by IRIB News, is or whether the US has agreed to the terms.

**[OFFICIAL]** The White House dismissed the report as "a complete fabrication".

**[VERIFIED]** Oil markets nonetheless reacted strongly to the Iranian claim: WTI plunged 6% to $88.3 per barrel on Wednesday after Iranian state television said Tehran is committed to restoring commercial shipping through the Strait of Hormuz to pre-war levels within a month.

**Assessment:** This is a classic pattern in high-stakes negotiations—one side leaks favorable terms, the other denies it. The fact that markets moved sharply on the Iranian report (despite the US denial) suggests traders believe the broad framework is real, even if specific details are contested. The 30-day Hormuz normalization timeline is consistent with previously reported terms.

#### 3. Oil Prices Plunge Despite Mixed Signals

**[VERIFIED]** At 9 a.m. Eastern Time today, Brent sits at $96.28 per barrel—a decrease of $3.92 since yesterday morning.

**[VERIFIED]** Brent fell 3.5% to $96.12 a barrel at 5 a.m. ET; WTI slipped almost 4% to $90.10 a barrel. "Investors' optimism about a pathway to peace remains intact despite threats by the IRGC to retaliate." Analyst Stephen Innes cautioned: "Markets continue pricing diplomacy first and physical oil market fragility second, leaving crude vulnerable to violent repricing if normalization through Hormuz proves slower than expected."

**[VERIFIED]** For context, this is a major reversal from recent highs: On May 20, 2026, the price of oil was $110.34 per barrel—meaning Brent has fallen ~13% in one week.

**Assessment:** Markets are decisively betting on a deal. The reversal from yesterday's ~$100 Brent to today's ~$96 is significant. However, the analyst warning about "violent repricing" if talks fail is worth noting—the downside risk is asymmetric.

#### 4. Strait of Hormuz: First Meaningful Transits in a Week

**[REPORTED]** Commercial shipping through the Strait of Hormuz gained modest momentum over the past 24 hours as at least two non-Iranian supertankers exited the Persian Gulf. In total, five ships were seen crossing Hormuz in either direction. It's the first time in a week that four million barrels of unsanctioned crude have been seen crossing the waterway, with ships leaving in bunches and then lower volumes passing in subsequent days.

**[VERIFIED]** Major issues remain unresolved, including Iran's frozen assets and guarantees over shipping access. The Strait remains largely closed, although two non-Iranian supertankers crossed Hormuz on Tuesday for the first time in a week.

**Assessment:** This is the first sign of physical movement in Hormuz since the Monday strikes. It's a small sample, but it suggests Iran is allowing some transit even as talks continue—a confidence-building measure rather than a breakthrough.

#### 5. Iran Internet Restored After 88-Day Blackout

**[VERIFIED]** On 25 May, President Masoud Pezeshkian gave an order to end the internet blackout. On 26 May, Iran partially restored internet.

**[VERIFIED]** NetBlocks posted: "Confirmed: Live metrics show a partial restoration to Internet connectivity in Iran on day 88, after 2,093 hours of near-total isolation from international networks, the longest nationwide Internet shutdown in modern history. It is unclear if the restoration will be sustained."

**[VERIFIED]** The monthslong internet shutdown has cost the country's economy an estimated $30 million to $40 million a day.

**[REPORTED]** NetBlocks' research director said there were signs of "more extensive filtering" than prior to January—"including additional restrictions to messaging apps like WhatsApp".

**Assessment:** The internet restoration is significant for two reasons: (1) it signals the regime is trying to reduce domestic pressure, possibly to create space for a deal, and (2) it will allow outside observers to monitor internal conditions for the first time in three months. However, the restoration is partial and comes with heavier controls.

#### 6. Israel Escalates Lebanon Campaign to Deadliest Day Since Ceasefire

**[VERIFIED]** Israeli airstrikes intensified sharply on Tuesday, killing at least 31 people, including six children, and wounding dozens across southern Lebanon. The dead included 14 people in a single attack on Borj El Chmali, east of the coastal Tyre city.

**[VERIFIED]** Israeli officials claimed to have struck over 100 Hezbollah targets, including infrastructure and fighters, in what they described as the most aggressive night of bombing since a U.S.-mediated ceasefire took effect in mid-April.

**[VERIFIED]** Israeli forces issued around 50 forced displacement orders for towns and villages in the south and east, as well as of Nabatieh, a major Lebanese city—signalling the potential for a larger offensive.

**[REPORTED]** Reports suggest Israeli troops have advanced beyond the "Yellow Line" established under the 16 April ceasefire. This dual air and ground escalation comes just days before Israeli and Lebanese military delegations are due to meet at the Pentagon for US-mediated security talks.

**Assessment:** Israel appears to be deliberately escalating ahead of the May 29 Pentagon talks—likely to establish facts on the ground and ensure any US-Iran deal doesn't constrain Israeli operations. According to David Wood, senior analyst at the International Crisis Group, Israel's actions point to a broader strategic objective: ensuring that any US-Iran agreement does not limit its military freedom in Lebanon.

#### 7. Pentagon Funding Strain Emerging

**[REPORTED]** The Pentagon is feeling the financial squeeze and is struggling in some cases to carry out routine training and maintenance amid its ongoing operations against Iran. The Navy's top officer, Adm. Daryl Caudle, told House Armed Services Committee lawmakers his 2026 budget "didn't bake in (Operation) Epic Fury" and that the Navy faces impacts on "routine operations" as a result—including limiting training exercises, flight training hours, and training for new recruits. "My record recruiting is going to be thwarted without additional funding."

**Assessment:** This is the first concrete evidence of military readiness impacts from the Iran campaign. If Congress returns June 2 without a deal in hand, the supplemental funding debate could become contentious.
