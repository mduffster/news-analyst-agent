### Executive Summary

**MAJOR DEVELOPMENT: Framework Agreement Emerging**

The biggest development since May 22 is the emergence of a concrete framework deal between the US and Iran. Key changes:

1. **US-Iran "Memorandum of Understanding" nearing signature** — Senior Trump official says agreement "will be signed in the coming days" with Khamenei's approval of the template
2. **Oil crashes ~$7 to ~$98 (Brent)** — Sharpest decline of the crisis as markets price in potential Hormuz reopening
3. **Iran agrees "in principle" to dispose of enriched uranium** — But details deferred to 60-day negotiation period
4. **Israeli opposition attacks deal** — Lapid calls it "a disaster" that fails to achieve any of Israel's war goals
5. **Three LNG tankers transit Hormuz** — First sign of physical easing in shipping blockade

**Bottom line:** After weeks of stalemate, a framework deal has emerged that would extend the ceasefire 60 days, reopen Hormuz, allow Iran to sell oil, and defer nuclear issues to subsequent talks. This represents a significant pivot from the previous impasse. However, the deal defers rather than resolves the core disputes. Iran has not committed to surrendering its enriched uranium upfront—only to "disposing" of it during negotiations. Israel is notably unhappy and elections are coming. The most likely near-term outcome has shifted from extended stalemate to "formalized interim agreement with unresolved fundamentals."

### Critical New Developments (May 22-25)

#### 1. Framework Agreement Emerging — "Signed in Coming Days"

**[OFFICIAL]** A senior US official briefed reporters that an agreement with Iran "will be signed in the coming days," and that Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei has approved the broad framework of the agreement. Still, nothing is done until it's done, the official reportedly clarifies, and there is still wording that each side wants to work on.

**[OFFICIAL]** The agreement the U.S. and Iran are close to signing involves a 60-day ceasefire extension during which the Strait of Hormuz would be reopened, Iran would be able to freely sell oil, and negotiations would be held on curbing Iran's nuclear program.

**[OFFICIAL]** Rubio, on an official visit to India, said the agreement being discussed with Iran was "a pretty solid thing on the table in terms of their ability to open up the strait." He said the U.S. intended to engage in "a very real, significant, time-limited negotiation on the nuclear matter."

**[OFFICIAL]** According to the official, the potential deal would make sure Iran can never possess a nuclear weapon and would commit it to giving up highly enriched uranium. "The important part of how this is structured is, if Iran doesn't perform, they don't get anything. No dust? No dollars. As the Strait opens, the blockade loosens proportionately," the official said. "This is 'trust but verify' on steroids."

**[OFFICIAL]** Russia has offered to take the enriched uranium.

**Assessment:** This represents a significant breakthrough from the impasse of May 22. The framework creates a structured path forward, but defers the hardest decisions (uranium disposal mechanics, enrichment rights, missile program) to subsequent talks. The 60-day clock creates both opportunity and pressure.

#### 2. Iranian Position: Uranium Commitment Disputed

**[OFFICIAL]** Iran's state-backed ISNA news agency quoted a senior Iranian diplomat as saying that Iran has not made any commitment in negotiations on the initial agreement on nuclear issues, including highly enriched uranium.

**[OFFICIAL]** Iran's foreign ministry spokesperson Esmaeil Baghaei said on Monday that Iran was negotiating an end to the war and was not currently discussing nuclear issues. The spokesperson added that a framework had been reached but no one could say an agreement between the United States and Iran was imminent. The potential memorandum of understanding contained no specific details about the management of the Strait of Hormuz, which belongs to the coastal countries, he said.

**[OFFICIAL]** A senior Iranian source told Reuters that Tehran has not agreed to hand over its highly enriched uranium stockpile under the preliminary agreement.

**[REPORTED]** Iran has agreed in principle to dispose of highly-enriched uranium in negotiations with the U.S., a senior Trump administration official said Sunday morning. The official said the U.S. believes Iran's supreme leader has approved the template for a deal, but a final agreement still needs to be made before anything is signed. They said it is still an open question as to whether that will come to fruition.

**Assessment:** The US and Iran are publicly offering different interpretations of the same framework. The US says Iran agreed "in principle" to uranium disposal; Iran says no nuclear commitments were made. This gap is significant—either one side is mischaracterizing the deal, or the framework is deliberately ambiguous. Both sides may be positioning for domestic audiences.

**[VERIFIED]** Crude Oil fell to 91.73 USD/Bbl on May 25, 2026, down 5.05% from the previous day. Over the past month, Crude Oil's price has fallen 4.82%, but it is still 50.64% higher than a year ago.

**[VERIFIED]** Oil prices fell 5% to two-week lows on Monday, as optimism grew that the U.S. and Iran were moving closer to a peace deal.

**[VERIFIED]** WTI crude futures tumbled nearly 6% to below $91 a barrel on Monday, as optimism grew that the US and Iran could soon reach an agreement to end the conflict and reopen the Strait of Hormuz. Still, uncertainty remains high, with officials on both sides signaling that key issues are yet to be resolved.

**[REPORTED]** Despite the lingering uncertainty, ship-tracking data indicated a gradual easing in regional disruptions, with three liquefied natural gas tankers recently passing through the Strait of Hormuz en route to Pakistan, China, and India.

**Assessment:** Oil's move from ~$104 to ~$98 (Brent) represents the largest single-day decline of the crisis. Markets are pricing in a high probability of Hormuz reopening. The LNG tanker transits are the first concrete sign of physical easing, though traffic remains at ~2% of pre-war levels.

#### 4. Israeli Opposition Attacks Deal — Elections Loom

**[OFFICIAL]** The deal being discussed between the U.S. and Iran fails to achieve any of Israel's goals for the war, Israel's opposition leader Yair Lapid said on Monday. "The deal is bad for Israel, bad for the region, bad for the citizens of Iran," Lapid told reporters in Jerusalem. Israel and the U.S. launched the war on Feb. 28 vowing to destroy Iran's ballistic missile program, end its support for proxy militant groups across the region and end Iran's ability to pursue a nuclear bomb.

**[OFFICIAL]** Addressing reporters ahead of his Yesh Atid party's weekly faction meeting in the Knesset, Opposition Leader Yair Lapid calls the agreement currently shaping up between Washington and Tehran a "disaster," arguing that "the regime did not fall, it grew stronger" and that the emerging agreement does not deal with the Iranian ballistic missile threat.

**[OFFICIAL]** "The Israeli government is at an all-time low in its ability to influence decisions in Washington," he said, noting that Trump said last week: "Netanyahu will do whatever I want him to do."

**[VERIFIED]** They have once again merged their parties into single faction headed by Bennett as they attempt to unseat Netanyahu in elections which will be held by the end of October. Lapid has served as Israel's opposition leader since Netanyahu returned to power in late 2022, while Bennett took a break from politics.

**[REPORTED]** According to Axios, the Memorandum of Understanding being discussed makes clear that the conflict between Israel and Hezbollah will end, though Israel will be permitted to strike Hezbollah if the terror group instigates or carries out attacks.

**Assessment:** The emerging deal is becoming a major Israeli election issue. Netanyahu faces pressure from multiple directions: the opposition says he failed to achieve war goals, while hawks in his coalition may oppose any deal short of Iranian capitulation. Trump's reported comment that "Netanyahu will do whatever I want him to do" underscores Israel's diminished leverage in Washington.

#### 5. Strait of Hormuz: First Signs of Easing

**[VERIFIED]** The U.S. blockade of the Strait of Hormuz has now hit the century mark. There have now been 100 commercial ships either entering or leaving Iranian ports redirected by the blockade.

**[REPORTED]** Ship-tracking data indicated a gradual easing in regional disruptions, with three liquefied natural gas tankers recently passing through the Strait of Hormuz en route to Pakistan, China, and India.

**[VERIFIED]** Per IMF PortWatch, 2 vessels transited the Strait of Hormuz on 2026-05-17 — 2% of typical. On MAY 17, IMF PortWatch's most recent published day, commercial transit through the Strait reached 2% of pre-crisis volume — 2 vessels against a typical 95 per day.

**[OFFICIAL]** Tasnim news agency accused the U.S. of "obstructionism" over the release of some frozen Iranian funds. The news agency also said the agreement being negotiated called on Iran to restore the number of ships transiting through the vital waterway to pre-war levels within 30 days and for the U.S. to completely lift its blockade within the same time. Another state-backed agency, ISNA, said Iran would insist on administering the strait along with Oman.

**Assessment:** The strait remains effectively closed (2% of normal traffic), but the LNG tanker transits and emerging deal suggest a path to reopening. The proposed 30-day timeline for restoring pre-war traffic levels is ambitious given 1,500+ stranded vessels and extensive mine-clearing requirements.

**Assessment:** The Lebanon ceasefire has been extended 45 days (through early July), but fundamental issues—Israeli withdrawal, Hezbollah disarmament—remain unresolved. The emerging Iran deal may link Lebanon outcomes to broader negotiations, potentially freezing the status quo.

### Key Emerging Issues

1. **US-Iran Interpretation Gap** — The US says Iran agreed to uranium disposal; Iran denies making nuclear commitments. This ambiguity may be intentional to allow both sides to sell the deal domestically, but creates fragility.

2. **Israeli Elections Factor** — Bennett and Lapid have merged their parties to unseat Netanyahu in elections by the end of October. Netanyahu faces pressure from opposition (deal is too soft) and coalition hawks (same). His political survival may depend on how he spins the emerging agreement.

3. **60-Day Clock** — The framework creates a structured negotiation period but also a deadline. The official said it's possible the deal won't even last the full 60 days if the U.S. believes Iran is not serious about nuclear negotiations. July becomes the next potential crisis point.

4. **Russia as Uranium Repository** — Russia has offered to take Iran's enriched uranium. This creates geopolitical complications—Russia gains leverage over the deal's success while the West depends on Moscow's cooperation.

5. **Congressional Preemption** — If Trump signs a deal before Congress returns June 2, war powers votes become moot. The administration may be racing to lock in the framework before the House can act.

### Bottom Line

**The situation has fundamentally shifted from extended stalemate to active deal-making.**

Three days ago, the assessment was that "structural obstacles to a comprehensive deal are hardening." Today, a framework agreement is reportedly days away from signature. This reflects either genuine breakthrough or a mutual decision to paper over differences with an interim deal.

**What the deal does:** Extends ceasefire 60 days, reopens Hormuz, allows Iran to sell oil, lifts blockades, creates negotiation period for nuclear issues.

**What the deal doesn't do:** Require Iran to surrender enriched uranium upfront, address missiles or Hezbollah, guarantee permanent peace, satisfy Israeli war goals.

**The most likely near-term outcome has shifted from "extended stalemate" to "formalized interim agreement with unresolved fundamentals."** Oil markets are pricing this in aggressively. The question is whether the 60-day negotiation period produces substantive nuclear concessions or simply delays the next crisis.

**Israel is the wildcard.** Netanyahu has not publicly responded to the emerging deal. With elections by October and coalition hawks demanding maximalist outcomes, Israeli actions during the 60-day period could derail the framework. "The prime minister emphasized that Israel will preserve its freedom of action against threats in all arenas, including Lebanon."
