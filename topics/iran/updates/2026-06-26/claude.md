## US-Israel-Iran Conflict: Daily Update — June 26, 2026

### Summary

**KEY DEVELOPMENTS: The IMO has paused its Strait of Hormuz ship evacuation operation after Iran struck a Singapore-flagged cargo ship with a drone on Thursday — the first attack since the MOU was signed. US officials attributed the attack to the IRGC, marking a significant escalation. Israel-Lebanon talks in Washington extended to a fourth day amid deadlock on IDF withdrawal and pilot zones. Four IDF soldiers were wounded in a clash with Hezbollah in southern Lebanon overnight. Oil prices fell further to ~$73.74, continuing the post-MOU decline despite the ship attack. Technical US-Iran talks remain scheduled for June 29-30.**

### Key Developments Since June 25

#### 1. CRITICAL: IRGC Confirmed Behind Ship Attack — IMO Pauses Evacuation

**[REPORTED]** A Singapore-flagged cargo ship was struck by a drone Thursday while leaving the Strait of Hormuz, U.S. officials said, attributing the attack to Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps.

**[VERIFIED]** The update came minutes after U.S. officials confirmed Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) attacked and hit Singapore-flagged cargo ship "Ever Lovely" while exiting the Strait of Hormuz with a drone.

**[REPORTED]** U.S. officials attributed the attack to the IRGC, per reporting by the Jerusalem Post citing the Wall Street Journal. That attribution has not been confirmed by a CENTCOM or State Department primary statement as of publication.

**[OFFICIAL]** IMO Secretary-General Mr. Arsenio Dominguez stated: "Following the launch of the IMO's evacuation plan, through which several vessels have already been successfully evacuated, I have decided to temporarily pause its implementation in order to reconfirm that the necessary safety guarantees continue to be in place for the ships on our evacuation list and all those in the region. I have been informed of an attack today in the Gulf of Oman on a vessel which passed through the Strait of Hormuz."

**[VERIFIED]** This vessel did not transit under IMO's evacuation framework.

**[VERIFIED]** The attack came hours after Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) had warned ships that they must coordinate with its navy and travel through a designated route through Iranian waters. After the U.S. and Iran agreed last week to reopen the Strait of Hormuz toll-free for 60 days as part of a preliminary agreement, ships had rushed to travel through the corridor. Many used an alternative route on the southern side of the Strait along the Omani coast.

---

#### 2. Iran's "Persian Gulf Strait Authority" Claims Control

**[OFFICIAL]** Following reports of the attack, Iran's Persian Gulf Strait Authority — a new government agency established to control shipping in the strait — wrote on X that transit outside its own designated routes "will not be covered by the guarantee of safe passage."

**[VERIFIED]** "The only authorized route for passing through the Strait of Hormuz is the one declared by the Islamic Republic of Iran," the Iranian force said. "Vessel traffic outside these routes is extremely dangerous and prohibited." "Violators will be dealt with," it added, without elaborating.

**[REPORTED]** On Wednesday, the Guard threatened one tanker over the radio, with a soldier warning, "You are in range of my missiles and maybe (I) fire on you," according to the private security firm Ambrey.

**[REPORTED]** Al Jazeera's Resul Serdar, reporting from Tehran, said the IRGC appeared frustrated because the Omani route partially bypasses Iran's direct control over shipping. "The control of the Strait of Hormuz has been a huge leverage for Iran to put pressure on its adversaries and the global economy since the beginning of the war," Serdar said.

---

#### 3. Oil Prices Continue Decline Despite Attack

**[VERIFIED]** At 9 a.m. Eastern Time today, oil was priced at $73.74 per barrel with Brent serving as the benchmark. That's a drop of 28 cents compared with yesterday morning.

**[VERIFIED]** The price of Brent crude briefly rose more than two percent to around $75.50 a barrel on Thursday before falling around two percent in early trading on Friday to $74.03 a barrel. U.S. West Texas Intermediate saw a similar jump on Thursday before sinking more than two percent to $70.38 a barrel on Friday.

**[VERIFIED]** Brent crude is on track for a third straight weekly drop.

---

#### 4. Israel-Lebanon Talks Extended to Fourth Day — Deadlock on Withdrawal

**[VERIFIED]** The U.S.-mediated talks, originally set to last three days, had yet to reach an outline for a Israel-Hezbollah cease-fire and IDF withdrawal plans.

**[VERIFIED]** Israel and Lebanon will wrap up their third day of US-mediated talks in Washington without an agreement on a partial Israeli withdrawal from southern Lebanon, but the sides have agreed to extend negotiations for a fourth day that will take place Friday at the State Department. The fifth round was supposed to wrap up Thursday at the State Department, with the US hoping it would culminate with the signing of a framework agreement that would include Israel partially withdrawing from small areas of its large buffer zone in southern Lebanon.

**[REPORTED]** A regional source told Haaretz that the delay in talks between Israel and Lebanon stems from disagreements over the proposed pilot zones from which Israel is expected to withdraw. According to the source, the sides were presented with a map, but neither side has validated it.

---

#### 5. IDF Soldiers Wounded in Southern Lebanon Clash

**[VERIFIED]** Four IDF soldiers, including two officers, were injured in a clash with a Hezbollah gunman in southern Lebanon on Thursday night, the military said Friday. It came as the head of the Hezbollah terror group said Israel must leave southern Lebanon, arguing that a US-Iran understanding spelled "defeat" for Israel. According to an IDF probe of the Thursday incident, at around 10 p.m. in the Beit Yahoun area, a Hezbollah operative who was hiding in a building threw a grenade at Israeli troops.

**[OFFICIAL]** Qassem claimed that the US-Iran deal signed last week was a declaration of defeat for Israel and America. "They wanted a major war… to eliminate our existence," Qassem said. "We were able to stop this aggression and achieve a great victory… We have shattered the Israeli-American project and entered a new phase."

---

#### 6. Defense Minister Katz Warns Iran

**[OFFICIAL]** Israeli Defense Minister Israel Katz said in a Farsi-language post on X that a top commander in an elite unit of the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps, Esmail Qaani, has been "constantly threatening Israel these days," warning that if the "Islamic Republic attacks Israel, it will commit its biggest mistake." "Our forces are ready to finish the job," he added.

---

#### 7. US-Iran Technical Talks Still Scheduled

**[VERIFIED]** American and Iranian negotiators have met to flesh out the terms of a final deal, and Secretary of State Marco Rubio said on Wednesday that technical talks will begin on June 30, with working groups focused on Iran's nuclear program and sanctions.

### Emerging Signals

**IRGC asserting dominance over Hormuz governance:** The establishment of the "Persian Gulf Strait Authority" and its public claim to sole route authority signals Iran is not conceding control over the strait. The attack on the Ever Lovely appears to be enforcement of this claim. This directly contradicts the MOU's "toll-free" provision and Rubio's insistence that "no country on Earth has a right to charge for the use of international waterways."

**Dual-route conflict crystallizing:** The IRGC says Oman and the IMO announced the new shipping corridor without consulting Tehran. "Certain authorities have announced a new shipping route through the Strait of Hormuz without prior notification to or coordination with the Islamic Republic of Iran. The proposed route is unacceptable and poses serious safety risks. The only authorised transit routes through the Strait of Hormuz are those designated by the Islamic Republic of Iran." This sets up a direct confrontation between the IMO/Omani southern corridor and Iran's northern route.

**Markets discounting risks:** Oil at $73.74 despite the attack suggests markets are still betting on a successful deal, or that supply disruptions are already priced in. This creates economic pressure on all parties to resolve the Hormuz dispute, but also reduces the urgency that might force concessions.

**Israel-Lebanon talks hitting structural wall:** The disagreement over pilot zone maps indicates fundamental incompatibility between Israeli demands for security guarantees and Lebanese/Hezbollah demands for full withdrawal. Without external pressure from the US-Iran track, this appears headed for indefinite stalemate.

**Day 9 of the 60-day window saw the first confirmed IRGC attack on a commercial vessel since the MOU was signed — a Singapore-flagged cargo ship hit by a drone near Oman. Iran's newly established "Persian Gulf Strait Authority" is claiming exclusive route authority, directly conflicting with US and IMO positions. Oil prices continued their decline despite the attack, suggesting markets are still betting on a successful outcome. Israel-Lebanon talks extended to a fourth day amid deadlock on pilot zones, while four IDF soldiers were wounded in overnight clashes with Hezbollah. Technical US-Iran talks remain scheduled for June 29-30, but the Hormuz governance dispute now threatens to overshadow nuclear discussions.
