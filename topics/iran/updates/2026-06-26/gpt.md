- A **cargo vessel was hit by a projectile** near Oman while transiting a **new Oman/UN-backed route**, per **UKMTO**, and **the UN effort to move stranded ships/seafarers was paused** afterward. **[VERIFIED]** ([apnews.com](https://apnews.com/article/862164c2aecbdc376dea434198eaf75f?utm_source=openai))

- This directly complicates yesterday’s takeaway that the evacuation/corridor framework was moving smoothly from “plan” to “execution.”

**What changed / what it means:**  
This is the first major data point in the last ~24 hours that **re-raises the probability of a corridor “spoiler cycle”**: attacks (or ambiguous attribution) → pauses/slowdowns → insurance and freight spikes → pressure for U.S./partner escorts/retaliation. Even if volumes don’t collapse again, the **credibility of the corridor** is now more fragile—and that fragility tends to **pull the conflict toward prolonged, managed confrontation** rather than clean de-escalation.

### 2) Verification track hardens: Grossi reiterates inspections are part of the interim deal (but implementation remains the choke point)
- Reuters reports **IAEA DG Rafael Grossi reiterated** that the interim U.S.–Iran peace arrangement **requires IAEA access/inspections in Iran** to comply with the deal. **[REPORTED]** ([zonebourse.com](https://www.zonebourse.com/actualite-bourse/l-accord-entre-les-etats-unis-et-l-iran-autorise-l-acces-aux-sites-nucleaires-de-teheran-repete-gro-ce7f5fd9db8ff223?utm_source=openai))  

**What changed / what it means:**  
This doesn’t resolve the “war of words,” but it **tightens the public IAEA position**: inspections aren’t optional “later,” they’re presented as **intrinsic to compliance**. That raises the near-term stakes for the **June 29–30 technical talks**—because if Iran resists modalities/timelines, the U.S./Israel side will frame it as **non-compliance**, increasing the chance of renewed coercive pressure (sanctions escalation, strikes, or maritime enforcement).
