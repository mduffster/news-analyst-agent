### Summary

**The Lebanon question is now the central flashpoint. Hours after the U.S.-Iran MOU was digitally signed, Trump publicly rebuked Netanyahu at the G7, saying he "has to be more responsible with respect to Lebanon" and calling weekend strikes "vicious" and "too much." Iran's Foreign Minister Araghchi escalated simultaneously, declaring any Israeli attack on Lebanon or continued occupation would constitute "a violation of the MOU." Oil plunged another ~5-6% to ~$78-79/barrel—down 17% from deal announcement levels—as markets bet on the deal holding despite Israel-Hezbollah fighting continuing on the ground. The $300 billion "investment fund" for Iran emerged as a major controversy, with Trump denying the U.S. would invest "any money" while reports indicate a private-sector reconstruction mechanism is indeed part of the framework. ### Key Developments Since June 15

#### 1. Trump Publicly Criticizes Netanyahu Over Lebanon

**[OFFICIAL]** Trump said Tuesday that Netanyahu "has to be more responsible with respect to Lebanon," while criticizing the scale of Israeli military operations against the Lebanese group Hezbollah. "I would say, of all countries, they've been treated the worst, and they can't defend themselves, and they have Hezbollah, which is a problem for them, so no, I'm not happy with the way Israel has handled themselves with Lebanon and with Hezbollah."

**[OFFICIAL]** Trump said last weekend's strikes in Lebanon were "vicious" and "too much." He made the comments from Évian-les-Bains, France, where he is attending the G7 summit.

**[OFFICIAL]** Trump also underscored U.S. support for Israel, saying, "Without the US, there'd be no Israel," adding that Israel "would have been blown up a long time ago had I not gotten involved." Alluding to the impact on civilians of Israel's attacks on Lebanon, he said: "You don't have to knock down an apartment house every time you're looking for somebody because there's a lot of people in those apartment houses — and they're not all Hezbollah."

**[OFFICIAL]** Asked whether the US-Iran deal can survive even if Israel launches more attacks in Lebanon, Trump said: "It can." He added: "I consider that the minor war."

#### 2. Iran: Any Israeli Attack on Lebanon Violates MOU

**[OFFICIAL]** Speaking at a meeting with foreign diplomats in Tehran, Araghchi said Iran will view any military attack on Lebanon by Israel and the continuation of its occupation as a "violation of the MOU."

**[OFFICIAL]** Araghchi stated: "The new round of Iran-US negotiations will begin on Friday." He added: "Since Monday morning, Tehran time, when the agreement was finalized, the end of the war was also announced, but the official start of this memorandum will be on Friday."

**[OFFICIAL]** Araghchi emphasized that without the withdrawal of Israeli forces from Lebanese territory, a complete end to the war would not be achieved.

#### 3. Netanyahu: Israel Will Remain in "Security Zones"

**[OFFICIAL]** Netanyahu said Monday that Israel's "struggle is not over" while pledging to remain in "security zones" in Lebanon.

**[OFFICIAL]** Netanyahu held a press conference, his first in three months, during which he asserted that the fighting was successful but admitted he still does not know what is written in the deal. Israel was sidelined entirely throughout the negotiating process, a fact highlighted when Netanyahu informed a reporter that Israel does not yet know the details of the deal.

**[REPORTED]** Israel asked the U.S. to see the memorandum of understanding, but the request was denied, according to an Israeli source and a U.S. official.

#### 4. Israel-Hezbollah Fighting Continues, Though Eased

**[VERIFIED]** Fighting between Israel and Hezbollah in Lebanon eased but did not halt entirely on Monday after the US signed a memorandum of understanding with Iran, as Israeli leaders vowed that the agreement did not prevent it from continuing to operate in south Lebanon. The IDF said Monday evening that Hezbollah had fired several rockets at Israeli troops in southern Lebanon, one of several attacks carried out by the terror group throughout the day.

**[VERIFIED]** The IDF also confirmed striking Hezbollah operatives who "posed a threat" to forces in southern Lebanon on Monday. According to the military, the rockets fired at Israeli forces were intercepted by air defenses. No sirens sounded in any towns in Israel.

#### 5. $300 Billion "Investment Fund" Controversy

**[REPORTED]** A $300 billion private investment fund designed to spur investment into Iran is outlined in the US-Iran framework agreement, with more than half of the sum already committed, Reuters reported on Tuesday, citing a source with direct knowledge of the deal.

**[REPORTED]** The source said the fund was designed to give both sides an economic incentive to conclude a final deal, adding that it would contain no government money or grants. Companies based in the US, Persian Gulf states, Asia, South America and Africa had agreed to commit financing, with pledged investments spanning energy, logistics, manufacturing and transport.

**[OFFICIAL]** Trump said Tuesday: "We are not investing any money in Iran, by the way, and ... that rumor got out there yesterday was ridiculous. We have the right to go in some day and do, if I want to do something, or if somebody wants to do something, but we are not investing any money."

**[REPORTED]** The draft agreement includes a commitment by the United States and its regional partners to establish a fund of at least $300 billion to support Iran's postwar reconstruction and economic development, contingent on a final deal. Diplomats told the Times the American side deliberately avoided the words "compensation" or "reparations," opting instead for "international investment fund."
