## Daily incremental update — US–Israel–Iran conflict  
**As of Tue, June 16, 2026 (ET)**  
**New since Mon, June 15, 2026 (ET)**

### 1) Iran is now explicitly conditioning the U.S.–Iran “end-of-war” deal on Israel’s actions in Lebanon
- **Iranian officials say the deal requires Israel to withdraw from Lebanon** (despite Israel not being a party to the U.S.–Iran agreement, and the text still not public). **[REPORTED]** ([apnews.com](https://apnews.com/article/d79458506c46e3f4a78aef0f9d8b9250?utm_source=openai))  

**What changed / what it means:**  
This is a meaningful escalation of the *implementation risk*: it reframes the agreement from a U.S.–Iran track (Hormuz/ceasefire/nuclear freeze) into a **de facto regional package** where Tehran can claim noncompliance if Israel doesn’t alter its Lebanon posture. That increases the chance of **blame-shifting and stall tactics** during the 60‑day window—and raises the odds that Lebanon becomes the primary “tripwire” even if U.S.–Iran deconfliction holds.

---

### 2) The “signed vs signing” ambiguity sharpened: electronic signature is being treated as real, while Hormuz reopening is still framed as post‑Geneva
- U.S. accounts are increasingly explicit that the deal was **electronically signed** (Trump/Vance and Iran’s Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf), while a **formal ceremony remains slated for Friday in Geneva (June 19, 2026)**. **[REPORTED]** ([axios.com](https://www.axios.com/2026/06/15/trump-iran-deal-questions-strait-funds?utm_source=openai))  
- Reporting also stresses that **Hormuz may not materially reopen until after Friday’s ceremony**, even if a ceasefire extension is “in effect” now. **[REPORTED]** ([axios.com](https://www.axios.com/2026/06/15/trump-iran-deal-questions-strait-funds?utm_source=openai))  

**What changed / what it means:**  
The process picture is clearer (electronic signature already done; paper/ceremony Friday), but the **operational deliverable** markets care about—**safe, insured commercial transits**—still looks gated on *implementation details* and confidence. This keeps a near-term window where any incident in/near Hormuz or Lebanon can be interpreted as “proof the deal is fake,” increasing volatility risk through June 19.

---

### 3) Follow-on maritime security planning is emerging: allies push a demining/escort concept for Hormuz
- AP describes a proposed **follow-up naval mission** discussed by U.S. allies focused on **demining and potentially escorting ships** to reassure crews/insurers for Hormuz transits. **[REPORTED]** ([apnews.com](https://apnews.com/article/80c149a4367dd31c6e85e9b25daa4129?utm_source=openai))  

**What changed / what it means:**  
This is a practical acknowledgment that even if leaders declare the strait “open,” **shipping normalization is a security/insurance problem**, not just a diplomatic statement. If an escort/demining mission forms, it could reduce the chance of a sudden escalation from a single maritime incident—but it also increases the odds of **close-quarters interactions** (and miscalculation) between coalition naval assets and Iran-aligned forces.

---

### 4) On-the-ground Lebanon signal: fighting appears more limited, but not resolved
- AP reporting from southern Lebanon indicates **some displaced residents are checking homes** amid perceptions of a truce, while **fighting has not stopped**, only become **more limited** since the U.S.–Iran deal was reached. **[REPORTED]** ([apnews.com](https://apnews.com/article/b6c0d1ee59da0491631ce901a3cd8956?utm_source=openai))  

**What changed / what it means:**  
This supports the assessment that the conflict is shifting into a **stop–start, multi-front entanglement** pattern: de-escalation *signals* exist, but Lebanon remains active enough to spoil the broader framework.
