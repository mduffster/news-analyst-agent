# US-Israel-Iran Conflict: Three US Carriers Deploy as Netanyahu-Zamir Align on Iran Assessment; UKMTO Confirms Fresh Tanker Attacks

## November 16, 2026 — Day 262

**Assessment:** The conflict's trajectory shifted meaningfully over the past 72 hours with two countervailing developments. The arrival of the USS Theodore Roosevelt brings three US aircraft carriers to the region — the biggest surge in US airpower in the Middle East since the Iraq War. Simultaneously, Israeli sources report that "it appears the prime minister and the IDF chief of staff are on the same page" regarding Iran, with IDF Chief of Staff Lt.-Gen. Eyal Zamir and Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu reaching "the same conclusions about Iran" without contradicting each other during Sunday's Security Cabinet discussion. This alignment defuses the civil-military tension that dominated last week's reporting. Meanwhile, two tankers were attacked in the Strait of Hormuz on Monday, with both incidents resulting in engine-room fires, according to UKMTO. Despite Iran's claim that six tankers struck mines in 48 hours, oil prices fell more than 1% as signs of recovering oil flows through the Strait of Hormuz reinforced expectations of surplus supply.

---

## 1. Third US Carrier Arrives — Largest Middle East Air Power Concentration Since 2003

The USS Theodore Roosevelt aircraft carrier arrived in the Middle East on Sunday. It means Washington now has three aircraft carrier strike groups in the US Central Command's area of responsibility. [VERIFIED]

Following the arrival of the USS Theodore Roosevelt in the US Central Command area of responsibility, approximately 53,000 US troops are currently stationed in the Middle East, a source familiar with the details told The Jerusalem Post. Three US aircraft carriers are now operating in the Middle East under CENTCOM's command. This is a situation not seen since April and, before that, since 2003, during the Second Gulf War.

The Makin Island Amphibious Ready Group, carrying more than 2,000 Marines, had also followed the USS Theodore Roosevelt to the Middle East.

The Roosevelt was initially supposed to relieve the USS George Washington, although that carrier remains in the region for the moment. Despite the expanded carrier presence, the US military recently denied an Israeli media report alleging it was preparing to resume operations against Iran, dismissing claims of regional force posture changes as "pure speculation."

**Significance:** The three-carrier posture provides Trump with maximum military flexibility — either for deterrence, potential escalation, or sustained blockade operations — while maintaining ambiguity about intentions. The deployment's scale undercuts any suggestion of de-escalation while the carriers remain in theater.

## 2. Netanyahu-Zamir "On Same Page" — Prior Civil-Military Tension Defused

On Sunday evening, Israel's Security Cabinet convened for an extended discussion on Iran. Two Israeli sources told the Post that "it appears the prime minister and the IDF chief of staff are on the same page" regarding Iran. "IDF Chief of Staff Lt.-Gen. Eyal Zamir and Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu reached the same conclusions about Iran and did not contradict each other during the discussion." [REPORTED]

The Security Cabinet meeting was held in the Operations Directorate's underground command center at the Kirya military headquarters in Tel Aviv, where senior IDF officials said that the Iranian regime continues to invest significant resources in rebuilding its missile and drone capabilities, despite the country's deteriorating economic situation.

This marks a significant shift from last week's reporting. The warning followed a report Tuesday by Channel 12 that Netanyahu and Army Chief of Staff Eyal Zamir were again at odds over possible action against Iran. Reports emerged Wednesday evening that the cabinet had instructed the military to step up preparations for a possible attack on Iran and a potential military confrontation.

**Significance:** The reported alignment substantially reduces the probability of an Israeli unilateral strike in the near term. Last week's narrative — with Zamir as the sole institutional check on Netanyahu's push for military action — has been superseded. The alignment could reflect: (1) agreement on threat assessment but not necessarily immediate action, (2) Netanyahu moderating his demands, or (3) the earlier reports overstating disagreement. The lack of an immediate strike authorization suggests alignment on intelligence assessments rather than operational decisions.

## 3. Iran Launches Second Bahrain Attack in Three Days — Six Drones at US Base

Bahrain said it repelled an Iranian attack on Sunday, the second such assault in three days, as tensions across the Middle East simmer and the U.S.-Iran war remains in limbo. [VERIFIED]

The Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps said Sunday it launched six drones at the radar of a US air defense site at the Riffa base in Bahrain. It described it as a response to the "blatant aggression of the US military." The IRGC reported a "successful impact," while Bahrain said its air defenses intercepted several Iranian aerial attacks and destroyed the projectiles.

Bahrain's Foreign Ministry said in a statement that it holds Iran "fully responsible" for drone attacks targeting the Gulf kingdom on Friday and Sunday, describing the incidents as constituting "a resumption of Iran's aggression against Bahrain and other countries in the region."

The Friday and Sunday attacks were the first to target Bahrain in about two months. The incidents could indicate that Iran is preparing to ramp up hostilities against its neighbors and U.S. forces stationed across the region.

**Significance:** Iran has broken a two-month lull in attacks on US basing in Gulf states. The Bahrain attacks represent a deliberate escalation strategy — targeting the headquarters of the US Navy's Fifth Fleet — while Iran simultaneously signals diplomatic openness. Iran targeted Bahrain for the second time in three days, the first spate of direct attacks on the Persian Gulf state in weeks, even as it reiterated that a path to ending its conflict with the US remains open.

## 4. UKMTO Confirms Two Tanker Attacks — Iran Claims Six Mine Strikes in 48 Hours

Two tankers were reportedly attacked in the Strait of Hormuz on Monday, November 16, with both incidents resulting in engine-room fires and one vessel left disabled, according to UKMTO. At approximately 0558 UTC, the company security officer of a tanker reported that the vessel had been struck by an unidentified projectile while transiting outbound through the Strait of Hormuz. The impact sparked a fire in the engine room. [OFFICIAL]

Two oil tankers hit mines in the Strait of Hormuz, leading to explosions, Iran's Revolutionary Guards said on Monday. Six tankers caught fire in similar incidents in the strategic waterway on Sunday and Monday, the IRGC said. [REPORTED — Iran claim, UKMTO confirms two attacks]

Data compiled by oil and shipping market analyst Morgan Downey from vessel reports and maritime tracking show that 114 tankers have been attacked or caught up in maritime incidents in the Persian Gulf and the Strait of Hormuz between early March and November 13.

The latest JMIC assessment recorded 83 U.S.-facilitated transits between November 12 and 14, while separately tracked, non-facilitated commercial traffic remained at single-digit to low-double-digit levels per day.

**Significance:** UKMTO confirmation of two attacks with engine-room fires establishes the incident as verified rather than relying solely on Iranian claims. Iran's broader assertion of six mine strikes in 48 hours cannot be independently verified. The escalation in maritime attacks coincides with renewed Gulf basing attacks, suggesting a coordinated Iranian pressure campaign.

## 5. Oil Markets Shrug Off Attacks — Hormuz Flows Recovering

Oil prices fell more than 1% as signs of recovering oil flows through the Strait of Hormuz reinforced expectations of surplus supply, though fresh tanker incidents underscored risks to Middle East exports. [VERIFIED]

Brent fell to 94.88 USD/Bbl on November 16, 2026, down 1.00% from the previous day. Over the past month, Brent's price has fallen 13.39%, but it is still 47.79% higher than a year ago.

The global oil market will return to a surplus in the fourth quarter of this year, the International Energy Agency said on Friday, as Middle East exports recover faster than global oil demand despite continued attacks on shipping.

US Secretary of State Marco Rubio says oil shipments through the Strait of Hormuz have recovered to more than four-fifths of their pre-war volume on some nights. "Oil is getting out – on some nights it's 80-85 percent of what was getting out pre-conflict."

"For the first time since the US-Iran conflict began, traders are actively contemplating the possibility of excess supply rather than persistent tightness," said KCM Trade's Waterer.

**Significance:** The disconnect between escalating maritime violence and falling oil prices reveals a fundamental market judgment: the US Navy's escort operation is succeeding in restoring flows despite Iran's mining campaign. This reduces Iran's economic leverage and suggests the "extended stalemate" is economically manageable for the West.

## 6. Coalition Talks Begin — No Progress with Likud

Representatives of Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's Likud, Gadi Eisenkot's Yashar and Naftali Bennett's B'Yachad (Together) parties met on Sunday for initial coalition negotiations and agreed to continue discussions on forming a government. "The meeting took place in a businesslike atmosphere, and it was agreed that dialogue between the sides would continue." [VERIFIED]

No progress was reported in Yashar-Likud unity talks. Parallel negotiations between Eisenkot-led bloc and UTJ are said to advance significantly, though the Haredi party chief denies this, and Shas remains loyal to Netanyahu.

Yashar denied an Army Radio report that it was considering offering Likud a rotation agreement contingent on Eisenkot serving first as prime minister, telling The Times of Israel that the only proposal on the table was for Likud to join a government led by Eisenkot.

President Herzog gave Eisenkot until December 6 to form a government.

**Significance:** Coalition talks are proceeding without substantive progress. Eisenkot's path to 61 seats likely runs through the ultra-Orthodox parties (Shas, UTJ) rather than Likud. The December 6 deadline gives 20 days remaining on Eisenkot's initial mandate.

## 7. IAEA Board Meeting This Week — Iran Rejects Normal Inspections

The IAEA Board of Governors meets November 16-20 in Vienna. [OFFICIAL]

Iran's Mission to the UN Office in Vienna criticized the IAEA Board of Governors' resolutions on Iran as legally flawed, saying normal implementation of safeguards cannot resume under the abnormal circumstances created by attacks on the country's safeguarded nuclear facilities.

As stated in IAEA Director General reports to the Board of Governors, "as of today the IAEA has not been able to verify that there has been no diversion of nuclear material required to be safeguarded under Iran's CSA." One area of particular concern is Iran's previously declared nuclear material — which includes 440kg of High Enriched Uranium.

**Significance:** The IAEA Board meeting represents a potential trigger for international action. The agency's inability to verify 440kg of highly enriched uranium creates significant uncertainty about Iranian capabilities and provides ammunition for both diplomatic pressure and arguments for military action.
