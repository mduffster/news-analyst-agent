## Daily Update: US-Israel-Iran Conflict — June 15, 2026

### Summary

**CONFIRMED DEAL: The U.S.-Iran MOU was signed "digitally" on Sunday by Trump, Vance, and Iran's Parliament Speaker Ghalibaf. An in-person ceremony is set for Geneva on Friday, June 19. The agreement ends active warfare "immediately and permanently" and commits to reopening the Strait of Hormuz within 30 days. Oil prices have plunged to ~$83-84/barrel—down nearly 20% from war highs—as markets price in deal implementation. However, major gaps persist: Israel insists it will not withdraw from Lebanon and won't be bound by the MOU; the deal does not require Israeli withdrawal as a condition; and nuclear provisions remain vague. Netanyahu is expected to give a statement tonight that could signal Israel's next moves.**

---

### Key Developments Since June 12

#### 1. MOU Signed Digitally — Formal Ceremony Friday

**[OFFICIAL]** While an in-person signing ceremony is apparently set for Friday in Switzerland, US Vice President JD Vance says the MOU between the US and Iran was signed "digitally" yesterday. "We already signed the deal digitally yesterday," Vance said during an interview with ABC's "Good Morning America."

**[OFFICIAL]** Iran Parliament Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf signed on behalf of Tehran, the US official says.

**[OFFICIAL]** Pakistan's foreign minister confirmed the Swiss city of Geneva as the venue for the agreement signing on Friday. Ishaq Dar wrote on X: "We look forward to the formal signing ceremony on 19 June in Geneva…" describing the development as a "significant breakthrough" that "provides much-needed confidence and stability to global markets and the world economy."

**[OFFICIAL]** President Donald Trump suggested today that he would not attend a formal signing ceremony for the Iran agreement. Trump, speaking alongside French President Emmanuel Macron at the G7 summit in Évian-les-Bains, said Vice President JD Vance would come to Geneva for the event.

---

#### 2. Deal Terms: What We Know

**[REPORTED]** According to diplomatic sources familiar with the text, the key provisions include: an immediate and lasting ceasefire across all active fronts, including Lebanon; a commitment to reopen the Strait of Hormuz to full commercial traffic within approximately 30 days without transit tolls; a 60-day extension of the existing ceasefire to allow for nuclear and sanctions negotiations; access by Iran to a portion of its previously frozen assets during the negotiation window; and a commitment by Iran never to acquire a nuclear weapon — though that commitment is not backed by a named verification mechanism in the initial MOU.

**[REPORTED]** The U.S.-Iran MOU includes a 30-day gradual Hormuz reopening via mine clearance, on-site uranium dilution under IAEA supervision, phased sanctions relief and a 60-day oil sales waiver.

**Nuclear Provisions:** That stockpile exceeds 9,000kg, the bulk at low enrichment levels, but 440kg sits at near weapons-grade concentration. The floor commitment is dilution of all material on-site, supervised by the International Atomic Energy Agency.

**[OFFICIAL]** "This is a performance-based thing," Vance said. "If we see the Iranians making, for example, taking action to eliminate their stockpile of enriched material, then yes, sanctions relief will follow. If we see the Iranians taking action to allow the kind of verification regime that we need to see to know that they're not going to build a nuclear weapon. Yes, sanctions relief will follow."

---

#### 3. Strait of Hormuz: "Partially Open," Full Opening Friday

**[OFFICIAL]** Trump said "It's open now, but it opens completely" on Friday when the formal signing takes place.

**[VERIFIED]** As of June 14, the Strait remains effectively closed to commercial shipping. Only 2 ships transited vs ~94/day normal.

**[REPORTED]** A senior US official says that the MOU signed yesterday with Iran is not conditioned on Israel withdrawing from Lebanon. However, the deal still envisions a ceasefire that covers Lebanon, as well as Iran. "The deal is a ceasefire, but it will not be a one-way ceasefire. If Iran is not able to control Hezbollah, and if they attack Israeli positions or Israeli towns, Israel will have the right to defend themselves and respond."

---

#### 4. Contradictory Statements Persist

**[VERIFIED]** Since announcing the agreement, Iran and the U.S. have issued statements that contradict each other on key aspects of the framework.

**On Hormuz control:** "Iran said ships passing through the strait would be regulated by Iran and Oman — suggesting Tehran would seek to retain some control over the waterway," Bloomberg reported. Meanwhile, "President Trump said in an interview on Sunday afternoon that the agreement he had reached with Iran would ultimately assure that the Strait of Hormuz was 'permanently toll-free.'"

**[REPORTED]** Araghchi confirmed on Iranian state television that under the agreement the waterway would not return entirely to its pre-war status: Iran would not charge transit tolls but would charge "service fees" on vessels. The distinction between a toll and a service fee remains contested.

#### 5. Israel's Position: Won't Be Bound by Deal

**[OFFICIAL]** The Trump administration's agreement with Iran does not include a requirement that Israel withdraw from Lebanon. "Their withdrawal was not a condition of the deal," the official said.

**[OFFICIAL]** Katz insisted that Israel will not withdraw from southern Lebanon, where it is battling the Iran-backed Hezbollah "despite all the existing pressures and those that will still come."

**[OFFICIAL]** Netanyahu, like most Israeli officials, is unhappy with the emerging deal, but is unlikely to publicly oppose Trump. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu will give a video statement tonight at 8 p.m. It will be Netanyahu's first statement since the signing of the memorandum of understanding between the US and Iran was announced.

**[REPORTED]** Trump criticized Netanyahu to The New York Times, claiming that Israel should be grateful to the United States. "He's a very difficult guy," Trump told the Times regarding Netanyahu, "and to be honest with you, he should be very thankful to us for doing this."

### Emerging Signals

**The Lebanon question is unresolved:** The MOU explicitly does not require Israel to withdraw from Lebanon. Iran's foreign minister demands "Israeli military action in Lebanon needs to be completely halted" with the "US bears responsibility for implementing." This creates a structural contradiction: Iran claims victory on a deal that doesn't deliver what Hezbollah wants.

**Vance emerges as key figure:** Vice President JD Vance assumed a central role in negotiations after President Donald Trump encouraged him to get directly involved. Vance led the push to finalize an agreement and is tentatively expected to attend an official signing ceremony. The vice president will then lead the US side in its next phase of negotiations toward a permanent truce.

**Nuclear provisions are deliberately vague:** The MOU commits Iran "never to acquire a nuclear weapon" but with no verification mechanism named. The 440kg of near-weapons-grade uranium will be diluted on-site, but the harder questions about enrichment capability are deferred to the 60-day negotiation window. This mirrors past "framework" deals that collapsed in details.

---

### Bottom Line

**The U.S.-Iran MOU is now signed—digitally—ending the immediate war. A formal ceremony is scheduled for Friday in Geneva, with VP Vance attending. Oil prices have plunged nearly 20% from war highs, reflecting market confidence in implementation. However, the deal's durability faces immediate tests: Netanyahu's statement tonight will signal whether Israel intends to operate within or outside the framework; the Lebanon issue remains explicitly unresolved; nuclear provisions are vague; and competing U.S.-Iranian narratives about the terms have not been reconciled. The next 72 hours will show whether this is a genuine turning point or another temporary pause before resumed escalation.**
