## Critical Developments (June 12-15)

### 1. MOU Signed Digitally — Formal Ceremony Friday in Geneva [OFFICIAL]

President Donald Trump and other world leaders have confirmed that the United States and Iran have reached a peace deal. Each side is expected to sign the deal electronically. An official signing ceremony will be on Friday, June 19, in Switzerland.

Iran and the United States have agreed to a memorandum of understanding to end more than 100 days of war, with Tehran saying the agreement includes Lebanon, which has been under intense Israeli attacks since March 2. Mediated by Pakistan and Qatar, the deal will be formally signed in the Swiss city of Geneva on Friday.

"The signing of this Memorandum of Understanding will be officially held on Friday, June 19," Iran stated. "Negotiations for a final agreement will be postponed until after the other party has fulfilled its obligations under the Memorandum of Understanding."

**Assessment:** The deal has moved from draft to digitally signed in 72 hours—a rapid diplomatic conclusion to weeks of stop-start negotiations. However, the logistical and political obstacles to implementation remain significant, and neither side has released the full text publicly.

### 2. Deal Terms: Key Provisions and Gaps [REPORTED]

The MOU would extend the ceasefire for 60 days, including in Lebanon, during which time nuclear negotiations would be held. The text includes a framework for addressing Iran's enriched uranium stockpile, though any action on Iran's nuclear program would depend on a second, more detailed accord.

**Key reported provisions:**

| Provision | U.S. Description | Iranian Description |
|-----------|------------------|---------------------|
| **Hormuz** | Will open to all shipping on Friday | Opens under "service fees" (not tolls) |
| **Naval blockade** | Immediate removal authorized | Lifted within 30 days |
| **Nuclear** | "Performance-based" — sanctions relief follows compliance | No new restrictions; NPT commitment reiterated |
| **Frozen assets** | Reuters reported draft includes US allowing release of $25 billion | $24 billion released during 60-day talks |
| **Lebanon** | Ceasefire covers Lebanon; Israeli withdrawal not required | "Permanent" ceasefire across all fronts including Lebanon |

A US official says Hormuz will be open toll-free for next 60 days, but unclear what will happen afterward.

**Assessment:** The fundamental gaps between U.S. and Iranian descriptions remain. The reported 14-point draft has not been independently verified, and neither Iranian nor US officials have publicly confirmed its contents. The nuclear provisions remain deliberately vague, with hard questions deferred to the 60-day negotiation window.

### 3. Israeli Officials Reject Deal — "Will Not Withdraw" [OFFICIAL/REPORTED]

Defense Minister Israel Katz vowed Monday that the Israeli military will remain in southern Lebanon and warned that if Iran strikes, it will be hit "with full force," promising that Israel will resist any pressure after the US and Iran agreed a deal to end the war that also reportedly includes a commitment to end hostilities in Lebanon.

Opposition leader Yair Lapid said Netanyahu "lost the war," and collapsed in the "moment of truth," adding: "There has never, ever, been a more absolute failure than Netanyahu."

Former prime minister Naftali Bennett slams Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's handling of the war, arguing that he would have been a better wartime leader. "The term of this Netanyahu government began with a civil war, continued with the October 7 massacre, and is now ending with a historic failure against Iran," Bennett told reporters at a press conference in Tel Aviv.

Netanyahu scheduled a statement on the Iran deal at 8 p.m. It will be Netanyahu's first statement since the signing of the memorandum of understanding between the US and Iran was announced. Netanyahu, like most Israeli officials, is unhappy with the emerging deal, but is unlikely to publicly oppose Trump.

**Assessment:** Israeli domestic reaction is uniformly negative across the political spectrum. The opposition calls it Netanyahu's "failure"; his coalition partners reject its terms. The key question is whether Netanyahu will publicly criticize Trump or pursue independent military action in Lebanon that could unravel the broader framework.

### 4. Israel-Hezbollah Fighting Continues — Structural Instability [VERIFIED/REPORTED]

Israel's more focused fight against the Iran-backed Hezbollah militia in Lebanon still has the power to disrupt broader American efforts to resolve its monthslong war with Iran. Israel's military said Sunday evening it was preparing for an incoming salvo of Iranian missiles.

But the attack never came and hours later, plans for the United States and Iran to sign a more permanent ceasefire deal in Geneva were being announced.

During the week of June 1-7, Hezbollah carried out 198 attack waves against Israel and IDF forces operating in Lebanon. Most of the attacks (168) targeted IDF forces in southern Lebanon, while 30 attack waves were directed at Israeli territory.

During the week, four IDF soldiers were killed in southern Lebanon. Two were killed by Hezbollah explosive drone strikes, one was killed by an anti-tank missile strike, and another was killed in an accidental discharge incident.

**Assessment:** By mid-April, more than 2,000 people had been killed in Lebanon, and over one million had been displaced. Prior ceasefire arrangements related to the Iran war did not formally include Lebanon, contributing to continued hostilities and complicating diplomatic efforts. The MOU nominally covers Lebanon, but Israel explicitly rejects withdrawal, creating structural instability.

### 5. Strait of Hormuz: "Toll-Free" Declared, But Effectively Closed [VERIFIED]

Ongoing military conflict between the US, Israel, and Iran since late February 2026 has kept commercial traffic through the Strait of Hormuz at a near-standstill for over 100 days. Daily transits have remained at roughly 5–10 vessels versus the pre-conflict norm of 100–160, with only limited or coordinated passages allowed under IRGC oversight.

Recent diplomatic progress, including a tentative U.S.-Iran peace framework and ceasefire extension announced around June 15, 2026, has lifted implied odds for normalized Strait of Hormuz traffic by July 31 to 63.5%. Pre-war daily transits averaged 130-160 vessels; post-February strikes, volumes fell to a trickle of under 10 ships.

Tanker executives note that credible security assurances could enable a rapid rebound above current levels, though full pre-conflict flows face logistical and demining hurdles.

**Assessment:** Trump's declaration that Hormuz is "open" is aspirational—the physical reality requires mine clearance, insurance normalization, and shipping company confidence. The market is pricing in a July-August normalization timeline rather than immediate reopening.
