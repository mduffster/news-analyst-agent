## Daily incremental update — US–Israel–Iran conflict  
**As of Mon, June 15, 2026 (ET)**  
**New since Fri, June 12, 2026 (ET)**

### 1) The “draft MOU” has advanced into an announced, time-bound preliminary agreement (with a Geneva signing date)
- **U.S. and Iranian officials say they’ve reached a preliminary agreement** to **halt the war**, **reopen the Strait of Hormuz**, and **move into a 60‑day ceasefire/negotiations window**, with an **official signing scheduled for Friday in Switzerland (Geneva)**. **[REPORTED]** ([investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/world-news/iran-us-agree-to-halt-war-and-reopen-hormuz-sending-oil-prices-tumbling-4741185?utm_source=openai))  
- **U.S. officials also characterized the MOU as “signed,”** while still describing a **Friday signing ceremony**—suggesting a split between *electronic/initial signature* vs *formal ceremony*. **[REPORTED]** ([uk.marketscreener.com](https://uk.marketscreener.com/news/u-s-officials-says-iran-pact-signed-hormuz-traffic-will-rise-significantly-ce7f5cdedf8af42c?utm_source=openai))  
- **Key nuclear placeholder now more explicit in reporting:** Reuters-described terms include an **Iranian “freeze” of nuclear activity pending a final agreement** (e.g., no further enrichment/expansion), with the hardest nuclear questions deferred into the **60‑day** talks. **[REPORTED]** ([marketscreener.com](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/what-the-us-and-iran-say-they-have-agreed-in-the-memorandum-to-end-the-war-ce7f5cdedb8bfe20?utm_source=openai))  

**What changed / what it means:** This is a material step up from June 12’s “leaked/contested draft” to an **announced framework with a calendar** (now through a Friday, June 19, 2026 ceremony). It increases the odds of a near-term operational de-escalation around Hormuz—but also **locks in a major failure mode**: implementation depends on *sequencing* (reopening + blockade lift + nuclear freeze + sanctions pathway), which is exactly where verification disputes tend to detonate.

### 2) Israel–U.S. friction is now the central spoiler risk (Lebanon front explicitly tied to the deal timeline)
- Reuters reporting (via ABC) says **Trump publicly criticized Netanyahu**, arguing **Israeli strikes in Lebanon delayed** the deal’s signing timeline. **[REPORTED]** ([abc.net.au](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-06-15/iran-us-peace-deal-sunday-donald-trump/106796760?utm_source=openai))  
- Reuters analysis frames the moment as **Trump and Netanyahu on a “collision course”** over whether Israel restrains Lebanon operations to preserve the U.S.–Iran track. **[REPORTED]** ([internazionale.it](https://www.internazionale.it/ultime-notizie-reuters/2026/06/15/analysis-netanyahu-and-trump-on-collision-course-as-us-iran-agree-to-halt-war?utm_source=openai))  
- AP reports **angry reactions in Israel** to the U.S.–Iran initial deal, with **no immediate Netanyahu statement** as of Monday evening local time—highlighting domestic Israeli pressure to reject/undercut the framework. **[REPORTED]** ([apnews.com](https://apnews.com/article/580112432fa563e6eb299640453e3ba9?utm_source=openai))  

**What changed / what it means:** The main risk to the framework has shifted from “U.S.–Iran text mismatch” to **U.S.–Israel operational divergence**, especially in Lebanon. That raises the probability that—even if the Hormuz piece de-escalates—**regional fighting continues via Israel–Hezbollah**, dragging the U.S. back into episodic crisis management.

- Oil **fell ~4%** on the announcement (Axios cites Brent around the mid‑$80s), consistent with markets pricing lower immediate risk to Hormuz shipping. **[REPORTED]** ([axios.com](https://www.axios.com/2026/06/14/oil-prices-us-iran-war-hormuz-strait-peace-deal?utm_source=openai))  

**What changed / what it means:** The market move supports that the “reopen Hormuz” track is being treated as credible *in the near term*. It does **not** imply the nuclear file is resolved; it implies traders expect **less imminent disruption**.
