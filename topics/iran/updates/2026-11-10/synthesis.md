# US-Israel-Iran Conflict: Trump Seeks Ally Repayment as Maritime Attacks Continue Despite "Effectively Over" Rhetoric

## November 10, 2026 — Day 256

**Assessment:** Day 256 reveals a growing divergence between political messaging and operational reality. President Trump's demand that Israel and Gulf allies repay $47 billion in war costs — the first time he has publicly named Israel — signals a significant post-midterm recalibration of U.S. relationships with its partners. Meanwhile, the operational picture contradicts de-escalation hopes: fresh shipping attacks occurred in the Strait of Hormuz and UAE waters, Houthis struck Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline and Riyadh, and the U.S. evacuated aircraft from a second UK base over Iranian threats. At the G7 in Paris, Secretary of State Rubio told allies that sanctions make major combat operations unnecessary — even as he acknowledged Iran's demands remain "unacceptable." Oil markets continue pricing in peace that has not materialized, with Brent falling below $91 for the first time since the war began.

## 1. Trump Demands $47 Billion War Repayment — Names Israel for First Time [OFFICIAL]

President Donald Trump has said the US plans to seek compensation from countries it "protected" during its war on Iran. In an interview with CBS News correspondent Ross Douthat that aired Sunday, Trump said such countries would "pay us any and all costs we had for the war."

For the first time, Trump also explicitly spoke of compensation from Israel, which he said "wouldn't exist" without US military intervention. "We decapitated their [Iran's] nuclear arsenal," he said. "If I didn't do that, there would be no Israel right now. Israel would be gone."

The US Department of Defense estimated that the war had cost the US an estimated $47.1 billion in military spending as of October 2026.

Analysts noted that from Trump's perspective, "Israel pushed him into a disastrous war with Iran." Gulf leaders would likely respond to any demand for direct repayment with "quiet resistance", arguing that they "already contribute significantly to the cost of regional security through defence cooperation, investment and economic partnerships with the US."

As for Israel, it looks unlikely to "reverse its longstanding role as a recipient rather than a provider of support in its relationship with the US", according to Marzieh Kouhi Esfahani of Durham University.

In November 2026, Trump announced he had come to an agreement with Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu to stop $4 billion in annual funding after 2026.

**Significance:** Trump's explicit naming of Israel marks a fundamental shift in U.S.-Israel relations. Combined with ending $4 billion in annual military aid, this signals the Trump administration is publicly distancing itself from Israel as domestic political costs mount. The demand is likely unenforceable, but the rhetoric creates unprecedented tension with a treaty ally.

## 2. Rubio at G7: "Devastating" Economic Campaign Eliminates Need for Major Combat [OFFICIAL]

Secretary of State Marco Rubio told G7 foreign ministers in Paris on Tuesday that the United States would keep imposing devastating economic costs on Iran and had options beyond resuming the war or accepting Tehran's demands, four G7 diplomats said.

"The economic campaign (against Iran) is so devastating that right now there is no need for major combat operations," Rubio said after a meeting of G7 foreign ministers in Paris. But if Iran were to cross "red lines" then the "US military stands ready," said Rubio, warning of a "terrible price" for Tehran.

Rubio warned however there had been "no progress" in talks with Iran whose demands he said were "simply not just unacceptable but, in some cases, outrageous and ridiculous and really not realistic."

French Foreign Minister Jean-Noel Barrot, who chaired the meeting in Paris, warned that the risk of a new escalation between the United States and Iran "has not been completely ruled out."

The Qatari prime minister, Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al Thani, flew on to Paris and made an unscheduled stop at the G7 meeting, where he met European ministers and Saudi Arabia's.

**Significance:** Rubio's G7 statement codifies the "maximum pressure without major escalation" strategy. The "devastating" characterization and claim of "no need" for combat operations represent the clearest articulation yet of the administration's preferred posture. However, the gap between Rubio's stated confidence and his acknowledgment of "no progress" in negotiations suggests this pressure campaign lacks a defined end state.

## 3. Maritime Attacks Continue: ADNOC Vessel Hit in UAE, Fresh Hormuz Strike [VERIFIED]

A vessel operated by ADNOC Logistics & Services was damaged in an attack in UAE territorial waters on Tuesday, as maritime authorities reported a separate apparent projectile strike against a tanker near the Strait of Hormuz, adding to a growing series of attacks on commercial shipping in the region. ADNOC Logistics & Services confirmed that one of its vessels sustained damage in an attack in the Persian Gulf on the afternoon of November 10.

According to UKMTO Warning 201-26, the incident occurred at 1338 UTC on November 10. The vessel's master reported that all crew members were safe and there was no environmental impact.

Despite market bets on a de-escalation in the Iran war, new evidence has emerged in recent days of strikes on tankers in the Strait of Hormuz and on energy infrastructure in the Middle East. However, there is little sign of attacks abating in November, after 37 ships were attacked in October, the highest monthly total since the start of the war, according to data from the Washington Institute for Near East Policy.

The Strait of Hormuz remains effectively closed to commercial traffic, with IMF PortWatch recording 1 vessel transiting on November 1 against a pre-crisis baseline of 85 per day. The lead development is a US strike on a commercial vessel attempting to breach the naval blockade.

**Significance:** October's 37 ship attacks — the highest monthly total since the war began — and continued November attacks directly contradict "effectively over" messaging. The UAE attack extends the threat geography beyond the Hormuz chokepoint. Markets are pricing in peace while insurance costs and attack frequency remain at war-time levels.

## 4. Houthis Strike Riyadh and East-West Pipeline — Intercepts Over Saudi Capital [VERIFIED]

The Saudi Arabia-led coalition fighting Yemen's Houthis announced it intercepted two missiles fired towards the kingdom's capital, Riyadh, and another aimed at its southern region.

Multiple explosions were heard in Riyadh, according to AFP news agency, and residents received text-message alerts instructing them to take shelter due to an "aerial threat."

On Monday, the East-West oil pipeline in Saudi Arabia's Medina region was attacked again. The official said that "pipeline operations were unaffected." The pipeline is a vital route for Saudi oil exports due to Tehran's restrictions in the Strait of Hormuz following the US-Israel war against Iran. The pipeline has come under attack several times, as the war on Iran fuels a global energy crisis.

Yemen's Iran-backed Houthi rebels announced on November 10 that they launched attacks targeting the international airport and financial district in the Saudi Arabian capital of Riyadh, as well as Aramco oil facilities in Abqaiq. The Houthis also attacked Aramco facilities in Abqaiq, a key Saudi oil installation.

Riyadh has used its East-West Pipeline to divert about 4 million barrels per day of crude to Yanbu, equivalent to about 4% of global supply.

**Significance:** The East-West pipeline represents Saudi Arabia's critical workaround for bypassing the closed Strait of Hormuz. Repeated attacks on this infrastructure — combined with Houthi strikes on Riyadh — demonstrate that proxy violence continues regardless of direct U.S.-Iran diplomatic signals. The pipeline remains operational, but its vulnerability creates persistent supply uncertainty.

## 5. US Evacuates Aircraft from Second UK Base — Iran Threat Widens [VERIFIED]

The United States has evacuated American military aircraft from a military base in England on Monday following a threat from Iran, according to The Wall Street Journal, which cited sources familiar with the matter.

Flight tracking data analyzed by CNN shows that 10 US refueling aircraft were relocated away from RAF Mildenhall on Monday.

The evacuation from RAF Mildenhall is the second UK base from which US aircraft have been evacuated in recent months, after US bomber aircraft at RAF Fairford were relocated in early October.

Iran previously stated that it considers UK air bases that were used by the US Air Force during strikes on Iran as fair targets for retaliation. The US also recently upgraded security postures at RAF Mildenhall and RAF Lakenheath to FPCON Charlie, a precaution taken when intelligence suspects that some form of terrorist action may be likely.

The source declined to detail the nature of the threat to the base, RAF Mildenhall, but said that the US intelligence community has in recent weeks picked up a wide range of "varied, extensive, and specific" plots being discussed by the Iranians against American targets.

**Significance:** Iran's demonstrated ability to project threats against Western basing — now affecting two UK installations — shows Tehran can impose costs asymmetrically even with degraded conventional capabilities. FPCON Charlie is a significant threat posture. This represents a new dimension of conflict extending beyond the Middle East theater.

## 6. France Attributes Supermarket Cyberattacks to IRGC — Seeks EU Sanctions [OFFICIAL]

France has opened an investigation and accused Iran of being behind recent cyberattacks targeting refrigeration units in supermarkets. French authorities said on Monday that they are probing a cyber unit affiliated with the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) over the attacks, which caused fires and large amounts of food waste.

On Monday, Barrot said Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) was behind cyberattacks targeting more than 700 French supermarkets on October 11 and 12, when refrigerators were remotely accessed "to initiate abnormal, indefinite defrost cycles."

On November 2, stores in at least nine other European countries were hit by a suspected cyberattack on their freezer systems.

Iran's Foreign Ministry on Tuesday strongly dismissed the French government's accusation, claiming it "is intended to divert public opinion from France's destructive policies toward Iran."

**Significance:** France's public attribution and sanctions push opens a new front in Iran's asymmetric campaign against Western states. Even if kinetic operations de-escalate, cyber and economic retaliation continues. This complicates any diplomatic off-ramp, as Tehran can argue Western pressure persists regardless of its concessions.

A new poll shows that 7 in 10 Americans want Congress to vote to end the Iran war.

If Speaker Mike Johnson cannot convince enough Republicans to show up, GOP sources acknowledge tonight's procedural vote will fail — teeing up a full impeachment vote as soon as Wednesday. Last night, 22 Republicans did not show up to vote.

House Speaker Mike Johnson said the vote that's planned for later Tuesday to sideline Massie's impeachment resolution against Hegseth is "not going to fail."

"If it comes over, if there's a vote, I'm likely to vote against Hegseth and for the impeachment," said Senator Thom Tillis, R-N.C.

Hegseth said each of the military engagements mentioned as part of the effort — including strikes on alleged drug-smuggling boats in the Caribbean and the war with Iran — were lawful actions directed by President Donald Trump. None of the operations were approved by Congress, which Massie pointed to in his articles of impeachment against Hegseth.

**Significance:** The impeachment vote outcome (expected late November 10) will be a key indicator of Republican cohesion on the war. With 22 absent Republicans and a razor-thin 218-214 majority, procedural failure could advance impeachment inadvertently. Even if blocked, Senator Tillis's statement about voting for impeachment signals potential Senate support that exceeds what conviction would require.

Brent fell to 91.68 USD/Bbl on November 10, 2026, down 0.96% from the previous day. Over the past month, Brent's price has fallen 14.04%, but it is still 40.70% higher than a year ago.

This disconnect creates significant snap-back risk if diplomatic progress fails to materialize.

## 9. Eisenkot Coalition Talks: 26th Knesset Sworn In [VERIFIED]

Israel's parliament was sworn in on Tuesday without a new government, after a deadlocked general election stripped Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu of his parliamentary majority but left his main rivals with no clear path to power. Lawmakers elected in the October 27 vote took their oaths of office as Gadi Eisenkot, a former general, sought to forge a coalition.

Eisenkot initially has until December 6 to form a government but he can seek a two-week extension to December 20.

Eisenkot and his allies also lack a majority and he has yet to strike deals with parties that could bring him to power. If both Eisenkot and Netanyahu fail to form coalitions, another election could follow.

Eisenkot has ruled out forming a government with the Arab-led parties, which would give him a 67-seat bloc.

**Significance:** Israel's political transition remains uncertain. Eisenkot has 28 days (extendable to 42) to form a government without formally including Arab parties. His success would likely ease one source of escalatory pressure; failure could return Netanyahu to power or trigger yet another election.
