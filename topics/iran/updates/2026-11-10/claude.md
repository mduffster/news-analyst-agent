### **TRUMP DEMANDS WAR COST REPAYMENT — NAMES ISRAEL FOR FIRST TIME** [VERIFIED]

In an interview with CBS News that aired Sunday, Trump said such countries would "pay us any and all costs we had for the war". Trump — for the first time — singled out Israel, which was a direct participant in the war against Iran from the outset.

Just days before the midterm vote, it emerged the Pentagon's estimate for the total cost of the war had surged to $47 billion. Speaking to CBS News' Ross Douthat in a *60 Minutes* interview, Trump said protected nations would "pay us back any and all costs that we've had for the war." He added, "We're gonna get all that money back."

From Trump's perspective, "Israel pushed him into a disastrous war with Iran", according to analysts quoted by Al Jazeera. Having already suffered extensive financial losses during the war, the US's Gulf allies will not take well to being asked to pay more for a war they did not start. Gulf states will be particularly averse to such a demand as "the US-Israeli war led to considerable damage to their [civilian] and military infrastructure when Iran struck back".

**[ANALYSIS]** This marks a significant rhetorical shift — Trump publicly naming Israel as owing war costs for the first time. Combined with his plan to end $4 billion in funding to Israel by end of 2026, this signals a meaningful deterioration in the U.S.-Israel relationship post-midterms. Gulf states are likely to resist strongly given their own infrastructure losses.

### **JIM JORDAN ELECTED HOUSE GOP MINORITY LEADER** [VERIFIED]

Rep. Jim Jordan of Ohio won the race Tuesday to serve as House Republican leader as the GOP prepares for its new role in the minority. Jordan, who serves as chairman of the House Judiciary Committee, defeated Rep. Steve Scalise of Louisiana in a secret ballot election. The vote was 106-73.

Jordan's victory marks a major shake-up in House Republican leadership, elevating a founding member of the hardline Freedom Caucus who campaigned as an alternative to the leaders who presided over the GOP's crushing midterm defeat.

Jordan is a hardline conservative and close ally of President Trump. His ascension to minority leader represents a significant shakeup for House Republicans, who are looking to rebuild after losing more than 40 House seats.

**[ANALYSIS]** Jordan's elevation signals Republicans will take a combative approach in the minority rather than seeking accommodation. This could complicate any efforts at bipartisan deals on the war. Jordan has historically been supportive of Trump's foreign policy.

### **US EVACUATES AIRCRAFT FROM 2ND UK BASE — IRAN THREAT** [VERIFIED]

The US evacuated aircraft from a second UK military base in response to ongoing threats from Iran, a month after its bombers left a different English airbase in the wake of an alleged terror plot. US refueling tankers on Monday left RAF Mildenhall in Suffolk, England for Germany, according to flight-tracking data and a person familiar with the matter, who confirmed the decision to move the planes related to unspecified threats from Iran.

The nature of the threat, reportedly linked to Iran, against RAF Mildenhall, about 80 miles north of London, was unclear. But it was serious enough that military officials repositioned at least 10 KC-135 Stratotankers to other bases.

The United States also upgraded the security threat level at RAF Mildenhall and another airbase RAF Lakenheath to "FPCON Charlie," according to The WSJ and the defense industry publication The War Zone.

The circumstances of the incident attracted huge international attention, coming at a time of heightened concern about the security implications for Western states of the war between the United States and Iran. RAF Fairford had been used by US bombers to carry out strikes on Iran.

**[ANALYSIS]** This represents continued Iranian asymmetric pressure on Western basing. Two UK bases now affected — Fairford (bombers) and Mildenhall (tankers). FPCON Charlie is a significant threat level. Iran is demonstrating it can impose costs beyond the immediate theater.

### **ADNOC VESSEL ATTACKED IN UAE WATERS + NEW HORMUZ STRIKE** [VERIFIED]

A vessel operated by ADNOC Logistics & Services was damaged in an attack in UAE territorial waters on Tuesday, as maritime authorities reported a separate apparent projectile strike against a tanker near the Strait of Hormuz, adding to a growing series of attacks on commercial shipping in the region. ADNOC Logistics & Services confirmed that one of its vessels sustained damage in an attack in the Persian Gulf on the afternoon of November 10.

According to UKMTO Warning 201-26, the incident occurred at 1338 UTC on November 10. The vessel's master reported that all crew members were safe and there was no environmental impact. A damage assessment was planned for first light.

In its November 8 advisory, the Joint Maritime Information Center (JMIC) maintained the threat level for the Strait of Hormuz at SEVERE, warning that further deliberate attacks were highly likely.

**[ANALYSIS]** Attacks on shipping continue despite Trump's "war effectively over" declaration. The pattern shows Iran maintaining pressure through maritime strikes even as markets price in peace. This underlines the gap between diplomatic rhetoric and operational reality.

### **HOUTHIS ATTACK SAUDI ARABIA — MISSILES INTERCEPTED OVER RIYADH** [VERIFIED]

Saudi Arabia downs missiles fired at Riyadh after Houthi attack on pipeline. Saudi forces intercept two missiles fired towards Riyadh, hours after reporting damage to key East-West pipeline.

On Monday, the East-West oil pipeline in Saudi Arabia's Medina region was attacked again. The pipeline has come under attack several times, as the war on Iran fuels a global energy crisis.

Saudi Arabia, the world's biggest oil exporter, has faced near-daily Houthi strikes since early October, including deadly attacks that closed Riyadh's international airport for two weeks.

Yemen's Iran-backed Houthi rebels announced on the 10th (local time) that they launched attacks targeting the international airport and financial district in the Saudi capital. The Houthis also attacked Aramco facilities in Abqaiq, a key Saudi oil installation.

**[ANALYSIS]** Saudi infrastructure continues to be targeted daily. The East-West pipeline attack is significant — it's one of Saudi Arabia's key workarounds to bypass Hormuz. Houthi escalation adds another vector of pressure on the energy supply chain.
