## Key Developments Since Prior Report (August 27)

### 1. Iran to Publish Formal Conditions List for Hormuz Reopening — DIPLOMATIC SHIFT

**[OFFICIAL]** Iranian security chief Mohsen Rezaei has said that Tehran is drafting a list of conditions to open the Strait of Hormuz in response to mediator requests, adding that the US must first take practical steps to fulfill these demands before the waterway reopens.

**[VERIFIED]** Qatar and Pakistan helped broker a memorandum of understanding in June that led to a ceasefire but that quickly unravelled due to disagreements between Iran and the U.S. over the strait. Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council, said on Thursday Iran was preparing a list of conditions for reopening the strait.

**[OFFICIAL]** A spokesperson for Iran's Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) said on Wednesday that Tehran would not allow a reopening unless Washington lifted its blockade of Iranian ports, paid compensation for war damages and removed sanctions.

**[VERIFIED]** In the past Iran's conditions have included an end to the U.S. blockade on Iranian ports, compensation and removal of sanctions. It remained unclear whether Iran intended to amend those demands in a way that might be acceptable to Washington.

*Assessment: The formal conditions list approach represents a shift from the IRGC's revenue-sharing announcement yesterday. Iran is moving from asserting control to establishing a negotiating framework. However, the known conditions — end blockade, remove sanctions, pay compensation — remain fundamentally incompatible with US positions.*

### 2. Goldman Sachs: Gulf Oil Exports at Two-Thirds Pre-War Level — MAJOR DATA

**[VERIFIED]** Oil exports from the Persian Gulf have recovered to around two-thirds of pre-war levels, limiting the Iran war's impact on global crude prices, according to Goldman Sachs Group Inc. Total exports of crude and oil products from the region have risen to 15 million to 16 million barrels a day, helped by higher crossings through the Strait of Hormuz. That's still 7 to 8 million barrels below pre-conflict levels, but well above a trough of 5 million to 6 million barrels a day in March.

**[VERIFIED]** These revisions suggest oil transits through the Strait of Hormuz are probably close to Trump administration's estimates of 8 million bpd to 10 million bpd. Goldman Sachs estimates the Gulf states are exporting roughly 15 million to 16 million barrels of oil per day, or more than 60% of the amount the region supplied to the world before the Iran war started. Oil exports from the Gulf are still 7 million bpd to 8 million bpd below the normal level before the war.

**[VERIFIED]** "Oil transits through the Strait of Hormuz alone are likely close to US officials' 8 million to 10 million barrel a day estimates. "The rise in dark crossings by specialized shippers, and in ship-to-ship transfers shows that producers and shippers are adapting to the Mideast conflict."

*Assessment: This is significant new data. Goldman's analysis suggests the oil market has adapted far more than headline "Hormuz is closed" reporting indicates. Much shipping is now "dark" (transponders off) or using ship-to-ship transfers. This explains why oil prices remain in the $85-90 range rather than spiking higher.*

### 4. Qatar PM Completes Tehran Talks — MEDIATION FRAMEWORK ESTABLISHED

**[VERIFIED]** Qatari Prime Minister and Foreign Minister Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al Thani said on X that he underscored to senior Iranian officials "the importance of respecting freedom of navigation in the Strait of Hormuz in accordance with international law." Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council, later said Iran was preparing a list of conditions for reopening the strait after mediators asked Tehran to set them out.

**[VERIFIED]** Qatar's Prime Minister and Foreign Minister Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al Thani travelled to Tehran, where he held talks with Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi and met other senior Iranian leaders, including President Masoud Pezeshkian and Parliament Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf.

**[VERIFIED]** The talks focused on efforts to reduce tensions and create conditions for renewed dialogue between Iran and the US. The two officials also discussed ongoing talks on a proposed phased plan for the Strait of Hormuz, including a temporary shipping corridor and a joint project to clear mines from the waterway.

*Assessment: Qatar's engagement produced a concrete outcome: Iran agreed to formalize its conditions rather than simply maintaining ambiguity. This creates potential for structured negotiations even if the conditions themselves are difficult. Qatar pushed specifically on "freedom of navigation in accordance with international law" — a direct challenge to IRGC toll claims.*

**[VERIFIED]** Tehran and Washington have exchanged little more than bellicose statements in recent weeks, leaving the diplomacy to third parties. Trump declared the June MOU "over" on July 8 and has cranked up efforts to wreck Iran's economy.

*Assessment: The US has explicitly disengaged from direct talks, leaving all diplomacy to Qatar and other mediators. This is consistent with the "maximum pressure" strategy of letting economic pain force Iranian concessions.*

### 8. Strait Traffic — EFFECTIVELY CLOSED BUT OIL FLOWING

**[VERIFIED]** Effectively no, it is closed to commercial shipping as of 28 August 2026. 3 ships transited on August 23 vs ~85/day normal. Brent $89.30.

**[VERIFIED]** Traffic through the Strait of Hormuz is back down following a brief pick-up. After an almost daily back and forth over whether the strait is open or closed, blockaded or not, vessel traffic is nowhere near the hundred-plus ships a day that were traversing the strait before the war.

**[VERIFIED]** It has become difficult to figure out how much oil is flowing from the Gulf because information on tanker traffic in the region is limited and delayed due to the war. More tankers are leaving the region with their transponders turned off so they don't show up in real time on ship-tracking platforms. Satellite coverage to detect vessels is limited and ship-to-ship transfers outside Hormuz have increased.

*Assessment: The apparent contradiction — "closed" but 15-16 million bpd flowing — is explained by dark shipping, ship-to-ship transfers, and alternate routes. The headline "3 ships/day" vastly understates actual oil flow. Commercial shipping remains deterred but oil is finding ways through.*

### 9. Iran Fuel Crisis — QUOTA CUTS EXPECTED

**[VERIFIED]** Panic buying intensified the strain after Iranian officials signaled possible reductions in fuel quotas and higher prices for purchases beyond allotments. Mohsen Haji-Mirzaei, chief of staff to President Masoud Pezeshkian, said quotas would be "cut" while the subsidised price remained unchanged, with excess purchases facing higher costs.

**[VERIFIED]** A top official in charge of domestic energy supply said the gasoline market had a daily deficit of 14-15 million liters due to record demand, damage incurred in the war and "changes in the national budget's priorities."

**[VERIFIED]** Mohammad-Jafar Ghaem-Panah, executive deputy to Iranian regime president Masoud Pezeshkian, admitted that an increase in gasoline prices is inevitable and acknowledged the high costs of importing fuel. Referring to the impact of the naval blockade, he stressed that domestic gasoline production is insufficient to meet the country's needs.

*Assessment: Iran is moving toward quota cuts rather than immediate price increases — a politically safer approach given 2019 fuel protest history. The daily deficit of 14-15 million liters (~10% of consumption) is severe but manageable through rationing.*

## Emerging Signals

**"Dark" Oil Market:** Goldman's revelation that much Hormuz traffic is invisible (transponders off, ship-to-ship transfers, delayed data) fundamentally changes understanding of the crisis. The strait is not "closed" in the way headline reporting suggests — it's operating through workarounds that don't appear in real-time tracking.

**Iran's Negotiating Posture:** The shift from IRGC asserting revenue-sharing to Rezaei announcing a formal conditions list suggests internal Iranian decision to prioritize negotiation framework over unilateral control claims. This could indicate pragmatists gaining influence or simply tactical flexibility.

**US Disengagement as Strategy:** The explicit "no talks" stance leaves all diplomacy to third parties. This may be deliberate — letting mediators explore Iranian flexibility without US commitment, while economic pressure continues.

**Pipeline Bypass Momentum:** Goldman Sachs analysts estimate that, by the end of 2027, bypass projects will add approximately 3.8 million barrels per day to export capacity outside the Strait of Hormuz. This figure could reach 7.3 million barrels per day by the end of 2028. Long-term, Hormuz is becoming less critical regardless of this crisis's resolution.
