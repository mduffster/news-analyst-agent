## Key Developments Since Prior Report (August 4)

### 1. Iran-Oman Finalize Draft Hormuz Agreement — Awaiting Khamenei Approval

**[OFFICIAL]** Iran said an agreement with Oman has been reached on a proposed route for shipping through the Strait of Hormuz. A joint statement from Tehran and Muscat is under review and in the final drafting stage, Iranian foreign ministry spokesman Esmail Baghaei told reporters on Wednesday.

**[OFFICIAL]** Iran and Oman have agreed on the geographical coordinates of a proposed safe commercial shipping route through the Strait of Hormuz and are finalizing a joint statement. "Provided that certain third parties do not obstruct the process, a joint statement by the two countries outlining the main considerations and points agreed upon is also being reviewed and finalized," Baghaei said.

**[REPORTED]** Iranian and Omani negotiators have finalized a draft agreement to reopen the Strait of Hormuz and are awaiting final approval from Iran's Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei, the Associated Press reported Wednesday, citing two regional officials. The proposed temporary arrangement would route ships entering the Persian Gulf through an Iranian-controlled lane and outbound vessels through an Omani-controlled route, with service fees charged for security and environmental protection.

**[REPORTED]** A senior Iranian official said only one or two issues remained unresolved before an agreement could be reached. Any closure or reopening of the Strait of Hormuz will depend on measures taken by Washington.

*Assessment: This represents concrete progress — geographic coordinates agreed, joint statement in drafting, awaiting Supreme Leader approval. However, the structural gap with US demands remains: Iran is insisting on service fees and Iranian-controlled routes, while US officials demand "freedom of movement" with no tolls.*

---

### 2. Deal Structure Reveals Core Compromise — Iranian Control With Fees

**[REPORTED]** Under the emerging agreement, ships would enter the Persian Gulf through an Iranian-controlled route and exit through a route controlled by Oman, with service fees charged for providing security and preserving the maritime environment. Negotiations are still underway and the final agreement could take a different form, with any deal linked to lifting the US blockade on Iran's ports.

**[REPORTED]** The emerging deal would meet some of Iran's demands for greater control over traffic in the Strait of Hormuz — control it didn't have before the war. According to two regional sources, the deal under discussion sets up a 60-day temporary arrangement between Oman and Iran in the Hormuz strait, which could be extended.

**[OFFICIAL]** US officials said the Trump administration wants total freedom of commercial navigation through the waterway without Iranian tolls. A US official familiar with the negotiations told The Associated Press that any temporary arrangement would not involve Iranian approval for ships to transit or charges for using the waterway. Washington remained committed to restoring the previous system in which "no party controls the lanes or the ability to transit through them".

*Assessment: The gap is now explicit: Iran's draft includes service fees and Iranian-controlled routes. The US categorically rejects both. This disconnect suggests the Iran-Oman bilateral deal may proceed separately from a US-Iran arrangement.*

### 3. US Removes Sanctions on IRGC-Linked Airlines — Apparent Confidence-Building

**[VERIFIED]** The United States has removed counterterrorism sanctions from two aircraft and three airlines with links to Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps, according to details posted to the U.S. Treasury Department's website on Wednesday.

*Assessment: This is a significant goodwill gesture, removing sanctions that were added just days ago on IRGC-linked entities. It signals US willingness to make tangible concessions as talks advance. This may be intended to encourage Iranian leadership to finalize the deal.*

### 4. Trump: "A Lot of Progress" — Deal Could Come Wednesday

**[OFFICIAL]** President Donald Trump said a deal on the Strait of Hormuz could come as early as Wednesday, as Iran and Oman inched toward an agreement. "It could happen. Tomorrow or the next day," the president said. "A lot of progress has been made."

**[OFFICIAL]** President Trump told reporters "a lot of progress had been made" on the subject of reopening the Strait of Hormuz. Iran and Oman have been in talks over the strait, and an Iranian source familiar with the discussions told CBS News they are largely focused on the mechanics of reopening the waterway.

**[REPORTED]** Two regional sources said Iranian Foreign Minister Araghchi agreed in principle over the weekend but still needed approval from Iran's Supreme Leader, Mojtaba Khamenei, and the Supreme National Security Council. A U.S. official and a regional source said Iranian leadership completed its approval process on Tuesday.

*Assessment: If the Axios report is accurate that Iranian leadership "completed its approval process on Tuesday," a deal announcement today or tomorrow is plausible. However, Iranian state media has not confirmed this.*

### 5. Reuters: US Has Depleted "Virtually All" Long-Range Precision Missiles

**[VERIFIED]** The U.S. has used nearly all of its global stockpile of long-range precision missiles during the war with Iran. Specifically, the U.S. has used virtually all of its Army Tactical Missile Systems, known as ATACMS, and Precision Strike Missiles.

**[VERIFIED]** The US military has burned through nearly four-fifths of its THAAD missile inventory compared to pre-war numbers and roughly half of its Patriot interceptors since the start of the war. The disclosure of the low THAAD inventory has not been previously reported.

**[VERIFIED]** Trump's decision last week not to launch new strikes on Iran was partly due to warnings from military advisers about the depleted missile stockpiles, as well as pressure from U.S. allies in the Persian Gulf.

*Assessment: This is a major revelation that explains Trump's repeated pull-back from announced strikes. The US has limited ability to sustain intensive military operations, creating pressure for a diplomatic resolution.*

### 6. Houthis Threaten to Expand Attacks to Northern Red Sea

**[OFFICIAL]** Yemen's Houthi militant group said it would attack Saudi oil tankers in the northern Red Sea, a potential fresh escalation in its attacks on shipping. Houthi military spokesperson Yahya Saree said the move was a result of the kingdom diverting ships away from the Bab el-Mandeb chokepoint.

**[REPORTED]** The Houthis have claimed responsibility for another attack on commercial shipping in the Red Sea, targeting NCC Wafa, a Saudi Arabia-flagged tanker controlled by Bahri. On Tuesday, India-flagged dhow Faize Noore Oliya was also hit in the Red Sea. The vessel later capsized and sank, though all seafarers on board were rescued.

*Assessment: The Houthi front continues escalating independently of the US-Iran diplomatic track. The threat to attack Saudi tankers in the northern Red Sea would extend their operational range significantly.*
