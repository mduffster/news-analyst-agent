## Critical Developments (August 5)

### 1. CRITICAL: Iran-Oman Finalize Draft Hormuz Agreement — Awaiting Joint Statement [OFFICIAL/REPORTED]

Iran and Oman have agreed on the geographical coordinates of a proposed safe commercial shipping route through the Strait of Hormuz and are finalizing a joint statement, Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesperson Esmaeil Baghaei said on Wednesday.

"Provided that certain third parties do not obstruct the process, a joint statement by the two countries outlining the main considerations and points agreed upon is also being reviewed and finalized," Baghaei said.

A senior Iranian official said only one or two issues remained unresolved before an agreement could be reached. Iranian and Omani negotiators have finalized a draft agreement to reopen the Strait of Hormuz and are awaiting final approval from Iran's Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei, the Associated Press reported Wednesday, citing two regional officials.

Two regional sources said Araghchi agreed in principle over the weekend but still needed approval from Iran's Supreme Leader, Mojtaba Khamenei, and the Supreme National Security Council. A U.S. official and a regional source said Iranian leadership completed its approval process on Tuesday.

*Assessment: [OFFICIAL/REPORTED] This represents the most concrete progress to date. Geographic coordinates agreed, joint statement in final drafting, and reported Khamenei approval on Tuesday suggest a deal announcement could come today or tomorrow. The conditional language ("if certain third parties do not obstruct") provides diplomatic cover if the US rejects terms.*

### 2. CRITICAL: Deal Structure Reveals Dispute Over Fees — Conflicting Reports [REPORTED]

**AP/PBS Reporting (with fees):**
Under the emerging agreement, ships would enter the Persian Gulf through an Iranian-controlled route and exit through a route controlled by Oman, with service fees charged for providing security and preserving the maritime environment, two regional officials told The Associated Press.

**CBS News Reporting (no fees):**
The Iranian-Omani proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz would have no tolls or service fees for ships transiting the waterway. Under the proposal, ships entering the strait would use the route closest to Iran, under Tehran's coordination. To exit, ships would use the route closest to Oman, coordinated by Muscat. That proposal would also exclude tolls or service fees to transit the vital waterway, a major sticking point in the various talks.

If approved, the U.S and Iran would return to the negotiating table under terms set out in the memorandum of understanding in June, which calls for a 60-day period of free transit through the strait.

A senior Gulf official familiar with the talks suggested there is about a 50:50 chance of a deal by Friday.

*Assessment: [REPORTED — CONFLICTING] The fee question remains the core uncertainty. AP sources say fees will be charged; CBS sources say they will not. The 60-day structure suggests any fees would be deferred, consistent with the June MoU framework. Iran gets Iranian-controlled inbound routing regardless — this represents a structural gain over pre-war status.*

### 4. SIGNIFICANT: Trump Reaffirms Optimistic Timeline [OFFICIAL]

"US President Donald Trump said an agreement could be reached this week. "It could happen. Tomorrow or the next day," Trump said Tuesday. "A lot of progress has been made." But he again threatened to attack Iran should no deal be reached.

The proposed agreement is intended to restore the June 17 ceasefire between Washington and Tehran; resolve disputes over shipping through the strategic waterway, through which one-fifth of the world's oil supplies had been shipped in peacetime; and create space for broader negotiations on Iran's nuclear programme and other issues.

*Assessment: [OFFICIAL] Trump's timeline remains consistent with yesterday's "today or tomorrow" claim. The threat of strikes if no deal materializes maintains pressure on Iran while diplomatic machinery advances.*

### 5. SIGNIFICANT: US Missile Depletion Confirmed at Critical Levels [VERIFIED]

The US military has exhausted nearly 80% of its interceptors for a key missile defense system as senior US military commanders are warning that the Pentagon's munitions stockpile is "dangerously low." The US military has burned through nearly four-fifths of its THAAD missile inventory compared to pre-war numbers and roughly half of its Patriot interceptors since the start of the war.

The disclosure of the low THAAD inventory has not been previously reported. The Center for Strategic and International Studies estimates the United States had around 2,200 Patriots and 452 THAAD missiles in its stockpile before the Iran war started.

The long-range missiles that have been primarily used up during the war with Iran are known as Army Tactical Missile Systems (ATACMS) and Precision Strike Missiles (PrSM). The US' stockpile of Tomahawk missiles also remains significantly depleted, as close to half of those weapons have been used during the war.

Replenishment rates are low for key missiles — according to current fiscal year delivery schedules, the Pentagon is receiving roughly 15 new Tomahawks and 20 new Patriot missiles per month. There are no THAAD deliveries forecast in 2026. CSIS estimated it would take three or more years to rebuild those inventories to pre-Iran war levels.

*Assessment: [VERIFIED] The 80% THAAD depletion figure is newly reported and more severe than prior estimates. This explains Trump's pattern of announcing then canceling strikes — the US lacks the munitions for sustained high-intensity operations. This constraint fundamentally reinforces the diplomatic track.*

### 6. SIGNIFICANT: Houthis Threaten Northern Red Sea Expansion — Saudi Tanker Struck [OFFICIAL/VERIFIED]

Yemen's Houthi militant group said it would attack Saudi oil tankers in the northern Red Sea, a potential fresh escalation in its attacks on shipping. Houthi military spokesperson Yahya Saree said the move was a result of the kingdom diverting ships away from the Bab el-Mandeb chokepoint to the south of the waterway.

Yemen's Houthi rebels claimed responsibility Wednesday for a missile strike on a Saudi-flagged oil tanker in the Red Sea. Houthi military spokesperson Yahya Saree said the group struck the Wafa oil tanker with several ballistic missiles Wednesday morning in the northern Red Sea off the coast of Yanbu, a critical port city in Saudi Arabia.

The Wafa is the eighth Saudi oil tanker the Houthis have said their forces have struck since announcing the blockade. Mr. Saree has said the Houthis have turned away 29 other tankers since last month. The Red Sea has taken on an outsized role in transporting Saudi oil since Iran effectively closed the Strait of Hormuz this year.

On Tuesday (August 4), India-flagged dhow Faize Noore Oliya was also hit in the Red Sea. The vessel later capsized and sank, though all seafarers on board were rescued by Yemeni authorities. The Houthis are also thought to be behind that attack.

*Assessment: [OFFICIAL/VERIFIED] The Houthi front continues escalating independently of the US-Iran diplomatic track. The threat to attack Saudi tankers in the northern Red Sea — far from the Bab el-Mandeb chokepoint — represents significant operational expansion. A Hormuz deal does not resolve the Red Sea crisis.*

### 7. MODERATE: Israel Launches Strikes in Southern Lebanon [VERIFIED]

The Israeli military began a series of "precision strikes" Wednesday on a town in southern Lebanon after warning residents to evacuate, saying the Iran-backed militia Hezbollah violated the standing ceasefire. Casualties from the strikes are still unknown, and there was no immediate statement from the Israeli Defense Force or Hezbollah.

For the first time in weeks, Israel's military told residents of an area in Lebanon to evacuate. The military said it was in response to what it described as "a blatant violation of the ceasefire" by the Iran-backed Hezbollah militant group. Military spokesperson Lt. Col. Ella Waweya told residents of Mansouri to immediately move at least 1,000 meters north of the village.

The last evacuation order issued by the IDF was on June 14, when it warned residents of 29 towns and villages in southern Lebanon's Nabatieh and Sidon districts to evacuate, citing Hezbollah's violations of the cease-fire agreement.

*Assessment: [VERIFIED] The Lebanon front shows renewed volatility. This is the first IDF evacuation order in nearly two months, suggesting escalation rather than progress toward the broader ceasefire. Even if Hormuz de-risks, the conflict system continues generating escalation via the Lebanon front.*
