# US-Israel-Iran Conflict: Second/Third Ship Sinks as Houthis Strike Aden Presidential Palace; GCC-EU Summit Postponed

## October 15, 2026 — Day 230

**Assessment:** The conflict intensified on multiple fronts today. Two cargo ships have now been confirmed sunk in the past 24-48 hours—GFS Pride and the bulk carrier Cape Dao—bringing total vessel losses to three since February. The Houthis struck Yemen's presidential palace in Aden with missiles, killing two guards while the Prime Minister survived. This attack, the first on Aden since fighting resumed, opens a dangerous new theater. The GCC-EU summit scheduled for October 24 in Riyadh was postponed due to sustained Houthi attacks and King Khalid Airport's closure. President Pezeshkian publicly acknowledged Iran faces its "most intense blockade since 1979"—a significant admission of pressure. The Pentagon is developing strike plans that include potential MOAB use against Iranian energy and nuclear sites for post-midterm action, though military officials doubt its effectiveness. Oil fell ~2% to $103-104 despite the escalation, while the rial held steady at 2,657,000/USD.

## Twin Ship Sinkings Confirm Escalating Maritime Crisis [VERIFIED]

The maritime situation in and around Hormuz is worse than previously understood. "A container ship and a dry bulk carrier have both sunk near the Strait of Hormuz in the past ‌24 hours after being hit by projectiles in separate incidents," according to Reuters. One ship was Singapore-flagged (GFS Pride) and the other Antigua and Barbuda–flagged (Cape Dao); one crew member died.

Two cargo ships have sunk near the Strait of Hormuz in the past 24 hours after being struck by unknown projectiles in separate incidents. The Singapore-registered container ship GFS Pride was struck on Tuesday while passing through the strait, catching fire and later sinking. All crew members were safely evacuated.

It is the third commercial ship sunk during the Iran war, according to the International Maritime Organization, a United Nations agency.

A dispute has emerged over the cause of at least one sinking. A US official said Iran lied about a cargo ship that sunk on Wednesday and that Iran attacked the ship, likely with a missile. Iran claimed the cargo ship sunk after striking a naval mine as it was attempting to navigate the Strait of Hormuz.

The attack tempo remains at record levels. The United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations said it has recorded 20 reported attacks in the Strait of Hormuz since October 1, with two incidents reported on Wednesday.

**Analysis:** The confirmation of three vessel losses (not two as previously reported) establishes a more severe baseline than yesterday's assessment. The mine-versus-projectile dispute matters: mines represent indiscriminate area-denial capability, while missiles indicate targeted strikes. Both remain dangerous, but mines would fundamentally change insurance calculations and the viability of even escorted transits.

## Houthis Strike Aden Presidential Palace — First Attack Since Civil War Resumed [VERIFIED]

The Houthis opened a new front by targeting Yemen's internationally recognized government directly.

Yemen's Houthi militia attacked the Presidential Palace in the southern city of Aden with five missiles on Thursday, the Information Minister Moammar Al Eryani said. He added that the Prime Minister and government members were safe. It was the first attack on Aden since the Houthis and government forces resumed fighting last month after a four-year lull in the civil war.

The government says the prime minister and other officials are safe, but two of his bodyguards were killed in the attack.

The Iran-aligned rebels claimed they were targeting an alleged Saudi military operations center inside the compound. The Houthis claimed the strike was part of a declared strategy of targeting Saudi military deployments wherever they are located.

**Significance:** This attack represents a geographic and symbolic expansion. The Houthis have now demonstrated willingness to strike at the heart of the internationally recognized Yemeni government, not just Saudi Arabia proper. The Houthi framing—that Aden hosts a "Saudi military operations headquarters"—signals they will target coalition assets anywhere in Yemen. This complicates any potential negotiations and demonstrates the Houthis are fighting a multi-front campaign.

## GCC-EU Summit Postponed — Houthis Achieve Diplomatic Impact [OFFICIAL]

"The second Summit between the European Union and the Gulf Cooperation Council, originally planned for 24 October 2026 in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia, has been postponed," said the European Council representing the 27-nation bloc's member states.

GCC Secretary General Jasem Mohamed Albudaiwi said in a statement that, in light of the Houthi group's continued attempts to target Saudi Arabia and its civilian objects, threatening the security and safety of innocent civilians, and given the extended suspension of flight operations at King Khalid International Airport, "the GCC General Secretariat announces the rescheduling of the GCC-EU Summit."

The postponement is the latest blow to Saudi Arabia's effort to host global events despite the escalating war with the Houthis. The summit had been intended in part to demonstrate European solidarity with Gulf ‌states and Saudi Arabia amid the war, making its postponement a sign of how seriously the security situation has deteriorated.

**Analysis:** The Houthis have achieved a significant strategic objective: forcing the postponement of a major international summit that was explicitly designed to show European solidarity with the Gulf states. This demonstrates that Houthi strikes can impose real diplomatic and economic costs beyond direct physical damage. The continued closure of King Khalid International Airport—Riyadh's main gateway—is now affecting international relations.

## Saudi Coalition: 19 Killed, 364 Injured in October Airport Attacks [OFFICIAL]

Nineteen people were killed and 364 injured in attacks by Yemen's Houthi group on Saudi airports, including King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh and Abha International Airport, the Saudi-led coalition said Thursday.

"King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh had been targeted in 20 Houthi attacks since the beginning of October. Seventeen people were killed and 333 others injured in attacks on the airport between October 7 and 11. Two people were also killed and 28 others injured in an attack on Abha International Airport in the southwestern Asir region."

According to al-Maliki, 1,104 missiles and 1,135 unmanned aerial vehicles have been launched at Saudi territory since the escalation began.

**Note:** These figures represent an updated tally covering October 7-11; the cumulative toll continues to rise with ongoing attacks.

## Pezeshkian: "Most Intense Blockade Since 1979" [OFFICIAL]

Iran's president made a striking public admission about the blockade's impact.

"Over the 48 years since the Revolution, we have never faced such intense blockade, war and difficulties as we are facing now," Pezeshkian said in a speech broadcast by state television.

Pezeshkian said the US was focusing its efforts on making "the people become upset and face difficulties, and then for social protests to emerge so that they can achieve their sinister objectives."

The US imposed a blockade on Iranian ports in response to Tehran's closure of the strategic Strait of Hormuz, diverting or stopping vessels traveling to and from Iranian ports.

**Analysis:** This is the most direct acknowledgment yet from Iranian leadership that the US blockade is having severe effects. Pezeshkian's framing—accusing the US of deliberately trying to provoke protests—reveals regime concern about internal stability. This statement aligns with prior reporting on 2025-2026 Iranian protests, suggesting the economic squeeze is generating real domestic pressure even if it hasn't yet produced strategic capitulation.

## Pentagon Develops MOAB Strike Plans for Post-Midterm Action [REPORTED]

The US is considering using its biggest non-nuclear bomb against Iran if President Donald Trump should decide to renew combat in the war. The military is planning for the potential use of the GBU-43/B Massive Ordnance Air Blast (MOAB) bomb—better known as the "Mother Of All Bombs"—to target Iranian energy infrastructure and nuclear sites.

The president has suggested there won't be any military action against the regime before the midterm elections in November. A source told CNN that Iranian nuclear sites could become targets. This includes Pickaxe Mountain, a site believed to house nuclear components.

"POTUS has unfortunately put himself in a situation where he is almost compelled to do something and, without the ammunition designed to penetrate that level…you have to look at psychological effects for which the MOAB is well-equipped," a source familiar with planning discussions said.

However, many within the administration as well as outside analysts agree that the only way to decisively defeat Iran would be through a major operation involving ground troops. The Pentagon considers all scenarios, so those plans exist, but as of now no one in the administration is arguing for such drastic action.

**Assessment:** The MOAB planning revelation signals post-midterm escalation is seriously being considered, but the "psychological effects" language suggests this may be theater rather than strategy. Military doubts about effectiveness indicate this is politically driven—designed to give Trump a dramatic response option rather than achieve strategic objectives. The "strike and withdraw" concept reported separately would fit a face-saving exit strategy.

## Netanyahu Rules Out Pre-Election Strike on Iran [OFFICIAL]

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said on Wednesday he is not in favour of a preemptive strike against Iran before Israel's October 27 election — but also warned Tehran and its proxies against striking Israel.

"It is not convenient for us, it's no good," Netanyahu said in an interview on Israel's Army Radio, when asked about the possibility of a preemptive strike and allegations by his political rivals that he was seeking a military confrontation ahead of the election.

**Analysis:** Netanyahu's statement aligns US and Israeli timelines: neither leader wants military escalation before their respective elections (October 27 for Israel, November 3 for US midterms). The window for resumed strikes opens after late October. Netanyahu's refusal to comment on post-election possibilities keeps options open.

## Medina Missile Intercepted; Houthis Deny Targeting Holy City [REPORTED]

The Saudi-led coalition battling the Iran-backed Houthis in Yemen said Thursday that it had intercepted a cruise missile over the Medina region in western Saudi Arabia.

The Houthis said "the Saudi enemy's claims of an attack on Medina are a shameless and brazen lie."

This follows an earlier incident. The Saudi-led coalition said that a power distribution station in Medina, which supplies electricity to the Masjid an-Nabawi (the Prophet's Mosque), was attacked on Tuesday, putting one transformer out of service but without affecting the electricity network.

**Note:** Attacks on Medina are particularly sensitive given its status as one of Islam's holiest cities. The Houthis consistently deny targeting the holy sites, likely aware of the religious backlash such attacks could generate across the Muslim world.

## Oil Prices Fall Despite Escalation; Rial Holds Steady

Brent fell to 103.89 USD/Bbl on October 15, 2026, down 2.05% from the previous day. Over the past month, Brent's price has fallen 1.84%, but it is still 70.14% higher than a year ago.

On Thursday 15 October 2026, the price of US Dollar in the market was 2,657,000 Iranian Rials. This is a 0% change compared to yesterday.

On the black market, $1 is worth about 2.63-2.66 million rials, while one euro is worth 2.95-2.98 million rials.

**Analysis:** The oil price decline (~2%) amid ship sinkings and continued attacks is counterintuitive but may reflect: (1) market expectation that Hormuz disruptions have already been priced in; (2) belief MOAB talk is political posturing; (3) technical correction from recent highs. The rial's stability at weakened levels suggests markets have adjusted to the current conflict intensity.
