## Key Developments (Past 24 Hours)

### **SECOND SHIP SINKS — CAPE DAO NOW LOST** [VERIFIED]

The bulk carrier Cape Dao, struck by a projectile on September 23, sank on Wednesday, weeks after the initial attack. One crew member was killed, with the rest evacuating immediately after the strike.

The GFS Pride and Cape Dao are the second and third vessels to have sunk since the US-Iran war began on February 28, according to an analysis of IMO casualty data.

While many vessels have been hit in the Strait of Hormuz this year, these are only the second and third to have sunk in the Middle East since the Iran war began in February.

**[ANALYSIS]** Yesterday's update counted GFS Pride as the "second" sinking — the Cape Dao sinking brings the total to three vessels lost. The delayed sinking (weeks after the initial strike) shows these attacks can have cumulative effects on damaged vessels. This is a more serious escalation than previously understood.

### **US MILITARY DEVELOPS MOAB STRIKE PLANS** [REPORTED]

The U.S. military has developed new strike plans that include potentially using the "mother of all bombs" to target Iranian energy infrastructure and nuclear sites should Trump give the order to resume combat operations. The use of the GBU-43/B Massive Ordnance Air Blast Bomb, the largest non-nuclear U.S. bomb, for the first time would mark a significant escalation in the war.

Trump has ordered the military to develop additional strike packages against Iranian targets that include "a lot of energy infrastructure" and the potential use of a MOAB. The same source said the phrasing used was that this attack would come "prior to US retrograde in CENTCOM AOR (area of responsibility)," indicating the administration could launch the additional strikes, attempt to declare victory and quickly pull everyone out.

Military plans for striking Iranian nuclear sites, including Pickaxe Mountain, also explicitly call for using the MOAB. Trump continues to emphasize that his goal is to ensure Iran never obtains a nuclear weapon.

Officials familiar with military planning discussions told US news outlet CNN and Al Jazeera that deploying the MOAB would be unlikely to help the US achieve its stated strategic goals, including to destroy Iran's nuclear capabilities. Brussels-based military expert Elijah Magnier told Al Jazeera that using the MOAB would not even guarantee the destruction of Iran's deeply buried underground nuclear installations and that such a strike would likely amount to little more than "political theatre".

**[ANALYSIS]** The MOAB planning leak is significant for several reasons: (1) it signals possible post-midterm escalation; (2) the "retrograde" language suggests a strike-and-withdraw strategy—a face-saving exit rather than sustained campaign; (3) military doubts about effectiveness suggest this is being driven by political rather than strategic considerations.

### **US-ISRAEL DIVERGE ON STRIKE TIMING** [OFFICIAL]

A group of senior US military officials, including Secretary of War Pete Hegseth, is pushing to resume the war against Iran, while advisers to US President Donald Trump favor continuing to "strangle Iran" through economic sanctions.

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said on Wednesday he is not in favour of a preemptive strike against Iran before Israel's October 27 election — but also warned Tehran and its proxies against striking Israel.

"It is not convenient for us, it's no good," Netanyahu said in an interview on Israel's Army Radio, when asked about the possibility of a preemptive strike and allegations by his political rivals that he was seeking a military confrontation ahead of the election.

The US and Iran have been at war since February, with no Israeli-Iranian fire exchanged since June.

**[ANALYSIS]** Key divergence emerging: Netanyahu says strikes "not convenient" before his October 27 election; Trump ruled out strikes before his November 3 midterms. The window between late October and early November is the danger zone where either leader could opt for resumed military action. The split within the Trump administration between Hegseth's hawks and economic-pressure advocates adds further uncertainty.

### **PEZESHKIAN: "MOST INTENSE BLOCKADE SINCE 1979"** [OFFICIAL]

"Over the 48 years since the Revolution, we have never faced such intense blockade, war and difficulties as we are facing now," Pezeshkian said in a speech broadcast by state television.

Pezeshkian said the US was focusing its efforts on making "the people become upset and face difficulties, and then for social protests to emerge so that they can achieve their sinister objectives".

**[ANALYSIS]** Pezeshkian's statement is significant as an official acknowledgment of how severely the US blockade is affecting Iran. His framing—blaming the US for trying to provoke protests—reveals regime fears about internal stability. The admission of unprecedented difficulty may indicate growing pressure on Iranian leadership.

### **MARITIME ATTACKS INTENSIFY — 10+ SHIPS HIT SINCE SUNDAY** [VERIFIED]

Attacks on vessels in and around the Strait of Hormuz have intensified, with at least 10 ships struck since Sunday, the highest number over the period since the US-Israel war with Iran began.

Data from the United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations (UKMTO), which monitors shipping incidents, shows just how high the risk is. Nine attacks in or near the Strait of Hormuz have been reported since Monday, including a drone strike on a tanker.

On Monday, 4.147 million barrels of oil were transported on the Strait of Hormuz, less than a quarter of the daily average before the start of the war.

The official described a previously unreported incident last week in which Iranian spotters on a dhow were "calling in" targets to the IRGC. US forces told the occupants to abandon the dhow and then destroyed it.

**[ANALYSIS]** The attack tempo is accelerating dramatically. Oil traffic has fallen to less than 25% of pre-war levels—worse than the 40% reduction reported yesterday. Iran is using new "spotter" tactics with dhows calling in targets, indicating tactical adaptation despite US naval presence.

## Assessment

**[ANALYSIS]** Several significant shifts today:

1. **The shipping situation is worse than understood.** Three vessels have now sunk (not two), and traffic has fallen to less than 25% of pre-war levels. Iran's maritime campaign is achieving its strategic objective despite US naval operations.

2. **Post-election strike planning is real but contested.** The MOAB leak reveals active Pentagon planning, but the "retrograde" language suggests this may be about creating a political exit—strike hard, declare victory, withdraw—rather than achieving strategic objectives.

3. **Pezeshkian's admission is telling.** When an Iranian president publicly acknowledges the blockade's severity, it signals genuine internal pressure. Combined with prior reporting on protests and regime instability, this suggests the economic squeeze is working—but not yet breaking the regime.

4. **The US-Israel timing gap matters.** With Netanyahu focused on October 27 and Trump on November 3, there's a narrow window where resumed strikes become politically viable. The Hegseth faction pushing for action adds pressure.

5. **The diplomatic track is active but uncertain.** Oman, Russia, and China are all engaged with Houthis, and the Houthis have signaled openness to a comprehensive deal. However, no concrete proposal has emerged, and continued attacks suggest no imminent breakthrough.
