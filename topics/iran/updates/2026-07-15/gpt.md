## Daily incremental update — US–Israel–Iran conflict  
**As of Wed, July 15, 2026 (ET)**  
**New since Tue, July 14, 2026 (ET)**

### 1) The blockade has effectively “re-started” as a policy—and the interim deal looks functionally broken
- The **U.S. “reimposed” a naval blockade on Iran** and **intensified** its strike campaign on July 15, per AP, explicitly tying the move to Iran’s attacks on shipping and describing the interim arrangement as getting shredded. **[REPORTED]** ([apnews.com](https://apnews.com/article/b7c592f269d822407dd6b5641602bf25?utm_source=openai))  
- Separately, Reuters-sourced reporting reiterated the **enforcement start time (July 14, 4 p.m. ET)** and described the blockade as covering **all Iranian ports / terminals / coastal areas** for vessel traffic. **[REPORTED]** ([defensenews.com](https://www.defensenews.com/news/your-navy/2026/07/13/us-to-begin-enforcing-maritime-blockade-on-iran-on-tuesday/?utm_source=openai))  

**What it means:** The conflict is now centered on **a coercive maritime regime** (blockade + strike suppression of Iran’s anti-shipping toolkit). That dramatically reduces “off-ramps” because each side’s credibility is tied to visible control of access/denial in and around Hormuz.

### 2) U.S. strikes expanded/clarified: Greater Tunb Island hit (chokepoint-relevant target)
- CENTCOM (via DVIDS) reported a **July 15 morning strike wave** that hit **coastal defenses and cruise-missile storage/launch sites on Greater Tunb Island** over ~90 minutes. **[OFFICIAL]** ([dvidshub.net](https://www.dvidshub.net/news/569969/centcom-conducts-morning-round-strikes-against-iran?utm_source=openai))  
- AP also highlighted **Greater Tunb** as a strategic point in the Strait and reported casualties per Iranian accounts. **[REPORTED]** ([apnews.com](https://apnews.com/article/b7c592f269d822407dd6b5641602bf25?utm_source=openai))  

**What it means:** This is a step toward **“terrain denial”** in the strait’s immediate geography (not just dispersed coastal sites). It signals the U.S. is willing to strike assets that Iran can portray as sovereign defensive positions—raising the risk of a broader Iranian retaliation menu (regional infrastructure, proxies, or attempts to expand the maritime fight).

### 3) Iran/IRGC message hardened: threaten to expand energy export disruption beyond Hormuz
- Reuters reported the IRGC threatened to close **“all other export corridors that benefit the U.S. and its allies”**—framed as “shared by all, or denied to all.” **[REPORTED]** ([investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/commodities-news/iran-threatens-to-block-more-vital-seaways-as-trump-orders-renewed-iran-blockade-4792147?utm_source=openai))  

**What it means:** This is a material escalation in stated intent: Iran is signaling it may move from **attacking ships** to **threatening the broader regional export system** (which would widen the set of stakeholders likely to support forceful countermeasures and could accelerate energy-market and insurance shocks).

---

### 4) Market signal: oil remains in “risk repricing” mode (not a one-day spike)
- Reuters reported oil extending gains on July 15 amid worsening hostilities and the blockade/IRGC threats; AP previously noted a sharp jump tied to the blockade announcement. **[VERIFIED]** ([live.euronext.com](https://live.euronext.com/en/financial-news/oil-prices-rise-1-hostilities-worsen-middle-east?utm_source=openai))  

**What it means:** Markets are treating this as **persistent disruption risk**, not a transient headline. That increases pressure on governments to either (a) enforce a sustainable corridor quickly or (b) force a diplomatic pause—both of which can produce dangerous “first enforcement incident” dynamics.
