## Critical Developments (July 14-15)

### 1. CRITICAL: Fifth Consecutive Day of US Strikes — Rare Daytime Operation [VERIFIED]

US Central Command says it carried out strikes on Iran for 90 minutes this morning, as tensions flare over the critical Strait of Hormuz. The strikes followed attacks overnight, which Tehran said killed at least seven military personnel in the southeast of Iran.

U.S. Central Command said in a statement the military had targeted coastal defense systems and cruise missile storage and launch sites on Iran's Greater Tunb Island. The U.S. conducted a new wave of strikes against Iran's coastal defense systems and cruise missile storage and launch sites on Wednesday after reimposing a naval blockade of Iranian ports.

**Overnight Strikes:** Late on Tuesday the U.S. military said it had hit dozens of military targets near the Strait of Hormuz and Iranian coastal areas in strikes lasting seven hours.

**Trump's Framing:** "We're going to hit them very hard tonight," he said. "We're going to hit them hard tomorrow night. We're going to hit them really hard the night after."

*Assessment: [VERIFIED] The shift to daytime strikes on Greater Tunb Island — a strategic point in the Strait itself — represents an intensification of the campaign. The US is now conducting round-the-clock operations targeting terrain-denial assets, not just dispersed coastal sites.*

### 2. CRITICAL: Iran Reports 30+ Civilians Dead, 260 Wounded [OFFICIAL]

More than 30 civilians have been killed and 260 wounded in US attacks on southern Iran over the past few days, Iranian officials say.

Iranian government spokesperson Fatemeh Mohajerani said at least 30 civilians had been killed in recent days due to the U.S. strikes on southern Iran, state media reported on Wednesday. Iran's army said at least seven active-duty and conscript personnel were killed in overnight U.S.

*Assessment: [OFFICIAL] Iran is now publicizing casualty figures — likely aimed at domestic mobilization and international sympathy. These figures cannot be independently verified, but they signal Tehran is building a narrative around civilian harm.*

### 3. CRITICAL: IRGC Launches Operation Nasr 2 — Multi-Country Retaliation [OFFICIAL]

The Islamic Revolution Guards Corps (IRGC) has announced a new series of retaliatory strikes against US military facilities in Kuwait, Bahrain, and Jordan, targeting key logistics, command, and military infrastructure following renewed US attacks on Iranian territory. The Corps announced the fresh reprisal in three separate statements on Wednesday, saying the strikes formed the fourth, fifth, and sixth waves of its Operation Nasr- (Victory) 2.

**Claims by Target:**
- The IRGC claimed it had inflicted heavy damage on the US Fifth Fleet headquarters, support facilities and fuel tanks in Bahrain, as well as a major US military logistics hub at Mina Abdullah in Kuwait.
- Footage shared on social media reportedly shows Iranian missiles evading US Patriot air defense systems before striking US-hosted bases in Jordan, including King Faisal Airbase. The IRGC said its forces "smashed the hangars of F-15, F-16, and F-35 fighter jets and destroyed a number of MQ-9 strategic drones" at the US base in al-Azraq, Jordan.

**Jordan Response:** Jordan's military said its air defences intercepted and shot down three Iranian ballistic missiles that entered the country's airspace early on Wednesday.

**Damage Unconfirmed:** At the time of publication, no officials from the U.S., Bahrain, Kuwait, or Jordan had confirmed the IRGC's claims regarding damage to their military bases and infrastructure.

**IRGC Warning:** The IRGC said on Wednesday that the Strait of Hormuz would remain closed until what it described as "the end of America's evils."

*Assessment: [OFFICIAL] The IRGC continues multi-front retaliation. Claims of destruction cannot be verified. The explicit "until ultimate victory" framing signals Tehran sees this as existential.*

### 4. CRITICAL: Trump Threatens Infrastructure Strikes Next Week [OFFICIAL]

"Next week it gets really bad for them because next week comes the power plants," he said. "Next week comes the bridges. We're going to knock out all their power plants. We're going to knock out all their bridges unless they get to the table and negotiate."

"I'll save the energy targets for last, but ultimately we'll hit energy targets," Trump said.

Trump said his military objectives in Iran, including keeping the Strait of Hormuz open, have been achieved despite traffic through the waterway being down roughly 90% from normal levels Monday.

*Assessment: [OFFICIAL] Trump has set a one-week deadline for Iranian cooperation before escalating to civilian infrastructure targeting. This would cross a significant threshold — infrastructure strikes affecting power and transportation would directly impact Iranian civilians at scale.*

### 5. SIGNIFICANT: IRGC Threatens Bab el-Mandeb Closure — "Everyone or No One" [OFFICIAL]

On Wednesday, the IRGC warned it could close "all other export corridors that benefit the US and its allies", according to Iran's IRNA news agency. "Regional energy exports are either shared by all, or denied to all," the statement said.

"If the current situation aggravates, the Bab al-Mandeb Strait and the Strait of Hormuz will be closed in an operational alliance," Iranian state TV quoted Mohammad al-Farah, a member of the political bureau of the Ansar Allah movement, as saying.

"If the current situation aggravates, the Bab el-Mandeb Strait and the Strait of Hormuz will be closed in an operational alliance. Oil prices would then skyrocket to $200 a barrel in a dreadful shock," he warned.

**Analyst Assessment:** Andreas Krieg of King's College London's School of Security Studies described the latest Houthi threat as "another nuclear option" for Iran after Hormuz, one that Tehran would likely employ only if it concluded that a return to all-out war had become unavoidable.

The Houthi threat to the Bab el-Mandeb Strait is particularly troubling because some countries, such as Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates, had figured out workarounds to Hormuz to get their oil to global markets. The Saudis shipped nearly half their oil on the East-West pipeline to the Red Sea, and the UAE did a bypass out to nearly the Indian Ocean. Those both sidestepped the threat of attacks from Iranian drones and missiles but walked right into the latent threat of Houthi projectiles.

*Assessment: [OFFICIAL] This is a material escalation in stated intent. Iran is signaling willingness to weaponize both major maritime chokepoints simultaneously — Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb. The "everyone or no one" framing indicates Tehran views this as economic warfare. If executed, this would threaten the alternative Saudi pipeline route that bypasses Hormuz.*

### 6. SIGNIFICANT: Senate Democrats Block $1 Trillion NDAA [VERIFIED]

Senate Democrats blocked a $1 trillion annual defense bill Tuesday, refusing to advance the bipartisan package that would substantially increase Pentagon spending, including a pay raise for the troops, in protest of President Donald Trump's war against Iran.

"The NDAA, in my view, has become a referendum on the Iran war," Democratic Sen. Richard Blumenthal of Connecticut told reporters Tuesday. In a 50 to 46 vote, the Senate opposed an initial procedural vote to advance the National Defense Authorization Act.

The New York Democrat railed against President Trump for "waging an unauthorized war, defying bipartisan majorities in Congress, refusing to level with the American people about the cost, the mission, or the end game." "The NDAA cannot become a permission slip for that recklessness that we see occurring in Iran," he added. "Donald Trump does not get to drag the American people deeper into a war he cannot explain and does not know how to end, and then demand Congress look the other way."

Sen. Tammy Duckworth, D-Ill., announced earlier today that she would vote against the NDAA unless her amendment — which would prohibit funds authorized in the bill from being used for the Iran war — was included. "[Fifteen] of our servicemembers have been killed, billions of dollars have been wasted and our economy is in tatters," she said in a statement.

**Budget Context:** This year, the 66th annual NDAA is running into a double-whammy of opposition. Lawmakers are fuming over Trump's military action against Iran and resisting the White House request to substantially increase Pentagon spending to $1.5 trillion, up from about $900 billion last year.

*Assessment: [VERIFIED] This is a significant domestic political development. The NDAA has passed for 65 consecutive years. Democrats are using the must-pass defense bill as leverage to force accountability. However, Republicans may bypass this via budget reconciliation.*

### 7. SIGNIFICANT: US Blockade Redirects First Vessels — Iran Attacks Continue [VERIFIED]

The US military says it has "redirected" two commercial vessels since reimposing its naval blockade of ships going to and from Iranian ports. It earlier said Iran has struck seven commercial ships in the last week, leaving nearly a dozen crew members killed, missing or injured.

Kpler, a company that tracks maritime traffic, said it recorded 21 ships transiting the Strait of Hormuz on Tuesday. They were primarily commercial ships carrying crude, liquefied petroleum gas, methanol and iron ore.

**Cumulative Toll:** "However, the security outlook deteriorated further as three additional attacks off Oman were verified, bringing the reported toll to 56 confirmed incidents and 17 seafarer fatalities," Kpler said online.

**Strait Status:** Effectively no, it is closed to commercial shipping as of 14 July 2026. 34 ships transited vs ~88/day normal.

*Assessment: [VERIFIED] The blockade is now operational with enforcement underway. Traffic remains at 60-75% below normal. The cumulative toll of 56 incidents and 17 deaths demonstrates the ongoing danger to maritime commerce.*

### 8. WATCH: Mojtaba Khamenei Still Absent — Brother Delivers Message [VERIFIED]

The Tehran memorial on Tuesday proceeded as planned, but Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei did not appear in person.

Iranian officials told The New York Times that Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei was barred from attending, as they feared Israel could assassinate him during the funeral or track him afterwards, revealing his place of hiding.

Mojtaba is thought to have been wounded in the attack that killed his father, his mother and his wife. He has remained in hiding since the war began in late February, communicating with his supporters only through written statements shared by Iranian media and attributed to him. He has never shown his face or used his voice.

*Assessment: [VERIFIED] Despite expectations, Mojtaba did not appear at the memorial — his brother Mostafa delivered his message. This continues the pattern of absence that has persisted since February 28. The regime continues functioning, but questions persist about who is directing Iranian decision-making.*

Brent climbed above $85 per barrel on Wednesday, advancing for a third consecutive session after the US launched another wave of strikes against Iran while reinstating its naval blockade of Iranian ports near the Strait of Hormuz. US forces targeted dozens of military assets along Iran's coastline and near the strategic waterway during a seven-hour operation aimed at weakening Tehran's ability to disrupt shipping through Hormuz.
