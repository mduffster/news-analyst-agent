## Key Developments (Nov 24-25)

### **HOUTHIS STRIKE RAS TANURA — SAUDI'S LARGEST REFINERY** [VERIFIED]

Yemen's Iran-aligned Houthis said on Wednesday they had attacked Saudi Arabia's largest oil refinery, Ras Tanura, which sits alongside one of the world's largest crude export terminals, as satellite images showed thick black smoke over the installation.

Houthi military spokesperson Yahya Saree said the refinery attack caused fires. Ras Tanura has the largest oil refinery in Saudi Arabia with a capacity of 550,000 barrels a day.

The coalition's spokesman Turki Al-Maliki said that Saudi air defence systems destroyed four drones in Ras Tanura in Saudi Arabia's east on Wednesday morning, adding that fragments fell due to the interception, with no casualties reported in the initial assessment.

On Wednesday, Houthi brigadier general Yahya Saree warned on X that the port of Jeddah and all Saudi ports along the Red Sea coast will become "targets for our forces," if Riyadh attacks the Yemeni Red Sea port of Hodeidah.

**[ANALYSIS]** This is a significant escalation — targeting energy infrastructure rather than symbolic buildings. The simultaneous claim of Riyadh financial district and Ras Tanura attacks shows Houthis can sustain multi-front pressure. The threat to expand targeting to Jeddah and Red Sea ports raises stakes substantially.

### **TRUMP CONFIRMS WAR "FUNDED" THROUGH JANUARY** [OFFICIAL]

U.S. President Donald Trump said that funding for the war with Iran is secured until about the end of January 2027.

Trump said he hoped Democrats would not stop the war "ahead of schedule." He claimed Iran's navy, air force and air defenses had been destroyed and said a Democratic move to halt the war would turn what he called a "complete victory" into a defeat.

Trump also admitted that Washington's goals in Iran diverge from Israel's, saying that while regime change is "probably doable," he's only concerned with Tehran's nuclear program.

The war is unpopular, Congress never approved it, and it contributed to Republican losses in the midterm elections.

**[ANALYSIS]** Trump's explicit acknowledgment of the January funding horizon confirms his reluctance to escalate before Democrats take control. His statement that regime change is "probably doable" but not his goal creates daylight between US and Israeli positions — significant given Netanyahu's push for more aggressive action.

### **ISRAEL ALLEGES IRAN CHEMICAL WEAPONS PROGRAM** [OFFICIAL]

Israeli caretaker Defense Minister Israel Katz said on Tuesday that Iran was developing chemical weapons and showing signs of seeking to reactivate its nuclear program.

Speaking to Israeli reporters at his office in Tel Aviv, Katz said Iran began developing chemical weapons after the joint Israeli-American military operation was launched Feb. 28.

The Times of Israel also cited a report on Wednesday saying Israel has known for more than a decade about Iran's development of chemical weapons. The report, from public broadcaster Kan, cited sources familiar with the matter and claimed that materials related to Iran's alleged chemical weapons program were found among Iranian nuclear archives seized by Israel in 2018.

Gulf countries, which would likely be heavily affected by a renewed conflict with Iran, have reportedly urged Washington to maintain its economic pressure on Tehran rather than return to military action. According to the US news website Axios, some senior US officials are also highly sceptical of Israel's claims, with one telling the outlet they appeared to be "driven by an agenda".

**[UNCONFIRMED]** Iran denies the allegations. Iranian foreign minister Abbas Araghchi accused Israel of falsely claiming Tehran was developing chemical weapons to drag the US into another war.

### **LIEBERMAN REJECTS IMMEDIATE STRIKE — SIGNALS COALITION UNITY** [VERIFIED]

After Defense Minister Katz said Iran is developing chemical weapons, Lieberman rejected a unilateral Israeli attack and said the defense minister and prime minister are driven by 'lust for power.'

Avigdor Liberman, who sealed a deal to enter a coalition with Eisenkot, said caretaker Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and Defence Minister Israel Katz had a "lust for power" that had driven them "out of their minds". "The statements about chemical weapons in Iran's hands are serious, but from an intelligence and operational standpoint, there is no justification for an independent Israeli attack in the immediate term," Liberman wrote on X.

Netanyahu's position is based on alleged developments in Iran's chemical weapons program, which Mossad chief Roman Gofman reportedly presented to American officials during a visit to Washington approximately two weeks ago. Experts in Israel estimate that it could take Iran two years or more to develop a system capable of delivering chemical weapons to Israel.

**[ANALYSIS]** Lieberman's public pushback against Netanyahu/Katz is strategically important. As a new coalition partner of Eisenkot, his rejection of an "immediate unilateral attack" signals the emerging government would take a more cautious approach. This reduces Netanyahu's domestic cover for a preemptive strike.

### **TWO MORE TANKERS HIT — ATTACKS NOW "ALMOST DAILY"** [VERIFIED]

Two oil tankers exploded early on Wednesday after hitting naval mines while attempting to transit the Strait of Hormuz on routes unauthorized by Iran, the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) Navy said. Two tankers hit naval mines off the southern and eastern coasts of the Strait of Hormuz early on Wednesday, resulting in fires erupting in their engine rooms, the IRGC said.

Iran has recently intensified its attacks on shipping in the Strait of Hormuz. The military reports explosions almost daily.

Attacks on ships in the Strait of Hormuz hit an all-time high in October, with at least 28 confirmed strikes — twice as many attacks as the previous month. The International Maritime Organization (IMO) Secretary-General Arsenio Dominguez warned that despite Trump's claims, the Strait of Hormuz remained unsafe for ships to transit due to attacks from mines, drones, and missiles.

The Iranian Navy said in a statement the waterway "will never be reopened until Iran's objectives and demands are met, and oil tanker owners are required to coordinate with the armed forces to ensure safe passage through this strategic strait."

### **OIL REBOUNDS ON RAS TANURA ATTACK** [VERIFIED]

Brent rose to 94.09 USD/Bbl on November 25, 2026, up 2.94% from the previous day.

Global oil prices remained under pressure on Wednesday, November 25, 2026, as expectations of an emerging supply surplus heading into 2027 continued to outweigh concerns over geopolitical tensions. Brent crude traded at approximately $91.34 per barrel.

Oil rose in thin trading as escalating attacks in the Middle East clouded the recovery in crude shipments from the region in recent months. Brent settled under $95 a barrel, the highest in over a week, as Saudi Arabian forces said early on Wednesday that they intercepted four drones fired at Ras Tanura.

**[ANALYSIS]** The Ras Tanura attack briefly reversed the three-month oil decline — Brent bounced from ~$88 to ~$91-94. However, the modest move despite a strike on major Saudi energy infrastructure suggests markets still expect limited escalation and adequate supply.

### **DECEMBER 11 FUNDING SHOWDOWN — DEMOCRATS DIVIDED** [VERIFIED]

Congress faces a December 11 deadline to pass a funding bill, with the Iran war emerging as a significant obstacle to reaching an agreement. The overwhelming majority of Democratic voters want to end the war with Iran now and more than half would support a government shutdown over the issue. The survey found that 80 per cent of Democrats want the war to end "now, regardless of terms" and 51 per cent favour including an Iran war-funding cutoff in the upcoming stopgap funding bill.

Party leaders are also backing away from a fight over funding for the war in Iran ahead of the December 11 government funding deadline. Democrats defending the approach argue that the biggest fights should wait until January, when the party takes control of both chambers and has the votes to act.

Maryland Senator Chris Van Hollen, joined by Senators Bernie Sanders, Michael Bennet and Tammy Duckworth, wants any stopgap passed by December 11 to bar money for the war. Van Hollen has said he would not vote for a funding bill that keeps paying for the war. Schumer, whom Van Hollen is challenging for majority leader, has not committed to that.

If the war continues beyond January, securing additional military funding could prove increasingly difficult for the administration, with Democrats set to take control of both the House and Senate. Congressional Democrats are already pushing to restrict funding for offensive military operations against Iran as part of government spending negotiations ahead of the Dec. 11 funding deadline.

**[ANALYSIS]** Democratic leadership appears to be choosing the January 3 takeover rather than December 11 as the decisive moment. This aligns with Trump's stated January funding horizon, suggesting both sides may effectively "run out the clock" on the current Congress without a dramatic confrontation.

### **YEMEN DISPLACEMENT CONTINUES** [VERIFIED]

The humanitarian situation in Yemen is "getting worse by the day," with more than 350,000 people forced from their homes since August, the UN said Thursday.

The UN Human Rights Office said it has verified more than 300 civilian casualties since the beginning of August, when hostilities surged, including more than 70 killed. Nearly half of the fatalities were children.

The Saudi Arabia-Houthi conflict is worsening, with the Iran-backed group targeting the kingdom's main financial district and energy ports, as well as making gains in Yemen. After capturing much of Yemen's western coastline, the group is pushing toward Aden, a key port city.

## Assessment

**[ANALYSIS]** Day 271 crystallizes the emerging endgame dynamic.

**The January Convergence:**
Trump's explicit statement that funding extends "through January" and Democratic leadership's decision to defer confrontation until their January takeover means both sides are effectively accepting a two-month continuation of current policy. The December 11 deadline appears likely to pass with a clean continuing resolution rather than a war-funding fight.

**The Chemical Weapons Card:**
Israel's public allegations of Iranian chemical weapons development looks like a last-ditch attempt to force escalation before the political window closes. Key indicators:
- Mossad chief Gofman presented intelligence to Washington two weeks ago
- US officials reportedly skeptical, calling claims "driven by an agenda"
- Lieberman's immediate rejection reduces domestic Israeli support
- Israeli experts estimate 2+ years before delivery capability

This appears to be Netanyahu's attempt to create a pretext for action before Eisenkot forms a government. The fact that Lieberman — his new coalition partner — immediately dismissed the need for action suggests it won't work.

**The Houthi Escalation:**
Targeting Ras Tanura represents a qualitative escalation. Striking symbolic targets (PIF Tower) is different from attacking a 550,000 bpd refinery adjacent to major export terminals. Combined with threats to expand to Jeddah and Red Sea ports, the Houthis are testing whether Saudi Arabia will request more aggressive US support. This is the most likely path to expanded US military action before January.

**Oil Market Paradox Continues:**
The modest 2-3% bounce despite a strike on Saudi Arabia's largest refinery confirms markets believe:
1. Saudi air defenses can limit damage
2. Supply disruption will be manageable
3. Neither side wants catastrophic escalation
