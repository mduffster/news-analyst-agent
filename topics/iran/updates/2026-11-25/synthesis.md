# US-Israel-Iran Conflict: Houthis Strike Saudi's Largest Refinery as Trump Confirms January Funding Horizon; Israel's Chemical Weapons Allegations Meet Skepticism

## November 25, 2026 — Day 271

**Assessment:** The conflict's endgame dynamics are crystallizing around a January 2027 inflection point. Trump's explicit confirmation that war funding extends "through January" aligns with Democratic leaders' decision to defer confrontation until they control Congress, effectively creating a two-month continuation of current policy. Trump said he hoped Democrats would not stop the war "ahead of schedule" and claimed Iran's navy, air force and air defenses had been destroyed, saying a Democratic move to halt the war would turn what he called a "complete victory" into a defeat. Meanwhile, the Houthi strike on Ras Tanura refinery represents a qualitative escalation toward energy infrastructure targeting, while Israel's public chemical weapons allegations appear designed to create a pretext for action before the political window closes—yet they are being rejected by Eisenkot's incoming coalition and met with skepticism by US officials.

## 1. Houthis Strike Ras Tanura — Saudi Arabia's Largest Refinery

Yemen's Iran-aligned Houthis said on Wednesday they had attacked Saudi Arabia's Ras Tanura oil refinery, the kingdom's largest, which is near a major export terminal for Saudi crude. [VERIFIED — Reuters, multiple sources]

Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree said the refinery attack caused fires. The Saudi-led coalition said earlier on Wednesday that air defenses had intercepted and destroyed four drones in Ras Tanura in Saudi Arabia's Eastern Province. It did not say the refinery there had been struck.

NASA satellite imagery reviewed by Reuters showed large swathes of thick black smoke around the refinery, covering an area spanning around 600 square kilometers. A source with knowledge of the matter confirmed an attack on the refinery.

Ras Tanura, which has a refining capacity of 550,000 barrels per day, has previously been targeted during the conflict with the Houthis as well as during the broader Iran war. Ras Tanura is also the kingdom's oldest refinery, accounting for about 16 per cent of its refining capacity.

**Houthi threats escalate:** Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree said on X that Jeddah port and all Saudi ports on the Red Sea coast would become "targets" if the coalition carries out its threat to strike Hodeidah port, calling on workers and shipping companies to evacuate immediately.

**Coalition strikes Hodeidah:** Yemen's Hodeidah port has come under attack from the Saudi-led coalition shortly after the coalition told civilians to evacuate the Houthi-run facility. This follows a drone attack Tuesday on a tower in Riyadh's King Abdullah Financial District, where Saudi Civil Defense said 11 people were injured.

**Significance:** This represents a qualitative escalation. Targeting symbolic buildings (PIF Tower) differs fundamentally from striking a 550,000 bpd refinery adjacent to major export terminals. The combined attack on Ras Tanura and continued strikes on Riyadh's financial district demonstrates Houthi ability to sustain multi-front pressure. An earlier coalition evacuation warning and strike on Hodeidah also took place on November 20, 2026, prompting similar Houthi threats against Jeddah and other Saudi ports.

## 2. Trump Confirms War Funded Through January — Creates Two-Month Window

President Donald Trump said the ongoing war with Iran is "funded" through January, when Democrats take control of Congress and could reign in his authority to authorize the conflict. [OFFICIAL — New York Post interview]

In an interview with The New York Post, Trump predicted that the Democrats will "take a total win and make it a loss" in Iran. "But up until really the end of January, pretty much, you know, we're funded," he added, in response to a question about the window for a potential new offensive.

Trump also admitted that Washington's goals in Iran diverge from Israel's, saying that while regime change is "probably doable," he's only concerned with Tehran's nuclear program.

The war is unpopular, Congress never approved it, and it contributed to Republican losses in the midterm elections.

**Congressional dynamics:** Senate Republicans are opening the door to a potential bipartisan deal on Democrats' demand to restrict funding for the Iran war in exchange for a significant defense spending boost. Democrats have been working on proposed language "that puts significant pressure" on the administration to end the war without compromising the ability to defend against attacks from Iran or facilitate commercial shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, said Rhode Island Sen. Jack Reed.

Senate Appropriations Chairwoman Susan Collins (R-Maine) has said she is exploring a deal to restock depleted Pentagon munitions while denying funds for continuing the Iran conflict.

**Significance:** Trump's explicit acknowledgment of the January funding horizon confirms his reluctance to escalate before Democrats take control. His statement that regime change is "probably doable" but not his goal creates daylight between US and Israeli positions—significant given Netanyahu's push for more aggressive action.

## 3. Israel's Chemical Weapons Allegations — Coalition Partner Rejects Strike

Defense Minister Israel Katz on Tuesday accused Iran of developing chemical weapons, warning that Israel is determined to prevent Tehran from obtaining such capabilities. [OFFICIAL — Israeli Defense Ministry]

Speaking to Israeli reporters at his office in Tel Aviv, Katz said Iran began developing chemical weapons after the joint Israeli-American military operation was launched Feb. 28. "The Iranians emerged from the tunnels after Operation Lion's Roar [that ended in an April ceasefire], understood the damage they had suffered and realized they needed a 'balancing' weapon," Katz said.

**Lieberman's rejection:** Avigdor Liberman, who sealed a deal to enter a coalition with Eisenkot, said caretaker Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and Defence Minister Israel Katz had a "lust for power" that had driven them "out of their minds". "The statements about chemical weapons in Iran's hands are serious, but from an intelligence and operational standpoint, there is no justification for an independent Israeli attack in the immediate term," Liberman wrote on X. [VERIFIED — multiple sources]

**US skepticism:** Gulf countries found the intelligence briefings "unserious." Netanyahu sent Mossad chairman Roman Gofman to Washington last week to present Trump officials with intelligence suggesting that Iran is advancing a chemical weapons program.

**Iran's response:** Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said Wednesday that Israel was trying to drag the United States into another war after an Israeli minister alleged Tehran is building chemical weapons.

**Significance:** Lieberman's immediate and forceful rejection of the need for an "immediate unilateral attack" is strategically important. As a new coalition partner of Eisenkot, his position signals the emerging government would take a more cautious approach to Iran. Combined with US skepticism of the chemical weapons claims, this significantly reduces Netanyahu's domestic and international cover for a preemptive strike before Eisenkot forms a government.

## 4. Hormuz Tanker Attacks Continue — Iran Claims Naval Mine Incidents

Two oil tankers struck naval mines, exploded and caught fire in the Strait of Hormuz on Wednesday, Iran's Revolutionary Guards Navy said in a statement carried by state media. [OFFICIAL — IRGC statement]

The statement said the vessels had been attempting to transit an unauthorised route through the waterway.

A ship carrying liquefied natural gas (LNG) hit a naval mine and caught fire in the Strait of Hormuz, Iran's Revolutionary Guards claimed on Wednesday evening. This brings the total to at least three vessels hit on November 25.

Iranian Army spokesperson Mohammad Akraminia said the military had deployed new systems and upgraded existing ones to strengthen surveillance and control of the strait. He warned that vessels passing through without coordinating with Iranian forces had faced greater risks and struck mines in recent weeks.

Tehran has told Washington it will not restore normal shipping through the Strait of Hormuz until its demands are met, including the lifting of a U.S. naval blockade of Iranian ports and an end to the war on all fronts. Under a proposal conveyed through Qatari mediators, Tehran offered to reopen the waterway within 48 hours of Washington meeting its conditions.

**Attribution uncertainty:** Iran's explicit "mine" framing represents a narrative shift from earlier "tanker incident/attack" descriptions. If the operational reality involves projectiles (drones/rockets) rather than mines, that points toward rapid-hit harassment; if mines, it suggests more deliberate area-denial. The information contestation increases miscalculation risk.

## 5. Eisenkot Coalition Progress — December 6 Deadline Approaches

Israel's Yashar party, led by former military chief Gadi Eisenkot, said on Tuesday it agreed to sign a coalition pact with a right-wing opposition party as it seeks to form a government following last month's election. [VERIFIED — Reuters]

Eisenkot and Liberman met late Sunday and "agreed to sign a coalition agreement," Eisenkot's Yashar party said in a statement. Together, the two parties hold 28 of the opposition bloc's 48 seats in parliament following the Oct. 27 election.

Eisenkot's centre-right Yashar party and patchwork coalition partners, including Lieberman, hold 48 seats. But they have potential allies in the parties representing Israel's Palestinian minority, which hold 19 seats and are aligned with Eisenkot in their vehement opposition to Netanyahu.

Lieberman could become defense minister if Eisenkot forms a government. A source in the Eisenkot bloc told i24NEWS that it aims to swear in a new government by the end of next week.

Eisenkot has until December 6 to form a government under a 28-day mandate, though he could ask for a 14-day extension.

At the same time, Eisenkot's party is still talking to Likud about a unity government. The two sides are due to meet again on Wednesday at 3 pm.

**Significance:** Lieberman's coalition commitment combined with his rejection of immediate military action signals coalition discipline on a more cautious Iran approach. If Eisenkot succeeds in forming a government—possibly with Arab party backing—Israel's posture toward the conflict would shift significantly.

## 6. Oil Markets Respond to Saudi Strike

Brent rose to 94.09 USD/Bbl on November 25, 2026, up 2.94% from the previous day. [VERIFIED — Trading Economics]

At 6:45 a.m. Eastern Time today, the price of oil sits at $91.46 per barrel. That's an increase of $1.04 since yesterday morning and roughly $29 more than at this time last year.

However, by later trading, Brent crude fell to around $90 per barrel on Wednesday, giving back gains from the previous session even as escalating attacks on vessels around the Strait of Hormuz kept supply risks elevated.

**Market interpretation:** The modest 2-3% bounce despite a strike on Saudi Arabia's largest refinery confirms markets believe Saudi air defenses can limit damage, supply disruption will be manageable, and neither side wants catastrophic escalation. The continued price volatility within the $87-94 range suggests the conflict has reached a painful but sustainable equilibrium.
