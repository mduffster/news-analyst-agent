## Key Developments (Past 24 Hours)

### **ISRAEL ELECTION: DEADLOCK — NEITHER BLOC REACHES MAJORITY** [VERIFIED]

Israel's partial election results show a tight race between Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and his main rival, Gadi Eisenkot, with neither likely to secure a clear path to a governing majority. With more than 84 percent of the votes counted, Netanyahu's bloc was on track to win 54 seats in the 120-seat Knesset. Eisenkot's alliance was set to win 49, and the Arab parties were on track for 17 seats.

Netanyahu's bloc of ultra-Orthodox and far-right allies has lost its majority, and is projected to collapse from 68 seats in the Knesset to 52, according to the exit poll from Channel 12 News. His Likud party will still be the largest single party, but the results represent a crash in public support.

The latest results by the CEC put 19 seats in the Arab parties, with the Joint List having 12 seats and Ra'am having seven seats in the next Knesset.

The opposition bloc and Arab parties have a clear majority, but some of Eisenkot's allies oppose working with Arab parties. Several scenarios are possible, while Israel's president called on parties to keep an "open mind" to avoid more elections.

If Eisenkot's bloc teams up with Arab parties, they could secure the 61 seats needed for a majority in parliament and depose Netanyahu. According to The Times of Israel, the latest partial results would give a partnership between Eisenkot's opposition alliance and Arab-led parties 66 seats.

However, both Netanyahu and Eisenkot have rejected the idea of partnering with them, citing a lack of shared values.

**[ANALYSIS]** The election has delivered the worst possible outcome for near-term clarity: neither bloc can form a government without either a unity coalition or breaking campaign pledges on Arab parties. Netanyahu remains caretaker PM during coalition talks, meaning **no change in Israeli Iran policy** is imminent. This could last weeks.

### **QATAR'S NEW DEAL: IRAN REJECTS NUCLEAR TERMS** [VERIFIED]

Qatari mediators presented Iran with a new draft deal on Sunday to end the war with the US and Israel, and Tehran is expected to send a response ‌to the draft by the ‌end of the week. The new draft being considered by Iran sets a seven-day timeline addressing both Tehran's and Washington's concerns, laying out daily confidence-building steps that each side must take.

However, a senior Iranian official said Doha had only put forward unacceptable ideas that did not amount to an offer. A senior Iranian official involved in the talks said Qatar had presented only "unacceptable ideas and not an offer", and that Tehran's policy on showing no flexibility over the nuclear issue remained unchanged.

"The only thing we received was a list of unacceptable ideas. We will not make concessions on the nuclear issue," the Iranian official noted.

**[ANALYSIS]** The Qatari initiative sought to break the "who moves first" deadlock with a synchronized daily schedule. Iran's instant rejection of nuclear concessions demonstrates the fundamental impasse: Tehran views nuclear capability as an existential hedge; Washington views it as the core threat. This gap remains unbridgeable.

### **IRAN THREATENS "PAINFUL RETALIATION" IF US RESUMES STRIKES** [OFFICIAL]

Iran would deliver a "painful" response to any renewed US attack, Ebrahim Azizi, chairman of parliament's National Security Committee, said. Azizi said Tehran would act according to its national interests and military doctrine regarding the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty (NPT) and use all available capabilities in any confrontation.

Araghchi says diplomacy still alive, Iran's plan could move faster. "While our Brave Armed Forces are fully prepared to defend the Homeland, Iran continues to give diplomacy a chance to succeed," Araghchi wrote on X. "We remain in close and ongoing contact with intermediaries to review ideas that can ensure implementation of our 7-Day Plan in even less than a week."

**[ANALYSIS]** Iran is signaling both deterrence and continued diplomatic interest. The reference to NPT doctrine hints at potential nuclear escalation if attacked. This dual messaging suggests Tehran seeks to avoid renewed strikes while maintaining leverage.

The reported preparations follow an earlier US decision to deploy a third aircraft carrier and additional Marines to the Middle East. A US official said on October 1 that the USS Theodore Roosevelt and additional Marine personnel were being sent to the region, expanding Washington's military options.

A US official said three carriers could be in the region as early as the end of October. Another assessment said three carriers and two amphibious landing groups could be around Iran by the end of November.

**[ANALYSIS]** The pattern is clear: diplomatic probes continue through midterms (Nov 3) while strike preparations proceed in parallel. The "short, intense" framing suggests plans for a decisive strike campaign rather than sustained attrition.
