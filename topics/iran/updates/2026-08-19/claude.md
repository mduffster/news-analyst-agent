## Key Developments Since Prior Report (August 18)

### 1. UAE Imposes Indefinite Trade Embargo on Iran — MAJOR DEVELOPMENT

**[OFFICIAL]** The United Arab Emirates has imposed an indefinite trade embargo on Iran after accusing Iranian forces of firing two ballistic missiles at the country. In a statement early on Wednesday, the UAE's Ministry of Foreign Affairs said the decision was made in "light of escalations that undermine peace and security in the region."

**[OFFICIAL]** "All trade, commercial exchanges and financial transactions with Iran have been halted until further notice."

**[VERIFIED]** Before the war, the UAE was one of Iran's biggest trade partners, providing more than 30% of its imports valued at some $21 billion, according to the World Trade Organization's latest figures from 2024. It was the destination for nearly 13% of its exports worth some $7 billion.

**[OFFICIAL]** The UAE's embargo has been described as highly significant by analysts, who say Iran is heavily reliant on its neighbour for critical imports and access to financial markets. "In many ways, the embargo being put on by the UAE is even more significant than the embargo being put on by the United States," Mark Kimmitt, a retired US general and former assistant secretary of state, told Al Jazeera.

**[OFFICIAL]** Iran denied launching missiles and described the reported attack as a "false flag operation" by Israel and the United States.

*Assessment: This is a MAJOR development. The UAE embargo cuts off Iran's primary sanctions-evasion route and access to global financial markets through Dubai. Combined with the US blockade, this significantly increases economic pressure on Tehran.*

---

### 2. Iran Warns Gulf States: Assisting US Equals "Participation in Operations"

**[OFFICIAL]** Iran's armed forces on Wednesday warned Gulf countries against assisting the US military, saying it would be tantamount to joining American military operations. "We wish to warn that any assistance or facilitation provided to the aggressor US military amounts to participation in the US military operation," armed forces chief of staff Ali Abdollahi said, according to Iran's Mehr news agency.

**[OFFICIAL]** "It seems unlikely that such a large number of military aircraft, particularly refuelling aircraft could be present at regional bases without knowledge of host countries," said Abdollahi.

*Assessment: This warning directly threatens Gulf states hosting US forces. Combined with the UAE embargo announcement, this represents an escalation of Iran's pressure campaign on regional neighbors. The warning is particularly pointed given US dependence on bases in Qatar, Bahrain, and Kuwait.*

---

### 3. Iran Considering Strikes on US Military Targets in Europe — FT Report

**[REPORTED]** Iran is considering attacking US military assets in Europe if US President Donald Trump decides to escalate the conflict, regime insiders told the Financial Times on Tuesday.

**[REPORTED]** According to the two sources, Iranian forces have weighed striking US military targets in south-eastern Europe such as in Bulgaria, which recently authorized US aircraft to refuel at its Bezmer airbase. Cyprus, where a British air base was hit by a drone in March, was also named as a potential target.

**[REPORTED]** Sub-sea fiber optic cables in the Strait of Hormuz have also been considered as a target in the event of any renewed US offensive.

**[OFFICIAL]** NATO is prepared to address any threat and would take necessary action to defend all its members amid reports that Iran was considering attacks on US targets in Europe, Reuters reported on Wednesday, citing an alliance official.

**[OFFICIAL]** "As demonstrated earlier this year when NATO air defenses successfully intercepted ballistic missiles heading to Turkey from Iran on four separate occasions, our deterrence and defence posture is strong and effective," the official says.

*Assessment: While analysts have previously assessed an Iranian strike on NATO territory as unlikely due to Article 5 implications, the FT report indicates Iran is actively considering escalation options. The undersea cable threat is notable as a lower-threshold escalation that could cause significant economic disruption.*

---

### 4. Ghalibaf in Baghdad — Pushing "New Regional Order"

**[OFFICIAL]** Iranian Parliament Speaker and top negotiator Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, who is currently in Baghdad, says his visit to Iraq is intended to strengthen what he called a "new regional order" without foreign interference. Speaking at the site where former General Qassem Soleimani was killed, Ghalibaf said: "Today's visit to Iraq is aimed at strengthening the new order in the region and accelerating mutual cooperation among all countries of the region, without the intervention of foreign powers."

**[OFFICIAL]** The speaker stressed that the Islamic countries of the region should pursue deep security interactions and ties, supported and reinforced by sustainable economic cooperation, adding that Iran is fully prepared to help realize such a regional model.

**[REPORTED]** Powerful Iran-backed armed groups in Iraq are refusing to disarm ahead of a government-imposed September 30 deadline, deepening friction with Baghdad.

*Assessment: Ghalibaf's Iraq visit is part of Iran's regional strategy to build an alternative order excluding US influence. The choice of the Soleimani assassination site as his first stop sends a defiant message. This aligns with Iran's broader effort to pressure Gulf states while strengthening ties with Iraq.*

---

### 5. Former President Khatami Questions War Strategy — Internal Dissent Signal

**[REPORTED]** Former Iranian President Mohammad Khatami has warned that some in Iran believe the nation's "life and survival lie in war," openly questioning the logic behind that stance, according to Iran's Fars news agency. "Some say: 'First, we will teach America a lesson here, then we will go to the heart of America and fight it there.' But what reality is this based on?" Khatami asked.

**[REPORTED]** The former president stressed that Iran could not indefinitely function under wartime conditions. "We cannot live with war. The people cannot, and it is not possible to continue daily life, meet people's needs and govern society under such conditions," he said.

*Assessment: Khatami's public criticism is notable as a signal of internal debate within Iran's establishment about war strategy. While Khatami holds no power, his willingness to publicly question the conflict suggests growing concern among some factions about the war's sustainability.*

### 6. Oil Approaches $92 — Fourth Consecutive Session of Gains

**[VERIFIED]** Brent rose to 91.52 USD/Bbl on August 19, 2026, up 0.55% from the previous day. Over the past month, Brent's price has risen 2.57%, and is up 36.92% compared to the same time last year.

**[VERIFIED]** Brent crude climbed toward $92 per barrel on Wednesday, extending gains for a fourth consecutive session as the US and Iran showed little indication of reaching an agreement to end the conflict and reopen the Strait of Hormuz, keeping supply concerns elevated. President Donald Trump said there were no ongoing negotiations with Tehran while confirming that the US naval blockade remains in effect.

**[VERIFIED]** Iranian forces have intensified hostilities over the past week, while eight attacks on vessels transiting Hormuz, including ships linked to the UAE and Saudi Arabia, have been reported so far this month.

*Assessment: Oil has moved from ~$91 to ~$91.50-$92, continuing the upward trajectory. The diplomatic collapse and UAE embargo add further upward pressure.*

### 7. Hormuz Traffic Continues to Decline — 6 Transits on Tuesday

**[VERIFIED]** Shipping through the Strait of Hormuz slowed, data showed on Wednesday, as most shipowners avoided the key waterway because of a lack of clear signalling on its reopening from a blockade during the Iran war. Six commodity vessels crossed the strait on Tuesday, Kpler data showed, down from nine a day earlier and below the 10-day daily average of 11.

**[VERIFIED]** At least 17 mariners have been killed in 65 attacks on commercial vessels since the U.S.-Iran war began on Feb. 28, according to the International Maritime Organization, a United Nations agency.

**[VERIFIED]** Is the Strait of Hormuz open? Effectively no, it is closed to commercial shipping.

*Assessment: Traffic has declined further from the already-low levels reported in the prior report. The trajectory is worsening, not improving.*

---

### 8. Iran-UAE Missile Incident Details

**[OFFICIAL]** The United Arab Emirates said that two ballistic missiles were fired toward the country from Iran, the Islamic Republic's first known attack on the Gulf nation since May as the wider Middle East war continues unabated. Both missiles fell into the sea, with one of them dropping within the UAE's territorial waters and the other outside, the country's Defense Ministry said Tuesday.

**[OFFICIAL]** Reuters confirmed via the defense ministry that two ballistic missiles were launched from Iran, and that one fell inside and another outside of territorial waters. Both missiles were reportedly targeting maritime traffic.

**[OFFICIAL]** Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesperson Esmail Baghaei denied accusations that Iran had launched a missile strike on the UAE in a statement early Wednesday morning.

*Assessment: The missile incident provided the justification for the UAE trade embargo. Iran's denial as a "false flag" is consistent with its past denials of attacks on Turkey and other regional states. Whether true or not, the diplomatic consequence is severe.*
