## Executive Summary

**KEY DEVELOPMENT: Trump convening Situation Room meeting for "final determination" on Iran deal; but Iran publicly disputes core terms**

**The conflict has reached its most pivotal moment: President Trump is meeting with advisers in the White House Situation Room today to make a "final determination" on the draft 60-day MOU with Iran. However, significant complications have emerged: Iran's Fars news agency, citing informed sources, pushed back on Trump's characterization of the deal as containing "issues that contradict the provisions of the agreement's text," while Iran's parliament explicitly stated Tehran will not transfer enriched uranium abroad.**

**The bottom line:** The gap between what Trump says is in the deal and what Iran says is in the deal has widened publicly just as both sides approach the signing stage. Iranian state news outlet Fars pushed back on Trump's post, saying it "raised issues that contradict the provisions of the agreement's text." Despite Trump's reference to a toll-free strait, there is "no such clause in the text of the agreement," Fars reported. The draft deal in discussion also contains no reference to Iran dismantling or destroying its nuclear materials. Meanwhile, Israeli troops pushed deeper into Lebanon even as Pentagon military talks began—creating structural tension with any deal that includes a Lebanon cessation clause.

## Critical Developments Since May 28

### 1. Trump's Decision Meeting — Contradictory Signals from Tehran

**[OFFICIAL]** President Donald Trump on Friday morning said he is meeting in the Situation Room "to make a final determination" after listing everything that Iran must do for him to approve a deal to end the war. "I will be meeting now, in the Situation Room, to make a final determination," he said.

**[OFFICIAL]** Iran "must agree" to never have a nuclear weapon, and the Strait of Hormuz must be "immediately open" to unrestricted shipping traffic, with no tolls being imposed, Trump demanded in the post. He also said that the retaliatory U.S. naval blockade against Iran in the Gulf of Oman "will now be lifted."

**[OFFICIAL]** Trump stated that enriched material buried at the site of last year's attacks on Iran's nuclear facilities will be "unearthed" by the U.S. "in close coordination and conjunction with the Islamic Republic of Iran, plus the International Atomic Energy Agency, and DESTROYED." "No money will be exchanged, until further notice."

**[REPORTED]** Iran's Fars news agency cites informed sources as saying the latest comments by US President Donald Trump about a potential deal to end the Middle East war were a "mixture of truth and lies." "Trump claimed that Iran was obligated to open the Strait of Hormuz without tolls, even though no such clause appears in the text of the agreement."

**[REPORTED]** Fars also asserted that "the most important part of the agreement" is "the immediate payment of $12 billion of Iran's frozen assets." Iran will refuse any further negotiations unless that payment is made.

**Assessment:** Both sides are publicly describing fundamentally different deals. Trump emphasizes uranium destruction and toll-free Hormuz; Iran emphasizes $12 billion in frozen assets and continued strait management. This public disagreement creates significant risk that even if both leaders approve the MOU, the implementation phase could collapse immediately over interpretation disputes.

### 2. Iran Parliament: No Uranium Transfer

**[REPORTED]** Iran's parliament said it has no plan to transfer the country's highly enriched uranium outside its borders. Ebrahim Azizi, chairman of the Iranian parliament's National Security and Foreign Policy Committee, told Russia's state-run RIA Novosti on May 29 that Iran has no intention of moving its highly enriched uranium "to a third country, a mediator or any other place."

**[REPORTED]** Azizi posted remarks on X framing three issues as untouchable principles: uranium enrichment, the lifting of sanctions, and control over the Strait of Hormuz.

**[VERIFIED]** Iranian Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei has issued a directive that Iran's enriched uranium should not be sent abroad, Reuters reported, quoting two unnamed senior Iranian sources. The future of the estimated 440kg of uranium enriched to 60 percent that Iran is believed to be holding remains a chief sticking point in peace negotiations.

**Assessment:** This is a significant development. While Azizi is a parliamentary figure (not the executive branch), his statement combined with earlier reporting on Supreme Leader Khamenei's directive signals internal Iranian consensus against uranium transfer. The MOU reportedly defers the nuclear question to subsequent 60-day talks, but this posturing suggests those talks would face immediate impasse.

### 3. Pentagon Lebanon-Israel Military Talks Begin — Amid Ongoing Combat

**[VERIFIED]** The entrance of Israel's troops into the village of Dibbine, near the town of Marjayoun, came as Israeli airstrikes killed at least six people. Five were killed in an airstrike on the villages of Deir Qanoun al Nahr and Abbasiyeh, while a municipal policeman was killed in the village of Ebba.

**[VERIFIED]** In Washington, a six-member Lebanese military delegation was meeting on Friday with Israeli military officials in the first direct military talks between the two countries in decades.

**[VERIFIED]** Israeli and Lebanese military officials are set to hold direct US-mediated security talks at the Pentagon on Friday focused on border security, Hezbollah's disarmament, and a timeline for an Israeli withdrawal from southern Lebanon. Military issues, including border arrangements, security coordination, and the mechanics of implementing any future steps will be the focus of the session.

**[VERIFIED]** The latest Israel-Hezbollah war has left 3,200 people dead in Lebanon and over 1 million people displaced.

**[REPORTED]** "Lebanon is prioritizing, in its direct talks with the Israeli side, the establishment of a ceasefire before turning to any broader political or security issues."

**[VERIFIED]** The Israeli military issued several evacuation warnings for southern Lebanon on Friday, forcing hundreds of families to flee to safer areas further north.

**Assessment:** The contrast is stark: military delegations meeting in Washington while Israel continues offensive ground operations. The issue of disarming Hezbollah has obstructed progress in previous rounds, which is why security negotiations are now taking place at the Pentagon separate from political negotiations at the State Department. This dual-track approach—negotiations amid active combat—creates continued tension with any US-Iran deal that includes a Lebanon cessation clause.

## Structural Analysis: The Interpretation Gap

**The most significant development today is not whether Trump signs, but what happens after.**

Three fundamentally different deals are being described:

1. **Trump's version:** Toll-free Hormuz, uranium destroyed, no money exchanged until further notice
2. **Fars/Iranian version:** Iran manages Hormuz, no uranium transfer clause, $12B frozen assets released immediately
3. **Likely actual MOU:** 60-day framework to negotiate these issues

Oil prices fell following Trump's post. The exact terms of the deal currently being discussed are unclear.

This interpretation gap creates three scenarios:

- **Scenario A (40%):** MOU signed; both sides claim victory on different elements; 60-day talks begin with fundamental impasse
- **Scenario B (30%):** Trump demands clarification before signing; decision delayed
- **Scenario C (20%):** MOU signed but collapses within days over interpretation disputes
- **Scenario D (10%):** Trump rejects deal outright; military pressure resumes
