### Executive Summary

**KEY DEVELOPMENT: Trump convenes Situation Room meeting for "final determination" on Iran deal**

The situation is reaching a decision point today. President Donald Trump is meeting with advisers in the White House Situation Room to determine whether to agree to a tentative deal with Iran. "I will be meeting now, in the Situation Room, to make a final determination," Trump wrote, spelling out the parameters of the agreement. Trump said Iran "must agree that they will never have a Nuclear Weapon or Bomb" and that the "Hormuz Strait must be immediately open" without any tolls or restrictions on traffic.

**However, a significant wrinkle has emerged from Tehran:**

**[REPORTED] Iran's parliament signals opposition to uranium transfer** — The head of Iran's parliament National Security Committee said Tehran does not intend to transfer its enriched uranium stockpile to a third country, rejecting one of the issues raised in discussions around a possible agreement with Washington. Ebrahim Azizi told Russia's Sputnik that the United States had not been trustworthy in negotiations and that no agreement would be reached if Washington did not change its conduct.

**Other key developments:**
- **Pentagon talks underway**: Israeli troops entered a southern Lebanese village early Friday, pushing deeper into the country as Lebanese and Israeli military officials began direct talks at the Pentagon over the deadly conflict.
- **Oil plunges on deal optimism**: At 9 a.m. Eastern Time today, oil was priced at $94.44 per barrel with Brent serving as the benchmark. That's a drop of $3.07 compared with yesterday morning.
- **IRGC claims Hormuz transit continues under its protocols**: Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps said on May 28 that 26 ships crossed the Strait of Hormuz over the past 24 hours after obtaining permission and coordinating with its Navy. ### Critical Developments (May 28-29)

#### 1. Trump's Decision Meeting

**[OFFICIAL]** President Trump said on Friday that he's convening his national security team in the White House Situation Room to make a final decision about the agreement reached between U.S. and Iranian negotiators. The signing of the memorandum of understanding (MOU) would be the most significant diplomatic breakthrough since the war started, but a final agreement tackling Trump's nuclear demands would require further negotiations. The text of the MOU — which involves a 60-day ceasefire extension and the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz — was largely agreed as of Tuesday, but Trump told the mediators he needed more time to make a decision.

**[OFFICIAL]** It appears unlikely Trump will sign off before receiving definitive word that Iran's Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei has given his approval to the document, one official said.

**[VERIFIED]** Iran's chief negotiator Ghalibaf said "no step will be taken before the other side acts," signaling that Iran is waiting for the US to move.

**Assessment:** Both sides are waiting for the other to move first—a classic standoff. Trump wants Khamenei's sign-off before he commits; Iran says it won't act until the US does. This increases the risk of talks stalling even as they approach the finish line.

---

#### 2. Iran Parliament Contradicts Draft Deal on Uranium

**[REPORTED]** Iran has no plans to take highly enriched uranium outside the country, Ebrahim Azizi, head of the Iranian parliament's national security and foreign policy committee, told RIA Novosti on Friday. "We have no plans to take highly enriched uranium outside the country. We have no intention of transferring our enriched uranium to third countries, intermediaries, or anywhere else," he said.

**[REPORTED]** Azizi also said the Islamic Republic was pursuing permanent management of the Strait of Hormuz, describing it as a long-term policy rather than a temporary measure.

**[VERIFIED]** Trump's position on uranium: On the thorny issue of Iran's stockpile of highly enriched uranium, Trump said it would be "unearthed by the United States" and destroyed in coordination with Iran. "No money will be exchanged, until further notice," he wrote.

**Assessment:** This is a significant development. While Azizi is a parliamentary figure (not the executive branch), his public statement contradicts the apparent draft deal framework. But the question of what happens to the uranium would remain unsettled and a key part of subsequent negotiations. Those talks would likely focus on the nearly 1000 pounds of uranium purified to 60%. This signals internal Iranian divisions or posturing before Trump's decision.

#### 3. Pentagon Lebanon-Israel Security Talks Begin

**[VERIFIED]** Israeli and Lebanese military officials are set to hold direct US-mediated security talks at the Pentagon on Friday focused on border security, Hezbollah's disarmament, and a timeline for an Israeli withdrawal from southern Lebanon. Military issues, including border arrangements, security coordination, and the mechanics of implementing any future steps will be the focus of the session. Separate political discussions are expected to continue next week at the State Department.

**[REPORTED]** Lebanon's position: A Lebanese official source said "Lebanon is prioritizing, in its direct talks with the Israeli side, the establishment of a ceasefire before turning to any broader political or security issues."

**[VERIFIED]** The increase in Israeli violence on Lebanon is widely believed to be an attempt to exert further pressure on Beirut ahead of Friday's talks at the Pentagon.

**Assessment:** The contrast is stark: military delegations are meeting in Washington while Israel continues offensive operations on the ground. The Israeli military is fighting Hezbollah "head on" across several parts of Lebanon, including north of the Litani River, Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said Friday. This dual-track approach creates significant tension.

#### 5. Strait of Hormuz: Warning Shots and Continued Restrictions

**[VERIFIED]** Meanwhile, Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps navy said it fired warning shots at four vessels near the Strait of Hormuz as they were reportedly trying to pass through the waterway "without prior coordination or authorization," according to a post on an affiliated Telegram account.

**[VERIFIED]** The Islamic regime fired at four ships attempting to cross the Strait of Hormuz on Wednesday night, state media site IRIB reported. The broadcaster alleged the ships attempted the journey to enter the Persian Gulf "without coordination with the security forces." "They were warned, but after they ignored the warning, warning shots were fired at them, forcing them to return."

**Assessment:** Iran continues enforcing its control over the Strait even as deal negotiations reach their conclusion. This maintains pressure and demonstrates that absent a signed agreement, Iran's chokehold remains operational.

Over the last seven days, 77 children have been killed or injured, UNICEF spokesperson Ricardo Pires told reporters in Geneva on Friday, citing figures from Lebanon's Ministry of Public Health. Most were killed or wounded by airstrikes in south Lebanon.

**[VERIFIED]** In total, at least 55 children have been killed and 121 wounded since April 17, when Washington ordered a ceasefire to come into effect between Israel and Lebanon.

### Emerging Signals

**Uranium becomes the key sticking point:** Even if Hormuz reopening and ceasefire extension are agreed, the nuclear issue is increasingly contentious. Iran's parliament says no transfer; Trump says the US will "unearth and destroy" it. The two U.S. officials claimed the Iranians had given verbal commitments during the negotiations about their willingness to make nuclear concessions, but "we will not know until we get in the room, which is why we want to do this MOU. It gets both sides into the room to negotiate directly."

**MOU as entry point, not resolution:** The emerging shape of any deal is increasingly clear: it's a framework to begin real negotiations, not a comprehensive solution. Both sides are trying to claim victory on the Hormuz reopening while deferring the nuclear issue.

**Lebanon as the wildcard:** The MOU would also state that the war between Israel and Hezbollah in Lebanon would end — an issue on which Trump and Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu have had at least one tense discussion. Netanyahu's ongoing operations directly contradict this provision.

**The conflict has reached a decision point: Trump is meeting now to make a "final determination," but Iran's parliament has just complicated the nuclear track.**
