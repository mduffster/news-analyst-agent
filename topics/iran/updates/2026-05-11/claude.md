## Daily Update: US-Israel-Iran Conflict — May 11, 2026 (Day 73)

### Critical New Developments (May 9-11)

#### Iran Submits Response; Trump Rejects It Entirely
**[OFFICIAL]** On May 10, Iranian state media reported that the country sent its response to the latest US proposal. Trump said on social media that Iran "has been playing games" and called Iran's most recent peace proposal "totally unacceptable." On May 11, Iranian state media reported that Tehran's counter-proposal included recognition of sovereignty over the Strait of Hormuz and a demand for compensation for war damages.

**[OFFICIAL]** President Donald Trump said the ceasefire between the US and Iran is on "massive life support" following Iran's latest counterproposal to end hostilities, which he called "simply unacceptable." He warned that until a deal is reached the ceasefire is "unbelievably weak."

**[OFFICIAL]** Iran's foreign ministry said earlier that its proposal, which state media reported included recognition of its sovereignty over the Strait of Hormuz, was "reasonable" and "generous."

**[REPORTED]** Iran offered to transfer some of its stockpile of highly enriched uranium to a third country in its response to the latest US proposal, but rejected the idea of dismantling its nuclear facilities, the Wall Street Journal reported.

**Assessment:** This is a significant deterioration from Friday's cautious optimism. Iran's response appears to accept some nuclear compromises (uranium transfer) while demanding concessions the US has explicitly rejected (Hormuz sovereignty, compensation). Trump's "massive life support" language is the most negative framing of the ceasefire to date.

---

#### Netanyahu Announces Plan to End US Military Aid
**[OFFICIAL]** Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said Sunday he intends to phase out Israel's reliance on U.S. military aid, drawing down the annual $3.8 billion in financial support to zero over the next decade. In an interview broadcast on CBS' "60 Minutes," Netanyahu said he has already conveyed the plan to President Donald Trump and to Israeli officials, some of whom reacted with surprise.

**[OFFICIAL]** "I want to draw down the American support for Israel to zero," he said in an interview on CBS's 60 Minutes that aired Sunday. "We've come of age."

**[REPORTED]** Only after the war began did Israeli planners recognize Iran's ability to close the strait, Netanyahu said. "It took a while for them to understand how big that risk is, which they understand now," he said.

**Assessment:** Netanyahu's announcement is strategically significant. It preemptively addresses growing US public opposition to Israel aid while positioning Israel for greater operational independence. The timing — during an active war — signals Netanyahu's confidence in Israel's position and potentially reduces US leverage over Israeli military decisions in Lebanon and elsewhere.

#### Trump State Visit to China Begins Wednesday
**[OFFICIAL]** US President Donald Trump will pay a state visit to China from May 13 to 15 at the invitation of President Xi Jinping. It will mark the first visit to China by an American president in almost nine years.

**[OFFICIAL]** A senior administration official told news outlets in an anonymous briefing on Sunday that Trump could "apply pressure" to China on Iran in areas such as oil sales and Tehran's purchase of potential dual-role military-civilian goods. US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent last week accused China of "funding" Iran.

**[REPORTED]** Some experts state that the ongoing 2026 Iran war could give China greater leverage when dealing with Donald Trump, given that the U.S. had diverted resources away from South Korea and Japan to the Middle East.

**Assessment:** Trump's China visit (May 13-15) overlaps with Lebanon-Israel talks (May 14-15), creating a week of parallel high-stakes diplomacy. The timing is significant — China may emerge as a potential broker or pressure point on Iran, or may extract concessions from a distracted administration.

#### Lebanon Casualties Continue to Mount
**[VERIFIED]** There were 32 fatalities from Israeli strikes on 8 May 2026. On 9 May, 39 people were killed by Israeli strikes, and on 10 May the daily death toll was above 40. An IDF soldier was killed on May 10.

**[VERIFIED]** Since April 16, Israeli forces have killed nearly 500 people, bringing the overall death toll since Israel's invasion and bombardment of Lebanon began on March 2 to more than 2,750 people, according to the Health Ministry.

**[OFFICIAL]** The United States will facilitate two days of intensive talks between the governments of Israel and Lebanon on May 14 and 15. Building on the April 23 round, which was led personally by President Trump, both delegations will engage in detailed discussions aimed at advancing a comprehensive peace and security agreement.

**Assessment:** The escalating violence in Lebanon — averaging 35-40 deaths daily over the weekend — severely complicates the scheduled talks. The gap between the nominal ceasefire and ground reality has never been wider.

#### Oil Prices Jump on Deal Rejection
**[VERIFIED]** As of 8:55 a.m. Eastern Time today, oil sold for $107.67 per barrel using Brent as the benchmark. That's 48 cents higher than yesterday morning and approximately a $43 rise over the past year.

**[VERIFIED]** Brent crude prices on Sunday rose 3.17% to $104.50 a barrel and US crude climbed 3.21%, to about $98.48 a barrel.

**[REPORTED]** Saudi Aramco CEO Amin Nasser warned the market is losing around 100 million barrels of supply each week and said prolonged disruptions could delay normalization until next year.

**Assessment:** Oil prices have jumped ~$3.60/barrel since Friday's close ($104) on the deal rejection news. The Saudi Aramco warning about year-long normalization delays underscores the lasting economic damage regardless of diplomatic outcomes.

#### GPS/Signal Interference Escalates
**[REPORTED]** Geolocation data for some ships in the Persian Gulf showed locations and speeds that defied logic, pointing to an increase in signal interference following fresh attacks by Iran on neighboring countries. The tracking data showed about 120 vessels in a circle about an hour's drive inland from Abu Dhabi, signaling they're traveling at nearly 50 knots but without changes to their location. A smaller gathering of about a dozen ships was indicating speeds of more than 100 knots near the Omani-UAE land border.

**Assessment:** Iran appears to be deploying electronic warfare capabilities to disrupt maritime navigation in the Gulf, adding a new dimension to the Hormuz crisis beyond physical attacks and blockades.

#### UN Chief Calls for Urgent De-escalation
**[OFFICIAL]** As the Strait of Hormuz crisis deepens and tensions between Iran and the United States remain unresolved, oil prices rose again early Monday, prompting the UN Secretary-General to call for a peaceful resolution. "My strong appeal is for the negotiations to go on until that diplomatic solution is found, the ceasefire to be maintained, and in between, the Strait of Hormuz to be completely open…Any restart of the fighting would have terrible consequences," António Guterres said.

**[VERIFIED]** Oman's foreign minister said there is an "urgent need" to free ships stranded along the strait, following a meeting with International Maritime Organization. The critical global shipping route passes between Iran and Oman.
