## Critical Developments (August 6-7)

### 1. CRITICAL: Mecca Joint Defence Agreement Signed — Saudi Arabia, Turkey, Pakistan [VERIFIED]

Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan signed a mutual defense pact that stipulates an attack against any one of them "shall be regarded as an attack against them all." It comes as Saudi Arabia anticipates "multiple coordinated attacks" on ports and airports by Iran-backed militias, a Saudi official told CNN.

On Friday, Oil-rich Saudi Arabia, nuclear power Pakistan and Turkey, which has NATO's second-largest army and a rapidly growing defense industry, signed a mutual defense agreement, which Saudi media outlet Al Arabiya dubbed the "Mecca Joint Defence Agreement".

Turkish media reports said it is likely to allow joint military exercises and training, technology transfers and the sharing of intelligence. A Turkish government official described the agreement as "purely defensive in nature," saying the sides have pledged mutual support only for defense.

The deal comes at a time when Saudi Arabia, whose critical infrastructure and oil facilities have come under attack as part of the US-Israeli war on Iran, has been looking to diversify its defense partnerships. A source close to the Saudi military and government told AFP that although the deal between Riyadh, Ankara and Islamabad had been under discussion for a long time, "the latest developments in the region expedite it".

A senior Iranian official warned the pact will not guarantee Saudi Arabia's security.

*Assessment: [VERIFIED] This is a major geopolitical development. The trilateral pact — building on the September 2025 Saudi-Pakistan bilateral agreement — creates a NATO-style collective defense arrangement among three major Muslim-majority powers, including a nuclear-armed state. The timing — during active IRGC-coordinated threats against Saudi Arabia — signals Riyadh is preparing for prolonged regional conflict regardless of Hormuz negotiations.*

### 2. CRITICAL: Hormuz Deal Remains Elusive — Iran-Oman "Close" But Not Finalized [OFFICIAL/REPORTED]

Iran and Oman are close to agreeing on new arrangements for the Strait of Hormuz, but not for a full reopening of the vital waterway, Iran's state news agency reported Thursday.

Tehran's deputy foreign minister said Iran and Oman are close to finalizing a proposed framework for commercial shipping through the Strait of Hormuz.

Iranian lawmakers are debating the wording of a proposed deal with Oman on the Strait of Hormuz, with US President Donald Trump insisting an agreement is getting closer.

**Fee Structure Dispute:**
Representatives of Iran and Oman are discussing the introduction of transit fees for vessels in the Strait of Hormuz ranging from 3% to 7% of the declared value of the cargo as part of negotiations to restore shipping operations, Reuters reported. The Iranian side insists on introducing a fee of 5-7%, while Oman proposes a lower rate of 3%.

The United States is categorically opposed to any service fees payable to Iran for transit through the Strait of Hormuz.

Ships transiting the strait would pay fees for maritime services—including insurance, bunkering and environmental protection—rather than cargo-based transit tolls. The source also rejected reports that Tehran and Muscat were divided over proposed charges of 7% or 3% of a vessel's cargo value, saying the fees would instead depend on the scope of maritime services provided.

*Assessment: [OFFICIAL/REPORTED] The deal that appeared imminent on August 5 has slipped. The core dispute — fees versus no fees — persists. Iran wants 5-7%; Oman proposes 3%; the US demands zero. The framing of fees as "maritime services" rather than "cargo tolls" may offer diplomatic cover, but the fundamental gap remains. Iran's parliament is simultaneously reviewing legislation that could provide legal basis for an indefinite blockade if talks fail.*

### 3. CRITICAL: Iran Parliament Reviews Maximalist Hormuz Bill — US/Israel Ban, Fees [OFFICIAL]

Iran's parliament is reviewing a draft bill that would significantly tighten state control over navigation in the Strait of Hormuz and the Persian Gulf, including by banning vessels linked to the United States, Israel and other countries Tehran designates as hostile, the semi-official Tasnim News Agency reported Thursday. Tasnim cited Abbas Salimi, a member of parliament's presiding board, as saying the preliminary text of the Strategic Action for the Security and Sustainable Development of the Strait of Hormuz and the Persian Gulf is being reviewed by parliament's National Security and Foreign Policy Committee.

The plan would impose fees of up to 7% of cargo value on commercial vessels that pass through the strait. It would also fine ships 20% of cargo if they violate Iran's conditions.

The bill further envisages Iran collecting fees for navigation, supervision, management and security services, with priority given to payments in the Iranian national currency.

The Trump administration has rejected the plan.

*Assessment: [OFFICIAL] The parliamentary bill represents Iran's maximalist position and provides legal framework for continued blockade if negotiations fail. The 20% penalty clause and US/Israel transit ban exceed anything discussed in the Iran-Oman diplomatic track. This may be posturing for leverage, but it signals domestic political pressure for hardline terms.*

### 4. SIGNIFICANT: Houthis Kill 58+ in Yemen — Deadliest Attacks in Four Years [VERIFIED]

The attacks killed at least 58 government troops and injured dozens, according to a military source. Yemen's Iran-backed Houthis killed at least 58 government troops in missile and drone attacks on Thursday in one of the deadliest days of the country's civil war in four years.

The Houthis struck troops, depots, and camps in three provinces across central and eastern Yemen after detecting a buildup of Saudi-backed forces preparing for an offensive, the group's military spokesman, Yahya Saree, said in a statement.

A senior Saudi official told journalists that a Houthi attack on southern Saudi Arabia on Thursday had wounded 11 civilians, including a young child.

On Friday, a senior Saudi official, speaking on condition of anonymity, told Reuters that intelligence suggests the Houthis and Iraqi armed groups may launch new attacks under the supervision of Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC), including on energy infrastructure, ports and airports.

The flare-up follows four years of relative calm in the small Arab nation, which has been plagued by civil war since 2014.

*Assessment: [VERIFIED] The Yemen front has dramatically escalated. After four years of relative quiet, Houthis have launched coordinated attacks that represent a significant widening of the conflict. The Saudi-led coalition resuming strikes for the first time since 2022 signals the Yemen civil war may be reigniting as a theater of the broader Iran conflict.*

### 5. SIGNIFICANT: Two Israeli Soldiers Killed in Lebanon — First Since June Truce [VERIFIED]

Two Israeli soldiers were killed in an explosion in Lebanon, Israel's military announced on Thursday in what are the first Israeli deaths since a truce between Israel and Hezbollah was announced nearly two months ago. Israel said four other soldiers were seriously wounded.

The latest strike came after two Israeli soldiers were killed, another critically wounded and several others injured when an improvised explosive device exploded inside a booby-trapped building in the southern Lebanese village of Majdal Zoun.

On Wednesday, Israel launched strikes in southern Lebanon in response to the deaths, describing it as Hezbollah's violation of the ceasefire and warning village residents to flee. Talks on Wednesday ended early due to the strikes, but resumed Thursday, focusing on the implementation of pilot zones where Israel would withdraw and Lebanon would take over.

More than 4,000 people have been killed by Israeli strikes in Lebanon since then, including hundreds of civilians. About 40 soldiers and three civilians - including a military contractor - have been killed on the Israeli side.

*Assessment: [VERIFIED] The fragile Israel-Lebanon truce is fraying. These are the first Israeli combat deaths since June, and Israel responded with strikes that disrupted ceasefire talks. The Lebanon front remains volatile despite diplomatic efforts.*

### 6. SIGNIFICANT: Trump Denies Munitions Shortage — Threatens Jail for Leakers [OFFICIAL/REPORTED]

President Donald Trump denied the US was running low on munitions, saying the country has "massive amounts" of them, while warning of punishment for those who say there's a shortage.

Trump made the comments on social media hours after the Washington Post reported he had clashed with Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth over shortages of weapons such as long-range guided missiles and air-defense interceptors that threatened to limit his military options in the war with Iran.

"The 'leakers' of these treasonous statements are being hunted down. Long term jail sentences will be sought!" he asserted.

Multiple administration officials took issue with an article by The Washington Post alleging that Trump berated Secretary of War Pete Hegseth over the purported munitions shortage.

The confrontation took place on the sidelines of a Cabinet meeting at Camp David on Friday, where Trump reportedly told Hegseth he believed the munitions problem had already been fixed, according to both sources. Shortages, particularly in long-range guided missiles and air-defense interceptors, have contributed to Trump's decision to hold off on launching further large-scale strikes against Iran in recent days, one source said.

*Assessment: [REPORTED — DISPUTED] The White House categorically denies the Camp David clash occurred, but the intensity of Trump's response — threatening jail for leakers — suggests the reports struck a nerve. Whether or not the specific confrontation happened, the underlying constraint (depleted stockpiles) was confirmed earlier this week by multiple outlets.*

Global oil prices steadied in early trade, holding onto gains of around 4% made yesterday after reports that Iran was drafting a bill to ban "hostile" vessels from transiting the Strait of Hormuz. Brent crude futures, the global oil benchmark, were down 0.4% at $82 a barrel at 6.20 a.m.
