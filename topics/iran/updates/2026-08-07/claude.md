## Key Developments Since Prior Report (August 5-6)

### 1. Iran Parliament Introduces Maximalist Hormuz Bill — Far Exceeds Deal Framework

**[OFFICIAL]** Iran's parliament is reviewing a plan to ban ships linked to the U.S., Israel and other "hostile countries" from transiting the Strait of Hormuz until Tehran is compensated for war damage. The plan would impose fees of up to 7% of cargo value on commercial vessels that pass through the strait. It would also fine ships 20% of cargo if they violate Iran's conditions.

**[OFFICIAL]** Iranian state media quoted the Foreign Ministry as saying that ships would enter through the strait's northern corridor near the Iranian coast and exit through the southern corridor near the Omani coast.

**[REPORTED]** Under the proposal, Iran would prohibit US and Israeli vessels from transiting Hormuz and require countries deemed hostile to pay compensation before being granted passage. Tehran also proposed imposing penalties on violators equivalent to 20% of the value of the cargo carried by a vessel and stated that a full reopening of the strait would depend on the lifting of the US maritime blockade.

*Assessment: This parliamentary bill represents a significant hardening of Iran's position from what was being negotiated with Oman. The 7% cargo fees + 20% penalties go far beyond the "service fees" previously discussed. This may be posturing to strengthen negotiating leverage, but it signals domestic political pressure for maximalist terms.*

### 2. Deal Status: "Close" But Not Finalized — No Wednesday/Thursday Announcement

**[OFFICIAL]** US President Donald Trump says a deal has not yet been reached with Iran to reopen the Strait of Hormuz but such an agreement could be reached soon. In the meantime, the US will maintain its blockade of Iranian ports.

**[OFFICIAL]** Iran and Oman are close to finalizing a proposed framework for commercial shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, Tehran's deputy foreign minister said. The agreement would not automatically reopen the waterway, he warned. Iran has also denied any direct negotiations with the US.

**[OFFICIAL]** A U.S. official said any of the temporary routes agreed to will be "without any impediments" like tolls. "Any temporary routes will be without any impediments — meaning no approvals or permissions and no tolls or charges."

**[REPORTED]** Iran and Oman are close to agreeing on new arrangements for the Strait of Hormuz, but not for a full reopening of the vital waterway, Iran's state news agency reported Thursday.

*Assessment: The anticipated Wednesday announcement did not materialize. The US insists on "no tolls or charges" while Iran's parliament is pushing for 7% cargo fees. This gap has widened, not narrowed. A deal structure is emerging, but final terms remain contested.*

### 3. Iran's Chief Negotiator Mocks Trump's "Theater Diplomacy"

**[OFFICIAL]** "'Massive attack coming… wait, never mind, they want to negotiate.' That's theater diplomacy on loop," Iran's chief negotiator Mohammed Bagher Ghalibaf wrote. "Using bullying + broken promises + fake news as leverage is a failed strategy. Acknowledge the facts and fulfill your commitments. We don't need more theater."

*Assessment: This signals Iran's negotiating team views Trump's repeated threats and pullbacks as weakening US credibility. Combined with munitions depletion reports, Tehran may be calculating it has time on its side.*

### 4. Houthis Launch "Large-Scale" Attack in Yemen — At Least 30+ Killed

**[VERIFIED]** The Houthi militant group said it conducted a "large-scale" attack against forces from Yemen's Saudi-backed government, raising concerns over a broadening of the US-Israeli war on Iran into a region-wide conflict.

**[VERIFIED]** Yemeni government sources told Al Jazeera that at least 30 government soldiers were killed and 15 others injured when Houthis fired eight missiles from al-Jawf governorate, north of Marib, targeting al-Thiniya and al-Abr in Hadramout and al-Rouk in Marib.

**[VERIFIED]** A military source says at least 58 people were killed in attacks by Iran-backed Houthi forces against Saudi-supported Yemen government troops. The fighting is the worst in more than three years in Yemen's civil war.

**[OFFICIAL]** The Saudi-led coalition has since launched strikes on Houthi targets in western Yemen, its first since 2022.

*Assessment: This is a major escalation in the Yemen theater. The Saudi-led coalition resuming strikes for the first time since 2022 signals the regional conflict is widening, not narrowing — regardless of US-Iran Hormuz talks.*

### 5. Saudi Arabia Braces for "Multiple Coordinated Attacks" — Iraqi Militia + Houthi Joint Operations

**[VERIFIED]** Saudi Arabia is bracing for "multiple coordinated attacks" by Iraqi militia factions and Iran-backed Houthis, a Saudi official told CNN, as the broader Middle East conflict threatens to widen. The official cited "multiple intelligence reports" from Saudi Arabia, the US and regional countries indicating some Iraqi militia members, "in coordination with the Houthis" are planning to attack the Gulf nation "in the very near future" under the guidance of Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC).

**[VERIFIED]** Saudi Arabia expects imminent coordinated attacks from the north and south by Iraqi militias and Yemen's Houthis under the supervision of Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps. The official said intelligence reports indicated civilian and economic sites could be targeted, including energy infrastructure, ports and airports. Saudi Arabia had also observed drones and missiles being moved, suggesting coordinated operations from both directions.

**[VERIFIED]** Shortly after the official's statement, the Saudi-led coalition said that 11 civilians were injured Thursday in a Houthi attack in southern Saudi Arabia near the border with Yemen. Seven of those wounded were Saudi nationals, including a woman and a 4-year-old child who suffered second-degree burns.

**[REPORTED]** On Friday, Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan will hold a summit in Mecca, Islam's holiest city, where a joint defense agreement is expected to be announced, according to regional sources.

*Assessment: This is a significant new development. IRGC-coordinated joint Houthi-Iraqi militia attacks on Saudi Arabia would represent a major regional escalation outside the US-Iran bilateral track. The Mecca summit suggests Riyadh is building a defensive coalition.*

### 6. Two Israeli Soldiers Killed in Lebanon — First Deaths Since June Truce

**[VERIFIED]** Two Israeli soldiers were killed in an explosion in southern Lebanon, Israel's military said Thursday, the first Israeli deaths since a shaky truce between Israel and the Lebanese militant group Hezbollah began nearly two months ago. Four other soldiers were seriously wounded.

**[REPORTED]** Israel, which launched more strikes in southern Lebanon on Thursday in response to Hezbollah allegedly killing two soldiers, has said it will only exit the region if the Iran-backed militant group disarms.

*Assessment: The fragile Israel-Hezbollah truce is fraying. Israeli casualties will likely trigger retaliation and potentially another Lebanon escalation cycle.*

### 7. Trump Threatens Jail for Munitions "Leakers" — Clash with Hegseth Reported

**[OFFICIAL]** "The U.S. has massive amounts of 'munitions,' especially of certain types. Additionally, large amounts are being manufactured and shipped to the U.S. as needed. Defense companies are building the largest number of plants and factories in our country's history. The 'leakers' of these treasonous statements are being hunted down. Long term jail sentences will be sought!"

**[REPORTED]** The Washington Post reported Wednesday that the president's frustration over the Iran war boiled over at Camp David last week, where he demanded answers from Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth on why he had apparently been misled on extreme munitions shortages that now threaten to limit military options with Iran.

**[OFFICIAL]** White House press secretary Karoline Leavitt has vehemently denied the exchange between Trump and Hegseth took place, telling The Washington Post the alleged conversation is "100% fake news" and "literally never happened."

**[VERIFIED]** Reuters news agency and CBS News reported that the military had burned through nearly all of its offensive Army Tactical Missile Systems (ATACMSs) and Precision Strike Missiles (PrSMs). A CNN report showing low levels of Patriot and Terminal High Altitude Area Defense (THAAD) interceptors has caused greater concern, with experts noting there is no viable short-term alternative to the air defence missiles.

**[REPORTED]** Replenishments of pre-war stockpiles are expected to take years.

*Assessment: Trump's angry response to leaks confirms the reports are concerning him. The threat to jail leakers is notable. Whether or not the Camp David clash occurred, the munitions issue is now a political liability.*
