### 1. US Announces "Indefinite" Blockade Strategy + New Sanctions

**[OFFICIAL]** Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth told reporters that the US military could keep a naval presence in the region to enforce its blockade of Iran, which has inflicted severe economic damage on the country. "Indefinitely the United States Navy can maintain a blockade like that because we'll rotate ships in and out, as we have, and we'll continue to," Mr. Hegseth told reporters on a trip to Panama.

**[OFFICIAL]** The move will be part of a 'one-two punch' that includes the continued blockade of Iran's ports, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said. The US will soon announce unprecedented economic measures against Iran. "Watch this space for more announcements coming next week because we are going to apply measures like have never been seen in the history of economic isolation on a country," Bessent said in an interview with Newsmax on Thursday.

**[OFFICIAL]** Bessent said coming actions "will be a combination of economic isolation like the world has never seen before, and the continued blockade in the Strait of Hormuz that will keep anything from going in or out of the Iranian ports."

*Assessment: This represents a significant hardening of US strategy. Rather than pursuing an imminent deal, the administration appears to be pivoting to a long-term attrition strategy, combining indefinite naval blockade with unprecedented economic measures. This substantially reduces near-term deal probability.*

### 2. USS Abraham Lincoln Crisis — Congressional Scrutiny Intensifies

**[VERIFIED]** A sailor went overboard earlier this month off of the USS Abraham Lincoln, three US officials told CNN, amid reports of worsening mental health for crew members on the ship during an extended deployment for the Iran war. The sailor was rescued within an hour, according to one of the officials, and was medically evaluated. A different official said that the sailor had gone overboard wearing a life vest and was not injured, adding that the Navy considered the incident a mental health episode.

**[VERIFIED]** Since the Lincoln left its home port of San Diego last November, the aircraft carrier has been deployed for more than 250 days after being redirected to the Middle East to support US operations in the war with Iran, now entering its sixth month. With more than 5,000 sailors and Marines aboard, it has not made a port call in more than 200 days, setting a modern-day record for consecutive days at sea.

**[REPORTED]** Rep. Mike Levin, D-Calif., sent a letter expressing serious concerns about conditions aboard the Lincoln. "Reports include concerns of moldy showers, broken toilets, lack of hot water, lack of access to fresh produce, and supply shortages," the letter said. Sailors and Marines are also said to have an "overwhelming workload," which is contributing to "extreme fatigue and burnout."

**[OFFICIAL]** The USS George Washington carrier group, which finished a port visit in Vietnam on August 5, is headed to the Middle East to replace the USS Abraham Lincoln, a US official told CNN. The official said that the swap had been previously planned.

**[OFFICIAL]** Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth said that conditions on the USS Abraham Lincoln had been "completely misrepresented" while talking to reporters.

*Assessment: This is a new domestic political vulnerability for the administration. The Lincoln crisis demonstrates strain on US naval capacity from an extended war. While the George Washington is en route, the timing is unclear, and the episode raises questions about sustainability of the "indefinite" blockade strategy.*

### 3. Iran Reasserts Hormuz Control Claims

**[OFFICIAL]** Tehran said Thursday the Strait of Hormuz is "under Iran's control and management" after President Trump said the U.S. has "total control" over the waterway. "I THINK WE WILL KEEP IT," Mr. Trump wrote Wednesday on his Truth Social platform.

**[VERIFIED]** Shipping traffic through the Strait of Hormuz fell to eight vessels on Tuesday, compared with a 10-day average of about 12 vessels, and 130 to 140 ships before the war.

**[VERIFIED]** Crude oil traded near $81 per barrel on Friday after declining in the previous session, as investors adopt a wait-and-see mode while monitoring diplomatic efforts to reopen the Strait of Hormuz. Despite the ongoing impasse, crude continues to flow out of the Persian Gulf, with some tankers sailing with their transponders switched off, although vessels navigating Hormuz remain exposed to persistent threats. The US also claims that as much as 9 million barrels of oil per day is currently transiting the waterway.

*Assessment: Both sides continue making contradictory claims about Hormuz control. Traffic data shows the strait remains functionally impaired — 8 vessels Tuesday vs. 130-140 pre-war. The US claim of "9 million barrels/day transiting" appears inflated relative to observed vessel counts.*

### 4. IEA & OPEC Cut Demand Forecasts — Global Supply Crisis Deepens

**[VERIFIED]** World oil demand is forecast to decline by 1.6 mb/d in 2026, 510 kb/d more than the IEA's estimate in last month's Report, as the ongoing closure of the Strait of Hormuz and elevated fuel prices continue to weigh on oil consumption. Annual contractions will nevertheless ease from 4.9 mb/d in 2Q26 to 2.8 mb/d in 3Q26, before returning to growth in the final quarter.

**[VERIFIED]** OPEC still expects demand to grow, though its estimate has been trimmed for a fourth consecutive month, to 580,000 b/d from 780,000 b/d.

**[VERIFIED]** The global oil balance is now expected to show a deficit of 1.8 million b/d in third-quarter 2026, more than double the estimate of around 800,000 b/d in last month's IEA report. "Although the market is projected to return to surplus towards the end of this year, risks remain substantial and the urgency of reopening the Strait has increased, as previously available inventory buffers are rapidly depleting."

**[VERIFIED]** Observed global stocks also dropped by 69 million barrels in July to just under 7.9 billion, down 410 million since the war began.

*Assessment: The IEA and OPEC forecasts reveal structural damage from the war. IEA sees outright demand decline in 2026 — the first since COVID-19 — while OPEC still forecasts modest growth. The 2.2 mb/d gap between their forecasts reflects uncertainty, but both agree on severe supply constraints and depleting inventories.*

The window created by that agreement is set to lapse around August 17, and no talks are currently underway to extend it.

Israel seized a swath of south Lebanon during a war with Hezbollah earlier this year and has said it will keep forces there to shield northern Israel from attack by the Iran-backed group, which ignited the latest hostilities when it opened fire at Israel two days into the U.S.-Israeli war on Iran.
