### Critical New Developments (May 18-19)

#### Trump Cancels Tuesday Attack at Gulf Allies' Request

**[OFFICIAL]** President Donald Trump said he is holding off on a military strike on Iran planned for Tuesday because "serious negotiations" are underway to end the war. "There seems to be a very good chance that they can work something out. If we can do that without bombing the hell out of them, I'd be very happy," Trump said at the White House on Monday evening.

**[OFFICIAL]** President Donald Trump said Monday he is calling off a plan to attack Iran on Tuesday after the heads of three regional powers in the Middle East asked him to "hold off." Trump, in a Truth Social post, said he has informed U.S. military leaders "that we will NOT be doing the scheduled attack of Iran tomorrow."

**[OFFICIAL]** Trump said the request came from Qatari Emir Tamim bin Hamad al-Thani, Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman and UAE President Sheikh Mohammed bin Zayed Al Nahyan.

**[OFFICIAL]** Trump said he had planned "a very major attack" but put it off — "for a little while, hopefully, maybe forever." He said America's allies in the Gulf asked him to wait for two to three days because they feel they are close to a deal.

**[VERIFIED]** Despite the pause, Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth and Joint Chiefs Chairman Gen. Dan Cain should still "be prepared to go forward with a full, large scale assault of Iran, on a moment's notice, in the event that an acceptable Deal is not reached," Trump said.

**[VERIFIED]** The president has repeatedly set deadlines for Tehran and then backed off. But he's also previously indicated he would hold off on military action to allow talks to continue — only to turn around and launch strikes.

**Assessment:** This marks the first time Trump has publicly disclosed a specific scheduled attack before cancelling it. The pattern of Gulf states restraining US military action has now been explicitly acknowledged by the president himself. However, Trump's instruction to maintain readiness for "a full, large scale assault" suggests this is a pause, not a pivot.

#### Iran Submits Revised 14-Point Proposal

**[VERIFIED]** Peace mediator Pakistan has shared with the United States a revised proposal from Iran to end the war in the Middle East, a Pakistani source told Reuters on Monday. Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesperson Esmaeil Baghaei later confirmed that Tehran's views had been "conveyed to the American side through Pakistan" but gave no details.

**[VERIFIED]** "We don't have much time," the Pakistani source said, when asked if it would take time to close gaps, adding that both countries "keep changing their goalposts".

**[REPORTED]** The Iranian proposal, as described by the senior Iranian source, appeared similar in many respects to Iran's previous offer, which U.S. President Donald Trump rejected last week as "garbage". It would focus first on securing an end to the war, reopening the Strait of Hormuz – a major oil supply route that Iran has effectively blockaded – and lifting maritime sanctions. More contentious issues around Iran's nuclear programme and uranium enrichment would be deferred to later rounds of talks.

**[REPORTED]** Iran is ready to hand over its enriched uranium to Russia instead of the United States, according to Tehran's updated draft conflict settlement agreement with Washington, quoted by the Al Hadath television.

**[OFFICIAL]** Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian responded to Trump's remarks with defiance: "Dialogue does not mean surrender," Pezeshkian said.

**[VERIFIED]** The regime is "facing serious challenges" in the aftermath of the five-week-long U.S.-Israeli strikes, Iran's President Masoud Pezeshkian admitted on Monday. Besides the decapitation of the regime's leadership, Iran's military-industrial infrastructure has been devastated by the operation Epic Fury, Pezeshkian confessed.

**Assessment:** The new proposal appears substantively unchanged from previous offers. Iran continues to insist on sequencing — end the war first, then discuss nuclear issues — while the US demands the reverse. The reported willingness to transfer enriched uranium to Russia rather than the US may offer a face-saving formula, but this has not been confirmed by US officials. Pezeshkian's public admission of "serious challenges" is notable candor, but his "no surrender" rhetoric signals no imminent breakthrough.

#### Oil Reverses, Jumps to $112.93

**[VERIFIED]** As of 9:20 a.m. Eastern Time today, oil is trading at $112.93 per barrel, based on the Brent benchmark. That's $2.85 above yesterday morning's level and about $47 higher than where it stood a year ago.

**[VERIFIED]** Prices moved higher again after Axios reported that Iran had submitted an updated peace proposal aimed at ending the conflict, though the White House reportedly viewed the offer as insufficient. Markets remain highly volatile, with oil prices elevated as the Strait of Hormuz stays largely shut and attacks on key infrastructure disrupt production.

**[VERIFIED]** The International Energy Agency warned on Monday that global oil inventories are declining rapidly.

**Assessment:** Oil's $2.85 jump today erases yesterday's diplomacy-driven decline and then some. Markets initially responded positively to Trump's attack cancellation but reversed when the Iranian proposal was deemed insufficient. The IEA warning about declining inventories adds fundamental pressure.

#### UAE/Saudi Drone Attacks — No Retaliation (Yet)

**[VERIFIED]** The UAE's Ministry of Foreign Affairs posted a statement on X on Sunday saying the country condemned "the unprovoked terrorist attack" in "the strongest terms". The statement added: "The UAE emphasised that it will not tolerate any threat to its security and sovereignty under any circumstances, and that it reserves its full, sovereign, legitimate, diplomatic, and military rights to respond to any threats."

**[VERIFIED]** Neighbouring Gulf states Saudi Arabia and Qatar condemned the attack on the Barakah plant. The Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Kuwait also issued a statement denouncing the attack, which it called "heinous". The Indian Ministry of External Affairs condemned the attack, calling it "unacceptable", saying it represented "a dangerous escalation" and urging a return to diplomacy.

**[REPORTED]** Iran has not claimed responsibility for the drone attacks, and there has been no public statement from Iran about the incident at Barakah.

**[VERIFIED]** Twenty-four hours after the drone attack on the Barakah nuclear plant, the UAE stated that it is investigating what it called "an unprovoked terrorist attack." The Emirates and the other countries in the Gulf and beyond did not accuse Iran of the attack, which may also signal a different origin of the drone attack, as the UAE and other Gulf states constantly monitor the airspace to detect Iranian attacks.

**Assessment:** The UAE's careful language — not directly blaming Iran — combined with the subsequent appeal to Trump to delay his attack suggests Abu Dhabi is prioritizing diplomacy over retaliation. This may explain the apparent contradiction of condemning the attack as "unacceptable aggression" while simultaneously urging the US not to respond militarily.

**[VERIFIED]** Since April 16, Israeli forces have killed nearly 500 people, bringing the overall death toll since Israel's invasion and bombardment of Lebanon began on March 2 to more than 2,750 people, according to the Health Ministry.

**[VERIFIED]** On 15 May, an Israeli airstrike targeted the town of Harouf, southern Lebanon, killing at least six people, including three paramedics.

**Assessment:** No significant change from yesterday's report. The ceasefire remains largely nominal, with continued Israeli strikes killing dozens per day during the "truce" period.

#### Hormuz Technical Talks Begin with Oman

**[REPORTED]** Iran's state media reported, citing the Iranian Ministry of Foreign Affairs, that technical teams from Iran and Oman met in Oman to negotiate a mechanism for safe transit in the Strait of Hormuz.

**Assessment:** This is a new development worth watching. If Iran is negotiating transit mechanisms with Oman (a neutral Gulf state), it may signal willingness to normalize some commercial shipping while maintaining its PGSA framework. This could provide a path to partial Hormuz reopening without full capitulation on either side.

### Key Emerging Issues

1. **Gulf State Leverage Exposed** — Trump's public acknowledgment that Qatar, Saudi Arabia, and UAE persuaded him to cancel attacks reveals the limits of US autonomy. This dynamic may encourage Iran to continue pressure on Gulf states as indirect leverage on US policy.

2. **Russia as Uranium Destination** — The reported offer to transfer uranium to Russia rather than the US could provide a face-saving compromise. However, this would require Russian cooperation in a verification regime, which is far from assured given current US-Russia relations.

3. **Iran-Oman Technical Talks** — The emergence of Oman as a parallel channel for Hormuz transit discussions suggests multiple diplomatic tracks are in play. If successful, this could allow partial normalization of shipping without resolving the broader conflict.

4. **Pezeshkian's Candor** — His admission that Iran faces "serious challenges" and should not "falsely claim we have no problems" is notable transparency. This may be positioning for eventual concessions, or could be domestic political management.

### Bottom Line

**The pattern is now explicit: Gulf states are restraining US military action.** Trump's public acknowledgment that he cancelled a "major attack" at the request of Qatar, Saudi Arabia, and UAE reveals the extent of regional leverage. This creates a diplomatic opening but also signals to Iran that time is on its side.

**Iran's proposal offers nothing new — and that may be strategic.** By continuing to demand the same terms Trump has repeatedly rejected, Tehran appears to be testing whether the Gulf state restraint can be sustained indefinitely. Pezeshkian's "dialogue does not mean surrender" statement suggests no imminent capitulation.

**Oil's reversal erases diplomacy optimism.** The $2.85 jump from $110.08 to $112.93 reflects market recognition that the "serious negotiations" Trump cited have not actually produced progress. The IEA's warning about declining inventories adds fundamental pressure.

**The stalemate is institutionalizing.** Iran's PGSA formalizes Hormuz control; Gulf states formalize their role as US restraints; the US maintains readiness without acting. This equilibrium is unstable — any party could break it — but there is currently no obvious catalyst for resolution.
