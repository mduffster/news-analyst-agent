## Critical Developments Since May 18

### 1. Trump Cancels Tuesday Attack at Gulf Allies' Request

**[OFFICIAL]** United States President Donald Trump says he has decided to pause an attack on Iran at the behest of Gulf leaders after Tehran sent a new peace proposal to Washington through Pakistan.

**[OFFICIAL]** President Donald Trump said he cancelled strikes on Iran planned for Tuesday, once again citing progress towards a deal to end the war. Trump's Monday announcement on Truth Social came a day after he warned Iran to "get moving, FAST, or there won't be anything left of them." The President said the leaders of several Gulf countries had requested that he "hold off on our planned Military attack" because "serious negotiations are now taking place."

**[OFFICIAL]** Trump instructed the U.S. military to be prepared to go forward with a full, large scale assault of Iran, on a moment's notice, in the event that an acceptable Deal is not reached.

**[VERIFIED]** WTI crude futures slipped toward $103 per barrel on Tuesday, giving back some recent gains after President Donald Trump said he called off a planned military strike on Iran following appeals from Persian Gulf allies. Trump stated that Saudi Arabia, Qatar, and the UAE asked him to "hold off," adding that serious talks were now in progress.

**[VERIFIED]** The President has on multiple occasions since the U.S. and Israel launched the war against Iran on Feb. 28 suggested that the war is nearing its end while also threatening further attacks on Iran. The U.S.-Iran cease-fire, which took effect on April 8, appeared to be on shaky ground in recent days, after Trump rejected the Iranians' counter to a U.S. proposal for a framework agreement for a longer-lasting peace.

**Assessment:** This marks the first time Trump has publicly disclosed a specific scheduled attack before cancelling it. The explicit acknowledgment that Qatar, Saudi Arabia, and UAE persuaded him to pause military action exposes Gulf state leverage over US decision-making. However, Trump's instruction to maintain readiness for "a full, large scale assault" on "moment's notice" keeps escalation risk high.

### 2. UAE Officially Attributes Barakah Attack to Iraq

**[OFFICIAL]** The drones that targeted the United Arab Emirates' Barakah nuclear power plant all came from Iraq, the country's Defense Ministry said Tuesday, likely signaling that Iranian-backed Shiite militias launched the assault. Such militias launched repeated drone attacks targeting Gulf Arab states after Israel and the United States began their war against Iran back on Feb. 28.

**[OFFICIAL]** "As part of the ongoing investigation into the blatant attack on the Barakah Nuclear Power Plant on May 17, 2026, technical tracking and monitoring confirmed that the three drones... all originated from Iraqi territory," the Emirati defence ministry said.

**[VERIFIED]** Militias in the past have provided Iran a means by which to deflect blame over such attacks. There were no reported injuries or radiological release at Barakah after the attack, which Emirati officials said hit a generator on the facility's perimeter.

**[VERIFIED]** There were three other drones that targeted the country over the last two days, the Emirati Defense Ministry added. Saudi Arabia, which had also condemned the nuclear plant attack, later said it had intercepted three drones that entered from Iraqi airspace.

**[REPORTED]** Iraqi government spokesman Bassem al-Awadi, without addressing the Emirati Defense Ministry's report, issued a statement saying that Baghdad "expresses its strong condemnation of the recent drone attacks targeting the UAE."

**Assessment:** The official attribution to Iraqi territory confirms an Iraq-based proxy launch corridor, which significantly expands the conflict's operational geography. This raises the prospect of counter-drone and counter-militia operations focused on Iraqi territory, increases Baghdad-Gulf diplomatic strain, and could lead to "attribution laddering" if explicit links to Tehran are asserted. The UAE's careful language—not directly naming Iran—combined with its appeal for Trump to delay attacks suggests Abu Dhabi is prioritizing diplomacy over escalation, at least for now.

### 3. Iran Submits Revised 14-Point Proposal; White House Deems It "Insufficient"

**[VERIFIED]** Iran submitted a revised 14-point proposal to Pakistani mediators on Monday, Iranian state-linked media reported. It's unclear whether the new proposal resolves key differences between the U.S. and Iranian negotiators, including disagreements around Iran's nuclear program and the Strait of Hormuz.

**[REPORTED]** Iran is considering transferring its stockpile of enriched uranium to Russia instead of the United States as part of a potential peace agreement with Washington. According to the report, Tehran has agreed in principle to a long-term freeze of its nuclear program rather than its complete dismantlement. The proposal reportedly includes transferring around 400 kilograms of highly enriched uranium to Russia instead of the United States.

**[VERIFIED]** Iran's Foreign Minister Araghchi confirmed he had spoken to Russian officials about an offer from Moscow to store Iran's enriched uranium. He said Iran may consider Russia's proposal at an "appropriate time." "When we come to that stage, obviously we will have more consultations with Russia and see if the Russian offer can help or not," he said.

**[REPORTED]** Washington has reportedly already dismissed Iran's latest proposal as insufficient, Axios reported on Monday, citing an unnamed American official briefed on the issue. According to the official, Tehran made only "token" improvements on the previous version. US President Donald Trump has repeatedly insisted that Iran's highly enriched uranium should be handed over to America.

**[REPORTED]** The problem is that Iran does not want to start with nuclear surrender. Tehran is trying to first secure a cessation of hostilities, economic relief and the opening of space for oil exports, and only then discuss long-term nuclear restrictions. The USA and Israel, on the contrary, they want to first receive nuclear guarantees and only then talk about major concessions.

**Assessment:** The revised Iranian proposal appears substantively unchanged from previous offers, though the reported willingness to transfer uranium to Russia rather than the US could offer a face-saving formula. However, Trump rejected a similar Putin offer in March. The fundamental sequencing dispute remains: Iran wants to end hostilities first, then discuss nuclear issues; the US demands the reverse. Markets initially responded positively to Trump's attack cancellation but reversed when the Iranian proposal was deemed insufficient.

### 4. Oil Rebounds to $112.93 as Breakthrough Hopes Fade

**[VERIFIED]** As of 9:20 a.m. Eastern Time today, oil is trading at $112.93 per barrel, based on the Brent benchmark. That's $2.85 above yesterday morning's level and about $47 higher than where it stood a year ago.

**[VERIFIED]** Earlier in the session, oil prices fell after Iranian media reported that the US had proposed a temporary waiver of oil sanctions pending a final agreement. Prices later reversed course and moved higher again after Axios reported that Iran had submitted an updated peace proposal aimed at ending the conflict, though the White House reportedly viewed the offer as insufficient. Markets remain highly volatile, with oil prices elevated as the Strait of Hormuz stays largely shut and attacks on key infrastructure disrupt production.

**[VERIFIED]** Earlier optimism had been supported by reports that Washington proposed a temporary waiver on oil sanctions, though this has not been confirmed by US officials. Meanwhile, the International Energy Agency warned on Monday that global oil inventories are declining rapidly.

**[VERIFIED]** More than ten weeks after the war in the Middle East began, mounting supply losses from the Strait of Hormuz are depleting global oil inventories at a record pace.

**Assessment:** Oil's $2.85 jump erases yesterday's diplomacy-driven decline and reflects market recognition that negotiations have not produced meaningful progress. The IEA warning about declining inventories adds fundamental pressure. The pattern is clear: prices fall on diplomatic hopes, then rebound when those hopes prove premature.

### 5. Lebanon Death Toll Exceeds 3,000 Despite Ceasefire Extension

**[VERIFIED]** The toll includes 211 children, 292 women and 116 medics. At least 740 people have been killed since the ceasefire was announced, figures by the Health Ministry show.

**[VERIFIED]** Lebanon's Ministry of Health said the number of people killed since the start of Israeli attacks on the country on March 2 had reached at least 3,020, with 9,273 others wounded.

**[VERIFIED]** On 15 May, the truce was extended for another 45 days.

**[VERIFIED]** Israeli attacks across Lebanon have killed at least seven people despite a United States-backed ceasefire extension. The attacks on Monday came three days after Lebanese and Israeli officials held talks in Washington, DC, which resulted in an agreement to extend their ceasefire for another 45 days. Israel has repeatedly violated the truce, which was initially agreed in April.

**[VERIFIED]** The war has precipitated a still-developing humanitarian crisis, resulted in deaths of more than 3,000 people (both militants and civilians) from Israeli strikes in Lebanon, seen the forced displacement of over 1 million (>20% of the country's population), and entailed the deployment of matériel and tactics that potentially constitute war crimes or crimes against humanity.

**Assessment:** The ceasefire remains nominal at best. With 740 deaths since the ceasefire was announced and ongoing daily strikes, this is a procedural arrangement, not a peace. The 45-day extension provides diplomatic cover but no substantive change on the ground.

### 6. Iran-Oman Technical Talks on Hormuz Transit Continue

**[VERIFIED]** Iran is in talks with Oman to construct a mechanism to direct commercial shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, Iran's Foreign Ministry said Monday, as Tehran looks to formalize control over the vital waterway. Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei said negotiations with Omani officials are ongoing, adding that the strait is too vital for just one nation to control.

**[VERIFIED]** The spokesman for Iran's Foreign Ministry says the country is engaged in continuous consultations with Oman to design a new mechanism governing transit through the Strait of Hormuz. He said the Strait of Hormuz is a "vital" maritime passage whose importance extends far beyond Iran, Oman, and the regional countries to the entire world. "The Islamic Republic of Iran has always made tremendous efforts to safeguard safe and secure transit through this route."

**[VERIFIED]** Iran's state media reported, citing the Iranian Ministry of Foreign Affairs, that technical teams from Iran and Oman met in Oman to negotiate a mechanism for safe transit in the Strait of Hormuz.

**Assessment:** The Iran-Oman channel represents a parallel diplomatic track that could allow partial normalization of shipping without resolving the broader conflict. If successful, this could provide a model for selective Hormuz reopening—reward partners, maintain control—while the PGSA institutionalizes Iran's permanent claim over the waterway.

### 7. Hormuz Traffic Remains Near Historic Lows

**[VERIFIED]** Traffic through the Strait of Hormuz has been at a near-standstill for weeks amid the Iran war, sending prices of oil soaring, gas prices surging and other key goods up.

**[VERIFIED]** Prior to the outbreak of hostilities, about 138 vessels transited the Strait of Hormuz daily. Since March 1, 2026, marine traffic data shows growing clusters of loitering vessels on both sides of the Strait of Hormuz, with many refraining from transiting due to safety concerns. Since May 6, open transits have fallen to near zero vessels per day.

**[VERIFIED]** The situation was described by The Guardian as a "dual blockade", with the US Navy blockading Iran and Iran blockading the Gulf.

**[REPORTED]** Some reports indicate traffic increased to approximately 55 vessels over May 11-17 from 19 the prior week, but this remains far below pre-war levels of approximately 140 vessels per day.

**Assessment:** Traffic remains at roughly 10% of pre-war levels, with the "dual blockade" dynamic persisting. Iran's PGSA formalization may allow selective increase in compliant traffic, but most commercial operators face an impossible choice between US and Iranian sanctions regimes.

## Emerging Analysis: The Iraq Corridor and Gulf Leverage

**Day 81 reveals two critical dynamics:**

**1. The Iraq-based proxy launch corridor is now officially confirmed.** The drones that targeted the UAE's Barakah nuclear power plant all came from Iraq, likely signaling that Iranian-backed Shiite militias launched the assault. Militias in the past have provided Iran a means by which to deflect blame over such attacks.

This expands the conflict's operational geography. If additional attacks originate from Iraqi territory, it may trigger:
- Counter-drone and counter-militia operations focused on Iraqi infrastructure
- Baghdad-Gulf diplomatic strain
- Potential "attribution laddering" directly to Tehran

**2. Gulf state leverage over US decision-making is now explicit.** Trump publicly acknowledged that Qatar, Saudi Arabia, and the UAE persuaded him to cancel a scheduled attack. This creates a diplomatic opening but also signals to Iran that time may be on its side—if the Gulf states will continue restraining US military action.

Even so, it would seem appetite for direct military engagement is low among Trump officials, as the U.S. has insisted that the cease-fire is on, even if Trump has said it is on "life support." Trump's decision to call off strikes on Monday suggests that, for all his threats, he is still hopeful of a deal that would avoid resuming the war.

## Bottom Line

**Day 81 establishes a new pattern: deadline-driven cycles of threats, pauses, and rejected proposals.** Trump's public acknowledgment that Gulf states restrained his planned attack—combined with his instruction to maintain readiness for "full, large scale assault"—creates a volatile equilibrium. The conflict is neither escalating to full-scale war nor moving toward resolution.

**The Iraq launch corridor is the day's most significant operational development.** The UAE's official attribution of the Barakah attack to Iraqi territory confirms that Iranian-backed militias have opened a sustained drone corridor targeting Gulf infrastructure. This expands the conflict's geography and raises the prospect of counter-militia operations in Iraq.

**Iran's proposal offers nothing fundamentally new—and the sequencing dispute remains the core barrier.** Tehran continues to insist on ending hostilities first, then discussing nuclear issues. The US demands the reverse. The reported uranium transfer to Russia offers a potential face-saving formula, but Trump rejected a similar offer in March, and there's no indication he has changed his position.

**The ceasefire is becoming institutionalized—not as peace, but as managed confrontation.** Iran's PGSA formalizes Hormuz control; Gulf states formalize their role as US restraints; the US maintains readiness without acting. This equilibrium is unstable—any party could break it—but there is currently no obvious catalyst for resolution or decisive escalation.
