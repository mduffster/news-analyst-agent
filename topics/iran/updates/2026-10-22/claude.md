### **NYT/NPR: US-ISRAEL PLANNING POST-MIDTERM STRIKES ON IRAN** [REPORTED]

The U.S. and Israel are planning for possible renewed strikes on Iran after the midterm elections on Nov. 3. The operation would be a short, intense mission that would include strikes on Iran's missile and drone capabilities, nuclear facilities and energy infrastructure.

Iran Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi told Iranian state media on Thursday "we are just as prepared for diplomacy as we are for war." "Any scenario is conceivable including the possibility of an attack by the U.S. and Israel," Araghchi said.

### **MAJOR ESCALATION: 7 TANKERS HIT OVERNIGHT IN STRAIT OF HORMUZ** [VERIFIED]

Seven oil tankers caught fire or were damaged in the Strait of Hormuz overnight after being hit by Iran or striking naval mines, Tehran said Thursday, a markedly high number as tensions rise over concerns about a return to all-out war.

The IRGC, speaking through Iranian state television, said its forces struck four tankers conducting what Tehran calls "unauthorized" ship-to-ship oil transfers in the northern Persian Gulf, while three more vessels hit mines it said it had placed west of the strait.

UKMTO confirmed two. Brent rose 3% to $112.

The claim is unverified: the IRGC supplied no vessel names, flag-state details, operator identities, or casualty figures.

UK Maritime Trade Operations (UKMTO), which tracks attacks on commercial shipping in the region, has not issued new incident reports corresponding to the claims.

**[ANALYSIS]** This is the largest single-night toll claimed since the war began. The gap between IRGC claims (7 vessels) and UKMTO confirmation (2) creates uncertainty — but even the lower figure represents continued escalation. The 7-vessel claim may be Iranian information warfare to maximize economic impact.

### **TRUMP ACCUSES IRAN OF "FAKE HITS" TO MANIPULATE OIL PRICES** [OFFICIAL]

US President Donald Trump on Thursday accused Iran of making up "fake hits, mines, and everything else" to drive up oil prices. "Iran is doing everything they can to drive up the price of Oil prior to the U.S. Midterm Election. They are even making up fake hits, mines, and everything else, because more Oil is coming through the Hormuz Strait now than it did even prior to the beginning of the Conflict," he said in a post on Truth Social.

Available shipping data shows traffic through the strait was running about 70 percent below prewar levels, according to maritime outlet gCaptain. Kpler data cited by geopolitical expert Brett Erickson showed just 2.9 million barrels passed through on Oct. 11, compared with a prewar average of about 17.1 million a day.

**[ANALYSIS]** Trump's claim that Hormuz is busier than pre-war is directly contradicted by shipping data. This appears to be pre-midterm messaging aimed at deflecting blame for high oil prices. The framing suggests Trump is feeling political pressure from energy costs as the election approaches.

### **IRAN'S SHADOW FLEET DEPLETED — $25B IN LOSSES** [VERIFIED]

"For most of the war, Iran had an invaluable cushion: dozens of oil tankers holding millions of barrels of crude sitting safely in waters in Asia. Tehran steadily sold the oil to China, bringing in billions of much-needed cash," the Wall Street Journal reports. "Now, that floating storage is gone. By mid-October, the reserves were fully depleted, according to analysts at Kpler, as a U.S. naval blockade prevented Iran from replacing the crude it normally sells to Chinese refineries."

Iran's shadow fleet oil reserve peaked at nearly 90 million barrels, but plummeted to less than 1 million barrels by Oct. 5, and is now hovering near nothing, according to Kpler. U.S. Central Command estimated on Saturday that the blockade has cost Iran about $25 billion.

"There's no more oil to discharge to China anymore," Homayoun Falakshahi, head of crude-oil analysis at Kpler, told The Wall Street Journal.

**[ANALYSIS]** This is a significant economic development. Iran's offshore reserves were a critical financial cushion; their depletion means Tehran is now fully dependent on whatever domestic reserves and revenue it can generate under blockade conditions. This increases economic pressure but may also increase Iran's incentive to escalate.
