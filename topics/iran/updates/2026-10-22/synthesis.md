## 1. US-Israel Post-Midterm Strike Plans Confirmed Across Multiple Sources [REPORTED]

The United States and Israel have drawn up plans for renewed military strikes on Iran following the American midterm elections on November 3, the New York Times reports. Citing US officials and others briefed on the matter, the report says the US and Israel have ramped up military drills ahead of what it describes as "a short but intensive operation," including flyovers of Iran's western neighbor Iraq to simulate strike maneuvers.

Possible targets are Iranian energy sites, nuclear facilities as well as Iran's arsenals of missiles and drones, with officials quoted by the newspaper claiming another major round of strikes could crater the Islamic Republic's economy and in doing so force it to make diplomatic concessions.

Military planners are targeting hardened sites such as Pickaxe Mountain and have discussed using the 22,000-pound MOAB bomb, though specialists doubt its ability to penetrate granite-encased facilities. The planned three-day joint military campaign is designed to target underground nuclear facilities, ballistic missile systems, and state infrastructure.

American officials say they wish to avoid oil refineries so as not to provoke a spike in global fuel prices. Trump has ruled out strikes before November 3, citing ongoing diplomatic talks and a wish to spare voters an energy price shock before the midterms. Three aircraft carrier strike groups, Marines and specialised aircraft will not be fully in position until early November, when longer nights over Iran also favour stealth operations.

According to the person briefed, the U.S. is now amassing forces in the region to be ready to attack by early November, after the U.S. midterm elections, but that these preparations could also be part of a campaign to pressure Iran to reach a diplomatic deal.

The report also says there is a small chance that US President Donald Trump could go ahead with the strikes already this month, while also noting he could ultimately decide not to authorize any military action against Iran.

**Netanyahu's Denial:** Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu on Thursday denied a Financial Times report that the Jewish state recently planned to attack Iran but was stopped by U.S. President Donald Trump. "This report is not true," Netanyahu said in an interview with Israel National News. According to Netanyahu, he made clear to his advisers that he intended to inform Trump of Israel's operational plans rather than request American permission to proceed. During a meeting with Trump, Netanyahu presented Israel's military preparations and offered Washington an opportunity to participate in the operation, particularly in targeting Iran's heavily fortified Fordow nuclear facility.

**Analysis:** The independent confirmation from NYT and NPR — combined with the detailed three-day operational framework — establishes that planning for post-election strikes is real and advanced, regardless of whether the FT's specific claim about Trump blocking October 10 operations is accurate. The force buildup timeline (three carriers by end of November) aligns with this window. Netanyahu's denial may reflect political positioning rather than substantive disagreement about the trajectory.

## 2. Largest Single-Day Tanker Attack: IRGC Claims Seven Vessels Hit [REPORTED]

Seven oil tankers caught fire or were damaged in the Strait of Hormuz overnight after being hit by Iran or striking naval mines, Tehran said Thursday, a markedly high number as tensions rise over concerns about a return to all-out war.

If verified, it would be the largest single-day number of vessels damaged in the waterway since the start of the Iran war on Feb. 28 when the United States and Israel launched joint strikes against Iran. Quoting a statement by the Revolutionary Guard's navy, the Iranian report said that four of the tankers were transferring oil when they were targeted and caught fire. Another three were damaged and immobilized when they hit naval mines.

The IRGC added in its statement that some of the vessels remained ablaze as of Thursday morning.

UK Maritime Trade Operations (UKMTO), which tracks attacks on commercial shipping in the region, has not issued new incident reports corresponding to the claims. For now, neither Tehran's claim that seven vessels were attacked nor Trump's assertion that the incidents were fabricated has been independently established.

**IRGC Warning to US:** Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps threatens to strike American bases across the Middle East if the US military continues to escort ships through the Strait of Hormuz. "The US military must know that if this trend continues, the conflict will extend to its bases in the region," the Guards say in a statement on their Sepah News website. "This is a serious warning," they add.

Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council, expanded the threat: "The US has valuable assets in the region. In case of war, cables, data centers and AI facilities could also come within range. If the conflict starts again, we will have a broader plan."

**Analysis:** The gap between IRGC claims (7 vessels) and UKMTO confirmation creates information uncertainty, but even partial verification represents major escalation. The explicit IRGC threat against US bases — combined with Rezaei's expansion to include data centers and cables — signals Tehran is preparing escalation options if strikes resume. The threat is designed to raise costs for Washington's protection operations.

## 3. Trump Accuses Iran of "Fake Hits" — Contradicted by Shipping Data [OFFICIAL/DISPUTED]

US President Donald Trump on Thursday accused Iran of making up "fake hits, mines, and everything else" to drive up oil prices. "They are even making up fake hits, mines, and everything else, because more Oil is coming through the Hormuz Strait now than it did even prior to the beginning of the Conflict," he said in a post on Truth Social.

**This claim is directly contradicted by available data.** As documented in prior reporting, the mine blockade has cut daily tanker transits from 85 to 4 according to IMF PortWatch data, with Kpler shipping data putting the throughput decline at 86 percent. What is clear is that merchant ships continue to face serious threats, even as oil producers find increasingly costly ways to keep cargoes moving.

**Analysis:** Trump's claim that Hormuz traffic exceeds pre-war levels appears designed for domestic political messaging ahead of the midterms rather than accurate assessment. The disconnect between presidential statements and verified shipping data creates uncertainty about whether Washington's policy is grounded in operational reality or electoral calculation.

## 4. Iran's Shadow Fleet Reserves Completely Depleted — $25 Billion in Losses [VERIFIED]

"For most of the war, Iran had an invaluable cushion: dozens of oil tankers holding millions of barrels of crude sitting safely in waters in Asia. Tehran steadily sold the oil to China, bringing in billions of much-needed cash," the Wall Street Journal reports. "Now, that floating storage is gone. By mid-October, the reserves were fully depleted, according to analysts at Kpler, as a U.S. naval blockade prevented Iran from replacing the crude it normally sells to Chinese refineries."

Iran's shadow fleet oil reserve peaked at nearly 90 million barrels, but plummeted to less than 1 million barrels by Oct. 5, and is now hovering near nothing, according to Kpler. U.S. Central Command estimated on Saturday that the blockade has cost Iran about $25 billion.

"There's no more oil to discharge to China anymore," Kpler's crude-oil analysis head Homayoun Falakshahi said.

"The drop in oil revenue is a further blow to Iran's struggling economy, already facing runaway inflation and widespread shortages."

**Analysis:** This represents a critical economic milestone. The depletion of offshore reserves removes Iran's financial cushion and means Tehran is now fully dependent on whatever domestic reserves and revenue it can generate under blockade conditions. This increases pressure on the regime but may also increase its incentive to escalate — the middle ground between capitulation and confrontation is narrowing.

## 5. Saudi Aramco Evacuates Headquarters — First Time Since War Began [VERIFIED]

Saudi Aramco evacuated its headquarters in Dhahran after an alarm on Thursday and staff have been asked to work from home, according to people familiar with the situation. The kingdom is on alert after a flurry of attacks from Yemen's Houthis in recent weeks, including lethal strikes on Aramco operations.

Reuters, citing six sources familiar with the matter, reported that it was the first time since the resumption of the war in Yemen in September 2026 that the Saudi oil giant had instructed staff to leave its main headquarters.

Two people died after the Shaybah oil field was targeted on Wednesday, and two more were killed at the airport in the eastern city of Dammam.

Three sources familiar with the matter, speaking on condition of anonymity for security reasons, said that most employees had already been working remotely since an attack targeted the company's headquarters last week. Only essential personnel had continued working on site before the latest directive ordered the complete evacuation of the premises as a precautionary measure.

The Houthis on Thursday claimed to have carried out numerous attacks on Saudi infrastructure over the last 48 hours, including targeting King Fahd International Airport. Other targets included Abha International Airport, the Saudi defence ministry in Riyadh and facilities belonging to Saudi Aramco in Yanbu, according to the Houthis.

**Houthi Campaign Toll:** In October 2026, the Houthis in Yemen launched a series of ballistic missile and drone attacks on three airports in Saudi Arabia, killing 21 people and injuring more than 370 others. It is the deadliest series of attacks in the country since the Qatif and Dammam mosque bombings in May 2015, and is the deadliest of the attacks on the Gulf Arab states since the beginning of the 2026 Iran war.

Saudi Arabia's General Authority of Civil Aviation says that the number of people killed in an attack on King Fahd International Airport in the eastern city of Dammam has risen to two – a Saudi national and an Indian resident.

**Analysis:** The Aramco evacuation is a powerful symbol of Houthi pressure on Saudi critical infrastructure. With 21 dead and 370+ injured in October's airport campaign, the Houthis have demonstrated sustained capability to threaten Saudi strategic assets. This pressure is likely accelerating regional coalition-building.

## 6. US Orders Diplomatic Departure from Saudi Arabia [OFFICIAL]

The United States Department of State has ordered the departure of nonemergency government employees and their family members from its mission in Saudi Arabia as attacks by the Iran-aligned Houthi movement on the kingdom have ramped up. "Iranian-supported Houthis have increasingly engaged in hostilities against Saudi Arabia, including attacks on civilian airports and other critical infrastructure resulting in civilian casualties and severe travel disruptions," State Department spokesperson Tommy Pigott said.

The embassy had been badly damaged at the start of the Iran war and had been operating with only a skeleton staff for months, although some diplomats had returned in recent months.

In a new travel alert, the department said, "Iranian-supported Houthis have engaged in hostilities against Saudi Arabia, including attacks on civilian airports and other critical infrastructure resulting in civilian casualties and severe travel disruptions. The hostilities have the potential to escalate rapidly beyond that immediate area."

Four US officials told Reuters that US missions in other Gulf states would also be ordering family members to leave.

**Analysis:** The diplomatic drawdown signals Washington's assessment that the Saudi security situation is deteriorating. The phrase "potential to escalate rapidly" suggests US intelligence anticipates further intensification.

## 7. Oil Prices Spike to Five-Month High [VERIFIED]

Brent rose to 111.97 USD/Bbl on October 22, 2026, up 2.90% from the previous day. Over the past month, Brent's price has risen 8.63%, and is up 71.50% compared to the same time last year.

Brent crude tops $113, highest level since May, after Trump hints at action on Iran. Brent crude futures rose 4% to $113.25 a barrel as of 1:54 p.m. ET.

By 8 a.m. Eastern Time today, oil had reached $113.21 per barrel, measured using the Brent benchmark. That's $2.47 more than it cost yesterday morning and about $49 above its price a year earlier.

The national average price for a gallon of gas was $4.27.

**Analysis:** The ~3% single-day spike reflects both the 7-tanker claims and Saudi attack fears. At $113+, Brent approaches May highs. The volatility creates political pressure for Trump ahead of the midterms while also increasing the economic burden on Iran's adversaries.

## 8. Egyptian Parliament Vote Remains Scheduled for October 26 [REPORTED]

Egypt's parliament has delayed a vote on authorising its armed forces to support Saudi Arabia, which has come under increasing attack from the Iran-aligned Houthis in Yemen. The vote has been adjourned to October 26, the House of Representatives said.

The postponement is significant, given that parliament, dominated by pro-government forces, routinely approves major executive decisions without prolonged debate. This time, MPs held consecutive sessions without reaching a vote before postponing it for another week.

Lawmaker Mostafa Bakry said on X that the weeklong delay in the vote was tied to Egyptian efforts to broker a political settlement in Yemen.

**Analysis:** The unusual delay suggests Egyptian leadership is balancing military intervention against diplomatic options. The October 26 vote — one day before Israel's election — could align with broader regional coordination decisions.
