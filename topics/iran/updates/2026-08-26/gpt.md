## Daily incremental update — US–Israel–Iran conflict  
**As of Wed, Aug 26, 2026 (ET)**  
**New since Tue, Aug 25, 2026 (ET)**

### 1) Oman channel produced a concrete (if narrow) Hormuz framework — **with a major catch: exclusion of military vessels**
- Iran’s deputy foreign minister **Kazem Gharibabadi** said Iran and Oman reached an **understanding on a new framework** for managing traffic in the Strait of Hormuz that would **exclude military vessels**, with inbound commercial traffic routed entirely through Iranian waters and outbound split between Iranian and Omani waters. **[REPORTED]** ([apnews.com](https://apnews.com/article/cb19bc3f0b467db591edbef52d0db273?utm_source=openai))  
- Multiple outlets also describe this as a **temporary corridor / route** alongside continued **technical negotiations** (not a full reopening). **[REPORTED]** ([investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/commodities-news/iran-and-oman-discuss-temporary-hormuz-corridor-as-impasse-with-us-drags-on-4876021?utm_source=openai))  
- Iran’s IRGC messaging (as relayed by Anadolu) indicates the Strait **will not be “reopened”** until the U.S. meets Iran’s conditions / “commitments” tied to the earlier MoU framing. **[REPORTED]** ([aa.com.tr](https://www.aa.com.tr/en/middle-east/hormuz-strait-to-remain-closed-until-us-fulfills-its-commitments-irgc/4038103?utm_source=openai))  

**What changed / what it means:**  
- **Change:** This is the first clearly articulated *operational* routing concept in the open reporting (not just “talks ongoing”). **[REPORTED]** ([apnews.com](https://apnews.com/article/cb19bc3f0b467db591edbef52d0db273?utm_source=openai))  
- **Meaning:** It modestly improves odds of a **commercial deconfliction mechanism**, but the **“no military vessels”** condition is a direct collision point with U.S./allied posture (escorts, mine countermeasures, ISR). Net effect: a **partial off-ramp for commerce** that can still **lock in strategic confrontation** over who gets to police the waterway.

### 2) U.S. sanctions campaign: new emphasis is the **China constraint** (enforcement credibility question)
- AP reporting today frames “Operation Economic Outcast” as facing a major challenge: **China**, with Beijing asserting its Iran cooperation is within international law. **[REPORTED]** ([apnews.com](https://apnews.com/article/dc38c3673332a6fe3f45c042eca83aca?utm_source=openai))  
- Treasury’s own rollout states it is **expanding categories of Iran-related conduct** that may be subject to **secondary sanctions** and says OFAC issued **additional guidance** tied to shipping risks/pressures in the Strait of Hormuz. **[OFFICIAL]** ([home.treasury.gov](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0613?utm_source=openai))  

**What changed / what it means:**  
- **Change:** The story is shifting from “big designation batch” to “can the U.S. actually coerce major enablers, especially China?” **[REPORTED]** ([apnews.com](https://apnews.com/article/dc38c3673332a6fe3f45c042eca83aca?utm_source=openai))  
- **Meaning:** If Washington avoids early high-profile secondary-sanctions against major China-linked nodes, the campaign may look **selective** (lower deterrence). If it *does* move hard against China-facing channels, that increases risks of **broader economic/geopolitical blowback**—either way reinforcing a **long-duration contest** rather than quick resolution.

- **Meaning:** This sustains the “gray-zone maritime” baseline, which is highly compatible with the emerging **corridor bargaining**: violence/harassment as leverage while diplomats negotiate routing/fees/controls.
