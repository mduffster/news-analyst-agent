## Critical Developments (August 26)

### 1. BREAKTHROUGH: Iran-Oman Hormuz Corridor Framework [OFFICIAL]

[OFFICIAL] Iran and Oman's top diplomats discussed a proposed framework for a temporary transit corridor through the Strait of Hormuz and a mine-clearing project in the key waterway.

[OFFICIAL] The two "discussed a phased framework that could provide a practical and implementable basis for moving forward." The proposed framework "includes the establishment of a temporary joint navigation corridor through the Strait of Hormuz and an agreement to implement a joint project to clear the Strait of mines."

[OFFICIAL] "Technical negotiations between the two sides will also continue with the aim of reaching an agreement on a permanent navigation corridor and future arrangements for managing the Strait, alongside establishing a mechanism for exchanging information, managing navigation traffic, and providing related navigational and security services."

[OFFICIAL] Omani FM Albusaidi wrote on X: "I am hopeful we will soon announce a temporary corridor for the Strait of Hormuz."

[REPORTED] According to Iranian Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi, "Traffic entering the Persian Gulf from the Gulf of Oman would pass through Iranian waters as outbound traffic would pass waters belonging to either Iran or Oman." For a 30- to 60-day period, both countries will hold negotiations to "reach an understanding on a new permanent and sustainable route."

[OFFICIAL] The Omani FM referenced Article 5 of the defunct Islamabad MoU, suggesting this builds on the June framework rather than replacing it.

**Assessment:** [VERIFIED] This is the most significant diplomatic development since the June MoU collapsed. The framework includes concrete elements: temporary corridor, joint mine-clearing, 30-60 day timeline for permanent arrangements, and technical talks on traffic management. However, key questions remain: (1) How does this interact with the US blockade? (2) Will the IRGC accept the terms? (3) What about military vessel transit? [REPORTED] One source indicates Iran wants to exclude military vessels from the corridor — a potential collision point with US/allied operations.

### 2. Trump Claims All Mines Cleared — BUT Maritime Authorities Disagree [DISPUTED]

[OFFICIAL] "I have just been informed by the United States Navy that all mines have been removed and/or detonated from within the International Waters of the Strait of Hormuz," Trump said on Truth Social.

[OFFICIAL] Trump said Iran had been warned that "any ship or boat placing new mines will be immediately and systematically destroyed," adding that a "Zero Tolerance" policy on mine placement was in effect.

[REPORTED] A US official confirmed to The Jerusalem Post that both inbound and outbound shipping lanes have been cleared of mines by CENTCOM.

**HOWEVER:**

[VERIFIED] The Joint Maritime Information Center stated there "remained a continued risk of drifting or uncharted mines in and near the TSS, with mine danger areas still active" and that "commercial traffic through the Strait of Hormuz remained at reduced levels."

[VERIFIED] JMIC continues to warn of mines despite Trump's claim. In its advisory: "There remained a continued risk of drifting or uncharted mines in and near the TSS, with mine danger areas still active."

[VERIFIED] This marks the second time in a week Trump has declared the mine threat eliminated — on August 18, Trump said the Strait of Hormuz was open and that "all water mines" had been removed or detonated.

**Assessment:** [DISPUTED] Trump's claims and JMIC's ongoing warnings are directly contradictory. The fact that the Iran-Oman framework specifically includes a "joint project to clear the Strait of mines" suggests both parties acknowledge mines remain. The most likely reality: US forces may have cleared mines from international waters/shipping lanes, but hazards persist in the broader area. JMIC's SEVERE threat level remains in effect.

### 5. Pakistan Reports "Significant Progress" — Mediation Continues [OFFICIAL]

[OFFICIAL] Pakistan said it had made "significant progress" in talks with Iran aimed at reviving an Islamabad-brokered agreement with the United States. Pakistan's army chief Field Marshal Syed Asim Munir and Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi held a series of meetings with Iran's leadership in Tehran as Islamabad seeks to restore the interim peace agreement it helped the two sides reach in June.

[OFFICIAL] Talks between the two sides focused on "measures to prevent further escalation, reopening of the Strait of Hormuz and an expedited termination of the conflict." The two sides "also exchanged views on regional peace and the way to arrive at a negotiated settlement to the dispute."

[OFFICIAL] "Significant progress was made, and the meeting concluded on a highly positive note," Interior Minister Naqvi said on X after meeting Iranian President Pezeshkian.

[OFFICIAL] Iran's response: the visit was "highly productive" and yielded "significant diplomatic achievements, the results of which will become apparent soon." Abbas Golrou, chairman of the Iranian parliament's Foreign Relations Committee, said Munir carried a message from the US.

[REPORTED] Analyst Andreas Krieg of King's College London noted that "Qatar is leading the political mediation and remains the essential communication bridge between Washington and Tehran." Munir's contribution is a "military-to-military channel into an Iranian system in which the IRGC and wider security establishment have become increasingly important to decisions about war and peace."

**Assessment:** [OFFICIAL] Both Pakistan and Iran describing the visit as yielding "significant progress"/"highly productive" with results to "become apparent soon" represents the most positive diplomatic signal in weeks. The military-to-military nature of Munir's channel may be particularly relevant given IRGC dominance in Iranian decision-making and questions about Khamenei's capacity.
