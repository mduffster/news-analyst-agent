## Key Developments Since Prior Report (August 25)

### 1. Iran-Oman Announce Hormuz Corridor Framework — MAJOR DEVELOPMENT

**[OFFICIAL]** Iran and Oman outlined a proposal Tuesday to jointly establish a temporary shipping channel through the Strait of Hormuz. Tehran and Muscat discussed a "phased framework" for a joint project facilitating a "temporary shipping corridor" and to "clear mines."

**[OFFICIAL]** Officials will carry on talks "with the aim of reaching agreement on a permanent shipping corridor and the future administration of the Strait," the statement added.

**[OFFICIAL]** After the meeting, the Omani foreign minister said he was "hopeful" both nations would "soon announce a temporary corridor" for the strait.

**[VERIFIED]** Technical negotiations between the two sides would continue with a view to agreeing on a permanent navigational corridor and future administration of the Strait, as well as a mechanism for information-sharing, traffic management, and the provision of relevant navigational and security services.

**[VERIFIED]** Both sides underscored the importance of convening joint discussions with regional states bordering the waters of the Persian Gulf.

**[REPORTED]** Iran and Oman will hold further talks to "negotiate a new permanent route within 30 to 60 days" for shipping through the vital waterway, according to Iranian Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi.

*Assessment: This is the most concrete diplomatic progress on Hormuz since the June MoU collapsed. The joint mine-clearing commitment and 30-60 day timeline for permanent arrangements suggests both parties are serious. However, implementation remains uncertain.*

### 2. Trump: "All Mines Removed" — BUT MINE DANGER PERSISTS

**[OFFICIAL]** "I have just been informed by the United States Navy that all mines have been removed and/or detonated from within the International Waters of the Strait of Hormuz," Trump said Tuesday on Truth Social.

**[OFFICIAL]** Trump also said Iran had been warned that "any ship or boat placing new mines will be immediately and systematically destroyed," adding that a "Zero Tolerance" policy on mine placement was in effect.

**[VERIFIED]** The Joint Maritime Information Center said in an advisory Tuesday that there remains a "continued risk of drifting or uncharted mines in and near the TSS, with mine danger areas still active." JMIC also said clearance and mine-surveying operations continue throughout the Strait and maintained its SEVERE threat assessment.

**[REPORTED]** Oman's reference to a mine-clearing mission appeared to contradict Trump's statement, according to CNBC.

*Assessment: Trump's claim and Oman's proposed mine-clearing mission are contradictory. JMIC's continued SEVERE threat assessment suggests the reality lies between these positions — some mines may have been cleared from international waters, but the overall strait remains hazardous.*

**[VERIFIED]** The younger Khamenei — who took over after his father was killed in US-Israeli strikes that started the war in February — has not been seen in public since, sparking wide speculation as to his fate.

**[VERIFIED]** Khamenei was injured in the airstrike that killed his father, and his appointment was followed by a prolonged absence from public view, causing confusion over his fate.

### 5. Pakistan Reports "Significant Progress" — Mediation Continues

**[OFFICIAL]** There has been significant progress during high-level talks in Tehran aimed at ending the US-Israel war on Iran, according to Pakistani officials. The talks took place on Monday during a daylong diplomatic mission to Tehran by Pakistan's army chief, Field Marshal Syed Asim Munir, and Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi.

**[OFFICIAL]** While in Tehran, Munir held meetings with several Iranian leaders, including President Masoud Pezeshkian, Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi and Supreme National Security Council Secretary Mohsen Rezaei.

**[REPORTED]** The trip by the Pakistani delegation is the second of its kind this month after Mr. Munir traveled to Tehran in early August to revive peace talks and push for a return to the Islamabad memorandum of understanding. Munir and Araghchi have also held at least two phone calls in the lead-up to this week's meetings.

**[REPORTED]** "There is a trust deficit on both sides," a third Pakistani source said about the U.S. and Iran, adding that Munir's aim was to mitigate the lack of ​mutual trust.

*Assessment: Pakistan's optimism about "significant progress" combined with the Iran-Oman corridor announcement suggests coordinated diplomatic movement. Munir's third visit this year and direct communication with both Trump and Iranian leadership positions Pakistan as the key backchannel.*
