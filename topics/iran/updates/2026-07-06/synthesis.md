### 2. Funeral Procession Underway — Tehran Airspace Closed [VERIFIED]

The funeral procession for Iranian Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei is under way in Tehran as authorities have hailed the government's resilience during the United States-Israel war on Iran and promised vengeance for his assassination. After lying in state for two days at Tehran's Grand Mosalla religious complex, Khamenei's body began a 12-hour journey on Monday through the capital, accompanied by large crowds of mourners.

The largest crowds yet are expected when the main funeral procession in Tehran begins at 6 a.m. local time. The procession, which includes funeral prayers, will follow the 10-kilometer (6.2-mile) distance from Imam Hossein Square to Azadi Square.

While Tehran is filled with mourners today, analysts — and even senior Iranian officials — caution that the crowd size cannot be equated to a popular endorsement of continued theocratic rule. "If anyone's thinking this is a litmus test for the popularity of the Islamic Republic, history tells us otherwise. It's a funeral, and Iranians do funerals very well," says Ali Ansari, professor of modern history at St. Andrews University in Scotland.

### 3. NEW: OPEC+ Announces 188,000 bpd Production Increase for August [OFFICIAL]

OPEC+ said on Sunday that seven member countries – Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, Kuwait, Kazakhstan, Algeria, and Oman – would raise output by 188,000 barrels per day from August after officials held a virtual meeting to "review global market conditions and outlook".

The production boost is the fifth consecutive increase announced by the OPEC+ members in as many months, continuing a gradual unwinding of production cuts announced in 2023.

Total OPEC+ production dropped to 33.13 million bpd in May, down from 42.77 million bpd in February, according to OPEC figures.

**Assessment:** The continued production increases signal OPEC+ confidence that Hormuz recovery will proceed. However, the reality gap is significant — total production fell nearly 10 million bpd during the war. The 188,000 bpd increase is largely symbolic while actual export capacity remains constrained by operational realities.

### 5. NEW: Iran-Qatar Maritime Trade Resumes [VERIFIED]

Maritime trade between Iran and Qatar has resumed after a roughly five-month suspension, Iran's commercial attaché in Doha told state media on Sunday. An interim deal between Tehran and Washington signed last month announced the end of hostilities after a four-month conflict and mandated a return to pre-war maritime traffic in the Gulf, although transit in and out of the Gulf remains contested. Abbas Abdolkhani said shipping between Iran's Dayyer port and Qatar's Al Ruwais port had resumed following coordination between the Iranian embassy in Doha and Qatari authorities.

In a statement posted on X on Sunday, Qatar's Ministry of Transport said maritime navigation had resumed for all types of vessels and ships from the date of the announcement. The ministry urged maritime operators to continue complying with existing regulations and safety instructions to ensure secure voyages.

Dayyer port was hit several times during the war. In late June, an official from the Trade Promotion Organisation of Iran told state media that Iranian goods were finally being cleared at the United Arab Emirates' Jebel Ali Port, the largest in the region, pointing to a gradual return of trade between the Gulf's two sides.

**Assessment:** This is a significant positive signal — selective bilateral arrangements are working even as the broader Hormuz governance dispute remains unresolved. The Iran-Qatar route is intra-Gulf (no Hormuz transit required), demonstrating that localized deals can bypass the chokepoint dispute. This could serve as a template for gradual normalization.

### 6. Strait of Hormuz: Volatile Traffic, Continued IRGC Pressure [VERIFIED]

Overall traffic through the Strait of Hormuz has remained "steady" over the past few days but doesn't appear to be increasing, particularly with regard to the southern Omani route, according to a maritime security authority.

On 4 July, 15 vessels transited inbound and 10 outbound through the Strait of Hormuz — 25 in total, with a further six vessels diverting or reversing course in the southern corridor (two diverted north, four turned back) following a reported IRGC VHF warning. Inbound traffic was tanker-heavy (6 of 15) with a notable Iranian-flagged bulk-carrier cluster; outbound was also tanker-led (6 of 10).

Two tankers diverted from the southern corridor northward, and four additional tankers appeared outbound in the southern corridor before reversing course back into the Gulf, following a reported IRGC Navy VHF radio warning to vessels using the US-backed corridor in the Strait of Hormuz.

Oil and gas shipping along a US-protected corridor in the Strait of Hormuz showed signs of recovering Sunday, a day after a batch of vessels performed unexplained U-turns and detours in the vital energy corridor.

Those are only what's observable, with many ships known to sail through with their transponders off to avoid digital detection. Western navies continue to say that, while traffic continues, the threat risk is "substantial" and that the center of the strait has been mined.

**IMO Evacuation Status:** IMO pauses evacuation in Strait of Hormuz following attack (25 June) — no change since the Ever Lovely incident.

The United Nations' maritime agency said on Friday that it had successfully evacuated about 2,500 stranded seafarers from the Persian Gulf before suspending the operation, after an attack on a commercial vessel exposed uncertainty over who can guarantee safe passage through the Strait of Hormuz. The International Maritime Organization (IMO) said that 115 ships carrying roughly 2,500 crew members had left the Gulf during the first three and a half days of the operation.

**Assessment:** Traffic recovery is real but fragile and contested. The emerging dual-corridor system remains a friction point — Iran continues to assert control via VHF warnings and the southern Omani route sees diversions. The IMO evacuation pause demonstrates that safety guarantees have not been restored.

A meeting with Trump at the White House would be hugely important for Netanyahu as he kicks off his campaign for the October elections in Israel, where polls currently have him trailing.
