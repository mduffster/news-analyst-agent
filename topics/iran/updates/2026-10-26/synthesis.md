# US-Israel-Iran Conflict: Egypt Authorizes Saudi Military Support as Tehran Summit Seeks De-escalation; US Forces Ready to Strike Within 96 Hours

## October 26, 2026 — Day 241

**Assessment:** Two major developments today sharpen the conflict's trajectory in opposite directions. Egypt's parliament approved military support for Saudi Arabia against Houthi attacks, expanding the regional coalition confronting Iran's proxy network. Simultaneously, high-level delegations from Saudi Arabia, Pakistan, and Turkey converged on Tehran in an unprecedented diplomatic push to avert renewed US-Israeli strikes. The juxtaposition captures the current moment: regional states are simultaneously preparing for prolonged conflict while racing to prevent escalation. US forces are positioned to strike Iran within 96 hours if ordered, but Trump has signaled the decision point comes after November 3 midterms. Israel votes tomorrow in an election neither bloc appears likely to win outright. Oil markets eased modestly from Friday's $116.72 peak to ~$115 as immediate strike fears receded, though prices remain 76% above year-ago levels.

## 1. Makkah Pact Delegations in Tehran: The Most Serious Diplomatic Push Since June [VERIFIED]

Top security officials from Saudi Arabia, Pakistan and Turkey met Iranian leaders in Tehran on Monday, an effort to defuse tensions and revive talks aimed at preventing another round of US-Israeli strikes on the Islamic Republic.

"As agreed, Saudi Defence Minister Khalid bin Salman, Pakistani army chief General Asim Munir and Turkish intelligence chief Ibrahim Kalin will participate in this meeting," said Ali Bagheri Kani, deputy secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council. The meeting also included Iran's security chief Mohsen Rezaei.

"The visit comes as part of the continued efforts by the Makkah Joint Defence Agreement countries for de-escalation between parties involved in the ongoing crisis," according to Saudi Deputy Minister for Public Diplomacy Dr. Rayed Krimly. The talks aim to support "the mediation efforts carried out by the State of Qatar and the Islamic Republic of Pakistan."

The meeting was held at Tehran's Saadabad complex, bringing together leading political, defence and intelligence figures from the four countries. Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian urged participants to put shared interests at the centre of regional diplomacy, saying differences could be addressed more effectively through constructive engagement.

**The Makkah Agreement context:** Saudi Arabia, Pakistan and Turkey signed the Makkah Joint Defence Agreement in August 2026. Under the pact, an armed attack against one member is considered an attack against all three, requiring the others to provide assistance. The agreement brings together Saudi Arabia's financial resources and influence in global energy markets, Pakistan's nuclear capabilities and Turkey's advanced defence industry and NATO membership.

This marks the first direct engagement since the Makkah Agreement was signed in August.

**Outcome:** Saudi Arabia termed discussions positive efforts to prevent tensions from escalating further and create conditions for an agreement. No final agreement or joint declaration was detailed following the meeting.

**Analysis:** The presence of Saudi Arabia's defense minister in Tehran — the same kingdom whose airports have been devastated by Houthi attacks — represents extraordinary diplomatic flexibility. That the Makkah Pact signatories chose direct engagement with Iran rather than escalation signals regional powers recognize the danger of the approaching post-midterm decision window. Whether this creates genuine space for de-escalation or merely buys time remains unclear.

## 2. Egypt Parliament Authorizes Military Support for Saudi Arabia [VERIFIED]

Egypt's parliament on Monday voted to authorize President Abdel-Fattah el-Sissi to provide Saudi Arabia with military support in defending the kingdom against attacks by Iran-backed Houthi rebels.

The parliament said in a statement that the vote was meant to give el-Sissi authorization to take necessary measures to back Saudi Arabia in the defense of its territory and sovereignty against an external "aggression."

The measure passed by a two-thirds majority in a closed roll-call session. The vote clears the constitutional threshold set by Article 152, which requires approval from at least 398 of the chamber's 596 members before Egyptian troops can be sent on a combat mission abroad.

The parliament didn't provide a breakdown of the closed-door vote, but the chamber is overwhelmingly dominated by pro-government lawmakers.

**Scope limitations:** Legislators approved military support amid Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia, but Cairo has ruled out strikes on Yemen. Egypt won't provide ground troops, according to a regional official.

**Analysis:** Egypt's authorization significantly expands the anti-Houthi coalition beyond the existing Makkah Pact framework. Even framed defensively, additional Egyptian air defense or naval assets around Saudi territory increases both coalition capabilities and the number of actors that could be drawn into escalation. The Houthis warned earlier that Egyptian involvement would threaten Cairo's "national security and internal stability" — creating another potential vector for retaliation.

## 3. US Forces Ready to Strike Within 96 Hours; Trump Frames "Deal or Finish" Decision [OFFICIAL/REPORTED]

US forces in the Middle East are prepared to launch new, potentially major, military operations against Iran "within 96 hours of receiving" an order from President Donald Trump, according to two US officials.

U.S. President Donald Trump said it's "getting to be decision time" on whether he will make a deal with Tehran or resume attacks as Iranian and Houthi threats against Americans in the Middle East continue to grow.

"I have a decision to make: Do I finish it off, which would be very easy to do, or do I make a deal? That's all," Trump said in an interview with Fox News' Sunday Morning Futures.

Trump has suggested that U.S. strikes on Iran could resume after the Nov. 3 midterm elections and he has sent additional forces, including a third aircraft carrier group, toward the Persian Gulf.

**Force posture:** The USS Theodore Roosevelt carrier strike group left San Diego on Sunday, bound for the region, followed by the Makin Island Marine Expeditionary Force carrying more than 2,000 Marines. By the end of November, three aircraft carriers and two amphibious landing groups will be positioned around Iran.

The USS George Washington is currently receiving large-scale replenishments of long-range cruise missiles for ground targets and aviation bombs designed to destroy fortified underground facilities, according to The Wall Street Journal.

Most people briefed on the planning expect any order to come after the midterms. Enough troops and equipment already sit in the region to act "within 96 hours of receiving" an order from Trump.

**Analysis:** The 96-hour readiness posture and active weapons loading demonstrate this is not deterrence theater — the strike option is operationally mature. Trump's "deal or finish" framing suggests he views this as a binary choice with an approaching deadline. The midterm election on November 3 appears to be the practical hold point, not any diplomatic milestone.

## 4. Israel Election Tomorrow — Neither Bloc Near Majority [VERIFIED]

Israelis vote on October 27, 2026, for the 120-seat 26th Knesset.

Polls show Gadi Eisenkot's new Yashar party and current Prime Minister Binyamin Netanyahu's Likud approximately tied as the largest party, but most polls do not show either camp near 61 seats.

Polling shows Israel's October 27 election will be a very close contest between Netanyahu's Likud and Eisenkot's Yashar, with neither camp near a 61-seat majority in most polls. The result may be decided less by voters switching sides than by turnout and by which small parties clear the 3.25% threshold.

Whatever happens on election night, the real outcome will be decided in the coalition negotiations that follow, and another election cannot be ruled out.

**Recent poll snapshot:** The final polls before the election silence (which began October 23) showed Yashar averaging 22-23 seats, Likud at 19-21 seats, with the opposition bloc averaging around 50-54 seats compared to 49-50 for Netanyahu's allies.

**Analysis:** The likely outcome is prolonged coalition negotiations regardless of which bloc edges ahead. Netanyahu's security-focused campaign — including the West Bank lockdown and regime-change rhetoric — has not moved polls decisively in his favor. An unstable coalition or caretaker government could complicate US-Israel coordination on Iran through the critical post-midterm window.

## 5. West Bank Lockdown Expands: 3,000+ Palestinians Displaced [VERIFIED]

On 23 October 2026, a "full lockdown" of the West Bank was announced by Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu in response to a shooting that killed two Israeli soldiers. The Israel Defense Forces then announced the closure of the Hebron area, and began a large-scale operation, resulting in the widespread destruction of infrastructure and the displacement of at least 2,200 Palestinians.

More than 3,000 Palestinians have been forced from their homes in the occupied West Bank city of Hebron as the Israeli army escalates a sweeping military operation.

Israel says it will close all land crossings with the occupied West Bank on the day of its general election this coming week.

Netanyahu announced that the IDF was preparing to conduct its largest military operation in the territory since the Six-Day War in 1967.

**Analysis:** The displacement figure has grown from 2,200 to over 3,000 in 72 hours. The operation continues to serve dual security and political purposes with the election tomorrow. The closure of crossings on election day — ostensibly for security — also prevents Palestinian workers from entering Israel, adding economic pressure.

## 6. Houthi Campaign: 86% of Riyadh Flights Cancelled, 22 Killed This Month [VERIFIED]

Attacks by Yemen's Iran-aligned Houthis have nearly emptied the skies over Riyadh this month, with cancellations grounding almost nine out of every 10 scheduled flights at the Saudi capital's airport. About 86% of scheduled flights at Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport, or 4,071 out of 4,736, were cancelled between October 8 and October 25.

In October 2026, the Houthis launched a series of ballistic missile and drone attacks on three airports in Saudi Arabia, killing 22 people and injuring more than 380 others.

An attack on King Fahd International Airport in Dammam killed two people — a Saudi national and an Indian resident — on October 21.

The Houthis have stepped up attacks on Saudi Arabia this month, targeting airports, ports and oil facilities with ballistic missiles and drones.

**Analysis:** The Houthi campaign has achieved strategic paralysis of Saudi commercial aviation despite Saudi intercepts and coalition strikes. This sustained disruption — with civilian casualties including a US citizen — increases pressure for both escalation (to "end" the threat) and deal-making (to stop the bleeding). The 86% cancellation rate demonstrates that Saudi air defenses, while intercepting most projectiles, cannot prevent operational disruption.

## 7. Oil Markets Ease from Peak; Structural Concerns Remain [VERIFIED]

Brent fell to 114.53 USD/Bbl on October 26, 2026, down 1.87% from the previous day. Over the past month, Brent's price has risen 17.07%, and is up 76.48% compared to the same time last year.

As of 8:15 a.m. Eastern Time today, oil sold for $115.41 per barrel (using Brent as the benchmark). That's 23 cents lower than yesterday morning and approximately a $49.50 rise over the past year.

Brent oil is expected to trade at 120.58 USD/BBL by the end of this quarter, according to Trading Economics global macro models and analysts expectations. Looking forward, it is estimated to trade at 138.56 in 12 months time.

**Analysis:** The modest pullback from Friday's $116.72 close reflects markets processing Trump's "no strike today" (October 25) and the Tehran diplomacy, rather than fundamental reassurance about supply. At $115, Brent remains at levels that create significant domestic political pressure in the US ahead of midterms while straining global consumers. Market expectations of $120+ by quarter-end and $138 in 12 months suggest analysts see sustained conflict premium.

1. **Regional diplomacy intensifies as force posture matures.** The Saudi defense minister meeting Iranian officials in Tehran while US carriers load bunker-busters captures the moment: maximum diplomatic and military pressure simultaneously. Neither track has yielded results.

2. **The decision point is November 3, not any diplomatic milestone.** Trump's "deal or finish" framing and 96-hour readiness posture indicate the midterm election is the practical constraint on action. Diplomatic progress would need to materialize within eight days to change this calculus.
