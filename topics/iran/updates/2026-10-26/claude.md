### **TRUMP RULES OUT IMMEDIATE STRIKE, FRAMES "DEAL OR FINISH" DECISION** [OFFICIAL]

US President Donald Trump ruled out an immediate military strike on Iran on October 25, easing concerns over a near-term escalation of the conflict as oil prices fell. Asked by reporters whether the United States would strike Iran, Trump said, "The answer is no," adding, "There will not be a strike today."

President Donald Trump said Sunday that he is nearing a decision on whether to reach a deal with Iran or launch another round of military strikes. "Do I make a deal with them or do I finish them off? That's the only question," Trump said during an interview on Fox News' *Sunday Morning Futures*.

However, Trump has not ruled out further military action. Speaking at a rally in Fort Myers, Florida, on Friday, he said, "We may do another little Epic Fury."

**[ANALYSIS]** This walks back the Barrack "this weekend" comments from October 23. The strike timeline now appears to reset to post-midterms (Nov 3), though Trump's "deal or finish" framing keeps both options live. Markets reacted with modest relief.

### **SAUDI, PAKISTAN, TURKEY DELEGATIONS IN TEHRAN TODAY** [VERIFIED]

High-level delegations from Saudi Arabia, Pakistan and Turkey were in Tehran on Monday for meetings with Iranian authorities to try to avert renewed fighting between Iran and Israel and the United States, two regional officials said.

Saudi Defense Minister Khalid bin Salman, Pakistani army chief Field Marshal Asim Munir and Ibrahim Kalin, the head of Turkey's intelligence service, met with Iran's top security officials and negotiators to push for a deal between Tehran and Washington.

Qatari mediators were in Tehran on Sunday, working in coordination with the U.S., as part of ongoing efforts to de-escalate regional tensions, according to a regional official speaking on condition of anonymity due to the sensitivity of the talks.

**[ANALYSIS]** This is the most significant diplomatic push since the Istanbul 8-nation summit on Oct 23. Having Saudi Arabia's defense minister in Tehran alongside Pakistani and Turkish officials represents a coordinated regional effort to prevent renewed strikes. The timing — one week before midterms and the day before Israeli elections — suggests urgency.

### **IRAN REJECTS REPORTS OF NEW PROPOSALS** [OFFICIAL]

On the diplomatic track, Iranian media, relayed by ANI News, deny that Tehran made any offer to the United States or sent a proposal through a Pakistan mediator on reopening the strait; no talks involving named parties on both sides are reported in this window.

Iran remains open to U.S. talks under a Pakistan-brokered interim deal reached in June to end the conflict, according to Tehran's ambassador to Islamabad, accusing Washington of undermining diplomacy and prolonging the standoff over the Strait of Hormuz.

Iranian officials, including Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi, have repeatedly stated that Tehran remains open to diplomacy but insists on the full implementation of prior commitments and its legitimate conditions before any reopening of the waterway or return to broader talks.

**[ANALYSIS]** Iran's position appears unchanged: it insists on implementing the Islamabad Memorandum framework (June 2026) and rejects what it characterizes as US "surrender demands." The gap between the two sides remains significant.

### **ISRAEL ELECTION TOMORROW — NEITHER BLOC NEAR MAJORITY** [VERIFIED]

Just two days before Israelis head to the polls, the four latest election surveys show a similar picture: Gadi Eisenkot's Yashar party leads Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's Likud, but neither of the two major political blocs has enough seats to secure a majority in the 120-seat Knesset.

Ahead of the election for Israel's 26th Knesset, the average of the four latest polls shows Eisenkot's Yashar holding a lead of just over two seats over Netanyahu's Likud. Across the four polls, Yashar averages 22 seats, compared with 19.75 for Likud.

Most polls put Netanyahu's bloc, made up of Likud, two far-right parties led by Bezalel Smotrich and Itamar Ben-Gvir, and two ultra-Orthodox parties, at 49 to 55 seats, short of the 61-seat majority.

Across the four polls, the opposition parties average 54 seats, compared with 49.5 for Netanyahu's bloc. The Arab parties average 14.5 seats combined.

**[ANALYSIS]** The result may be decided less by voters switching sides than by turnout and by which small parties clear the 3.25% threshold. A hung parliament with prolonged coalition negotiations appears likely regardless of outcome.

### **WEST BANK LOCKDOWN INTENSIFIES** [VERIFIED]

On 23 October 2026, a "full lockdown" of the West Bank was announced by Israeli prime minister Benjamin Netanyahu in response to a shooting by two Palestinian gunmen which killed two Israeli soldiers and injured three others at a checkpoint in Hebron. The Israel Defense Forces (IDF) then announced the closure of the Hebron area, and later that day began a large-scale operation, resulting in the widespread destruction of infrastructure and the displacement of at least 2,200 Palestinians.

Netanyahu then ordered a full lockdown of the West Bank, and announced that the IDF was preparing to conduct its largest military operation in the territory since the Six-Day War in 1967.

The Israeli military will be imposing a lockdown on the West Bank during Israel's national elections on Tuesday, as well as preventing Palestinian laborers from entering settlements on Sunday and Monday. The IDF says that following a fresh assessment, on Sunday and Monday, Palestinians with work permits will not be allowed to enter Israeli settlements across the West Bank and Jordan Valley.

**[ANALYSIS]** The operation continues to serve dual security and political purposes with the election tomorrow.

### **HOUTHI ATTACKS CONTINUE — RIYADH AIRPORT LARGELY SHUT** [VERIFIED]

Saudi Arabia has intercepted drones and missiles over its capital, Riyadh, and southern areas, the Saudi-led coalition has said, as fighting with Yemen's Houthis continues. The interception of Houthi ballistic missiles caused two injuries in the country's south.

Attacks by Yemen's Iran-aligned Houthis have nearly emptied the skies over Riyadh this month, aviation data showed on Monday, with cancellations grounding almost nine out of every 10 scheduled flights at the Saudi capital's airport. About 86% of scheduled flights at Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport, or 4,071 out of 4,736, were cancelled between October 8 and October 25.

In October 2026, the Houthis in Yemen launched a series of ballistic missile and drone attacks on three airports in Saudi Arabia, killing 22 people and injuring more than 380 others.

**[ANALYSIS]** The Houthi campaign has effectively paralyzed Saudi commercial aviation despite Saudi intercepts. This represents sustained strategic pressure that could factor into regional calculations about US military action.
