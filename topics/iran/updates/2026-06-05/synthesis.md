## Critical Developments (June 5)

### 1. Lebanese President Aoun Publicly Breaks with Iran — Major Shift

**[OFFICIAL]** Lebanese President Joseph Aoun accused Iran of using Lebanon as a bargaining chip in its negotiations with the United States on Friday, in some of his toughest criticism yet of Tehran and its Lebanese ally Hezbollah as it wages war with Israel. In a CNN interview, Aoun said "the people of Lebanon are paying the price ... for the sake" of Iran's interests, and were "fed up" with war between Israel and Hezbollah.

**[OFFICIAL]** "It's not your country, it's our country," Aoun said addressing Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC). "(Iran is) using Lebanon as a bargaining chip in their negotiation with US." Instead, Aoun has watched his presidential palace tremble under Israeli strikes as Israel widens its campaign in Lebanon.

**[OFFICIAL]** Aoun rejected a statement issued Wednesday by the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps demanding that Israel withdraw from Lebanon as part of any US-Iran ceasefire deal. "They are using Lebanon as a bargaining chip in their negotiations with the US. This is unacceptable," he said.

**[OFFICIAL]** Aoun said he spoke to Lebanese people across different religious sects, including Shiites, who have told him they are "fed up" with Hezbollah's war with Israel. "They are Lebanese people. They are not Naim Qassem's people," he said in a rare public criticism of the Iran-backed Shiite militant leader.

**[OFFICIAL]** The US-brokered agreement reached Wednesday in Washington sought to pull Lebanon away from Iran with a statement that any agreement to cease hostilities must be reached directly through Lebanon and Israel "and not through any separate track." Aoun said Hezbollah should also understand that negotiations and diplomacy are the only way "to save what's left" of Lebanon. Speaking at a meeting at government headquarters in Beirut, Prime Minister Salam said: "Let me be clear in front of you. The people of south Lebanon are not part of the war between Iran and America."

**Assessment:** This is the most significant public fracture between Lebanon's government and Iran/Hezbollah since the conflict began. Both the president and prime minister are now publicly distancing from Tehran. This could eventually strengthen the Lebanese government's independent negotiating position, but Hezbollah's military control of southern Lebanon limits what Aoun can deliver.

### 2. Iran Reveals $24 Billion "Trust Test" Demand — Talks "Deadlocked"

**[OFFICIAL]** "If he (Trump) wants to reach an agreement with Iran, this $24 billion is a test of trust that Iran wants to have with Trump – this is a test that America must pass and the path will be opened," Rezaei said. "This is our own money, not America's money." Rezaei warned that Iran will "drag the war" beyond the Persian Gulf if the US resumes the conflict, potentially expanding military operations from the Strait of Hormuz to the Indian Ocean, the Bab al-Mandab Strait, the Red Sea and the Mediterranean Sea.

**[REPORTED]** Iran has reportedly demanded the release of $12 billion in frozen funds as soon as once an interim agreement is signed with the US, and another $12 billion at a later stage. US officials are concerned that any unfreezing of funds at this stage could remove a key leverage point over the regime.

**[OFFICIAL]** Rezaei cast doubt on the durability of a nuclear agreement with Trump, citing his withdrawal from the 2015 Iran nuclear deal and what he said was his strategy of "ambiguity" in talks. Should talks fail, Rezaei said Iran is prepared for a potential US invasion of its territory, "then the world will understand Iran's true capabilities, because our land power is many times greater than our missiles."

**Assessment:** This is the clearest articulation yet of Iran's core demand. The $24 billion "trust test" framing signals Iran sees this as a non-negotiable precondition. Trump cannot politically afford to release what would appear as "pallets of cash"—this creates a fundamental impasse.

### 3. Israeli Strikes Continue in Lebanon — Nine Killed Friday

**[VERIFIED]** The comments by the country's leaders came as the Israeli military struck multiple parts of southern Lebanon and issued evacuation warnings for nine villages, including one that has sheltered thousands of people displaced by the three-month war. The strikes killed nine people in six locations in southern Lebanon, the state news agency reported.

**[VERIFIED]** Four people were killed in Israeli strikes on southern Lebanon on Friday, according to the state-run National News Agency (NNA). The strikes, which took place in the towns of Nabatieh and Bint Jbeil, came despite a US-mediated ceasefire agreed by the Israeli and Lebanese governments on Wednesday. The ceasefire is contingent on a "complete cessation of Hezbollah fire and the evacuation of all Hezbollah operatives" from an area of Lebanon south of the Litani River.

**[OFFICIAL]** Lebanon's parliament speaker and Hezbollah ally Nabih Berri said Friday that he would agree to the withdrawal of the Iran-backed group from south of the Litani River if Israel also withdrew its forces from occupied areas. In a statement from his Amal Movement party, Berri criticized the US mediated ceasefire framework, and added that he would agree to a total ceasefire "without restrictions" and which is "unconditional by land, sea and air."

**[VERIFIED]** The Lebanese health ministry reported that at least 35 people were killed in Israeli attacks in the previous 24 hours, increasing the Lebanese death toll to 3,468. The Lebanese health ministry reported that at least 48 people were killed in Israeli attacks in the previous 24 hours, increasing the Lebanese death toll to 3,516.

**Assessment:** The ceasefire announced Wednesday is functionally dead. Israel is treating it as permission to continue operations while Hezbollah and its allies demand Israeli withdrawal first. The total Lebanese death toll has now exceeded 3,500 since the war began.

### 4. US Boards Iranian Tanker in Indian Ocean — Global Enforcement Continues

**[VERIFIED]** U.S. forces conducted an interdiction of the sanctioned stateless oil tanker Davina overnight in the Indian Ocean, the U.S. military's Indo-Pacific Command said on Friday.

**[OFFICIAL]** "We will continue global maritime enforcement to disrupt illicit networks and interdict vessels providing material support to Iran, wherever they operate," the Indo-Pacific Command wrote in an X post.

**[VERIFIED]** Washington has imposed a blockade on Iran's sea trade while Tehran has fired on ships to prevent them sailing through the Strait of Hormuz waterway at the entrance to the Middle East Gulf. U.S. forces have intercepted multiple commercial and oil tankers in the Indian Ocean in recent months.

**[VERIFIED]** The Davina, a supertanker capable of carrying up to two million barrels of crude oil, was placed under U.S. sanctions in October 2024 for Iranian oil trading, according to ship tracking data. The vessel, also known as the Lenore, was last seen on June 5 off Sri Lanka's southern coast, ship tracking data on the MarineTraffic platform showed on Friday. The vessel's draft indicated that it was almost fully laden with an oil cargo.

**[REPORTED]** Iran's crude exports fell 84% in May from the previous month, Lloyd's List reported, as US pressure on Tehran's oil network and shipping disruption intensified.

**Assessment:** The US is maintaining maximum pressure on Iran's oil exports even as talks continue. The Indian Ocean interdiction demonstrates the global reach of the US blockade and underscores the economic pressure on Tehran—potentially strengthening US leverage but also hardening Iranian positions.

## The Lebanon Fracture: What It Means

**Today's development represents the clearest public split yet between Lebanon's government and Iran:**

Shi'ite Muslim Hezbollah, founded by Iran's Revolutionary Guards in 1982, ignited the latest hostilities more than three months ago when it opened fire in solidarity with Tehran as Iran came under U.S.-Israeli attack. Aoun, who led the Lebanese military before becoming head of state, is a Maronite Christian, as required by Lebanon's sectarian power-sharing arrangements. He has pressed for the peaceful disarmament of Hezbollah since being elected head of state by parliament last year.

**Key implications:**

1. **Creates potential for separate track:** If Lebanon can negotiate directly with Israel without Iranian/Hezbollah involvement, it could eventually delink the Lebanon-Iran nexus.

2. **But limited leverage:** "I will try… to negotiate and reason with them," Aoun said in reference to Hezbollah and disarmament. "Eventually they will be persuaded, but the cost will be high." Hezbollah still controls southern Lebanon militarily.

3. **Domestic risks:** Aoun's public attack on Iran and Hezbollah could deepen Lebanon's confessional divisions and create domestic backlash.

## The $24B Impasse: Why It Matters

**Iran's demand creates a fundamental trap for Trump:**

- Iran frames it as "our own money" and a "trust test" — non-negotiable precondition
- Trump cannot politically deliver billions without appearing to send "pallets of cash"
- This echoes the Obama-era $1.7B payment he has explicitly criticized
- US officials are concerned releasing funds removes key leverage

**Possible pathways:**
- Humanitarian-only fund (medicine, food, agricultural goods) — some mediators have proposed this
- Phased release tied to verification milestones
- Third-party escrow mechanism
- But all require political cover Trump may not have
