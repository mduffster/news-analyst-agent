## Daily Update: US-Israel-Iran Conflict
### June 5, 2026

### Executive Summary

**KEY DEVELOPMENTS: Lebanese president publicly attacks Iran; Senior Iranian official reveals $24B "test of trust" demand; War reaches 98th day with 100-day milestone on Saturday**

The most significant development today is Lebanese President Joseph Aoun's extraordinary public attack on Iran, accusing Tehran of "using Lebanon as a bargaining chip" in US negotiations. This represents the clearest fracture yet between Lebanon's government and Iran/Hezbollah. Meanwhile, a senior Iranian official told CNN that a peace deal hinges on Trump releasing $24 billion in frozen assets—framing it as a "test of trust." Israel continues strikes in Lebanon despite the ceasefire announcement, killing six people on Friday.

---

### Key Developments Since Yesterday

#### 1. Lebanese President Aoun Publicly Breaks with Iran — Major Shift

**[OFFICIAL]** Lebanese President Joseph Aoun accused Iran of using Lebanon as a bargaining chip in its negotiations with the United States on Friday, in some of his toughest criticism yet of Tehran and its Lebanese ally Hezbollah as it wages war with Israel. In a CNN interview, Aoun said "the people of Lebanon are paying the price ... for the sake" of Iran's interests, and were "fed up" with war between Israel and Hezbollah.

**[OFFICIAL]** Aoun told CNN's Christiane Amanpour, "This is not your country – it is our country." Aoun rejected a statement issued Wednesday by the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps demanding that Israel withdraw from Lebanon as part of any US-Iran ceasefire deal. "They are using Lebanon as a bargaining chip in their negotiations with the US. This is unacceptable," he said.

**[OFFICIAL]** Aoun said Hezbollah leader Naim Qassem doesn't represent the Lebanese people. Directly addressing Qassem, he said, "the Lebanese people are not your people." Aoun said he spoke to Lebanese people across different religious sects, including Shiites, who have told him they are "fed up" with Hezbollah's war with Israel. "They deserve not seeing their homes destroyed every five to 10 years," Aoun said.

**Assessment:** This is a major public fracture between Lebanon's government and Iran/Hezbollah. Aoun is essentially demanding Iran release its grip on Lebanese sovereignty. This unprecedented public criticism may strengthen the Lebanese government's negotiating position with the US and Israel, but it also risks deepening domestic divisions.

---

#### 2. Iran Reveals $24 Billion "Trust Test" Demand — Talks "Deadlocked"

**[OFFICIAL]** A potential peace deal between the United States and Iran hinges on the Trump administration agreeing to release $24 billion in frozen Iranian assets, a top Iranian official told CNN on Friday, warning that the US would "enter into a dark corridor" should it resume fighting. "The negotiations are at a deadlock and (US President Donald) Trump must break this deadlock," Mohsen Rezaei, military adviser to Supreme Leader Ayatollah Mojtaba Khamenei, told CNN. "The ball is in Trump's court."

**[OFFICIAL]** Rezaei framed the demand as a trust-building measure: "If he (Trump) wants to reach an agreement with Iran, this $24 billion is a test of trust that Iran wants to have with Trump – this is a test that America must pass and the path will be opened." He warned that Iran will "drag the war" beyond the Persian Gulf if the US resumes the conflict, potentially expanding military operations from the Strait of Hormuz to the Indian Ocean, the Bab al-Mandab Strait, the Red Sea and the Mediterranean Sea.

**[REPORTED]** Iran has reportedly demanded the release of $12 billion in frozen funds as soon as once an interim agreement is signed with the US, and another $12 billion at a later stage. US officials are concerned that any unfreezing of funds at this stage could remove a key leverage point over the regime.

**Assessment:** This is the clearest articulation yet of Iran's core demand. The $24 billion figure has been reported before, but Rezaei's framing as a "trust test" that "America must pass" signals Iran sees this as a non-negotiable precondition. This directly contradicts Trump's insistence on a deal that appears stronger than the 2015 JCPOA.

---

#### 3. IAEA: Progress on Nuclear Framework, But Access Still Limited

**[REPORTED]** IAEA Director General Rafael Grossi indicated that discussions with Iran may be moving toward a preliminary nuclear framework, but outcomes remain uncertain. The main obstacle to resuming full nuclear verification in Iran is the lack of consistent access for inspectors following recent disruptions, even though Iran remains legally bound to cooperate, Grossi said during a press conference Friday. On Iran's 60% enriched uranium, Grossi said there are several technically feasible options—such as exporting it, diluting it, or keeping it under IAEA control—but these are ultimately political decisions.

**Assessment:** Grossi's statement provides a rare positive signal on the nuclear track, but the access limitations remain a major obstacle. The gap between technical solutions and political will persists.

---

#### 4. Israel Continues Lebanon Strikes — Six Killed Friday

**[VERIFIED]** Four people were killed in Israeli strikes on southern Lebanon on Friday, according to the state-run National News Agency (NNA). The strikes, which took place in the towns of Nabatieh and Bint Jbeil, came despite a US-mediated ceasefire agreed by the Israeli and Lebanese governments on Wednesday. Additional reporting puts the total at six killed, with evacuation warnings issued for nine villages, including Anqoun. Hundreds of families fled Anqoun and Aarnaya due to the strikes.

**[VERIFIED]** Israeli strikes continue across the country despite a newly reached ceasefire deal. The Israeli military also continues to order the forced displacement of residents in southern Lebanon, telling people in at least nine towns and villages to flee under threat of attack.

**[OFFICIAL]** Lebanon's parliament speaker and Hezbollah ally Nabih Berri said Friday that he would agree to the withdrawal of the Iran-backed group from south of the Litani River if Israel also withdrew its forces from occupied areas. He criticized the US-mediated ceasefire framework, and added that he would agree to a total ceasefire "without restrictions" and which is "unconditional by land, sea and air."

**Assessment:** The ceasefire announced Wednesday is functionally dead. Israel is treating it as permission to continue operations while Hezbollah and its allies demand Israeli withdrawal first. More than 3,500 people have been killed in Lebanon since the war began. The fighting has killed at least 29 Israeli soldiers and three civilians.

---

#### 5. Trump Claims Hezbollah "Called Us" Seeking to Stop Fighting

**[OFFICIAL]** President Donald Trump said "progress has been made" in ending the fighting in Lebanon, after the leader of the militant group Hezbollah rejected the ceasefire agreement reached between the Lebanese government and Israel. Hezbollah "called us and they said, 'How about stopping?'" Trump said Thursday. The president also said he talked about the issue with Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and Hezbollah.

**[OFFICIAL]** Trump, asked if Iran killing US troops would cross a redline, said it would be a good reason to restart the war. "Well, it would be a good reason. I'd be honest with you," the president said. "If they killed US troops, I think I would do that very quickly. Yeah, that's a very interesting question."

**Assessment:** Trump's claim that Hezbollah contacted the White House directly is significant if true—no US president has ever spoken with Hezbollah, either directly or via intermediaries. Washington has designated the group as a "terrorist" organisation. The claim contradicts Hezbollah's public rejection of the ceasefire.

---

#### 6. Senate War Powers Vote Still Pending

**[VERIFIED]** In the Senate, Democrats are hopeful their efforts may be gaining momentum. Last month, they won support on a procedural measure to set up a war powers vote after a handful of Republicans broke ranks to join them. A final vote has yet to be scheduled.

**[VERIFIED]** The Senate has yet to take a final vote to approve or reject its own war powers resolution. Secretary of State Marco Rubio warned Wednesday at a House Foreign Affairs Committee hearing that the Iranians would think that the administration's "hands are going to be tied" if Congress approved a war powers resolution.

**Assessment:** The House resolution passed Wednesday (215-208) now goes to the Senate, but a final Senate vote remains unscheduled. Even if passed, Trump would likely veto, and a two-thirds majority to override appears unattainable.

---

#### 7. Oil Markets: Continued Pullback

**[VERIFIED]** Brent rose to 95.25 USD/Bbl on June 5, 2026, up 0.23% from the previous day. Brent crude futures traded near $95 per barrel on Friday after losing nearly 3% in the previous session, weighed down by hopes that the US and Iran could still find a diplomatic solution to end the war and reopen the Strait of Hormuz.

**[REPORTED]** President Donald Trump has reportedly been hesitant to reengage in a full-scale war with Iran despite recent confrontations, and would only consider ending the current truce if Tehran kills American troops. Even so, the international oil benchmark is still up more than 4% for the week, as negotiations between Washington and Tehran have yet to show meaningful progress, while Israel's ongoing military operations in Lebanon is becoming a key obstacle.

### Emerging Signals

**The Lebanon fracture is real and significant:** Aoun's CNN interview was not an off-the-cuff remark—it was a deliberate, extensive public attack on Iran with specific policy demands. This suggests the Lebanese government is positioning itself to negotiate separately from Hezbollah, potentially creating a parallel track that could eventually delink Lebanon from US-Iran talks.

**Iran has laid its cards on the table:** The $24B demand is now explicit and framed as non-negotiable. This clarity helps define the negotiating space but also reveals how far apart the parties are. Trump cannot politically afford to release "pallets of cash" while Iran insists it's a precondition.

**The IAEA signal shouldn't be ignored:** Grossi's indication of progress toward a nuclear framework, while cautious, represents one of the few positive diplomatic developments in weeks. If nuclear issues can be sequenced separately from the broader conflict, it might create a pathway for incremental progress.
