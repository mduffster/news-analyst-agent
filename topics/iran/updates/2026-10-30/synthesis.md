## October 30, 2026 — Day 245

**Assessment:** The conflict's trajectory crystallized today on two fronts. CENTCOM chief Admiral Brad Cooper briefed Trump in a rare White House meeting on strike options against Iran, while a limpet mine attack on the MT Louise 1 near Oman—the first successful use of such weapons since the war began—introduced a new threat to commercial shipping that bypasses existing escort defenses. Trump predicted "This thing is going to be over soon," while maintaining he won't strike before the November 3 midterms. ## 1. CENTCOM Briefs Trump on Strike Options; Decision Pending [VERIFIED]

The top US military commander overseeing the war against Iran briefed President Donald Trump in the White House on Thursday in a rare face-to-face meeting as the administration weighs options to resume the conflict. Brad Cooper, who heads US Central Command, offered updated assessments on options to resume strike operations against Iran.

The White House meeting also involved Pentagon Chief Pete Hegseth and Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff Dan Caine. The paper noted that this was Cooper's "rare in-person visit to the White House as the U.S. weighs resuming major combat operations."

The high-level discussions followed a separate meeting Wednesday at the White House involving Cooper, other senior military officials, and Vice President JD Vance. Cooper rarely briefs Trump in person, raising the possibility that the administration is moving closer to authorizing significant military action.

The meeting marks a rare in-person briefing between Trump and the CENTCOM chief, who is known to be supportive of restarting the bombing campaign. Reporting from Axios earlier this month described CENTCOM leadership and Secretary of War Pete Hegseth as the "strongest backers" of restarting major strikes against Iran.

**Trump's "Wrapped Up Soon" Remarks:** US President Donald Trump, speaking ahead of November 3 midterm congressional elections, said on Friday he believed the war with Iran will end soon, but did not say how that would happen. "It's going to get wrapped up soon," Trump said in an interview with Kansas City radio station KCMO. "We need a guarantee there'll never be a nuclear weapon."

However, sources cited by the Associated Press emphasized that Trump has not yet made a final decision regarding new strikes. The latest briefing does not mean that Trump has decided to launch another military campaign.

**Significance:** The combination of Cooper's rare in-person White House briefing—following a meeting with Vance the previous day—and Trump's optimistic prediction creates ambiguity. Trump's statement that the war will end "soon" could mean diplomatic resolution or military conclusion, but his simultaneous emphasis on nuclear guarantees suggests continued hardline positioning. The CENTCOM leadership's known support for resuming strikes adds weight to military track preparations.

## 2. Limpet Mine Attack Confirms New Maritime Threat [VERIFIED]

A small boat attached two limpet mines to the hull of a tanker in the Strait of Hormuz causing a fire in its engine room and temporarily disabling the vessel, in the first successful use of such explosives in the waterway since the US war with Iran began in February, maritime sources said on Friday.

Martin Kelly, Head of Advisory at EOS Risk Group, identified the vessel as the LOUISE 1. A Dubai-owned tanker with 27 Indian sailors aboard was struck by limpet mines off the coast of Oman. The crew, who are confirmed to be safe, put out the fire, following which the tanker resumed its journey.

According to UKMTO Warning 187-26, the incident occurred approximately 25 nautical miles northeast of Khasab, Oman, as the tanker was transiting outbound from the strait. A small boat approached the tanker's port side and attached two explosive devices above the waterline. The resulting explosions sparked a fire in the engine room and temporarily disabled the ship.

"The apparent ability of a small craft to approach a transiting tanker, deploy explosive devices and withdraw presents a significant security concern, particularly in the Strait's congested shipping lanes," Greece-based maritime risk management group Marisks said.

**Iran's Unverified Mine Claims:** Iran's Revolutionary Guard says four tankers struck mines in the Strait of Hormuz on unauthorized routes, a claim carried by 11 outlets including Al Jazeera, AP News and Arab News; no maritime authority has confirmed it. The Strait of Hormuz remains at the center of an intensifying maritime crisis, with Iran's Revolutionary Guard claiming four tankers may have struck mines on routes it calls unauthorized; no maritime authority has confirmed it.

UKMTO clarified that another tanker that was hit on Thursday night was hit by explosives attached to its hull by a small boat, not a projectile as first reported.

Oman has rescued 25 crew members from a burning crude oil tanker as ships continue to be targeted in the Strait of Hormuz. The US military denies the naval mine claims. On Friday, United States Central Command chief Brad Cooper said the Strait of Hormuz's "internationally recognised primary transit routes" had been successfully cleared of Iranian sea mines.

**Significance:** The UKMTO-verified limpet mine attack represents an important shift in Iranian maritime tactics. Unlike sea mines (which can be cleared from shipping lanes), limpet mines attached by small boats can target specific vessels even in escorted convoys. Marisks noted that "limpet mines are cheap and easily available," suggesting this tactic could proliferate. Iran's continued claims about sea mines—contradicted by CENTCOM's assertion that main routes are clear—may represent information warfare designed to deter shipping regardless of actual mine presence.

## 3. Vance Diplomacy: Iran Awaits Response as Qatari Mediators Visit Tehran [REPORTED]

A delegation from Qatar landed in Tehran on Friday for further talks with Iranian leadership, just days after Vice President J.D. Vance sent a letter requesting a resumption of direct negotiations. An unnamed senior adviser to Qatar's prime minister and foreign minister led the delegation to the Iranian capital for talks on "issues of mutual interest," Iran's IRNA news agency reported.

The Washington Post reported that Vance's letter to Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of the Supreme National Security Council, said Washington's main aim is to keep Iran from a nuclear weapon while recognizing Iran's interest in rebuilding its economy, asked Tehran to name negotiators authorized on the nuclear file, and floated Istanbul or Doha for a meeting.

Iran confirmed on Thursday that it received the letter but had not delivered an official response. Earlier this month, he posted on X that no negotiations would take place until Washington fulfills Iran's demands over the Strait of Hormuz, frozen assets, sanctions relief and a withdrawal of forces from the region.

Iranian Parliament Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf said Washington's 'carrot-and-stick approach' will not change Tehran's position. Iran insisted progress depends on acceptance of its seven conditions, including sanctions relief and a ceasefire, before negotiations can advance.

IRGC-affiliated Tasnim reported that any departure from its seven conditions—lifting the blockade, releasing frozen assets, recognizing its claimed authority over Hormuz—is "unacceptable." CTP-ISW assesses a positive response is unlikely, since Vance wants the nuclear program on the table from the outset.

**Significance:** The fundamental diplomatic impasse remains unbridged. Vance insists nuclear discussions begin immediately; Iran demands sanctions relief and blockade-lifting first. The Qatari delegation's arrival in Tehran on Friday represents active mediation, but Iran's public statements remain uncompromising. CTP-ISW's assessment that a positive response is "unlikely" reflects the structural gap between negotiating positions. With only three days until the November 3 midterms, the diplomatic window is narrowing rapidly.

## 4. Israel Coalition: Final Results Lock In Deadlock, Fraud Narrative Persists [VERIFIED]

Israel's final election count left both Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's right-wing bloc and the opposition led by former military chief Gadi Eisenkot short of the 61 seats needed to form a government. The outcome sets up weeks of coalition bargaining and political uncertainty, with the opposition alliance facing an uphill battle to assemble a majority government in the 120-seat Knesset.

Netanyahu's Likud party and its hard-right allies won 53 seats in the Knesset, according to the Central Elections Committee, short of the 61 needed for a majority and down from 64 seats in the 2022 election. The United Arab List, or Ra'am, and the Joint List secured a combined 19 seats.

Although the vote count is complete, the results remain subject to possible "minor corrections" before they are certified and submitted to President Isaac Herzog next Wednesday, according to the Central Elections Committee.

Netanyahu may get the first call to kick off the 28-day process to build a coalition that can be extended by up to 14 days.

Netanyahu's party has ramped up the rhetoric that the Arab-led parties have "stolen" the election.

**Foreign Influence Probe:** The Mossad is reportedly joining a probe of alleged foreign influence on the vote by Qatar and Turkey.

**Significance:** "He can try and persuade Eisenkot and others to form a coalition with him and in the meantime he can delegitimise the Arab parties to make it difficult to form an alternative government," analysts told Al Jazeera. The deadlock creates political bandwidth constraints during a critical period for Iran policy. Netanyahu's fraud narrative—lacking evidentiary support—may be positioning for either a grand coalition negotiation or another election rather than reflecting genuine electoral irregularities.

Israel demolished on Friday the homes of the assailants who killed two Israeli soldiers at a checkpoint in the occupied West Bank city of Hebron last week, part of an ongoing operation that has forcibly displaced over 3,000 Palestinians.

Smoke, dust and debris rise as the Israeli army demolishes two houses with explosives in connection with an October 23 shooting attack that killed two Israeli soldiers, in the West Bank city of Hebron.

Despite rejecting IDF Chief of Staff Zamir's earlier recommendation to exit the West Bank city, Netanyahu may show more flexibility now that the election has passed.

## 6. Houthi Operations: Six Attacks Claimed, Drone Intercepted Near Mecca [VERIFIED]

Iran-backed Houthi forces have claimed a total of six attacks on airports and oil facilities across Saudi Arabia despite a rare meeting of Saudi and Iranian senior officials in Tehran earlier this week after which the two sides agreed to resolve differences in the region through dialogue. Houthi spokesperson Yahya Saree said in a statement posted on X that the attacks targeted vital commercial and military infrastructure which spanned Saudi Arabia from its east and west coasts to Riyadh and the southern region of Najran.

The announcement by the Houthi spokesman on Thursday came after the Saudi-led coalition said its air defences intercepted and destroyed two ballistic missiles fired by the Houthis towards the capital. Saudi Arabia's Civil Defence said shrapnel from the interception wounded one Saudi citizen in Riyadh.

The Saudi-led coalition in Yemen has reported that it intercepted a drone launched by the Houthis towards Saudi Arabia's Mecca region. It was also reported that a missile aimed at western Saudi Arabia was earlier brought down.

A Houthi spokesperson denied targeting the Muslim holy city or surrounding region, reiterating that the group's cross-border attacks remain strictly focused on Saudi energy and military infrastructure.

**Significance:** The Houthi attacks continue unabated despite Saudi-Iranian diplomatic engagement, demonstrating that this front operates on its own dynamic independent of US-Iran negotiations. Any talks with Iran, which appear to be happening, will take the Houthis into account. The second drone intercept near Mecca this month—regardless of Houthi denials—represents a provocative development.

## 7. Oil Markets: Brent Rebounds on Strike Fears [VERIFIED]

Brent rose to 113.58 USD/Bbl on October 30, 2026, up 2.51% from the previous day. Over the past month, Brent's price has risen 11.02%, and is up 75.36% compared to the same time last year.

For the week, Brent is on track to increase by 0.64 per cent, while WTI would climb nearly 1 per cent. On a monthly basis, the benchmarks are on track to spike by about 15 per cent and 12 per cent, respectively.

Oil prices jumped by more than 2 per cent on Friday, on pace for a fourth straight monthly gain, as a tanker was struck in the Strait of Hormuz and the US reiterated the possibility of renewing hostilities in Iran.

Fears of US strikes on Iran kept upwards pressure on crude, "keeping supply risk premiums elevated despite earlier diplomatic outreach efforts," said Ryan McDaniel, president of Duncan Oil Company.

**Significance:** Thursday's diplomacy-driven decline completely reversed. Markets are weighting the CENTCOM briefing and Hormuz tanker attack more heavily than the Vance letter. The ~15% monthly gain reflects entrenched conflict premium as traders price in post-midterm strike risk.
