## Key Developments (Past 24 Hours)

### **CENTCOM CHIEF BRIEFS TRUMP ON IRAN STRIKE OPTIONS** [VERIFIED]

The Wall Street Journal reported on Thursday, October 29, 2026, that the commander of United States Central Command (CENTCOM) presented military strike options regarding Iran to Donald Trump during a rare White House meeting.

According to U.S. officials and individuals familiar with the meeting, Admiral Brad Cooper was joined by U.S. Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth and General Dan Caine, Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff.

This high-level session took place one day after top military commanders met with U.S. Vice President JD Vance.

CENTCOM had already submitted its plans to resume and intensify US strikes across Iran to the White House earlier this month. It was not immediately clear, following the principals' meeting on Thursday, whether Trump had decided to restart the war.

The meeting marks a rare in-person briefing between Trump and the CENTCOM chief, who is known to be supportive of restarting the bombing campaign. Reporting from Axios earlier this month described CENTCOM leadership and Secretary of War Pete Hegseth as the "strongest backers" of restarting major strikes against Iran.

The Pentagon has dispatched additional air defense systems, ammunition, and equipment to the Middle East while relocating certain U.S. troops away from vulnerable bases. The U.S. military is also transferring an additional Patriot missile battery to Israel to bolster the country's air defense capabilities.

**[ANALYSIS]** The timing is significant: Trump is running two parallel tracks — Vance's diplomatic outreach and CENTCOM's strike preparations. Cooper's rare in-person White House visit indicates serious strike planning is underway. The dual-track approach maintains maximum pressure on Iran while testing diplomatic alternatives before the midterms.

### **QATARI DELEGATION IN TEHRAN; IRAN HOLDS TO PRECONDITIONS** [REPORTED]

A delegation from Qatar landed in Tehran on Friday for further talks with Iranian leadership, just days after Vice President J.D. Vance sent a letter requesting a resumption of direct negotiations. An unnamed senior adviser to Qatar's prime minister and foreign minister led the delegation to the Iranian capital for talks on "issues of mutual interest," Iran's IRNA news agency reported.

The Washington Post reported that Vance's letter to Mohsen Rezaei said Washington's main aim is to keep Iran from a nuclear weapon while recognizing Iran's interest in rebuilding its economy, asked Tehran to name negotiators authorized on the nuclear file, and floated Istanbul or Doha for a meeting.

However, Iran continues signaling hard conditions: Iran has demanded that Washington satisfy seven conditions before the strategic waterway can reopen. Tasnim emphasized Tehran's opposition to proposals that depart from those demands, reporting that "any position that undermines this firm framework established by Iran is unacceptable."

IRGC-affiliated Tasnim reported that any departure from its seven conditions — lifting the blockade, releasing frozen assets, recognizing its claimed authority over Hormuz — is "unacceptable." CTP-ISW assesses a positive response is unlikely, since Vance wants the nuclear program on the table from the outset.

**[ANALYSIS]** The Qatari delegation's arrival in Tehran represents active mediation, but Iran's public statements remain uncompromising. The fundamental gap persists: Vance insists on nuclear discussions from the start; Iran demands sanctions relief first. CTP-ISW's assessment of a positive response being "unlikely" is notable.

### **HOUTHIS CLAIM SIX ATTACKS ON SAUDI ARABIA; ONE WOUNDED** [VERIFIED]

Iran-backed Houthi forces have claimed a total of six attacks on airports and oil facilities across Saudi Arabia despite a rare meeting of Saudi and Iranian senior officials in Tehran earlier this week after which the two sides agreed to resolve differences in the region through dialogue.

Houthi spokesperson Yahya Saree said in a statement posted on X that the attacks targeted vital commercial and military infrastructure which spanned Saudi Arabia from its east and west coasts to Riyadh and the southern region of Najran.

The announcement by the Houthi spokesman on Thursday came after the Saudi-led coalition said its air defences intercepted and destroyed two ballistic missiles fired by the Houthis towards the capital. Saudi Arabia's Civil Defence said shrapnel from the interception wounded one Saudi citizen in Riyadh.

On October 30, Saudi Arabia intercepted and destroyed a cruise missile launched by the Houthis; the missiles had been aimed at the western region.

Saudi-led coalition shoots down Houthi drone heading toward Makkah on October 30, the second interception near the holy city in recent weeks.

**[ANALYSIS]** The Houthi attacks continue despite Saudi-Iran diplomatic engagement, demonstrating that the Houthi front operates on its own dynamic. The claim of six attacks spanning coast-to-coast underscores the scale of threat to Saudi infrastructure. The intercept near Makkah is particularly provocative.

### **HEBRON: DAY 8 — ATTACKERS' HOMES DEMOLISHED** [VERIFIED]

Israel demolished on Friday the homes of the assailants who killed two Israeli soldiers at a checkpoint in the occupied West Bank city of Hebron last week, part of an ongoing operation that has forcibly displaced over 3,000 Palestinians.

The demolitions came on the eighth day of a military offensive and siege on the southern city in the occupied West Bank.

Israeli officials had threatened to speed up demolitions in Area C, the part of the West Bank under full Israeli control, in line with measures approved by Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich after the attack.

Nairat said the operation was driven by political rather than security aims. "It is as if you have turned Palestinian society back to 20 or 40 years ago in terms of fragmentation and turned the entire West Bank into a military zone ruled and controlled by the Israeli army," he said.
