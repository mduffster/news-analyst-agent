# US-Israel-Iran Conflict: Vance's Letter to Tehran Introduces Diplomatic Variable as Maritime Tensions Spike in Hormuz

## October 29, 2026 — Day 244

**Assessment:** The conflict's trajectory shifted today with two competing developments: VP Vance's direct letter to Iran's security chief Rezaei represents the most concrete diplomatic initiative since June talks collapsed, while Iran's declaration that three tankers struck mines in the Strait of Hormuz—partially corroborated by UK maritime monitors—signals intensified maritime coercion. The letter has reportedly been "received well" by Iran, and Tehran confirmed via Tasnim that it received the letter and leadership is crafting a response. However, Tehran remained firm in its demand that the United States meet seven conditions set by Iran, adding that any position undermining that framework would be unacceptable. Meanwhile, Israel's election deadlock has metastasized into a delegitimization dispute, with Netanyahu alleging Arab parties "stole" the election—claims police say have no evidentiary basis. Oil markets retreated on diplomacy hopes, with Brent falling toward $114-115 after touching $116+ yesterday. Five days remain until the post-midterm decision window opens on November 3.

## 1. Vance Letter to Rezaei: 11th-Hour Diplomatic Push Before Post-Midterm Strikes [REPORTED]

US Vice President JD Vance sent a letter to Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council, in an effort to revive peace negotiations, the Wall Street Journal reported, citing US officials and people familiar with the matter.

The letter was passed to Rezaei by Qatari mediators on Sunday. Vance called for direct talks and said Iran's nuclear programme would have to be on the table from the outset. The letter and follow-up discussions did not cover specific peace terms, but rather a potential "framework for negotiations." The goal was to see if "Tehran and Washington could find enough common ground to persuade Trump not to give a final order for a short, intense attack on Iran in the coming weeks."

The letter was sent via Qatari mediators this week calling for a resumption of direct negotiations. The vice president would reportedly lead the new American delegation, and the letter stressed the importance of discussing Iran's nuclear program immediately.

**Iran's Response:** Tehran has received the letter, according to Iranian state media. Tasnim reported that leadership is crafting a response. However, Tasnim reported Tehran remained firm in its demand that the United States meet seven conditions, adding that any position undermining that framework would be unacceptable.

**Trump's Denial:** Trump acknowledged that Vance wrote to Iranian authorities, but downplayed the significance, saying it was focused on offering medical supplies. But Trump said "no" when asked by reporters if he had directed Vance to restart peace negotiations. He acknowledged giving the Vice President his approval to reach out to Tehran. "They need help with medical supplies and various things. He asked me what I thought, I said if you want to do that, that's OK. Basically it's about getting supplies in to help people that are not well."

The letter focused on a possible framework for negotiations rather than the specifics of a peace proposal. It was reportedly sent at Trump's direction.

**Iran's Seven Conditions:** Iranian Parliament Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf said Thursday that progress in negotiations would depend on Washington agreeing to Tehran's seven conditions. "Iran's conditions have been repeatedly and clearly stated to the mediators. The carrot-and-stick approach has no effect on Iran. Progress can only be achieved once Iran's seven conditions have been agreed upon."

**Significance:** The dual-track approach—Vance's diplomatic outreach combined with continued strike preparations—maintains maximum pressure while testing whether an off-ramp exists. Trump's immediate denial creates strategic ambiguity: either coordinated good-cop/bad-cop positioning, or genuine internal disagreement about diplomatic versus military options. Iran's insistence on its seven conditions—including ending the naval blockade, releasing frozen funds, and stopping military operations—suggests the fundamental gap remains unbridged.

## 2. Strait of Hormuz: Iran Claims Three Tankers Hit Mines; UKMTO Partially Corroborates [REPORTED/PARTIALLY VERIFIED]

Iran said three oil tankers exploded and caught fire after striking naval mines over the past 24 hours while attempting to transit an unauthorized route in the Strait of Hormuz, according to the state broadcaster, which cited the Revolutionary Guard Corps.

The report did not identify the tankers or provide details about casualties. The Associated Press could not immediately confirm the report. Iran's navy said the tankers switched off their radar and navigation systems and ignored warnings as they attempted to exit through a restricted route. It said the damaged vessels were abandoned off the southern coast of the waterway.

**UKMTO Corroboration:** The United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations reported a vessel fire in the Strait of Hormuz on Thursday; the master of a passing vessel observed another ship on fire approximately 17 kilometers north of Oman's Khasab Port. The identity of the vessel, the status of its crew, and the extent of the damage have not been disclosed.

The UKMTO later said the ship previously spotted "on fire" has sunk. The incident was reported about nine nautical miles north of Khasab, Oman. "Regional authorities have identified the vessel as a dhow, which was involved in a maritime security incident, igniting a large fire onboard causing the dhow to list and sink. Local authorities have confirmed that the crew have been safely evacuated."

**Iran's Closure Declaration:** Iranian military forces have asserted full control over the Strait of Hormuz, declaring the vital maritime corridor closed to non-compliant commercial traffic after a tanker was damaged and set ablaze by a naval mine.

"The Strait of Hormuz is closed to violating ships that intend to pass through prohibited and unsafe areas without coordination and relying on the lies of the US President," the navy statement added. "The Strait of Hormuz will remain closed until Iran's conditions are met, under the control of the warriors of Islam," Major General Yazidi said.

The deputy commander of the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps stated that the Strait of Hormuz remains closed and is under the control of Islamic fighters. As long as the conditions proposed by Iran have not been met, the strait will continue to be blocked.

**Strait Status:** Commercial transits through the Strait of Hormuz are running below a tenth of the pre-crisis vessel count. As of 29 OCT 2026, it is day 242 since Iran closed the strait on 28 February 2026.

**Significance:** The discrepancy between Iran's claim (three large tankers) and UKMTO's confirmation (one dhow) suggests either Iranian exaggeration for psychological effect or additional incidents not yet corroborated. Regardless, the UKMTO verification of at least one vessel fire from a "maritime security incident" confirms active risk. This pattern—combined with Iran's renewed closure rhetoric—could spike insurance premiums and force routing changes even if the full Iranian claim proves exaggerated.

## 3. Israel Election: Deadlock Confirmed, Netanyahu Launches Fraud Allegations [VERIFIED]

As of Thursday, Israel's Central Election Committee said it had counted nearly 94% of votes. The partial results indicated that Netanyahu's Likud party would win 26 seats, with Eisenkot's Yashar party on 21 seats. The CEC projected that Netanyahu's coalition was on course to secure 53 seats, slightly ahead of Eisenkot's bloc on 48 seats. Neither coalition was on course to achieve a 61-seat majority.

**Fraud Allegations:** Netanyahu alleged Israel's election had been "stolen by Arab parties that support terrorism and the elimination of the Jewish state," alleging serious criminal offenses, including bribery, threats, violence, forgery and the use of prohibited software to mobilize voters.

Israel's Central Election Commission has asked the Shin Bet security service to investigate alleged foreign interference. However, the request itself reportedly did not specifically accuse Arab-led parties of wrongdoing. AP also reported that the commission said there was no basis, to its knowledge, for claims of systematic violations of election integrity.

Israeli police say they have found no evidence to support claims of widespread election fraud. Likud accused Arab-led parties of stealing the election and called on police and Shin Bet to investigate. But authorities say there is no evidence to substantiate the allegations.

Eisenkot accused Netanyahu of delegitimizing election, vowing not to allow obstruction of Knesset swearing-in. The Elections panel director says claims of widespread fraud appear to be baseless; vote was conducted lawfully.

**Significance:** Netanyahu's fraud narrative—rejected by police and election officials—represents a shift from "deadlock" to "contested legitimacy." This creates higher domestic volatility during the coalition formation period. Both Netanyahu's and Eisenkot's blocs have rejected partnering with Arab parties (who hold 19 seats), making majority formation mathematically difficult without a grand coalition or policy reversals.

## 4. US Strike Preparations Continue Despite Vance Diplomacy [REPORTED]

The US has warned several Gulf Arab countries to be prepared for a short, intense military offensive against Iran after the US midterm elections on 3 November, The Wall Street Journal reported on Wednesday.

The warning sent by diplomatic and military channels is the latest indication that the US and Israel are preparing to launch another round of attacks against Iran. US forces have been restocking their munitions in the region, and bomber aircraft and jet fighters are preparing for attacks.

The US military is moving a Patriot missile defence system from Texas to Israel to support defences. Next month, an aircraft carrier strike group and a Marine expeditionary unit will also join around 20 other warships operating in the Middle East.

United States President Donald Trump has said the US will not attack Iran before the November 3 midterm elections. "We will not be attacking Iran at any time prior to the midterm elections."

The Trump administration has vowed renewed military strikes on Iran after the U.S. midterm elections on Nov. 3.

US President Donald Trump has remained ambiguous over what course of action he will take in the near future. "My question is, do I make a deal with them or do I finish them off?" He had previously said attacks on Iran would not take place until after the US midterms on November 3.

**Significance:** The Vance letter operates alongside—not instead of—active strike preparations. This dual-track approach gives Trump maximum optionality: if Iran responds constructively to Vance, a diplomatic path opens; if Tehran stonewalls, strike plans are ready. The explicit Gulf ally warnings suggest this is not posturing but operational preparation.

## 5. Hebron: Lockdown Day 7 Continues Amid International Condemnation [VERIFIED]

The Israeli army is continuing its military lockdown of Hebron for the seventh consecutive day, carrying out raids and tightening restrictions in the occupied West Bank's largest city.

Two Palestinians were injured and seven detained by Israeli forces on Thursday, as Israeli settlers attacked Palestinians and their vehicles. Israel has imposed a tight siege of Hebron since Friday last week, closing all entrances and exits and restricting residents' freedom of movement and their ability to access critical services. More than 3,000 people have been forced out of the city as a number of Palestinian homes have been converted into military posts and barracks.

UN High Commissioner for Human Rights Volker Turk condemned the Hebron operation. He said threats to empty and erase neighbourhoods raised "serious concerns of ethnic cleansing." According to Turk, the lockdown had already forcibly displaced more than 2,000 Palestinians, led to the demolition of homes and prevented residents from moving freely.

**Note:** Displacement figures vary across sources—some report ~3,000, earlier reports cited ~5,000. The operation continues regardless of election results, confirming it operates independently of political outcomes.

## 6. Oil Markets Retreat on Diplomacy Hopes [VERIFIED]

Brent fell to 114.76 USD/Bbl on October 29, 2026, down 0.49% from the previous day. Over the past month, Brent's price has risen 17.07%, and is up 78.28% compared to the same time last year.

Brent crude fell toward $114 a barrel on Thursday, moving away from four-year highs as markets weighed renewed diplomatic efforts to prevent further US military action against Iran. Reports suggested that US Vice President JD Vance had contacted Iranian national security official Mohsen Rezaei.

By 8:45 a.m. Eastern Time today, oil had reached $115.40 per barrel, measured using the Brent benchmark. That's 85 cents less than it cost yesterday morning.

**Significance:** Markets immediately responded to Vance's diplomatic outreach with modest declines, reversing the prior day's gains. However, prices remain elevated near four-year highs, indicating traders still expect continued conflict. The ~$50/barrel year-over-year increase reflects entrenched conflict premium.
