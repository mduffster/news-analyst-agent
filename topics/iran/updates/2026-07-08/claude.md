## Daily Update: July 8, 2026

### Summary

**CEASEFIRE NEAR COLLAPSE: Trump declares MOU "over" and calls Iran leaders "scum" at NATO summit after overnight exchange of over 165 military strikes. CENTCOM hit 80+ Iranian targets including air defenses, radar, missile sites, and 60+ IRGC small boats after Iran attacked three tankers in Hormuz. Iran retaliated by targeting "85 US military installations" in Bahrain and Kuwait; air raid sirens sounded in both countries. US Treasury revoked Iran's oil sales waiver, effective July 17. Oil surges 5-7% to ~$78/barrel (Brent). Mojtaba Khamenei again absent as funeral procession reached Najaf; burial tomorrow in Mashhad. Defense Secretary Hegseth cancels planned Israel visit. This is the most severe escalation since the MOU was signed on June 17 — the 60-day window is now effectively in question.**

### Key Developments Since Yesterday

#### 1. CRITICAL: Trump Declares MOU "Over" — Calls Iran Leaders "Scum"

**[OFFICIAL]** President Trump said Wednesday he believes the current ceasefire with Iran is over after an exchange of attacks between the U.S. and Iran. "I think it's over. I don't want to deal with them anymore. They're scum," Trump told reporters in Ankara, Turkey, where he is attending the NATO summit.

**[OFFICIAL]** Trump told reporters at a NATO summit in Ankara, Turkiye, that he thinks the memorandum of understanding (MoU) with Iran is "over". While he "might" allow peace talks to continue for now, he said, he believed they were a "waste of time". In an angry tirade, he referred to the Iran leadership as "scum".

**[REPORTED]** "I'm with you [on Iran]," responded NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte. Earlier, Rutte had insisted the US strikes were "absolutely necessary," noting that Iran had violated the ceasefire, which was signed three weeks ago.

*Assessment: This is a dramatic rhetorical shift from Trump, who had been celebrating the MOU signing just three weeks ago. However, Trump notably did not rule out further negotiations, saying he would "speak to our negotiators." The gap between Trump's rhetoric and actual policy remains uncertain.*

#### 2. MAJOR: US Strikes 80+ Iranian Targets — Biggest Attack Since MOU

**[VERIFIED]** U.S. Central Command said Tuesday that it launched a "series of powerful strikes" on Iran in response to Iranian attacks on three commercial vessels in the Strait of Hormuz. CENTCOM said the operation targeted air defenses, radar sites and anti-ship missile sites, as well as dozens of small boats used by Iran's Revolutionary Guard.

**[VERIFIED]** Two Iranian military bases were struck by the US on Wednesday morning, Iran's semi-official Fars News Agency cited an official Iranian source as saying. Earlier, US Central Command (CENTCOM) confirmed that the United States had struck southern Iran on Qeshm Island, in Bandar Abbas, and in Sirik overnight.

**[VERIFIED]** The strikes on Wednesday were four or five times bigger in scope and power than the strikes 10 days ago, another US official told Axios.

**[REPORTED]** One base was struck in Dashti county and another near the town of Chogadak, both in Bushehr province, early in the morning. No deaths or injuries have been reported so far as a result of the attacks, Fars said.

#### 3. MAJOR: Iran Retaliates — Claims 85 Strikes on US Bases in Bahrain & Kuwait

**[OFFICIAL]** Iran's military said Wednesday it conducted retaliatory strikes against the United States, targeting dozens of U.S. assets in Bahrain and Kuwait. In a statement carried by state-run Islamic Republic of Iran Broadcasting, Iran's Armed Forces said they had attacked 85 "key U.S. military installations" at Salman Port and the 5th Fleet, both in Bahrain, and Ali Al Salem Air Base in Kuwait.

**[VERIFIED]** Air raid sirens sounded in Bahrain and Kuwait, officials said. The Kuwaiti army said air defences were confronting "hostile" missile and drone attacks.

**[OFFICIAL]** "We will not allow U.S. interference in the affairs of the Strait of Hormuz," Khatam al-Anbiya Central Headquarters, Iran's top military command body, said in a statement.

**[REPORTED]** The statement was followed by a second one announcing a "new wave" of missiles targeting U.S. bases in Bahrain. The extent of the damage, if any, was not immediately clear.

*Assessment: This is the largest exchange of direct US-Iran strikes since the MOU was signed. Both sides appear to have deliberately limited targets to military assets and avoided mass casualties, suggesting neither wants full-scale war resumption — yet.*

#### 4. US Treasury Revokes Iran Oil Sales Waiver

**[OFFICIAL]** "The Office of Foreign Assets Control is revoking GL X, which authorized the sale of Iranian oil. As President Trump and the administration have repeatedly affirmed, the MOU in effect with Iran is entirely performance-based," a U.S. official told CBS News in a statement. "Iran will only reap benefits if they exhibit good behavior. Iran's actions in the Strait were wholly unacceptable to the United States and will be met with consequences."

**[VERIFIED]** Under the interim U.S.-Iran agreement, the U.S. Treasury issued a June 22 general licence to allow the sale of crude oil and petrochemical and petroleum products of Iranian origin through August 21. In revoking that licence on Tuesday, it gave Iran until July 17 to wind down any transactions.

**[REPORTED]** "The significance of this move cannot be overstated. It is a complete destruction of the Memorandum of Understanding between the United States and Iran," Brett Erickson, managing principal at Obsidian Risk Advisors, said on X. "The oil sanction waivers were the only notable up front concession that Iran received for the joint lifting of blockades in the Strait of Hormuz."

*Assessment: The oil waiver revocation is arguably more consequential than Trump's rhetoric. This removes Iran's primary economic incentive under the MOU framework. The 10-day wind-down period (until July 17) creates an inflection point where Iran must decide whether to escalate further or seek de-escalation.*

#### 5. Iran Accuses US of MOU Violations

**[OFFICIAL]** The Iranian Foreign Ministry condemned a US Treasury move to revoke the temporary suspension of sanctions on Iranian oil sales, saying that Iran "holds the United States responsible for violating the memorandum of understanding, and will take any measures we deem necessary to safeguard our interests and national security." Iran's foreign ministry added that "The US has repeatedly violated the terms of the memorandum of understanding over the past 20 days, either directly or through the actions of the Zionist entity against Lebanon."

**[REPORTED]** Alaeddin Boroujerdi, a member of the Iranian parliament's National Security and Foreign Policy Committee said on Wednesday that the U.S. had violated the MOU by rejecting Tehran's role in managing traffic through the Strait of Hormuz. He said that the deal had established that ships would pass through the strait "under the supervision and management of the Islamic Republic of Iran."

**[OFFICIAL]** Senior advisor to Iran's supreme leader, Ali Akbar Velayati, has blamed the US for the resumption of hostilities in the Gulf region. Velayati posted on his X account that Trump's "verbal admission" of cancelling the US-Iran MoU had once again "driven the region towards fire". The advisor further stated that Iran had "its finger on the trigger", and would not remain "silent against humiliation and adventurism".

#### 6. Oil Prices Surge 5-7% — Highest Since Late June

**[VERIFIED]** Brent rose to 77.92 USD/Bbl on July 8, 2026, up 5.06% from the previous day. Over the past month, Brent's price has fallen 14.80%, but it is still 11.01% higher than a year ago.

**[VERIFIED]** At 8:30 a.m. Eastern Time today, the price of oil sits at $78.17 per barrel, using Brent as the benchmark. That's an increase of $4.88 since yesterday morning and roughly $7.30 more than at this time last year.

**[VERIFIED]** Crude oil prices surged 7% to $75.6 a barrel on Wednesday as escalating tensions in the Middle East fueled concerns over further supply disruptions. President Trump said that as far as he is concerned, the ceasefire is over and threatened additional strikes on Iran and a new blockade.

**[REPORTED]** Brent crude rose 6 percent to $78 a barrel, while European stocks fell 1.6 percent, following his remarks.

*Assessment: The ~$6/barrel spike (from ~$73 yesterday to ~$78 today) is significant but still well below war peak levels (~$117+). Markets are pricing in elevated risk but not a return to full strait closure.*

#### 7. Mojtaba Khamenei Absent Again — Day 6 of Funeral

**[VERIFIED]** The new supreme leader of Iran and son of Ali Khamenei, Mojtaba Khamenei, who has not been seen in public since his appointment, was not expected to attend the proceedings raising further questions about his health and who is leading the country. However, Khamenei's three other sons, Mostafa, Meysam and Masoud Khamenei, attended the funeral.

**[OFFICIAL]** Mojtaba Khamenei, who has not yet appeared in public since taking over as the supreme leader shortly after his father's assassination, has been notably absent from the processions. Officials have said he was wounded in the air strikes that killed his father, but the severity of his injuries remains unclear.

**[VERIFIED]** On 8 July 2026, the fifth day of the funeral ceremonies, the public funeral procession in Najaf began at 6:00 a.m. local time. According to Saad Maan, spokesperson for the Supreme Committee for Funeral Ceremonies, the procession will continue to the city of Karbala at 4:00 p.m. local time.

**[VERIFIED]** According to Iraq's Popular Mobilisation Forces (PMF), more than 2.3 million people participated in the funeral procession.

*Assessment: Tomorrow's burial in Mashhad is the final and most significant ceremony. If Mojtaba doesn't appear at his own father's burial, it will definitively confirm he is physically incapacitated and raise acute succession questions.*

#### 8. NATO Summit Declaration Addresses Iran/Hormuz

**[OFFICIAL]** In their final declaration, leaders reaffirmed their commitment to Article 5 collective defense, stressed that Iran "must never have a nuclear weapon," and called on Tehran to fully respect freedom of navigation in the Strait of Hormuz. After the leaders' meeting, which lasted more than three hours, allies issued a joint statement announcing more than $50 billion in new defense procurements and pledging €70 billion in military equipment, assistance and training for Ukraine in 2026.

**[REPORTED]** No distinct NATO role in the Gulf is envisaged, however. A coalition of the willing led by France and the United Kingdom, with other countries expected to join, is preparing to contribute assets such as minesweepers. However, the coalition's rules of engagement are still unclear, according to one NATO official, who pointed out that "mixed signals" were coming out of Iran about whether other countries' vessels would be allowed to operate in the Strait of Hormuz.

#### 9. Hegseth Cancels Israel Visit

**[VERIFIED]** US Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth canceled a planned visit to Israel on Wednesday, amid renewed hostilities between Washington and Tehran and Israeli concerns over the possible sale of American F-35 stealth fighter jets to Turkey. An Israeli official confirmed the planned visit and its cancellation to The Times of Israel following reports on the matter.

**[REPORTED]** Hebrew-language media reports, citing sources with knowledge of the matter, said late Wednesday morning that the visit, which would have been Hegseth's first as Pentagon chief, had been cancelled. The Ynet news site said that Israel had been informed of the cancellation against the backdrop of rising tensions between the US and Iran.

*Assessment: The cancellation signals the Pentagon is in crisis management mode. The F-35-to-Turkey issue, which Netanyahu has vocally opposed, remains unresolved.*

#### 10. Maritime Threat Level Raised to "Severe"

**[VERIFIED]** The US Navy-led Joint Maritime Information Center (JMIC) raised the threat level to transit the strait to "severe" from "substantial" on Tuesday following the attacks, citing deliberate hostile action likely under current conditions, the first time the threat level has been set at that severe status since June 15.

**[VERIFIED]** Following the attacks, maritime authorities raised the threat risk for vessels transiting the Strait of Hormuz to "severe". While traffic through the waterway has picked up in the last week, it remains tentative, ranging between one-third and one-fifth of its pre-war levels.

### Emerging Signals

**War-but-not-war dynamics:** Both sides conducted significant strikes overnight but neither appears to have caused major casualties. This suggests a strange equilibrium where military exchanges occur but both sides avoid actions that would force full war resumption. The question is whether this equilibrium is stable.

**Negotiator back-channel may survive:** A Bloomberg article included "A US official said negotiators would continue to work toward a final deal." Trump's rhetoric and actions may diverge — the administration may be maintaining negotiating channels while projecting toughness.

**Iran's oil deadline:** The July 17 wind-down for oil transactions creates a hard deadline. Iran must decide within 9 days whether to seek de-escalation to preserve some economic benefit or to fully abandon the MOU framework.

**IRGC naval capacity question:** CENTCOM claims to have destroyed 60+ IRGC small boats. If accurate, this significantly degrades Iran's ability to interdict shipping using swarm tactics — potentially affecting the balance of power in subsequent negotiations.

**Saudi-Qatar-Iran triangle:** Both Saudi and Qatari tankers were attacked. The GCC has rallied behind Qatar, with the GCC Secretary-General calling for the international community to adopt a "firm and deterrent stance against these repeated Iranian attacks." He added that the GCC stood "united with the State of Qatar and expressed its full solidarity with all measures" taken in response by Doha.

### Bottom Line

**The US-Iran ceasefire framework is in acute crisis. Trump's declaration that the MOU is "over," combined with the largest exchange of military strikes since June 17, the revocation of Iran's oil sales waiver, and the elevation of Hormuz threat level to "severe," represents a fundamental shift from yesterday's tense-but-functioning framework. However, this is not yet full war resumption: neither side caused mass casualties, negotiators reportedly continue working, and the 10-day oil waiver wind-down creates space for de-escalation. Tomorrow's burial of Khamenei in Mashhad marks the end of the funeral period — what Iran's leadership says and does after that will determine whether this is a temporary escalation or the beginning of war's resumption. The probability that the MOU collapses entirely within the 60-day window has jumped from 31% to 70%.**
