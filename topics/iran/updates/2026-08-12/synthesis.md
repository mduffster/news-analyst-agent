## Critical Developments (August 12)

### 1. CRITICAL: Ceasefire Extension — Conflicting Claims [CONFLICTING]

The US and Iran have "agreed" to extend a 60-day ceasefire under the Islamabad Memorandum of Understanding as the agreement is set to expire on Aug. 17, Pakistani government sources told Anadolu Agency on Wednesday. "Both sides have conveyed their consent to the mediators to extend the deadline," a source close to the mediation process said. "However, the two sides are exchanging messages to decide on the extension period."

However, Iran denied this account: "There were no discussions between Iran and the United States on extending the ceasefire," a senior Iranian source told Reuters. "The source says that from Tehran's perspective, the deal had no start date, and therefore there is nothing to extend."

"One of the issues that is being discussed via mediators is the U.S. returning to the interim agreement and defining a timeframe for implementing its commitments. There has been absolutely no progress on this issue," the Iranian source stated.

*Assessment: [CONFLICTING] The disconnect between Pakistani claims and Iranian denials reflects a fundamental gap. Pakistan may be reporting on indirect messaging while Iran insists nothing substantive has changed. This pattern suggests the ceasefire framework is effectively dead, though both sides may avoid formally declaring it so.*

### 2. CRITICAL: IRGC Adviser Explicitly Declares "Prolong War" Strategy [OFFICIAL]

"We have to attain deterrence so that the enemy never dares to attack us, so we can live with security," Mohammad Reza Naqdi told PBS. "One way is to prolong this war until we get to the next term of the presidency and cause attrition, so that if anyone else wants to attack Iran, they will know there is a cost." Naqdi served as coordinating deputy of the IRGC from 2019 to 2025, a senior headquarters position responsible for coordination across the organization.

This was a rare interview, as "the IRGC rarely gives interviews, and almost never to Western media." But Mohammad Reza Naqdi sat down in Tehran for an exclusive interview.

When asked if he believes Iran is currently in control of the war, Naqdi said: "Certainly, victory is on our side." Washington is conducting a "war without strategy," he said.

*Assessment: [OFFICIAL] This is the most explicit articulation of Iran's attrition strategy to date. Unlike prior analyst speculation, this comes from a senior IRGC official speaking to Western media — clearly intended to be heard in Washington. Combined with Monday's hardliner appointments, Iran is now publicly signaling it will wait Trump out.*

---

### 3. CRITICAL: Houthi Attack Kills Six — First Red Sea Shipping Deaths [VERIFIED]

At least four crew members were killed in a suspected attack by Iran-backed Houthis on a small cargo vessel in the Bab el-Mandeb strait on Tuesday while two members of the forces deployed on a rescue mission to evacuate the ship were also killed. The initial salvo targeting the ship "caused a fire to break out aboard the vessel and inflicted extensive damage."

The Yemeni Transport Ministry said in a statement that the ship was carrying food and was hit while traveling through the Bab al-Mandab Strait. One Indonesian and three Pakistani crew members were killed in the attack after a fire broke out on board the Tihamah. The Yemeni coast guard said later Tuesday that two members of the armed forces were also killed during a rescue mission when another Houthi missile was launched at the ship.

The fatalities aboard the Egyptian-owned Tihamah, if confirmed, would be the first deaths in a Houthi strike on shipping since the Iran war began on February 28.

Pakistan's foreign minister confirmed three Pakistanis were killed in the attack.

*Assessment: [VERIFIED] The double-tap nature of this attack — striking the ship, then striking again during rescue operations — represents significant escalation. With both Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb now producing casualties, global shipping faces multi-chokepoint lethality.*

### 4. SIGNIFICANT: US Navy Disables Third Blockade-Running Ship [VERIFIED]

The US military disabled a Panama-flagged cargo vessel sailing toward an Iranian port on Tuesday by firing two hellfire missiles into its engine room, US Central Command announced. The M/V Vela Nova "attempted to transit the Gulf of Oman and violate the US blockade against Iran." The crew "ignored repeated warnings from American forces," leading to a US Navy MH-60 helicopter firing the missiles and disabling the ship's steering gear.

It was the third time the United States had forcibly halted a vessel since reinstating the ports blockade on July 14.

"As of Aug. 11, CENTCOM has redirected 55 commercial vessels attempting to run the blockade, disabled 3 non-compliant vessels, and boarded 2," CENTCOM stated.

U.S. forces fired missiles at a container ship attempting to breach Washington's blockade of Iranian ports in the Gulf of Oman. The twin incidents show how the war is inflicting a toll on two of the world's most critical shipping lanes.

*Assessment: [VERIFIED] The US is actively maintaining the blockade with lethal force. Combined with the Houthi attack, both sides demonstrate continued kinetic operations even amid diplomatic posturing.*

### 5. SIGNIFICANT: IEA Slashes 2026 Demand Forecast — Inventory Buffers "Rapidly Depleting" [VERIFIED]

World oil demand is forecast to decline by 1.6 mb/d in 2026, 510 kb/d more than the IEA's estimate in last month's Report, as the ongoing closure of the Strait of Hormuz and elevated fuel prices continue to weigh on oil consumption.

Global oil supply rose by 2.4 mb/d to 101.5 mb/d in July, but remained 6.3 mb/d below year-ago levels, with 8.3 mb/d of Gulf output still shut in. Renewed hostilities and maritime disruptions in July and early August undermined the recovery efforts.

The IEA said Wednesday that global observed oil inventories fell below 7.9 billion barrels in July for the first time since April 2025. "Although the market is projected to return to surplus towards the end of this year, risks remain substantial and the urgency of reopening the Strait has increased, as previously available inventory buffers are rapidly depleting," its report said.

A recovery in movement through the strait of Hormuz would reverse a global annual supply contraction of around 4.3mn b/d in 2026 into supply growth of 8.3mn b/d in 2027. Consequently, this would flip a projected supply deficit of 1.3mn b/d in 2026 into a 4.6mn b/d surplus in 2027.

*Assessment: [VERIFIED] The IEA report quantifies the structural damage: 8.3 mb/d of Gulf output shut in, inventories at lowest since April 2025, buffers "rapidly depleting." The agency's assumption of eventual de-escalation may be optimistic given Iran's explicit "prolong war" strategy.*

### 6. SIGNIFICANT: Oil Retreats to ~$88-91 on Conflicting Signals [VERIFIED]

At 6:35 a.m. Eastern Time today, the price of oil sits at $91.60 per barrel, using Brent as the benchmark. That's a decrease of 94 cents since yesterday morning and roughly $25 more than at this time last year.

Brent crude oil fell toward $87 per barrel on Tuesday, before recovering to $88.7 per barrel, after Pakistan signaled that the US and Iran could be close to an arrangement aimed at easing tensions.

The comments prompted oil prices to reverse earlier gains, highlighting the market's sensitivity to conflicting signals over the negotiations.

*Assessment: [VERIFIED] Oil has retreated ~$4 from yesterday's $92+ spike, demonstrating extreme price sensitivity to diplomatic signals. The intraday swing from $87 to $91+ shows markets remain vulnerable to any headline.*

---

### 7. MODERATE: Hormuz Remains Effectively Closed [VERIFIED]

The Strait of Hormuz is effectively closed to commercial shipping as of 12 August 2026. Convoys are moving under naval escort. 8 ships transited on August 8 vs ~73/day normal.

Traffic through the Strait of Hormuz is back down following a brief pick-up. After an almost daily back and forth over whether the strait is open or closed, blockaded or not, vessel traffic is nowhere near the hundred-plus ships a day that were traversing the strait before the war.

Prolonged instability in the Strait could persist. Periodic Iranian attacks against shipping and retaliatory U.S. strikes against Iran have severely disrupted traffic through the Strait for most of the past five months, as of early August 2026.

*Assessment: [VERIFIED] Traffic remains at ~10% of normal despite various claims of progress. Iran's denial of ceasefire discussions and explicit "prolong war" strategy suggest no near-term normalization.*
