## Key Developments Since Prior Report (August 11)

### 1. Ceasefire Extension Agreed — Parties Negotiating Duration

**[REPORTED]** The US and Iran have "agreed" to extend a 60-day ceasefire under the Islamabad Memorandum of Understanding as the agreement is set to expire on Aug. 17, Pakistani government sources told Anadolu on Wednesday.

**[REPORTED]** "Both sides have conveyed their consent to the mediators to extend the deadline," a source close to the mediation process said. "However, the two sides are exchanging messages to decide on the extension period," the source added.

**[VERIFIED]** The US and Iran agreed to a Pakistan-brokered ceasefire in April and later signed a memorandum of understanding on June 17, launching negotiations toward a final agreement. However, the talks later stalled over disagreements on security guarantees and freedom of navigation through the Strait of Hormuz.

*Assessment: This is a significant development that reduces the risk of imminent escalation. However, the ceasefire extension does not resolve the underlying deadlock on Hormuz or compensation demands — it merely prevents a collapse to active combat before the August 17 deadline.*

### 2. IRGC Adviser Explicitly States Iran Strategy: "Prolong War Until Trump Leaves"

**[OFFICIAL]** Iran could "prolong" the war with the US until President Donald Trump is out of office, a top adviser to the commander of Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps has said. "We have to attain deterrence so that the enemy never dares to attack us, so we can live with security," Mohammad Reza Naqdi told PBS in an interview published yesterday.

**[OFFICIAL]** "One way is to prolong this war until we get to the next term of the presidency and cause attrition, so that if anyone else wants to attack Iran, they will know there is a cost."

**[VERIFIED]** Naqdi served as coordinating deputy of the IRGC from 2019 to 2025, a senior headquarters position responsible for coordination across the organization. He is now a senior adviser to the IRGC commander, a position that carries less command authority but likely preserves access to the IRGC's top leadership.

**[OFFICIAL]** When asked if he believes Iran is currently in control of the war, Naqdi said: "Certainly, victory is on our side."

*Assessment: This is the most explicit public articulation yet of Iran's attrition strategy. Unlike informal speculation, this comes from a senior IRGC official in a Western media interview — suggesting it's intended to be heard in Washington. The strategy aligns perfectly with the hardliner appointments announced Monday: Iran is now publicly signaling it will wait Trump out.*

**[REPORTED]** Pakistani Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi arrived in Tehran to hold talks with his Iranian counterpart, the Islamic Republic's semi-official Iranian Students' News Agency reported.

**[REPORTED]** Talks between Iran and Oman about reopening Hormuz to some maritime shipping have reached an advanced stage, Al Jazeera reported earlier, citing a Qatari Foreign Ministry spokesperson.

### 4. Houthi Attack Kills Six — First Red Sea Shipping Deaths Since War Began

**[VERIFIED]** Iran-backed Houthi rebels killed six people aboard a cargo ship in the Bab el-Mandeb Strait on Tuesday, the first reported fatalities from attacks targeting Red Sea shipping in more than a year.

**[VERIFIED]** Yemen's internationally recognised government has accused the Houthi rebel group of killing at least six people and wounding 10 others in a double-tap strike on a cargo vessel in the Bab al-Mandeb strait. The attack on the Egyptian-owned Tihamah on Tuesday marks the first shipping deaths linked to the Iran-aligned Houthis since the United States and Israel launched the war on Iran on February 28.

**[VERIFIED]** The Yemeni Transport Ministry said in a statement that the ship was carrying food and was hit while traveling through the Bab al-Mandab Strait. One Indonesian and three Pakistani crew members were killed in the attack after a fire broke out on board the Tihamah. The Yemeni coast guard said later Tuesday that two members of the armed forces were also killed during a rescue mission when another Houthi missile was launched at the ship.

**[OFFICIAL]** International Maritime Organization Secretary-General Arsenio Dominguez released a statement on Wednesday condemning Tuesday's deadly attack on a cargo ship off the coast of Yemen. At least six people were killed in two attacks on the Egyptian-owned Tihamah cargo ship on Tuesday, Yemeni officials said.

*Assessment: The Houthis' return to lethal attacks represents a significant escalation that compounds the Hormuz crisis. With both Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb now dangerous, two of the world's most critical chokepoints are effectively compromised simultaneously.*

### 5. US Navy Fires on Ship Breaching Iran Blockade — Third Ship Attacked Since July

**[VERIFIED]** The U.S. military said Tuesday that its forces fired on a Panamanian-flagged ship in the Gulf of Oman that tried to evade the American blockade on Iranian ports. The move came as President Donald Trump tries to keep economic pressure on the Islamic Republic. U.S. Central Command said the vessel had ignored warnings to stop, prompting an American helicopter to fire missiles into the engine room of the M/V Vela Nova, disabling the ship.

**[VERIFIED]** The missile strike caused a fire aboard the Vela Nova, but it was later extinguished, Vanguard said in information reported by The Washington Post. All 17 crew members were accounted for.

**[VERIFIED]** Reuters reported that the Vela Nova was the 12th vessel attacked by U.S. forces since the blockade was announced in April and the third since it was reimposed on July 14.

**[VERIFIED]** Central Command said it has redirected 55 commercial vessels attempting to run the blockade, disabled three non-compliant vessels and boarded two.

*Assessment: The US is actively enforcing the blockade with lethal force. Combined with the Houthi attack the same day, this demonstrates how both sides continue kinetic operations even as ceasefire extension talks proceed — validating Iran's "neither war nor peace" strategy.*

### 6. IEA Slashes 2026 Oil Demand Forecast — Warns Inventory Buffers "Rapidly Depleting"

**[VERIFIED]** Oil demand is set to fall by 1.6 million barrels a day in 2026, the International Energy Agency said Wednesday, raising its forecast last month of close to 1 million barrels a day. The IEA forecast demand will drop by 1.6 million barrels a day in 2026. That's 510,000 barrels a day more than its last monthly prediction in July.

**[VERIFIED]** Regional exports, including routes bypassing the Strait of Hormuz, fell by a sharp 2.1 mb/d to 15 mb/d after the key passageway was effectively closed again in early July and oil infrastructure and tankers came under attack. Loadings peaked at 20 mb/d at the start of July but dropped to around 12 mb/d later in the month.

**[VERIFIED]** The global oil balance is now expected to show a deficit of 1.8 mb/d in 3Q26, more than double the estimate of around 800 kb/d in last month's Report. After some brief respite in June, global observed oil inventories plunged by 69 mb, or 2.2 mb/d, in July, dragged lower almost entirely by a drop in oil on water. By the end of July, observed stocks had fallen below 7.9 billion barrels for the first time since April 2025.

**[VERIFIED]** "Although the market is projected to return to surplus towards the end of this year, risks remain substantial and the urgency of reopening the Strait has increased, as previously available inventory buffers are rapidly depleting," its report said.

*Assessment: The IEA report confirms the structural damage from prolonged Hormuz closure. The warning that inventory buffers are "rapidly depleting" suggests the world has limited ability to absorb continued disruption without more severe consequences.*

### 8. Trump Administration Claims on Oil Flows Contradicted by Data

**[OFFICIAL]** Energy Secretary Chris Wright said Tuesday that oil exports from the Middle East rose above pre-war levels on Sunday and have been right around normal for a week — a highly dubious claim that does not appear to be backed up by observable shipping traffic. Wright said in a post on X that the oil flows were restored to normal levels after the US military coordinated a plan with allied Gulf nations to get crude through the Strait of Hormuz and Middle Eastern countries bypassed the waterway via pipelines or other export facilities. It's not clear how Wright arrived at that conclusion.

**[VERIFIED]** Shipping traffic through the Strait of Hormuz fell to six on Monday, compared with a 10-day average of about 11 vessels, shipping data showed, amid fading hopes of a peace deal between the U.S. and Iran. In pre-war days, about 130 to 140 ships typically transited the strait.

**[VERIFIED]** Effectively no, it is closed to commercial shipping as of 12 August 2026. 8 ships transited on August 8 vs ~73/day normal.

*Assessment: The Trump administration's claim that oil flows are at "pre-war levels" is contradicted by all independent shipping trackers showing traffic at ~5-10% of normal. This appears to be political messaging rather than an accurate assessment.*
