## Critical Developments (August 7-10)

### 1. CRITICAL: Trump Signals Strategic Shift — "Low-Keying It" [OFFICIAL]

After months of US military strikes on Iran, sanctions and a blockade, President Donald Trump said on Sunday that his administration is watching economic pressure mount on Iran.

"We are low-keying it," Trump said during a brief phone call. "We are only semi-negotiating with them. We are just watching Iran with its huge inflation and the fact they have no money."

He stressed that Iran "is in very bad shape" economically and has no money to pay its troops. The US naval blockade has exacerbated the Iranian regime's economic crisis, Trump said.

"It will work out. It always works out. It's like a chess game," Trump said of the back-and-forth with Iran.

Behind the scenes: US officials said Trump was leaning toward resuming major combat operations against Iran a week ago but was convinced to de-escalate for now.

*Assessment: [OFFICIAL/REPORTED] This represents a significant tonal and strategic shift. Trump is explicitly backing away from military threats in favor of economic pressure. The admission that he was "convinced to de-escalate" suggests internal administration debate has tilted toward caution.*

---

### 2. CRITICAL: Joint Chiefs Chairman Seeking "Off-Ramp" [REPORTED]

Over the last few weeks, Chairman of the Joint Chiefs Gen. Dan Caine has privately made clear to other top Trump advisers that the US needs to find an off-ramp from the war with Iran — because the military options on the table to escalate the conflict could backfire and airpower alone is unlikely to achieve President Donald Trump's stated objectives. "Caine is looking for an off ramp," one of the sources said bluntly. Caine is not alone in his view that the war has reached a crossroads.

He has discussed concerns about the military options for escalating the conflict and raised the prospect of finding an off-ramp from the war with other key Cabinet officials, including CIA Director John Ratcliffe, Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Vice President JD Vance.

Trump has consistently appeared averse to the idea of deploying ground troops in Iran, instead signaling he believes that air bombing can force them to agree to a deal on his terms. Caine and others in Trump's cabinet privately view that outcome as unlikely.

*Assessment: [REPORTED] The nation's top military officer is now essentially arguing there is no easy military solution to the conflict. This, combined with munitions depletion and Trump's "low-keying" posture, represents a significant constraint on US escalation options.*

---

### 3. CRITICAL: Iran Escalates Demands — Nuclear Deal Conditions Now Apply to Hormuz [OFFICIAL]

There are no ongoing negotiations between Tehran and Washington, Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said Sunday. Tehran has vowed not to reopen the shipping lane "until America corrects its behavior," despite a looming agreement with Oman.

A senior Iranian government official declared Saturday that the Strait of Hormuz would not open until the Trump administration meets a wide-ranging set of demands. Iranian security official Mohammad Bagher Zolghadr wrote in a statement that the United States must lift its naval blockade, end sanctions, release frozen Iranian assets, end the war in Iran and pay restitution for damages.

Until several weeks ago, these demands were a prerequisite for a nuclear deal. Now, Iran is presenting them as conditions just for reopening the strait.

Iran's IRGC said any reopening of the strait depended on Washington accepting Tehran's terms, unrelated to ongoing Oman-brokered negotiations.

*Assessment: [OFFICIAL] Iran has significantly raised the bar. By making nuclear deal conditions prerequisites for Hormuz reopening, Tehran is signaling it expects fundamental concessions, not just a technical shipping arrangement. This dramatically widens the gap between US and Iranian positions.*

---

### 4. SIGNIFICANT: Iran-Oman Deal "Very Close" But Unsigned [OFFICIAL/REPORTED]

Iran says it's close to reaching a deal with Oman on managing the Strait of Hormuz, but warned that it would demand concessions from the US before reopening the waterway. Iranian officials insist the strait will not fully open until the US agrees to end the war, withdraw troops and make other long-sought concessions.

The two nations appeared to have made progress on a deal, with Trump stating a little over a week ago that he was holding off on attacking Iran as it continued to be hammered out. However, no agreement has yet been announced. Iran and Oman, which both border the Strait of Hormuz, have also been negotiating a deal over the vital waterway, and appeared to be close to closing in on an agreement.

However, on Saturday, an Iranian official, who reportedly has since been replaced, laid out a list of Iran's demands for the US before the strait could be reopened. The demands included a cessation of military action against Iran and its regional allies, the lifting of the blockade and sanctions, as well as war reparations.

*Assessment: [OFFICIAL/REPORTED] The Iran-Oman technical framework has been "very close" for over a week without materializing. Internal Iranian politics — with differing signals from diplomats, the IRGC, and the Supreme National Security Council — appear to be preventing finalization.*

### 5. SIGNIFICANT: Iran Attacks ADNOC Tanker — 16th Vessel Since War Began [VERIFIED]

An Abu Dhabi National Oil Company (ADNOC) oil tanker was targeted by a missile as it transited the Strait of Hormuz this morning, the company said in a statement.

The UAE condemned the strike as a "flagrant violation" of a UN Security Council resolution protecting freedom of navigation and commercial vessels.

The company said 15 of its vessels had been attacked by missiles and drones while transiting the strait since the start of the conflict, including three this week, killing one crew member and injuring 20 others.

Gulf Cooperation Council Secretary-General Jassim Mohammed al-Budaiwi condemned the latest attack as a "dangerous and unacceptable escalation", calling for international action to protect maritime navigation.

*Assessment: [VERIFIED] Iran continues attacking vessels even while negotiating with Oman. This undermines confidence that any technical shipping arrangement would actually prevent attacks and demonstrates Iran's willingness to use Hormuz as leverage regardless of diplomatic progress.*

---

### 6. SIGNIFICANT: Houthis Strike Aramco Jizan Refinery [VERIFIED]

A fire reported at a refinery belonging to the state oil giant Aramco has been extinguished. The blaze broke out early on Sunday at a site in the city of Jizan on the Red Sea, the Saudi Ministry of Energy wrote in a statement. No casualties were reported.

Yemen's Houthis said they attacked an oil facility Sunday in southwestern Saudi Arabia, the latest attack by the Iranian-backed rebels on the kingdom.

The attacks have added to growing strains on a 2022 truce in the war between the Houthis and Yemen's internationally recognized government. The Houthis, who took control of Sanaa in 2014, also declared a blockade on Saudi Arabia in the Red Sea last month.

*Assessment: [VERIFIED] Houthi attacks on Saudi energy infrastructure are intensifying. This is at least the second fire at the Jizan facility within a month, signaling sustained pressure on Saudi Arabia despite the new defense pact.*

### 7. MODERATE: Oil Rising — $84-87 Range [VERIFIED]

As of 7:15 a.m. Eastern Time today, oil sold for $87.55 per barrel (using Brent as the benchmark). That's 11 cents higher than yesterday morning and approximately a $21 rise over the past year.

Brent climbed above $84 per barrel on Monday, marking a third consecutive session of gains as uncertainty persisted over efforts to reopen the Strait of Hormuz. Iran said over the weekend that talks with Oman on establishing a shipping route through the strategic waterway were nearing an agreement, but warned that any arrangement would not lead to an immediate reopening.

Oil reversed some gains in post-settlement trading after reports that the US could lift its naval blockade once commercial shipping through the strait resumes without restrictions.

*Assessment: [VERIFIED] Markets are pricing in continued uncertainty. The $84-87 range reflects expectations that deal delays will persist while stopping short of crisis pricing.*

### 8. MODERATE: Lebanon Front — Rome Talks Continue [REPORTED]

Israel said two of its soldiers were killed in southern Lebanon on Wednesday while Lebanese authorities said Israeli retaliatory attacks killed at least one person, a flare-up in violence that coincided with the latest round of talks between the two countries. The Rome talks, which began on Tuesday and are expected to continue through Thursday, come after the two countries agreed in June to a US-brokered security arrangement intended to ease hostilities along the border.

Lebanon and Israel are holding talks in Rome over Israel's withdrawal from southern Lebanon, but the session is unlikely to yield further pullbacks as the two sides remain split over disarming Hezbollah. The negotiations are the seventh overall, following five sessions in Washington. They aim to shore up a fragile ceasefire and build on a framework agreement signed in June.

*Assessment: [REPORTED] The Lebanon front remains tense but stable. The two Israeli soldier deaths on August 6 were the first since June, but talks have continued rather than collapsed. The fundamental issue — Hezbollah disarmament — remains unresolved.*

## Emerging Signals

**Strategic Shift Confirmed:** President Donald Trump said Sunday the United States is "low keying it" with Iran for now, signaling he will allow mounting economic pressure and the US naval blockade to squeeze Tehran rather than immediately resume major military operations. This is the clearest signal yet that the administration is pivoting from strike threats to economic attrition.

**Military Leadership Aligned on Off-Ramp:** Gen Dan Caine privately urged senior Trump administration officials to seek an off-ramp from the war with Iran. The discussions reflected concern over military limits, retaliation risks and strained US weapons stocks. The top military officer's view is now aligned with the president's shift.

**Iranian Internal Discord:** Axios noted the discord present in Iranian leadership, which seemingly remains unaligned on achieving an end to the conflict. A diplomat from one of the mediating countries said Zolghadr's statement reflected the political infighting within the regime. Different Iranian power centers are sending conflicting signals.

**Oman Warns Attacks Could Derail Talks:** Oman has also warned that ongoing attacks on shipping could derail progress. Even the mediator is expressing concern about Iran's continued vessel strikes.

**Vice President Signals Long Game:** Speaking with Fox News on Saturday, Vice President JD Vance signaled that the US was taking a multifaceted approach to dealing with Iran, explaining that it would be some time before the conflict would reach a conclusion. "We're really in the middle of the game here. This thing is not over. It's obviously not at the beginning," Vance said. "We're applying a whole host of tools–diplomatic, economic, military tools–to ensure that we get the best outcome for the American people."
