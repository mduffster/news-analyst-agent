## Key Developments Since Prior Report (August 7-10)

### 1. Trump Signals Strategic Shift — "Low-Keying It" Replaces Threats

**[OFFICIAL]** Trump said during a brief phone call with Axios: "We are low-keying it. We are only semi-negotiating with them. We are just watching Iran with its huge inflation and the fact they have no money."

**[OFFICIAL]** He stressed that Iran "is in very bad shape" economically and has no money to pay its troops. The U.S. naval blockade has exacerbated the Iranian regime's economic crisis, Trump said.

**[OFFICIAL]** "It will work out. It always works out. It's like a chess game," Trump said of the back-and-forth with Iran.

**[REPORTED]** U.S. officials said Trump was leaning toward resuming major combat operations against Iran a week ago but was convinced to de-escalate for now.

*Assessment: This represents a notable tonal shift from Trump's pattern of alternating threats and pullbacks. The "low-keying it" posture suggests the administration is pivoting from military escalation to an economic pressure strategy, likely influenced by the munitions depletion issue and Gen. Caine's advocacy for an off-ramp.*

### 2. Chairman of Joint Chiefs Privately Seeking "Off-Ramp" from Iran War

**[REPORTED]** Chairman of the Joint Chiefs Gen. Dan Caine has privately made clear to other top Trump advisers that the US needs to find an off-ramp from the war with Iran — because the military options on the table to escalate the conflict could backfire and airpower alone is unlikely to achieve President Donald Trump's stated objectives. "Caine is looking for an off ramp," one of the sources said bluntly. Caine is not alone in his view that the war has reached a crossroads.

**[REPORTED]** He reportedly discussed the possibility of an off-ramp with other top advisers to US President Donald Trump, including Vice President JD Vance, CIA chief John Ratcliffe and Secretary of State Marco Rubio.

**[REPORTED]** In meetings with the president, Caine has made clear the potential downsides of continued U.S. actions in Iran, but he also reassured Trump that "we could absolutely wreck" the Iranians if he would like to proceed.

*Assessment: This is a significant development. The top US military officer is now essentially arguing there is no easy military solution. Combined with munitions depletion, this constrains Trump's options and strengthens the case for a negotiated settlement.*

---

### 3. Iran-Oman Deal "Very Close" But Full Reopening Tied to Steep US Demands

**[OFFICIAL]** Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said that Iran is "very close" to reaching a deal with Oman on managing the Strait of Hormuz. But he cautioned that the reopening of the strait was "subject to other conditions," including the US making amends for what he said was a violation of the memorandum of understanding agreed between the two countries in June.

**[OFFICIAL]** There are no ongoing negotiations between Tehran and Washington, Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said Sunday. Tehran has vowed not to reopen the critical shipping lane "until America corrects its behavior," despite a looming agreement with Oman reported to have been close for days.

**[REPORTED]** Mohammad Bagher Zolghadr, secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council, presented Tehran's key demands Saturday. He called on the U.S. to end its naval blockade in the strait, lift sanctions on Tehran and pay war reparations. He also demanded that the U.S. military withdraw from Iran and stop attacks on Tehran's allies.

**[REPORTED]** Until several weeks ago, these demands were a prerequisite for a nuclear deal. Now, Iran is presenting them as conditions just for reopening the strait.

*Assessment: Iran has escalated its demands — what were previously conditions for a nuclear deal are now presented as conditions merely for reopening Hormuz. The gap between US and Iranian positions has widened significantly.*

### 4. Saudi-Turkey-Pakistan "Mecca Joint Defence Agreement" Signed

**[VERIFIED]** The Mecca Joint Defence Agreement is a trilateral mutual defence pact signed on 7 August 2026 in Mecca, Saudi Arabia, by Saudi Arabia, Pakistan, and Turkey. Signed by Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan, and Pakistani Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif, the agreement establishes a collective defence arrangement under which an armed attack against any one of the three states is to be regarded as an attack against all three.

**[VERIFIED]** The deal will harness the military and financial strengths of each country. While Turkiye has NATO's second-largest military, Saudi Arabia is the world's top oil exporter, and Pakistan is the only Muslim nation with nuclear weapons.

**[REPORTED]** The Trump administration, which wants to see more burden-sharing from top US allies, will likely look favorably on a pact meant to advance collective regional deterrence with an eye toward Iran.

*Assessment: This is a major regional realignment. The "NATO-style" Article 5 commitment creates a new collective defense bloc that positions these three powers between Israel and Iran. The nuclear dimension (Pakistan) adds significant deterrence weight.*

### 5. Iran Attacks ADNOC Tanker in Hormuz — 16th Vessel Hit Since War Began

**[VERIFIED]** An Abu Dhabi National Oil Company (ADNOC) oil tanker was targeted by a missile as it transited the Strait of Hormuz, the company said in a statement released by the UAE's state-run WAM news agency.

**[VERIFIED]** The UAE's foreign ministry and Gulf Cooperation Council explicitly blamed Iran for the attack.

**[VERIFIED]** The company said 15 of its vessels had been attacked by missiles and drones while transiting the strait since the start of the conflict, including three this week, killing one crew member and injuring 20 others.

**[VERIFIED]** Gulf Cooperation Council Secretary-General Jassim Mohammed al-Budaiwi condemned the latest attack as a "dangerous and unacceptable escalation", calling for international action to protect maritime navigation.

*Assessment: Iran continues to attack vessels in Hormuz even while negotiating with Oman. This undercuts confidence that a technical shipping arrangement would actually prevent attacks.*

### 6. Houthis Strike Aramco Jizan Refinery — Second Fire in Weeks

**[VERIFIED]** A fire reported at a refinery belonging to the state oil giant Aramco has been extinguished, authorities in Saudi Arabia say. The blaze broke out early on Sunday at a site in the city of Jizan on the Red Sea, the Saudi Ministry of Energy wrote in a statement on X. No casualties were reported.

**[OFFICIAL]** Houthi military spokesperson Yahya Saree said in a post on X that Yemen's armed forces had targeted the Aramco refinery in Jizan with a drone, describing the strike as precise. He said the operation was carried out in response to what he described as Saudi violations of Yemeni airspace over Saada and Hajjah provinces.

**[REPORTED]** Sunday's blaze was at least the second fire at the facility within a month. Satellite images showed a storage tank burning in late July after Yemen's Iran-aligned Houthi movement claimed it had targeted Aramco installations in Jizan and Yanbu.

**[UNCONFIRMED]** NASA satellite data confirmed a significant thermal event at the Berri Gas Plant near Jubail, one of the world's largest petrochemical hubs, with fire power reaching approximately 125 MW. Arab sources reported gas facilities targeted in an explosion, though Saudi officials have not confirmed the cause.

*Assessment: Houthi attacks on Saudi energy infrastructure are intensifying, with multiple facilities now targeted. The unconfirmed Jubail incident, if verified, would represent a significant escalation.*

### 7. Houthis Attack Saudi Base in Yemen — Escalation Continues

**[VERIFIED]** The Houthis on Friday carried out an attack on Saudi forces at a base in eastern Yemen, according to the group's spokesperson. Saree accused Saudi Arabia of continuing to "mass its forces, incite them and push them into the furnace of a futile and losing war of aggression." He said the Houthis targeted concentrations of Saudi forces, warehouses, vehicles and military equipment at the Sahn al-Jinn camp.

**[VERIFIED]** The border strike came hours following a large-scale Houthi missile and drone assault on military camps in the Marib and Hadramout governorates. At least 58 government soldiers were killed and dozens wounded in the attacks, making it the deadliest strike against Yemen's internationally recognized government forces since 2022.

*Assessment: The Houthis are now directly targeting Saudi military personnel in Yemen, not just Saudi-backed Yemeni government forces. This represents an escalation in the Yemen theater.*

### 8. Lebanon Front Stable But Tense — Rome Talks Continue

**[VERIFIED]** Two Israeli soldiers were killed in an explosion in Lebanon in what are the first Israeli deaths since a truce between Israel and Hezbollah was announced nearly two months ago. Israel said four other soldiers were seriously wounded.

**[VERIFIED]** A truce has been held since June 20, though Israel's military continues to occupy parts of southern Lebanon.

**[REPORTED]** The talks in Rome have focused on implementation of "pilot zones" where Israeli forces would withdraw and the Lebanese army would take control. Implementation began last month with an initial pilot zone in an area that wasn't occupied by Israeli forces.

*Assessment: No major escalation since the August 6 soldier deaths. The Rome talks continue, but the fundamental issues — Israeli withdrawal, Hezbollah disarmament — remain unresolved.*

### 9. Oil Rising — $84-87 Range

**[VERIFIED]** As of 7:15 a.m. Eastern Time today, oil sold for $87.55 per barrel (using Brent as the benchmark). That's 11 cents higher than yesterday morning and approximately a $21 rise over the past year.

**[VERIFIED]** Brent climbed above $84 per barrel on Monday, marking a third consecutive session of gains as uncertainty persisted over efforts to reopen the Strait of Hormuz.

**[REPORTED]** Oil reversed some gains in post-settlement trading after reports that the US could lift its naval blockade once commercial shipping through the strait resumes without restrictions.

*Assessment: Oil continues to rise as Hormuz deal delays persist. Markets are pricing in continued uncertainty rather than imminent resolution.*

## Emerging Signals

**Strategic Shift in US Posture:** Trump's "low-keying it" represents a departure from the threat-pullback-threat cycle. Combined with Gen. Caine's off-ramp advocacy, this suggests the administration is pivoting to an economic attrition strategy rather than military escalation.

**Iranian Demands Escalating, Not Moderating:** Iran has raised the bar — conditions previously required for a nuclear deal are now demanded just for Hormuz reopening. This may reflect internal hardliner influence or a calculation that time favors Tehran.

**Regional Realignment Accelerating:** The Mecca defense pact creates a new security architecture. Turkey's inclusion — a NATO member — adds complexity to the US-Iran dynamic. This could pressure Iran or complicate US freedom of action.

**Houthis Operating on Multiple Fronts:** Attacks on Saudi energy infrastructure (Jizan refinery), military bases (Sahn al-Jinn), and continued Yemen operations show Houthi capacity undiminished despite Saudi coalition response.

**Iran Still Attacking Vessels While "Negotiating":** The ADNOC tanker attack on Saturday — the 16th vessel hit — demonstrates that tactical arrangements with Oman won't guarantee safe passage. Iran appears to be using Hormuz as leverage regardless of any deal framework.
