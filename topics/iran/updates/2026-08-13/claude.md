## Key Developments Since Prior Report (August 12)

### 1. Iran Flatly Denies Ceasefire Extension Agreement

**[OFFICIAL]** There were no discussions between Iran and the US to extend the ceasefire because from Tehran's perspective the deal had no start date and therefore there was nothing to extend, a senior Iranian source told Reuters.

**[OFFICIAL]** "There is no talk of an extension because, from Iran's perspective, there is no period that began and therefore nothing to extend. The United States violated the interim agreement 48 hours after it was reached and withdrew from it a few days later."

**[OFFICIAL]** According to the Iranian source, one issue currently being discussed through mediators is a possible US return to the interim agreement and the establishment of a timeline for implementing its commitments. "There has been absolutely no progress on this issue," the source said.

**[REPORTED]** President Donald Trump said the deal was "over" on July 7, while Iran's Foreign Ministry declared it "suspended" a week later.

*Assessment: This is a significant contradiction to yesterday's Pakistan-sourced reports claiming both sides had "agreed" to extend the ceasefire. Iran's position that the original deal never entered force — and that the US violated it "48 hours" after signing — reveals a fundamental disagreement about the status of the June MOU. Yesterday's optimism was premature.*

### 2. Araghchi Warns US of "Bigger Miscalculation" on Hormuz

**[OFFICIAL]** Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi posted on X: "The U.S. has long miscalculated due to intelligence failures. Case in point: The war on Iran. Now, an even bigger miscalculation on the Strait of Hormuz. Worse than fake news is fake intelligence."

**[OFFICIAL]** "Now, the even bigger miscalculation is the Strait of Hormuz. Fake intelligence is worse than fake news. Be careful," Araghchi emphasized.

**[REPORTED]** Iran has accused the US of allowing faulty intelligence to drive it into war, as new reports revealed that CIA analysts doubted an Israeli warning that triggered a secret security operation during President Donald Trump's visit to Turkey.

*Assessment: Araghchi's statement is a direct response to Trump's claim yesterday that the US has "total control" over Hormuz. The "fake intelligence" framing also appears to reference emerging reports questioning US intelligence assessments that preceded the war.*

**[VERIFIED]** Traffic through the vital waterway remains minuscule, despite President Donald Trump's insistence that the strait is open and the US still controls it.

**[VERIFIED]** Traffic through the Strait of Hormuz is back down following a brief pick-up. Vessel traffic is nowhere near the hundred-plus ships a day that were traversing the strait before the war.

**[VERIFIED]** Traffic continues to flow through Strait of Hormuz despite increased kinetic military activity. Preliminary data show 78 transits during 3 to 9 August, down from 95 the previous week. Non-Iranian-linked traffic is above levels seen just after the collapse of the MoU, but a far cry from normal volumes.

*Assessment: Trump's escalating rhetoric — from "total control" to wanting to "KEEP" Hormuz — conflicts with both traffic data and Iran's counterclaims. Lloyd's List data shows only 78 transits in the week of Aug 3-9, compared to ~600+ that would be normal.*

### 4. Pakistan Confirms 3 Nationals Killed in Houthi Attack — Creates Diplomatic Tension

**[OFFICIAL]** Pakistan's Deputy Prime Minister and Foreign Minister Ishaq Dar on Wednesday confirmed that three Pakistanis were killed and another was injured in the attack. The fourth crew member killed was an Indonesian national.

**[VERIFIED]** The attack on the Egyptian-owned Tihamah on Tuesday marks the first shipping deaths linked to the Iran-aligned Houthis since the United States and Israel launched the war on Iran on February 28. Yemen's Transport Ministry said three Pakistani nationals and one Indonesian national were killed after the Houthis fired three ballistic missiles at the commercial ship.

**[VERIFIED]** Two Yemeni rescuers from the National Resistance Forces (NRF), which opposes the Houthis, were also killed when the Houthis allegedly struck again after rescue teams arrived.

**[REPORTED]** The attack comes at a particularly sensitive moment for Pakistan. Just days ago, Pakistan, Saudi Arabia and Türkiye signed a landmark mutual defence agreement in Makkah under which an armed attack against one member is to be treated as an attack against all three.

*Assessment: The killing of three Pakistani nationals by Iranian-backed Houthis creates an awkward position for Pakistan, which is simultaneously mediating the US-Iran conflict and bound by a new mutual defense pact with Saudi Arabia. This tests Pakistan's balancing act.*

### 6. Iran-Oman Hormuz Talks Continue — But Reopening Still Conditional

**[VERIFIED]** Oman's Foreign Ministry said today that the talks have been "positive and constructive," and Iran's Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said the two countries are "very close" to a deal on managing the waterway. Araghchi warned, however, that the agreement alone won't resolve the crisis. The foreign minister said reopening the vital strait would still be "subject to other conditions," including compensation from the US.

**[VERIFIED]** Tehran has laid out the conditions for a Hormuz reopening, with Iran's top security official, Mohsen Rezaei, saying the strait would remain closed unless the US releases Iran's frozen assets and puts an end to conflicts throughout the region, including in Lebanon.

*Assessment: Despite technical progress on Iran-Oman arrangements, Iran continues to link any actual reopening to broader US concessions. A technical agreement is achievable; implementation remains contingent on the wider political deadlock.*
