## Critical Developments Since May 15

### 1. Drone Strike Hits UAE's Barakah Nuclear Plant

**[VERIFIED]** A drone strike has sparked a fire on the perimeter of the Barakah Nuclear Energy Plant in the United Arab Emirates (UAE), raising new concerns over a potential new regional escalation amid a fragile ceasefire between Iran and the United States.

**[OFFICIAL]** The United Arab Emirates intercepted three UAVs that entered the country from its western border. Two of the UAVs were successfully intercepted, while the third "struck an electrical generator outside the inner perimeter of the Barakah Nuclear Power Plant in the Al Dhafra Region."

**[VERIFIED]** No injuries were reported, and officials said radiation levels remained normal. "The IAEA has been informed by the UAE that radiation levels at the Barakah nuclear power plant remain normal and no injuries were reported." "Emergency diesel generators are currently providing power to the nuclear power plant's Unit 3."

**[OFFICIAL]** The International Atomic Energy Agency on X says its head, Rafael Grossi, voiced concern and adds: "Military activity that threatens nuclear safety is unacceptable."

**[OFFICIAL]** The UAE's Ministry of Foreign Affairs posted a statement on X on Sunday condemning "the unprovoked terrorist attack" in "the strongest terms." The statement added: "The UAE emphasized that it will not tolerate any threat to its security and sovereignty under any circumstances, and that it reserves its full, sovereign, legitimate, diplomatic, and military rights to respond."

**[VERIFIED]** Three drones entered from the western border; two were intercepted by air defences, while one caused a fire outside the plant's inner perimeter. Officials have not attributed responsibility; the reference to a western border raises questions over potential non-Iranian launch points.

**[REPORTED]** The UAE and other Gulf states have come under heavy attack from Iran since the war broke out on February 28. A conditional ceasefire agreed between the US and Iran on April 8 led to a halt in hostilities for several weeks, but Tehran resumed strikes on the Emirates this month.

**Assessment:** This is the most significant escalation since the last update. Targeting a nuclear facility — even its perimeter infrastructure — crosses a critical threshold. The IAEA's explicit statement that "military activity that threatens nuclear safety is unacceptable" signals international alarm. The "western border" launch point suggests Iraq-based proxies, maintaining Iranian deniability while demonstrating capability to strike the UAE's most sensitive infrastructure.

### 2. Iran Formalizes Hormuz Transit Toll Regime via PGSA

**[VERIFIED]** On May 16, 2026, Iran confirmed it will soon unveil full details of a transit toll mechanism for the Strait of Hormuz, administered by the newly established Persian Gulf Strait Authority (PGSA). Iran established the Persian Gulf Strait Authority in May 2026, presenting it as a sovereign regulatory body governing transit through the Strait.

**[VERIFIED]** Vessels intending to transit receive an email from info@PGSA.ir setting out the requirements. Operators must then submit a "Vessel Information Declaration" covering ownership, insurance, crew manifests, cargo, and intended routing.

**[REPORTED]** Iran is moving to formalize a state-administered transit-toll regime under the new Persian Gulf Strait Authority (PGSA), with reported per-transit payments of up to $2 million settled in Chinese yuan. No official tariff has been published, but reports indicate vessels have paid up to $2 million per transit, settled in Chinese yuan. Earlier reporting referenced a $1.00-per-barrel toll for tanker cargoes.

**[VERIFIED]** Iran has now institutionalised its control over the Strait of Hormuz by creating the Persian Gulf Strait Authority (PGSA), which requires vessels to obtain permits and pay tolls before transiting. Nations such as India and Pakistan have negotiated passage rights, while ships linked to the US or Israel face outright denial. The system is already in force, with some vessels reportedly paying up to $2 million in Chinese yuan for clearance.

**[VERIFIED]** New billboards in Tehran's metro system claim Iran could generate up to $100 billion annually from Strait of Hormuz revenues.

**[REPORTED]** Six India-flagged vessels transited inbound on May 18 as a coordinated cluster following bilateral engagement with Iran, indicating functional safe-passage arrangements outside the coalition framework.

**[REPORTED]** The PGSA is the executive arm of a twelve-article bill, the Law on Establishing Iran's Sovereignty over the Strait of Hormuz, ratified by the Majlis National Security and Foreign Policy Committee on 21 April and awaiting full-chamber vote. The bill bans Israeli vessels absolutely, conditions the passage of vessels from "hostile" nations on Supreme National Security Council approval, denominates all transit fees in Iranian rial, and authorises confiscation of up to twenty per cent of cargo value as penalty for non-compliance.

**Assessment:** The PGSA represents a fundamental transformation from wartime blockade to permanent institutional control. Iran is building the infrastructure for indefinite administration of Hormuz, regardless of whether a peace deal is reached. The $100 billion revenue projection on Tehran billboards indicates this is now regime policy. The India carve-out demonstrates the selective nature of the system: reward partners, punish adversaries.

### 3. Israel-Lebanon Ceasefire Extended 45 Days

**[OFFICIAL]** On 15 May, the truce was extended for another 45 days.

**[VERIFIED]** Israel and Lebanon have agreed to a 45-day extension of the ceasefire US President Donald Trump announced last month, the US State Department said on Friday. The announcement came as Israeli strikes continued across southern Lebanon overnight and into Saturday.

**[OFFICIAL]** The two countries also agreed to expand military-to-military communication and co-ordination through a new effort being overseen by the Pentagon. In addition to the diplomatic effort, a parallel track will be launched at the Pentagon on May 29 with military delegations from both countries.

**[VERIFIED]** The death toll in the latest round of fighting between Israel and the Iranian-backed Hezbollah in Lebanon has surpassed 3,000, Lebanon's health ministry said Monday. The ministry said the toll is now 3,020 in the fighting that has not stopped despite a fragile ceasefire, including 292 women and 211 children.

**[VERIFIED]** Lebanon, whose government has committed to disarming Hezbollah, is demanding a halt to Israel's strikes and full Israeli withdrawal, while Israel, which accuses Beirut of failing to disarm Hezbollah, demands that the terror group give up its weapons. Hezbollah itself has rejected the direct talks and insisted its weapons are not up for discussion.

**[VERIFIED]** The war has precipitated a still-developing humanitarian crisis, resulted in deaths of more than 2,800 people (both militants and civilians) from Israeli strikes in Lebanon, seen the forced displacement of over 1 million (>20% of the country's population), and entailed the deployment of matériel and tactics that potentially constitute war crimes.

**Assessment:** The 45-day extension establishes a more structured process with Pentagon military-to-military coordination and State Department political negotiations (June 2-3). However, with 3,020 Lebanese deaths since March 2, including continued strikes during the "ceasefire," this remains a ceasefire in name only. The fundamental gap — Lebanon wants Israel to stop strikes, Israel wants Hezbollah disarmed — is unbridged.

### 4. US-Iran Negotiations: Reported Sanctions Waiver Offer

**[REPORTED]** A source close to the Iranian negotiating team told Tasnim that, unlike its previous texts, the United States has accepted in its new proposal to waive Iran's oil sanctions during the negotiation period. Iran insists that the removal of all sanctions against the country must be part of the US commitments. The United States, however, has proposed a waiver of OFAC (Office of Foreign Assets Control) sanctions until a final understanding is reached.

**[REPORTED]** An Iranian media outlet reported that the US proposed a temporary waiver of sanctions on the Islamic Republic's oil, a key demand by Tehran to agree to a peace deal and reopen the Strait of Hormuz. The US hasn't confirmed offering a waiver.

**[REPORTED]** US President Donald Trump will freeze sanctions on Iranian oil during ongoing nuclear talks, the semi-official Tasnim News Agency reports. In its new proposal, the source says, Iran has again focused on securing an end to the war, reopening the Strait of Hormuz, and lifting maritime sanctions. The more contentious issues around Iran's nuclear program and uranium enrichment, which remain the most difficult parts of the negotiations, have been deferred to later rounds of talks.

**[OFFICIAL]** On May 16, Trump sent Iran a nuclear proposal and warned that swift progress was necessary to avoid serious consequences. The Trump administration has increasingly demanded that Iran abandon uranium enrichment, making this a central issue. Khamenei rejected the proposal, dismissing this US demand as "excessive and outrageous". He accused Trump of lying about seeking peace and declared him unworthy of a response. Khamenei also reiterated that Israel is "cancerous tumour" that must be uprooted.

**[OFFICIAL]** US President Trump posted on social media that "For Iran, the Clock is Ticking, and they better get moving, FAST, or there won't be anything left of them. TIME IS OF THE ESSENCE!" Meanwhile, Tehran stated on Monday that it is continuing dialogue with the US via Pakistan.

**[UNCONFIRMED]** The reported US sanctions waiver has not been confirmed by US officials. Iranian media reports conflict — Tasnim claims concessions while other outlets emphasize US "excessive demands."

**Assessment:** If the US has offered temporary oil sanctions waivers, it represents meaningful movement from previous "maximum pressure" positions. However, the lack of US confirmation and conflicting Iranian media reports suggest either disinformation, internal Iranian divisions, or negotiating posturing. The fundamental gap on enrichment remains: the US demands abandonment; Iran considers enrichment a non-negotiable right.

### 6. Hormuz Traffic Remains Near Zero

**[VERIFIED]** Prior to the outbreak of hostilities, about 138 vessels transited the Strait of Hormuz daily. Since March 1, 2026, marine traffic data shows growing clusters of loitering vessels on both sides of the Strait of Hormuz, with many refraining from transiting due to safety concerns. Since May 6, open transits have fallen to near zero vessels per day.

**[VERIFIED]** On 16 May 2026, 16 vessels transited the Strait of Hormuz — 6 inbound, 10 outbound. One outbound transit (~10%) crossed dark. The remaining transits were AIS-correlated; the inbound cohort splits evenly across tanker, bulk carrier, and cargo hulls.

**[VERIFIED]** Pre-conflict transit volumes through the Strait of Hormuz averaged approximately 140 vessels per day. Windward data indicates that early May transit volumes had dropped dramatically, with only nine transits on May 11.

**[VERIFIED]** Sixth consecutive day with zero crude liftings from Kharg, deepening the export bottleneck on Iran's primary crude outlet.

**Assessment:** Traffic remains at approximately 10% of pre-war levels (16 transits vs. 140 normal). The PGSA formalization may allow selective increase in compliant traffic, but most commercial operators face a choice between US and Iranian sanctions regimes. Iran's own exports remain bottlenecked with no crude liftings from Kharg for six consecutive days.
