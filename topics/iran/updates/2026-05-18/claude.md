## Daily Update: US-Israel-Iran Conflict (May 18, 2026)

### Executive Summary

**Drone strike hits UAE nuclear plant as Iran formalizes Hormuz toll regime. Oil slips on reported US sanctions waiver offer. Lebanon death toll passes 3,000. Negotiations continue via Pakistan despite Trump's "clock is ticking" threat.**

**Bottom line:** The weekend brought a significant escalation with a drone strike hitting a power generator at the UAE's Barakah nuclear plant, prompting condemnation from the IAEA and Gulf states. Iran has formalized its Hormuz "tollbooth" system through the newly created Persian Gulf Strait Authority, institutionalizing its control over the strait. Despite this, talks appear to be continuing — Iranian state media reports the US has offered a temporary sanctions waiver on oil, a potential breakthrough if confirmed, which contributed to oil falling to $110.08 from Friday's $111.04.

### Critical New Developments (May 16-18)

#### Drone Strike Hits UAE Nuclear Plant

**[VERIFIED]** The United Arab Emirates announced on Sunday that the country had come under attack by three drones, including one that struck a power generator near the Barakah nuclear power station. The UAE Ministry of Defense said the country's air defense systems intercepted three unmanned aerial vehicles that entered Emirati airspace from the western borders.

**[VERIFIED]** According to the ministry, two of the drones were successfully destroyed in the air by defense systems, while the third drone hit an electricity generator located outside the perimeter of the Barakah nuclear facility in the Al Zafra area.

**[OFFICIAL]** The UN atomic watchdog, the IAEA, expresses "grave concern" over the drone strike near a United Arab Emirates nuclear plant that triggered a fire, though it said that radiation levels remained normal. The International Atomic Energy Agency says its head, Rafael Grossi, voiced the concern and adds: "Military activity that threatens nuclear safety is unacceptable."

**[VERIFIED]** The International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) said Sunday's incident in the UAE forced one reactor to rely temporarily on emergency diesel generators.

**[OFFICIAL]** "Military activity that threatens nuclear safety is unacceptable," the IAEA director general Rafael Grossi said, urging maximum restraint near nuclear facilities. Sheikh Abdullah bin Zayed, Deputy Prime Minister and Minister of Foreign Affairs, on Sunday strongly condemned the "treacherous terrorist attack" that caused a fire near Abu Dhabi's Barakah Nuclear Energy Plant.

**[REPORTED]** The drone attack on the civilian Barakah Nuclear Power Plant was intended to "send a message" to the Emiratis, two sources familiar with the details of the strike told The Jerusalem Post on Sunday. According to one of the sources, the drone deliberately targeted one of the facility's energy suppliers in order to convey the message: "We can also strike the nuclear reactor itself and trigger a nuclear incident." "In any case, it's Iran who ordered the attack," the two sources told the Post.

**[VERIFIED]** The launch point of the drones remained unclear, but on Sunday, Saudi Arabia also reported it had intercepted three drones that had been launched from Iraq, where some Iran-allied groups operate. If Iranian Shahed-136 drones, which have an estimated range of 2,000km to 2,500km, were fired from Iraqi territory, both Saudi Arabia and the UAE would fall well within their reach.

**Assessment:** This is a highly significant escalation. While the attack did not damage the reactor itself, hitting a nuclear facility's power infrastructure is a deliberate message about Iranian capabilities. The use of Iraq as a potential launch point suggests Tehran is using proxies to maintain plausible deniability while demonstrating it can target the UAE's most sensitive infrastructure. The IAEA's explicit condemnation marks a shift from its typically measured language.

#### Iran Formalizes Hormuz Transit Fee Regime

**[VERIFIED]** Tehran has launched a formal body to manage and charge ships for transit through the Strait of Hormuz. On May 16, 2026, the chairman of Iran's Parliament National Security and Foreign Policy Committee, Ebrahim Azizi, confirmed that Tehran will "unveil soon" the full details of a new mechanism to regulate maritime traffic through the Strait of Hormuz, including fees collected for what Iranian officials describe as "specialized services." The mechanism is administered by the Persian Gulf Strait Authority (PGSA), a recently established body that vets and approves transits through a chokepoint that, in pre-conflict conditions, carried roughly 20% of global seaborne oil trade.

**[VERIFIED]** Vessels seeking transit must apply via info@PGSA.ir, disclosing ownership, insurance, crew manifests, and cargo before being granted a permit. Operators must then submit a "Vessel Information Declaration" covering ownership, insurance, crew manifests, cargo, and intended routing. A transit permit is issued only after the PGSA accepts the submission and a fee is paid.

**[REPORTED]** No official tariff has been published. Reports indicate some vessels have already paid up to $2 million per transit, with payment made in Chinese yuan. Earlier reporting also referenced a $1.00-per-barrel toll structure for tanker cargoes.

**[OFFICIAL]** New billboards in Tehran's metro system claim Iran could generate up to $100 billion annually from Strait of Hormuz revenues.

**[VERIFIED]** For commercial operators, the most consequential dimension of the new regime is not the cost of transit. It is the legal exposure created by paying for it. On May 1, 2026, the U.S. Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) issued guidance warning that payments made to Iranian-linked entities in exchange for safe passage through the Strait of Hormuz could constitute prohibited transactions under existing U.S. sanctions, exposing non-U.S. firms to secondary sanctions risk.

**Assessment:** The creation of the PGSA represents Iran's institutionalization of what began as ad-hoc extortion into a formal state mechanism. This is a shift from "temporary disruption" rhetoric to permanent sovereignty assertion. The OFAC warning creates a significant dilemma for shipping companies: pay Iran and face US sanctions, or don't pay and risk seizure/attack.

#### Israel-Lebanon Ceasefire Extended 45 Days

**[VERIFIED]** On 15 May, the truce was extended for another 45 days.

**[VERIFIED]** In Washington, Israel and Lebanon agreed to a 45-day extension of their ceasefire after two "productive" days of talks, which will be followed by more negotiations June 2-3, State Department spokesman Tommy Pigott said.

**[VERIFIED]** The two countries also agreed to expand military-to-military communication and co-ordination through a new effort being overseen by the Pentagon. Mr Pigott said that in addition to the diplomatic effort, a parallel track will be launched at the Pentagon on May 29 with military delegations from both countries.

**[VERIFIED]** At least 380 people have been killed by Israeli attacks, mainly in southern Lebanon, since the ceasefire took effect, according to Lebanon's Health Ministry.

**[VERIFIED]** The number of people killed in Israeli attacks on Lebanon since the start of the war has exceeded 3,000, according to health officials.

**Assessment:** The 45-day extension averts immediate collapse and establishes a more structured process with Pentagon involvement. However, the continued Israeli strikes killing hundreds during the "ceasefire" period, and the Lebanon death toll now exceeding 3,000, underscore that this is a ceasefire in name only.

#### US-Iran Negotiations: Sanctions Waiver Report

**[REPORTED]** The United States has accepted suspending Iran's oil sanctions during ongoing nuclear negotiations, Guards-linked Tasnim News Agency reported on Monday citing a source close to Iran's negotiating team. Washington's latest proposed text, unlike previous drafts, accepts waiving sanctions on Iranian oil exports during the negotiation period, the report said. The source said the proposal involves temporary waivers issued by the US Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) until a final agreement is reached, while Iran insists that all sanctions must be fully lifted as part of any deal.

**[REPORTED]** The US has spoken of establishing a development and reconstruction fund to meet Iran's war compensation demand, the IRGC outlet Tasnim reported. The US has also agreed to release 25% of Iran's assets and waive oil sanctions during the talks.

**[REPORTED]** The report said Tehran's new proposal includes more words on Iran's commitment not to pursue a nuclear weapon, but no detailed commitments about suspending uranium enrichment or handing over its existing stockpile of highly enriched uranium. The US official quoted by Axios also said no sanctions relief will happen "for free" without reciprocal action by Iran, after Iranian state media claimed the US had agreed to waive Iran's oil sanctions during the negotiations.

**[OFFICIAL]** Iran's Foreign Ministry spokesperson says Iran responds to US proposal through Pakistan.

**[OFFICIAL]** On Sunday, Iran's Fars news agency said Washington had presented a five-point list, which included a demand for Iran to keep only one nuclear site in operation and transfer its stockpile of highly enriched uranium to the United States. The US had refused to release "even 25 percent" of Iran's frozen assets or pay any reparations for war damage, according to Fars.

**[UNCONFIRMED]** The conflicting reports from Iranian media — Tasnim claiming the US offered oil sanctions waivers and 25% asset release, Fars claiming the US refused even 25% — suggest either disinformation or internal Iranian divisions about messaging.

**[OFFICIAL]** US President Trump posted on social media that "For Iran, the Clock is Ticking, and they better get moving, FAST, or there won't be anything left of them. TIME IS OF THE ESSENCE!" Meanwhile, Tehran stated on Monday that it is continuing dialogue with the US via Pakistan.

**Assessment:** If the US has indeed offered temporary oil sanctions waivers, it would represent a significant concession — though one short of Iran's demand for full, unconditional sanctions relief. The conflicting Iranian media reports make it difficult to assess what was actually offered versus Iranian negotiating posture. Pakistan continues to mediate actively.

#### Oil Market: Volatility on Diplomacy Reports

**[VERIFIED]** As of 9:10 a.m. Eastern Time today, oil sold for $110.08 per barrel (using Brent as the benchmark). That's $2.52 lower than yesterday morning and approximately a $44 rise over the past year.

**[VERIFIED]** Brent crude oil futures reversed earlier gains and fell more than 1% to around $108 a barrel on Monday after Iranian media reported that the US had proposed a temporary waiver of oil sanctions pending a final agreement. Separate reports also indicated Tehran may be willing to a long-term nuclear freeze agreement, though not a full dismantling of its atomic program. Tehran is also reportedly seeking a gradual peace arrangement with the US and Israel and wants its enriched uranium transferred to Russia rather than the US. According to reports, Iran dropped demands for direct US financial compensation in favour of economic concessions and international guarantees.

**[VERIFIED]** Over the weekend, energy infrastructure across the Persian Gulf also came under attack, including a nuclear facility in the United Arab Emirates, adding to concerns over regional stability and potential supply disruptions.

**Assessment:** Oil fell from $111.04 on Friday to $110.08 today, driven by reports of potential US sanctions waivers and Iranian flexibility. However, the Barakah attack and continued Hormuz closure limit downside. Markets are pricing in incremental diplomatic progress while remaining skeptical of breakthrough.

#### Shipping Crisis: Traffic Near Zero

**[VERIFIED]** Commercial shipping through the Strait of Hormuz remains largely frozen, with only limited vessel movements observed and most tied to Iranian-linked shipping. While commercial activity remains severely depressed, daily transits through the waterway did climb on Saturday to ten ships from just five the previous day, pushed by a small wave of incoming Iranian-linked vessels, tracking data compiled by Bloomberg show.

**[VERIFIED]** Since May 6, open transits have fallen to near zero vessels per day. Prior to the outbreak of hostilities, about 138 vessels transited the Strait of Hormuz daily.

**Assessment:** Traffic remains at approximately 5-7% of pre-war levels. The formalization of the PGSA toll system may allow some limited increase in compliant traffic, but most operators face a choice between US and Iranian sanctions regimes.

### Key Emerging Issues

1. **Nuclear Facility Targeting** — The Barakah attack establishes a dangerous precedent. The IAEA's "grave concern" language is unusually strong. Future attacks on nuclear infrastructure (including Iran's Bushehr) could have radiological consequences across the entire Gulf region.

2. **PGSA Institutionalization** — Iran's creation of a formal authority to regulate Hormuz traffic transforms a temporary disruption into a permanent sovereignty claim. The OFAC guidance creating sanctions risk for payments creates a legal/operational dilemma for shipping.

3. **Iraq as Launch Platform** — Both the UAE and Saudi Arabia reported drones from western/Iraqi airspace. Iran-backed militias in Iraq appear to be serving as launch platforms, maintaining Tehran's deniability while extending strike range.

4. **Sanctions Waiver Reports** — If the US has indeed offered temporary oil sanctions waivers, it represents movement from previous "maximum pressure" positions. However, conflicting Iranian media reports and US non-confirmation leave the actual offer unclear.

### Bottom Line

**The Barakah attack marks a dangerous escalation.** Targeting a nuclear facility's infrastructure — even its perimeter — crosses a line that could trigger severe retaliation. The IAEA's explicit condemnation signals international alarm. Combined with Saudi intercepts of Iraqi-origin drones, this suggests coordinated Iranian/proxy pressure on Gulf states hosting US forces or Israeli ties.

**Iran is institutionalizing Hormuz control.** The PGSA represents a shift from "temporary" to "permanent." Tehran is building the infrastructure for indefinite control of the strait, regardless of whether a deal is reached. The $100 billion revenue projection on Tehran billboards shows this is now regime policy, not leverage.

**Diplomacy continues despite everything.** The reported US sanctions waiver offer — if real — would be significant. The Israel-Lebanon extension buys time. But the gap between diplomatic language and ground reality (3,000+ Lebanese dead, Hormuz frozen, nuclear plants under attack) has never been wider.

**Trump's "clock is ticking" threat echoes previous unfulfilled ultimatums** — but the Barakah attack may push him toward renewed military action if Iran doesn't produce meaningful concessions soon.
