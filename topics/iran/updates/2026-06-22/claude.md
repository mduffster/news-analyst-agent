### Summary

**MAJOR DEVELOPMENT: First round of high-level US-Iran talks in Switzerland concluded early Monday with significant progress. Mediators announced agreement on a "60-day roadmap" toward a final deal, creation of a "de-confliction cell" for Lebanon, IAEA inspectors returning to Iran, and a Hormuz communication line. However, talks were briefly disrupted when Trump threatened to "hit Iran very hard again" mid-negotiation. Netanyahu ordered the IDF to hold fire across Lebanon except at Ali al-Taher Hill, where clashes continue. Lebanon's death toll rose by 145+ over June 19-20 — the heaviest casualties since the war began. Commercial shipping through Hormuz dropped sharply to just 5-12 vessels Sunday after Iran's "re-closure" announcement, though CENTCOM reported 55 ships transiting Saturday. Oil prices fell sharply to ~$78-79/barrel as markets bet on sustained de-escalation.**

### Key Developments Since June 19

#### 1. US-Iran Talks Conclude First Round with "Major Progress"

**[OFFICIAL]** The U.S. and Iran, along with mediating parties, established a road map for reaching a final deal within 60 days during Sunday's talks in Switzerland, according to a joint statement from the mediating countries.

**[OFFICIAL]** VP Vance said that negotiators had made "a lot of good progress" on ensuring that the Strait of Hormuz stays open and on setting up a "coordination mechanism" on demining it. He also said that Tehran agreed to invite International Atomic Energy Agency inspectors back into the country, which, he said, is "a major milestone for the American people and the first step in permanently denuclearising or permanently ending a nuclear weapons program in Iran."

**[VERIFIED]** Sunday's high-level talks were the first discussions under the U.S.-Iran memorandum of understanding in Bürgenstock, Switzerland, with officials from the U.S. — including Vice President JD Vance — present alongside Iran and mediating nations Qatar and Pakistan.

**[OFFICIAL]** Iran's foreign minister, Abbas Araghchi, said "major progress" had been made to end the war in Lebanon. He also added that "oil and petrol exports are waived, blockade lifted, some frozen assets released, and major reconstruction & development plan launched for Iran."

---

#### 2. Lebanon "De-Confliction Cell" Created — Israel Excluded

**[OFFICIAL]** Iran and the United States agree to set up a "de-confliction cell" with Lebanon to stop military operations there, Pakistan and Qatar, which are mediating the US-Iran negotiations in Switzerland, say in a joint statement. "The parties agreed on the creation of a de-confliction cell, between the parties, the Lebanese Republic and facilitated by the Mediators, to ensure the adherence of the termination of military operations in Lebanon."

**[VERIFIED]** High-level talks between the United States and Iran, mediated by Pakistan and Qatar, concluded with an agreement to establish a "de-confliction cell" aimed at ensuring compliance with a cessation of military operations in Lebanon, though Israel's involvement and adherence remain uncertain given its security concerns regarding Hezbollah.

**[OFFICIAL]** Araghchi called the creation of "a de-confliction cell," the framework meant to end the fighting in Lebanon, the "1st real test" of the negotiating process.

---

#### 3. Trump Threat Disrupts Talks Mid-Negotiation

**[VERIFIED]** Sunday's discussions were strained as President Trump threatened further attacks on Iran as Vance sat for negotiations with Iranian officials in Switzerland. "Iran must immediately stop their highly paid PROXIES in Lebanon from causing trouble. If they don't, we'll hit Iran very hard again, just like we did last week, only harder!!!"

**[VERIFIED]** Meanwhile, as the meeting went on, it was briefly suspended after threatening statements by US President Donald Trump to bomb Iran. Iran's delegation withdrew from the negotiations.

---

#### 4. Netanyahu Orders IDF to "Hold Fire" — Except at Ali al-Taher Hill

**[VERIFIED]** On 21 June, Benjamin Netanyahu and Israel Katz, ordered the IDF to stay put in the occupied Lebanese territories and to hold fire everywhere aside from Ali al-Taher Hill, where the IDF and Hezbollah have been involved in numerous clashes.

**[OFFICIAL]** IDF Chief of Staff Lt. Gen. Eyal Zamir says that Hezbollah is in a "very difficult situation" after several midlevel commanders in the terror group were killed in the past two days. "The ceasefire that has been declared is fragile, and we must remain at a high level of readiness for the renewal of combat operations, the thwarting of threats, and a rapid transition to offensive action if required."

**[OFFICIAL]** The IDF Home Front Command says it will be lifting all restrictions on communities near the Lebanon border, as the ceasefire with Hezbollah appears to hold. Following a fresh assessment, the Home Front Command says that from 6 a.m. tomorrow, the restrictions will be lifted.

---

#### 5. Lebanon Death Toll Surges — 145+ Killed in Two Days

**[VERIFIED]** On 20 June, Israel struck numerous locations in Lebanon, saying they were Hezbollah targets. The death toll from Israeli strikes increased by 145 people over two days.

**[VERIFIED]** Lebanon's Ministry of Public Health said 83 people were killed and 141 wounded in Israeli attacks on Friday, just after the renewed ceasefire was announced. Most of the casualties were in southern Lebanon with others in the east.

**[VERIFIED]** A total of 36 Israeli soldiers have been killed in southern Lebanon and northern Israel since the beginning of the conflict with the Iranian-backed Lebanese militia in early March. More than 4,000 Lebanese have been killed in Israeli strikes since then, according to the Lebanese Health Ministry.

---

#### 6. Strait of Hormuz Traffic Plunges — Contested Status Continues

**[VERIFIED]** The number of ships that passed through the Strait of Hormuz fell sharply on Sunday after Iran announced it had again closed the waterway, citing Israeli and US violations of the interim peace deal, shipping data showed. Five vessels passed the strait on Sunday, from 26 ships spotted a day earlier, data from analytics firm Kpler showed.

**[VERIFIED]** The strait's status remains contested: Iran's military declared it closed on June 20, but Iran's foreign ministry said shipping was "operating normally." US CENTCOM reports traffic still flowing with 55 ships transiting on June 21.

**[OFFICIAL]** Iran and the US have formed a "line of communication" aimed at ensuring "safe passage" in the Strait of Hormuz, mediators Qatar and Pakistan said in their joint statement on the recently concluded round of talks in Switzerland.

---

#### 7. Oil Prices Drop Sharply — Markets Bet on De-escalation

**[VERIFIED]** Crude Oil fell to 73.53 USD/Bbl on June 22, 2026, down 4.92% from the previous day. Over the past month, Crude Oil's price has fallen 21.69%, but it is still 7.33% higher than a year ago.

**[VERIFIED]** As of 8:45 a.m. Eastern Time today, oil sold for $79.25 per barrel (using Brent as the benchmark). That's $3.20 lower than yesterday.

**[VERIFIED]** Despite production outages and lower oil inventories, Brent fell sharply as reports of a possible US-Iran agreement weighed on prices. The conditional reopening of the Strait of Hormuz, the lifting of force majeure declarations by Kuwait, and the end of the US naval blockade have collectively convinced investors that the disruption which had pushed prices above $120 "is well and truly over."

---

#### 8. Hezbollah Leader Warns: "Any Violation, We Will Fight"

**[OFFICIAL]** Hezbollah leader Naim Qassem argued that Israel should not continue operating in Lebanon after Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said that Israeli troops would remain in the country "as long as necessary." "Israel will not stay in Lebanon, even if it increases its crimes. And we will defend (ourselves)," Qassem said in an address on Al-Manar TV on Sunday.

**[OFFICIAL]** "Any ceasefire under the heading of a comprehensive halt to the aggression - we are already committed to it. But we will not accept any violation. Any violation, we will confront. Any violation, we will fight."

### Emerging Signals

**The de-confliction cell excludes Israel:** According to the joint statement by the international mediators Pakistan and Qatar, the Lebanon De-confliction Cell formally includes "the Parties" — the United States, Iran, and the Lebanese Republic. Neither Hezbollah nor Israel are included in the cell. This creates a mechanism that operates around Israel rather than through it — a significant shift in regional dynamics.

**Trump's negotiating style creates fragility:** The president's threat to "hit Iran very hard" during active negotiations nearly derailed the talks and forced Iranian delegation into indirect communication only. This pattern could collapse the framework at any moment.

**Israel pivoting to direct Lebanon track:** With the US-Iran mechanism bypassing Israel, Netanyahu may accelerate bilateral negotiations with Lebanon's government as an alternative path. Tuesday's Washington talks become more consequential.

**IAEA return is a genuine breakthrough:** Iran agreeing to readmit inspectors after nearly a year of exclusion is the first tangible nuclear concession. The fate of ~400kg of 60% enriched uranium remains unclear, but verification restarting is significant.

**Market pricing in peace:** Oil's dramatic decline from $120+ to ~$78 represents markets concluding the crisis premium has evaporated. A collapse of talks would cause sharp reversals.

---

### Bottom Line

**The first round of US-Iran talks produced more concrete results than expected: a 60-day roadmap, IAEA inspector return, de-confliction cell for Lebanon, and Hormuz communication line. However, the framework is built around Israel rather than including it — the de-confliction cell explicitly excludes both Israel and Hezbollah. Netanyahu's hold-fire order is a significant de-escalation signal, but came only after 145+ Lebanese were killed over two days. Trump's mid-negotiation threat to bomb Iran again demonstrates the talks' inherent fragility. Technical negotiations continue through the week, with Israel-Lebanon direct talks in Washington on Tuesday becoming a critical parallel track. The 60-day clock is now Day 5, and the framework has both structure and vulnerability.**
