## Critical Developments (June 10-11)

### 1. Iran Declares Full Strait of Hormuz Closure — MAJOR ESCALATION [VERIFIED]

[VERIFIED] The Iranian military announced on Thursday, June 11, 2026, the complete closure of the Strait of Hormuz for shipping activities. Through Iran's national television broadcast, the Headquarters of Khatam Al-Anbiya stated that the strategic waterway was closed for all vessels, including tankers and merchant ships, due to what they referred to as insecurity in the region. The Iranian military warned that any movement in the Strait of Hormuz would be a target.

[REPORTED] Any vessel that tries to pass through the strait will be attacked and considered as "cooperation with the enemy." The IRGC warned: "We warn you that no ship should leave the port in the Persian and Oman Gulfs. Approaching the Strait of Hormuz will be considered as cooperation with the enemy."

[REPORTED] Following the first US-Israel strikes on Tehran on February 28, Iran closed the passage to shipping traffic. The waterway remains Iran's most significant leverage in ongoing peace negotiations. Since then, Iran has, at times, allowed some ships to pass—in some cases, reportedly charging tolls as high as $2m per ship.

[DISPUTED] US Central Command pushed back on the Iranian claim, saying that commercial ships continue to transit in and out of the strait—which the IRGC denied.

**Assessment:** This represents the most explicit closure declaration since the conflict began. While Iran has controlled the Strait since March 4, this statement escalates the threat level by explicitly warning all vessels will be fired upon. The distinction between previous "selective" closures and today's "total" closure signals Iran's response to continued U.S. strikes.

---

### 2. Trump Threatens to Seize Kharg Island — Rhetorical Escalation [OFFICIAL]

[OFFICIAL] President Donald Trump on Thursday threatened to seize Iran's oil infrastructure including its main export terminal Kharg Island "in the not too distant future." Trump said the U.S. military will attack Iran "VERY HARD" Thursday night after completing a round of airstrikes on Wednesday. The president threatened to take "total control" of Iran's oil and gas markets as the U.S. did in Venezuela earlier this year. Kharg Island is Iran's main oil export terminal, accounting for about 90% of its crude shipments before the war.

[OFFICIAL] In an interview with Fox News, Trump said that his "preference has always been, take Kharg Island." "I don't know that America has the stomach for it, to be honest with you," he added.

[REPORTED] Plans for the US military to try to capture the island have been drawn up for months but continuously shelved because the operation was considered too risky. Earlier this year, CNN reported that Iran has laid traps and moved additional military personnel and air defenses to Kharg.

[OFFICIAL] Iran will deliver a "firm, crushing, painful, and regret-inducing response" if the US attacks Kharg Island, a top Iranian official warned. Describing Trump as "confused and erratic," Ebrahim Azizi, head of the Iranian parliament's National Security and Foreign Policy Commission, said there was no doubt Iran would deliver a severe response.

**Assessment:** Trump's Kharg Island threat represents significant rhetorical escalation, though his own admission that America may lack "the stomach" suggests this is more coercive diplomacy than imminent operational planning. The threat serves as maximum pressure on negotiations, but the operational risks of seizure remain prohibitive.

### 3. U.S. Strikes Continue for Third Consecutive Night [VERIFIED]

[VERIFIED] President Trump said the U.S. will hit Iran with new strikes Thursday and threatened to seize Kharg Island, Iran's main oil export hub. This will be the third consecutive night the U.S. military strikes Iran.

[VERIFIED] Explosions from the strikes echoed around Iran's capital, as well as the port city of Bandar Abbas and other southern areas along the Strait of Hormuz. Iran's paramilitary Revolutionary Guard later said sites hit included a manufacturing complex, a military barracks and a local Guard base outside of Tehran.

[VERIFIED] CENTCOM said it has disabled nine vessels and redirected 135 others since the US blockade on Iranian ports began on April 13.

[OFFICIAL] "We dropped $250 million worth of bombs on them last night," Trump told Fox.

### 4. Three Indian Sailors Killed — First Blockade Fatalities [VERIFIED]

[VERIFIED] Settebello had 24 Indian sailors on board; three who were reported missing on Wednesday were confirmed killed on Thursday. The US attacks on vessels with Indian seafarers come at a time when Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi is likely to hold bilateral talks with President Donald Trump on the sidelines of the Group of Seven summit next week.

[OFFICIAL] "It is deeply unfortunate to learn of the tragic incident aboard the Palau-flagged MT Settebello," India's Shipping Minister, Sarbananda Sonowal, said in a statement. "Sadly, three Indian seafarers initially reported missing are now confirmed dead after bodies have been located and identified."

[OFFICIAL] Indian foreign ministry spokesperson Randhir Jaiswal told reporters the U.S. Navy had attacked three ships with Indian crews this week. "These attacks must cease and end," he said. "We also call for dialogue and diplomacy so that we can have an early return to peace and stability in the region."

[REPORTED] An Iranian cargo vessel sank in the Gulf of Oman after being attacked by the United States on the morning of June 11. According to the Sirik governor, the cargo ship departed the Omani city of Khasab and was struck five miles from Khasab port. The local official said all five crew members were rescued.

**Assessment:** The confirmed deaths of three Indian sailors mark the first fatalities from the U.S. blockade that began April 13. This adds significant diplomatic complexity as India maintains important relationships with both Washington and Tehran. The timing—just before a potential Modi-Trump meeting at G7—heightens the political sensitivity.

### 5. Qatari Mediation Fails — Ceasefire "Meaningless" [VERIFIED]

[VERIFIED] A Qatari diplomatic delegation, negotiating in coordination with the U.S., left Tehran on Thursday morning after holding talks, according to an official with knowledge of the team.

[VERIFIED] The new exchange of fire came as efforts to negotiate an end to the war appeared stuck, with U.S. President Donald Trump warning that Tehran would "pay the price" for stalled negotiations. Iran's Foreign Ministry said in a statement Thursday that the U.S. attacks had "effectively rendered the ceasefire ... meaningless," without saying it was abandoning it.

[REPORTED] CNN determined that between March 23 and June 9, Trump had claimed at least 38 times that a deal was imminent.

**Assessment:** The Qatari team's departure without a breakthrough, combined with Iran's statement that the ceasefire has been "rendered meaningless," suggests diplomacy is at a nadir. However, Iran has not formally withdrawn from negotiations—leaving a narrow path open.

### 6. IAEA Passes Resolution on Iran's Uranium Stockpile [VERIFIED]

[VERIFIED] The International Atomic Energy Agency's 35-nation Board of Governors on Wednesday approved a U.S.-backed resolution ordering the Iranian regime to account for its remaining enriched uranium and grant inspectors access to verify its stockpiles. The board passed the text in Vienna with 21 votes in favor and 10 abstentions, with Russia, China and Niger opposing.

[VERIFIED] The IAEA reported that it has been unable to track Iran's stockpiles of enriched uranium since the 12-day war in June 2025. Previous IAEA estimates indicate Iran retained approximately 440.9 kilograms enriched to up to 60% purity—which the agency says would be enough, if further refined, for about 10 nuclear weapons.

[OFFICIAL] Tehran condemned the resolution as "whitewashing" U.S. military action and warned it would decide how to respond.

[REPORTED] The resolution was passed just hours after Iran and the United States carried out mutual military strikes against each other.
