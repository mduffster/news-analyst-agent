## June 11, 2026 — Daily Update

### Summary

**Iran officially declares full closure of the Strait of Hormuz, warning all vessels attempting passage will be shot. Trump threatens to seize Kharg Island and "assume total control" of Iran's oil industry. A Qatari mediation team has left Tehran without a breakthrough as U.S. strikes enter their third consecutive night. Three Indian sailors were confirmed killed in a U.S. blockade strike, prompting India to summon a U.S. diplomat in protest. ### Key Developments Since June 10

#### 1. Iran Declares Full Strait of Hormuz Closure

**[OFFICIAL]** "Effective immediately, due to insecurity in the region, the Strait of Hormuz is declared closed to all vessels, including oil tankers and commercial ships," the IRGC said in a post on its official Telegram. "Any vessel attempting to transit the strait will be targeted."

**[VERIFIED]** On 11 June, Iran's joint military command declared the closure of the Hormuz, including for oil tankers and commercial ships after the American attacks, adding that any vessel attempting passage will be shot at.

**[REPORTED]** Iran's military ordered the complete closure of the Strait of Hormuz early on Thursday in response to US strikes on the country. The US responded by saying some ships were still getting through.

**Context:** Following the first US-Israel strikes on Tehran on February 28, Iran closed the passage to shipping traffic. The waterway remains Iran's most significant leverage in ongoing peace negotiations with the United States. Since then, Iran has, at times, allowed some ships to pass – in some cases, reportedly charging tolls as high as $2m per ship to pass.

**Assessment:** This is a significant escalation of the Hormuz blockade. While Iran has controlled the Strait since March 4, this is the most explicit threat to fire on all vessels. The distinction between previous "selective" closures and today's "total" closure signals Iran's response to the latest U.S. strikes.

---

#### 2. Trump Threatens to Seize Kharg Island

**[OFFICIAL]** President Donald Trump on Thursday said the U.S. would hit Iran "VERY HARD TONIGHT," threatening in a social media post to "assume total control" of Iran's oil and gas industries, including the key Kharg Island, in the "not too distant future."

**[OFFICIAL]** President Donald Trump on Thursday renewed threats to seize Kharg Island, which accounts for more than 90% of Iran's oil exports. "At some point in the not too distant future, we will be taking Kharg Island, and other oil infrastructure points, and assume total control of their Oil and Gas Markets, much like we have with Venezuela," Trump wrote on Truth Social.

**[OFFICIAL]** In an interview with Fox News, Trump said that his "preference has always been, take Kharg Island." "I don't know that America has the stomach for it, to be honest with you," he added.

**[REPORTED]** Plans to capture the island have been drawn up for months but continuously shelved because it was considered too risky, sources say.

**[OFFICIAL]** Iran will deliver a "firm, crushing, painful, and regret-inducing response" if the US attacks Kharg Island, a top Iranian official has warned. Describing Trump as "confused and erratic," Ebrahim Azizi, head of the Iranian parliament's National Security and Foreign Policy Commission, said there was no doubt Iran would deliver a severe response.

**Assessment:** Trump's Kharg Island threat represents significant rhetorical escalation, though his own admission that "America has the stomach" may not be there suggests this is more coercive diplomacy than imminent operational planning. The Venezuela comparison is telling—Trump envisions controlling Iranian oil revenues much as the U.S. now does with Venezuelan oil.

---

#### 3. U.S. Strikes Continue for Third Night

**[VERIFIED]** President Trump said the U.S. will hit Iran with new strikes Thursday and threatened to seize Kharg Island, Iran's main oil export hub. This will be the third consecutive night the U.S. military strikes Iran.

**[VERIFIED]** Explosions from the strikes echoed around Iran's capital, as well as the port city of Bandar Abbas and other southern areas along the Strait of Hormuz. Iran's paramilitary Revolutionary Guard later said sites hit included a manufacturing complex, a military barracks and a local Guard base outside of Tehran.

**[VERIFIED]** Explosions were heard in numerous locations across Iran in the early hours of June 11, according to scattered reports in the Iranian media. Explosions were reported in Sirik, Bandar Abbas, Qeshm, and Kharg Island.

**[OFFICIAL]** US Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth confirmed that Washington was launching strikes on "key facilities" in Iran, saying the attacks were part of attempts to secure a permanent ceasefire. Speaking outside CENTCOM headquarters in Tampa, Florida, Hegseth said President Donald Trump had ordered Iran to be hit "hard" and warned the strikes could continue for a second consecutive night if necessary.

#### 4. Iranian Retaliation Continues

**[VERIFIED]** Iran says it has launched retaliatory strikes against United States forces in the region, with the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) claiming attacks on bases in Kuwait, Bahrain and Jordan, according to Iranian state media reports.

**[VERIFIED]** Jordan said it intercepted 20 Iranian missiles fired toward an area that is home to a base hosting U.S. troops, though no one was hurt. Bahrain's Interior Ministry said an 11-year-old girl was hurt and cars and homes were damaged by debris from interceptions responding to the Iranian attack.

**[OFFICIAL]** "Wrong strategies and impulsive decisions will reset the entire board for the worse," Iran's Parliament Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf wrote in a post on X on Thursday. "Explode energy infrastructure and markets and create an endless quagmire that you will be stuck in for years. You will see a different Iran."

---

#### 5. Three Indian Sailors Killed in U.S. Blockade Strike

**[VERIFIED]** Three Indian sailors were killed in a U.S. strike on a commercial vessel in the Gulf of Oman Tuesday, according to India's shipping minister. U.S. Central Command accused the ship of violating the ongoing naval blockade of Iran's ports, saying the engine room of the Palau-flagged M/T Settebello oil tanker was fired on after the crew "repeatedly failed to comply with directions from American forces."

**[OFFICIAL]** India's foreign ministry summoned a top US diplomat in New Delhi after the Settebello attack to lodge "a strong protest".

**[VERIFIED]** At least nine ships have been disabled for not complying with U.S. forces since the blockade on Iran was initiated on April 13, according to CENTCOM.

**[REPORTED]** An Iranian cargo vessel sank in the Gulf of Oman after being attacked by the United States on the morning of June 11, Iranian media reported. According to the Sirik governor, the cargo ship departed the Omani city of Khasab and was struck five miles from Khasab port. The local official said all five crew members were rescued.

**Assessment:** The Indian sailor deaths mark the first confirmed civilian fatalities from the U.S. blockade. India's formal protest could complicate U.S. diplomatic positioning, particularly as New Delhi maintains important relationships with both Washington and Tehran.

#### 6. Qatari Mediators Leave Tehran Without Breakthrough

**[VERIFIED]** A Qatari delegation left Tehran after negotiations with Iranian officials, a diplomatic source said. The talks, conducted in coordination with the US, lasted into the early hours of Thursday morning local time, as the US was striking Iran.

**[VERIFIED]** A Qatari diplomatic delegation, negotiating in coordination with the U.S., left Tehran on Thursday morning after holding talks, according to an official with knowledge of the team who spoke on condition of anonymity to discuss the mediation.

**[OFFICIAL]** Iran's Foreign Ministry said in a statement Thursday that the U.S. attacks had "effectively rendered the ceasefire ... meaningless," without saying it was abandoning it.

**Assessment:** The Qatari team's departure without a breakthrough, combined with Iran's statement that the ceasefire has been "rendered meaningless," suggests diplomacy is at a nadir. Notably, Iran has not formally withdrawn from negotiations—leaving a narrow path open.

---

#### 7. IAEA Passes Resolution on Iran's Uranium Stockpile

**[VERIFIED]** The International Atomic Energy Agency's 35-nation Board of Governors on Wednesday approved a U.S.-backed resolution ordering the Iranian regime to account for its remaining enriched uranium and grant inspectors access to verify its stockpiles. The board passed the text in Vienna with 21 votes in favor, and 10 abstentions, with Russia, China and Niger opposing.

**[VERIFIED]** The resolution was passed just hours after Iran and the United States carried out mutual military strikes against each other.

**[OFFICIAL]** Tehran condemned the resolution as "whitewashing" U.S. military action and warned it would decide how to respond.

**[VERIFIED]** The IAEA reported that it has been unable to track Iran's stockpiles of enriched uranium since the 12-day war in June 2025. Previous IAEA estimates indicate Iran retained approximately 440.9 kilograms enriched to up to 60% purity, which the agency says would be enough, if further refined, for about 10 nuclear weapons.

**[VERIFIED]** By 9 a.m. Eastern Time today, oil had reached $95.15 per barrel, measured using the Brent benchmark. That's 88 cents less than it cost yesterday morning and about $24 above its price a year earlier.

**[VERIFIED]** Brent climbed toward $95 per barrel on Thursday, extending gains from the previous session as the US military launched strikes against Iran for the second day, raising fears that peace efforts could collapse and the conflict disrupting global markets could drag on.

**[VERIFIED]** In the US, crude stockpiles including strategic reserves fell by 15 million barrels last week and are down more than 70 million over five weeks, the steepest draw since the 1980s.

**[REPORTED]** Energy executives have warned the White House that key oil reserves being used to limit the Iran war's impact on prices are running dangerously low, adding further urgency to President Donald Trump's search for a way out of the conflict. The concerns conveyed to Cabinet officials and White House aides have centered on industry stockpiles that are projected to reach critical lows perhaps in a matter of weeks.

### Emerging Signals

**The ceasefire has become a legal fiction:** Both sides maintain they haven't formally abandoned the April 8 framework, but three consecutive nights of U.S. strikes and Iran's statement that it's "meaningless" make the distinction academic. The ceasefire exists only as a potential off-ramp neither side has yet closed.

**Inventory crisis looming:** U.S. crude stockpiles including strategic reserves have declined more than 70 million barrels over five weeks, the steepest draw since the 1980s. Industry warnings to the White House about reserves "reaching critical lows in weeks" may force a policy shift regardless of negotiating dynamics.

**Third-party casualties complicate U.S. position:** The Indian sailor deaths and formal diplomatic protest introduce a new variable. India is not the only country with nationals working on vessels in the Gulf. Further casualties could erode international support for the blockade.

**Trump's Kharg obsession:** He has had designs on it since at least 1988, when he told The Guardian newspaper that "I'd do a number on Kharg Island." The island represents Trump's maximalist vision for the conflict, but his simultaneous acknowledgment that Americans may lack "the stomach" for it reveals the gap between ambition and feasibility.

---

### Bottom Line

**The conflict has entered a new phase. Iran's formal declaration of complete Hormuz closure, Trump's explicit threats to seize Kharg Island, three consecutive nights of U.S. strikes, and the Qatari mediation failure collectively represent the most significant deterioration since the April 8 ceasefire. While neither side has formally abandoned the ceasefire framework, Iran's characterization of it as "meaningless" and Trump's pivot from deal-making to military maximalism suggest the framework exists now only as a face-saving fiction. The deaths of three Indian sailors in a U.S. blockade strike and urgent warnings about depleting oil reserves add new pressures that could force unexpected pivots. The next 48-72 hours will determine whether this escalation spiral continues or whether domestic and economic pressures force one or both sides back to the table.**
