U.S. and Iranian descriptions of deal terms are wildly divergent, Israel is explicitly rejecting key provisions, and the practical obstacles to implementation are enormous.

## Critical Developments (June 11-12)

### 1. Trump Cancels Strikes, Announces Deal "Approved" — MAJOR REVERSAL [OFFICIAL]

[OFFICIAL] Trump cancelled strikes planned for Thursday night and claimed a deal had been reached, writing on Truth Social that discussions "have been brought to the highest level of Iranian leadership and approved."

[VERIFIED] Four U.S. Air Force C-17 planes departed to Europe on Thursday, moving equipment for possible travel by Vice President Vance to a signing ceremony in Geneva in the coming days.

[REPORTED] According to two diplomats from two mediating countries and two U.S. officials, the tentative agreement was reached on Wednesday night after hours of negotiations between Qatari mediator Ali Al-Thawadi and Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi. During the talks in Tehran, Al-Thawadi spoke on the phone multiple times with Trump's envoys, Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner.

[REPORTED] Geneva is now under serious consideration as the venue for signing the deal, possibly as early as Sunday, June 14, 2026, according to individuals closely monitoring the negotiations. This year's G7 summit is scheduled for June 15–17 in Évian-les-Bains, in France's Alps.

**Assessment:** This represents a dramatic reversal from Thursday morning, when Trump threatened to hit Iran "VERY HARD" and seize Kharg Island. The logistical preparations (C-17 departures, VP travel planning) suggest this is more than rhetoric. However, CNN determined that between March 23 and June 9, Trump had claimed at least 38 times that a deal was imminent.

### 2. Draft MOU Details Leak — But U.S. and Iran Deeply Misaligned [REPORTED]

[REPORTED] The MOU calls for the strait to be reopened immediately without tolls, with a return to pre-war shipping volumes within 30 days. In return, the U.S. blockade would also be lifted.

[REPORTED] Iran's Mehr News Agency published what it described as a 14-point draft framework agreement. According to Mehr, the draft calls for a 'permanent and immediate ceasefire across all fronts, including Lebanon,' followed by a 60-day negotiation window to resolve nuclear issues and lift all U.S. sanctions. The agency added that the plan includes the release of $24 billion in frozen Iranian assets during the 60-day period — with half of that amount made available to Iran before formal negotiations begin.

[REPORTED] Final negotiations will not commence before the release of half of Iran's frozen funds, the suspension of oil sanctions and the lifting of the naval blockade. Discussions on Iran's missile program and support for resistance groups have been "definitively removed from the agenda." The list has not been confirmed publicly by either Tehran or Washington.

[OFFICIAL] Trump disputed Iranian leaks: "The terms that Iran leaked out to the Fake News have NOTHING to do with the terms that were agreed to, in writing."

[VERIFIED] The diplomat from one of the mediating countries said "the U.S. and Iran have agreed on the text of a deal," but acknowledged the deal still needed final sign-off. As of Thursday evening, the deal had been approved on the Iranian side at high levels but likely not by Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei.

**Key Discrepancies:**
| Issue | U.S. Description | Iranian Description |
|-------|------------------|---------------------|
| Nuclear program | "Conceptual" agreement; details in phase 2 | NPT commitment reiterated; no new restrictions |
| Missiles/proxies | Final deal will address | "Definitively removed from agenda" |
| Frozen assets | Relief tied to compliance | $24B released, half before talks begin |
| Hormuz control | No tolls, immediate opening | Opens "under Iranian arrangements" |

**Assessment:** The gap between U.S. and Iranian descriptions is fundamental, not technical. Several G7 officials remain cautious. They point out that previous rounds of talks also showed signs of progress — yet ultimately failed to yield a finalized agreement. The current picture is one of a fragile process: the likelihood of a deal is rising, but it has not yet crystallized into a concrete, enforceable outcome.

### 3. Israel Rejects Key Provisions — "Will Not Withdraw" [OFFICIAL]

[OFFICIAL] Israeli Defense Minister Israel Katz addressed the emerging agreement between the U.S. and Iran, stating that "Israel will not withdraw from the security zones in Lebanon, Syria and Gaza."

[REPORTED] Israeli Defense Minister Israel Katz said Israel was demanding the creation of what it called a demilitarized zone within Lebanon while being able to continue attacks against Iran-backed Hezbollah. He said Israel would not be withdrawing from the south.

[VERIFIED] The fresh fighting, and Hezbollah's outright rejection of the proposal, poured cold water on any immediate prospects of a wider ceasefire between the United States and Iran. Iran has said it will not agree to a ceasefire with the U.S. and Israel unless there is one in Lebanon.

**Assessment:** Israel's explicit rejection of withdrawal from Lebanon creates a direct contradiction with the MOU's reported Lebanon provisions. In recent weeks, Trump has only talked about a deal on Iran's nuclear program and the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz, and has moved away from demands on ballistic missiles or support for terrorism, issues Iran says it will not discuss. This signals the MOU may simply defer the Israel-Lebanon issue rather than resolve it.

### 4. Strait of Hormuz Remains Paralyzed — Zero Transits for Five Days [VERIFIED]

[VERIFIED] As of June 12, 2026, there are zero commercial vessels currently in transit outbound through the Strait. This complete halt in activity, extending beyond the early hours of the day, reflects a severe bottleneck crisis. No commercial vessels have exited the Strait today, June 12, nor were there any recorded outbound transits over the preceding four days, from June 8 to June 11.

[VERIFIED] Per IMF PortWatch, 2 vessels transited the Strait of Hormuz on June 7 at 2% of typical, effectively closed to commercial shipping.

[REPORTED] U.S. forces shot down two Iranian drones reportedly targeting commercial vessels near Hormuz on June 12.

[VERIFIED] Traders remained cautious, as even a breakthrough would face significant obstacles before oil flows fully normalize, including clearing mines from Hormuz, restarting idled production fields, and repairing energy facilities damaged by drone and missile attacks.

**Assessment:** Despite deal optimism, the Strait remains completely paralyzed. Even if an MOU is signed Sunday, the practical timeline to reopening—mine clearance, security guarantees, insurance normalization—extends well beyond July 1.

### 6. India Summons U.S. Diplomat Second Time — Diplomatic Pressure Intensifying [VERIFIED]

[VERIFIED] India's government summoned a senior US diplomat for a second time this week to protest US strikes on vessels carrying Indian crew members in the Gulf of Oman.

[VERIFIED] India summoned a senior US diplomat on Friday for a second time in two days, after American strikes on three largely Indian-crewed merchant vessels off Oman killed three Indians. The foreign ministry said it had summoned US Deputy Chief of Mission Jason Meeks to lodge "a strong protest … regarding the continuing attacks by US naval forces on commercial vessels carrying Indian mariners."

[VERIFIED] New Delhi called in US charge d'affaires Jason Meeks again on Friday, following a previous summoning on Wednesday, to lodge a "strong protest" over the attacks – rare diplomatic steps against its strategic partner in recent years. "Such actions are unacceptable and undermine the safety, security and stability of international maritime commerce."

[REPORTED] The attack on Jalveer was the third such instance of an attack on a vessel. On June 8, the first incident had happened after a blaze erupted on the MT Marivex following a strike. The tanker had 24 Indians, who were rescued safely. On June 10, another tanker identified as MT Settebello was attacked in the Gulf of Oman. The vessel had 24 crew members, of which 21 were rescued safely but three people died.

**Assessment:** India's repeated summons represent a significant escalation in diplomatic friction with Washington over blockade operations. This adds pressure on U.S. maritime enforcement rules and could complicate coalition dynamics even if U.S.-Iran violence pauses.

### 7. IAEA Nuclear Concerns Persist [VERIFIED]

[VERIFIED] The IAEA's report presents a picture of near-total, ongoing loss of monitoring of Iranian nuclear sites due to Iran's suspension of access nearly one year ago, an unaccounted-for enriched uranium stockpile, key activities that cannot be verified, and the persistence of long-standing compliance failures. In its latest safeguards report, the IAEA continues to declare Iran in violation of its Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty (NPT) safeguards agreement.

[VERIFIED] Iran chose the path of continued defiance of its obligations. Since the adoption of last year's resolution, Iran has not only not engaged the IAEA at all on the outstanding matters, but it has also doubled down on its non-cooperation, further deepening its non-compliance.

[VERIFIED] The International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) Board of Governors adopted a resolution on June 10, 2026, calling on Iran to provide detailed information about its enriched uranium stockpiles. The 35-member board approved the resolution with 21 votes in favor, three against, and 10 abstentions. According to diplomats, Russia, China, and Niger voted against the measure.

**Assessment:** Any MOU text would need to address IAEA verification. The text includes a framework for addressing Iran's enriched uranium stockpile, though any action on Iran's nuclear program would depend on a second, more detailed accord. The verification gap remains a fundamental obstacle to implementation.
