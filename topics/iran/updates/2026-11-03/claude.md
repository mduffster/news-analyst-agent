## Key Developments (Past 24 Hours)

### **US STRIKES QESHM ISLAND ON EVE OF MIDTERMS** [VERIFIED]

The US military carried out limited strikes Monday on Iran's Qeshm Island, according to US officials. The strikes, which appeared to be limited as opposed to a full return to hostilities, came on the eve of crucial American midterm elections.

The officials said the strikes Monday were not a resumption of major combat operations. They described the attacks as self-defense in response to Iran's ongoing efforts to target maritime shipping and US assets.

This is the first attack since June on Qeshm Island, which serves as the primary platform for Iran's "asymmetric" naval power, according to analysts.

Iranian state media reported that five explosions were heard Monday on Qeshm Island.

Asked on Fox & Friends what would happen once the elections were over, Trump said he wasn't looking to "kill lots of people," but "if we have to, we'll have to." The latest strikes were "just a minor attack," he said while insisting the situation does not constitute a war.

**[ANALYSIS]** This strike occurred just hours after Trump publicly said he was not considering striking Iran. The timing — on election eve — is significant. While officials frame it as defensive, it contradicts Trump's pledge not to strike before midterms and could signal to Iran that limited strikes will continue regardless of diplomatic posture.

### **IRAN'S REZAEI: US RESPONSE "NOT CONVINCING"** [OFFICIAL]

The US response to Iran's demands was not convincing, Iran's security chief Mohsen Rezaei said in a post on X on Tuesday, adding that Washington "must fully and explicitly address Iran's seven conditions." "Only in this case will the Strait of Hormuz be reopened," Rezaei said.

The statement follows Vice President JD Vance's letter to Rezaei and several days of talks between Iranian and Qatari officials. Despite Rezaei's statement, Qatar said that it remains "hopeful" about the prospects of a deal and that talks are ongoing.

According to Axios, Vance told Rezaei in the letter that, in order to prevent another round of US strikes against Iran, any negotiations "require discussions on the nuclear issue," an idea Iranian officials have repeatedly rejected.

**[ANALYSIS]** This is the formal response to the Vance letter that was pending. Iran's position remains unchanged: all seven conditions must be met before Hormuz reopens. The nuclear question remains the key sticking point — Iran refuses to include it in talks, US insists on it.

### **IRAN WARNS OF "NEW BATTLEFIELD" AND UPDATED TARGET BANK** [OFFICIAL]

Iran's parliament speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf warned on Tuesday that any US strategic error would open "a new battlefield in the war," hours after a US Air Force jet reportedly struck an IRGC missile system on Qeshm Island.

The remarks came after residents of Qeshm Island, Hengam and Bandar Abbas reported successive explosions and tremors overnight. Tehran-based Hamshahri newspaper reported on Tuesday that Iran had updated its "target bank," examining a scenario in which US support bases and infrastructure in Europe would be targeted under the "response without geographical limits" strategy.

Iranian deputy foreign minister Kazem Gharibabadi said on Tuesday that Tehran reserved the right to self-defence following US attacks on Qeshm island in the Strait of Hormuz.

Iran's Revolutionary Guards said its forces downed and captured a US MQM-170 drone near Qeshm Island. The IRGC also claimed it had destroyed an MQ-9 east of the strait and downed another MQM-170 reconnaissance drone south of Qeshm, capturing both.

**[ANALYSIS]** Iran's rhetoric has hardened significantly. The "target bank" update including European US bases is a notable escalation in threats. However, Iran has not retaliated with strikes against US targets — suggesting continued restraint despite the tough language.

### **ISRAEL: OPPOSITION FORMALLY UNITES BEHIND EISENKOT** [VERIFIED]

Israel's main opposition leaders have agreed to back former military chief Gadi Eisenkot as prime minister, promising to replace Benjamin Netanyahu's government as quickly as possible. In a joint statement, the leaders said all their parties would recommend Eisenkot, head of the Yashar party, to Herzog, and that negotiating teams would immediately begin drafting policy guidelines and coalition agreements.

"The party leaders are coordinated and will act immediately" to replace Netanyahu's coalition "with a national emergency government," the statement said. The leaders stood by four principles set out by Eisenkot, including that all parties in government recognise Israel as a Jewish state and label Hamas a "terrorist" group, conditions that could exclude some Arab-led parties.

Yashar chairman Gadi Eisenkot on Tuesday for the first time excluded United Torah Judaism from Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's political bloc, saying Netanyahu now has the support of only 46 lawmakers.

**[ANALYSIS]** The opposition unification is now official. Eisenkot's conditions (recognizing Israel as Jewish state, labeling Hamas terrorist) may create friction with Arab parties, but these could be worked around through external support arrangements. Herzog begins consultations tomorrow — the coalition battle is fully joined.
