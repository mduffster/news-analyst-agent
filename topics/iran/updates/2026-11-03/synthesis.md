# US-Israel-Iran Conflict: US Strikes Qeshm Island on Election Eve as Diplomacy Stalls

## November 3, 2026 — Day 249

**Assessment:** The conflict entered election day with US forces conducting "defensive" strikes on Iran's Qeshm Island overnight—contradicting Trump's earlier pledge not to strike before midterms and undermining diplomatic momentum that had driven a 7.6% oil crash just 24 hours earlier. The officials said the strikes were not a resumption of major combat operations but rather self-defense in response to Iran's ongoing efforts to target maritime shipping and US assets. Iran's security chief Mohsen Rezaei formally rejected US negotiating terms as "not convincing," while Parliament Speaker Ghalibaf warned Washington faces "a new battlefield" if it miscalculates. Tucker Carlson's unverified nuclear strike claim went viral, drawing concern across the political spectrum. In Israel, the opposition formally unified behind Eisenkot hours before President Herzog begins coalition consultations. The conflict now sits at an inflection point: the administration maintains both diplomatic rhetoric and kinetic operations simultaneously, while the post-midterm decision window opens within days.

## 1. US Strikes Qeshm Island on Election Eve [VERIFIED]

US forces struck Iran's Qeshm Island late Monday, the first attack on this strategic Iranian naval platform since June—and directly contradicting Trump's pre-election pledge to hold fire.

The US military carried out limited strikes Monday on Iran's Qeshm Island, according to two US officials. The strikes, which appeared to be limited as opposed to a full return to hostilities, came on the eve of crucial American midterm elections.

One official said the US attack came in response to Iran firing at a US aircraft. US officials described the strikes as "a defensive measure" in response to "Iranians using surface to air missiles against US assets."

Iran's Islamic Republic News Agency (IRNA) reported that five explosions were heard across Qeshm Island in succession. This was the first attack since June on Qeshm Island, which serves as the primary platform for Iran's "asymmetric" naval power, according to analysts.

It was the latest in a series of limited strikes carried out by Washington in recent weeks. Strikes have been carried out on a smaller scale and purposefully kept secret so as not to indicate any intent of resuming its major military campaign against Iran.

**Significance:** The timing is the key issue. Trump said "no" when asked Monday if he was considering striking Iran—hours before the Qeshm strike occurred. The administration's "defensive" framing allows continued strikes while maintaining diplomatic rhetoric, but Tehran will interpret this as continued aggression regardless of the label. The strike demonstrates that the post-midterm window isn't a binary on/off switch—kinetic activity continues on an operational cadence independent of political calendar.

## 2. Iran Rejects US Negotiating Terms as "Not Convincing" [OFFICIAL]

Iran's security chief Mohsen Rezaei formally responded to the Vance letter—and rejected US terms outright.

The US response to Iran's demands was not convincing, Iran's security chief Mohsen Rezaei said in a post on X on Tuesday, adding that Washington "must fully and explicitly address Iran's seven conditions." "Only in this case will the Strait of Hormuz be reopened," Rezaei said.

The statement follows Vice President JD Vance's letter to Rezaei and several days of talks between Iranian and Qatari officials. According to Axios, Vance told Rezaei in the letter that, in order to prevent another round of US strikes against Iran, any negotiations "require discussions on the nuclear issue," an idea Iranian officials have repeatedly rejected.

"Tehran's conditions must be met if the US wants Hormuz reopened," the Iranian official told Reuters, adding that nuclear talks could only start afterwards and that the country would not accept Washington's "excessive demands."

Despite Rezaei's rejection, Iran and the United States are far from reaching a deal to end the war, a senior Iranian official told Reuters on Tuesday, despite heightened diplomatic efforts to prevent a potential return to active conflict after the US midterm elections.

**Significance:** This formal rejection closes the narrow diplomatic opening that had emerged from the Doha talks. The fundamental impasse remains unchanged: the US insists nuclear discussions begin from the outset; Iran refuses to discuss nuclear issues until all seven conditions are met. Rezaei's "not convincing" language suggests he doesn't believe the US is serious about accepting Iran's terms, not that negotiations are impossible—but no path to bridging this gap is visible.

## 3. Iran Warns of "New Battlefield" and Expanded Target Bank [OFFICIAL]

Iranian officials issued their sharpest threats since the October exchanges, warning of responses "without geographical limits."

Iran's parliament speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf warned on Tuesday that any US strategic error would open "a new battlefield in the war," hours after a US Air Force jet reportedly struck an IRGC missile system on Qeshm Island.

"The countries of the region should know that, just as the Islamic Republic of Iran stands ready for battle with its finger on the trigger, it has also given the American side a powerful and rational response," Ghalibaf said. "The ball is now in America's court to prevent war."

According to Ghalibaf, senior military commanders and Mojtaba Khamenei—the new supreme leader in direct control of the IRGC—met last week and reportedly decided on "a response without geographical limits." Tehran-based Hamshahri newspaper reported on Tuesday that Iran had updated its "target bank," examining a scenario in which US support bases and infrastructure in Europe would be targeted.

The senior Iranian told Reuters Tehran would respond forcefully, in the region and beyond, to US strikes. "The enemy seeks to force the Iranian nation into submission through threats and military action, but they are sorely mistaken, Iran will not submit," Qalibaf also said on Tuesday.

**Significance:** The "Europe target bank" rhetoric represents a notable escalation in Iranian threats. However, rhetoric and action remain disconnected: Iran has not retaliated for the Qeshm strike with attacks on US assets. The "finger on the trigger" posture appears designed to deter further US strikes while keeping diplomatic channels notionally open. Ghalibaf's dual role as Parliament Speaker and lead negotiator reflects Iran's calibrated approach—threatening maximum retaliation while expressing willingness to negotiate.

## 4. Tucker Carlson Nuclear Claim Goes Viral [UNCONFIRMED]

Tucker Carlson's claim that Trump may be "days away" from a nuclear strike on Iran sparked alarm across the political spectrum—despite lacking verification.

US commentator Tucker Carlson claimed late Sunday that the Trump administration could be days away from launching a nuclear strike on Iran, alleging that Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu is pressuring President Donald Trump to authorize an attack on a suspected nuclear site in central Iran.

"Under pressure from Israeli prime minister Benjamin Netanyahu, the Trump administration may be within days of launching a nuclear strike on Iran," he wrote. "The target would be Pickax Mountain in central Iran, where Israeli intelligence claims the Iranian government has restarted its nuclear enrichment program."

Carlson said Trump could decide by Nov. 7 on a nuclear strike on Iran's Pickaxe Mountain under Netanyahu's pressure. "After the first nuclear strike, World War Three is inevitable," Carlson wrote.

Carlson's warning comes despite Trump explicitly ruling out nuclear weapons during an October 15 Newsmax interview. "I wouldn't use nuclear," the president said.

Carlson claimed that Secretary of State Marco Rubio, CIA Director John Ratcliffe and members of Democratic Party leadership are following Netanyahu's lead.

**Significance:** Carlson provides no independently verifiable evidence. Trump previously ruled out nuclear weapons, and conventional strikes on Fordow remain the more plausible escalation path. However, the claim's virality and the White House's non-denial response have injected nuclear fears into election-day discourse. The claim reflects deep MAGA movement divisions over the war and Netanyahu's influence—Carlson explicitly frames Vance and Witkoff as opposing the hawks around Netanyahu.

## 5. CIA Reportedly Advising "Maximum Pressure" Without War or Deal [REPORTED]

A reported internal administration consensus favors continued economic strangulation over military escalation or diplomatic settlement.

According to Axios, CIA Director John Ratcliffe is reportedly advising President Donald Trump not to resume the war with Iran or reach an agreement, but rather to allow economic pressure on Tehran to continue increasing. Ratcliffe is said to have told the US president that "a prolonged period of economic hardship will induce Iran to make greater concessions" and could also fuel enough domestic anger to threaten the government.

This approach reportedly has support from Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and Secretary of State Marco Rubio.

**Significance:** If accurate, this represents an emerging consensus that could become the dominant strategy post-midterms. "Maximum pressure without major escalation" would mean continued limited strikes, sustained sanctions, and indefinite Hormuz tensions—but no drive toward either a decisive military campaign or a negotiated settlement. This explains Trump's "no rush" posture while maintaining that he wants to destroy "one or two more targets." The goal shifts from immediate military victory to slow-motion regime destabilization.

## 6. Israel: Opposition Formally Unifies, Herzog Consultations Begin Wednesday [VERIFIED]

All major opposition leaders formally committed to Eisenkot ahead of coalition consultations, putting him on track for the first mandate attempt.

Israel's main opposition leaders have agreed to back former military chief Gadi Eisenkot as prime minister, promising to replace Benjamin Netanyahu's government as quickly as possible. The agreement comes a day before President Isaac Herzog begins consultations with parties on who should form the next government.

"The party leaders are coordinated and will act immediately" to replace Netanyahu's coalition "with a national emergency government," the statement said. In a joint statement, the leaders said all their parties would recommend Eisenkot, head of the Yashar party, to Herzog, and that negotiating teams would immediately begin drafting policy guidelines and coalition agreements.

The meeting brought together Eisenkot, former Prime Minister Naftali Bennett, Yisrael Beytenu leader Avigdor Liberman, Yair Golan of the Democrats, and Yair Lapid, who heads the Yesh Atid party.

The leaders stood by four principles set out by Eisenkot, including that all parties in government recognise Israel as a Jewish state and label Hamas a "terrorist" group, conditions that could exclude some Arab-led parties.

President Isaac Herzog plans to summon representatives of the parties elected to Israel's 26th Knesset to his official residence as early as Wednesday, November 4, even before the new parliament is sworn in, to begin the process of forming the country's next government.

President Isaac Herzog will open consultations on Israel's next government Wednesday with the smallest parties and finish with Likud, reversing the usual order. Arab parties holding a record 19 seats are leaning toward Gadi Eisenkot.

**Significance:** The opposition's formal unification, with immediate coalition drafting underway, puts maximum pressure on Netanyahu. The 60-46 endorsement advantage (if Arab parties follow through) makes Eisenkot the likely first mandate recipient. However, Eisenkot's conditions on recognizing Israel as a Jewish state and labeling Hamas a terrorist group create friction with full Arab party participation—likely requiring external support arrangements rather than coalition membership. Netanyahu's options narrow to either a unity government (with Trump/Herzog guarantees) or accepting opposition control.

## 7. Oil Markets Whipsaw on Mixed Signals [VERIFIED]

Oil prices partially recovered from Monday's historic crash following the Qeshm strike, with markets now pricing continued volatility rather than imminent resolution.

Brent crude futures rose 0.33% to $105.12 a barrel while US West Texas Intermediate crude was up 0.89% at $97.32 a barrel. Brent crude futures settled $8.66, or 7.63%, lower to $104.77 a barrel on Monday.

Brent crude was reported at $105.55 per barrel on November 3, approximately $0.78 or 0.74% above the previous settlement of $104.77.

Trading Economics reported Brent fell more than 3% to below $102 a barrel on Tuesday, heading toward a four-week low. However, it also climbed above $106 per barrel following reports of the US attack on Iran's Qeshm Island.

The intraday volatility reflects market uncertainty: diplomacy hopes drove Monday's crash, but the Qeshm strike demonstrates kinetic operations continue regardless.

**Significance:** The ~$104-106 Brent range represents a midpoint between pre-diplomacy highs (~$113) and current lows. Markets are now pricing neither breakthrough nor immediate major escalation—consistent with the emerging "maximum pressure without war" consensus. A major strike on Iranian nuclear facilities would likely push Brent above $115; sustained diplomatic progress could send it below $100.

## 8. US Midterms: Voting Underway [VERIFIED]

In the U.S. House of Representatives, Republicans hold a 218-212 majority over Democrats. All 435 seats are up for election, and the magic number to win a majority is 218.

Republicans' 53-47 Senate majority is under threat, but Democrats would have to flip at least two states President Donald Trump won by double digits in 2024. Presidential swing states like Georgia, Michigan and North Carolina have big races on tap.

Results will begin coming in tonight. Pre-election polling suggested Democratic advantages, though final outcomes remain uncertain.

**Significance:** The midterm results will shape Trump's political latitude on Iran policy. A Democratic House majority could constrain war funding and escalation options; Republican retention would preserve Trump's freedom of action. More immediately, the political calculus that delayed major strikes before the election will no longer apply after tonight.
