## Saudi Arabia Postpones FII "Davos in the Desert" to 2027 [OFFICIAL]

Saudi Arabia postponed its flagship investment conference, known as "Davos in the Desert," to 2027 on Monday amid attacks on the kingdom by Yemen's Iran-backed Houthi rebels on targets including Riyadh's international airport. The Future Investment Initiative, which typically attracts the CEOs of some of the biggest companies in business, finance and technology to Riyadh, will now be held in 2027. The event was originally scheduled for Oct. 26-29 this year in Riyadh.

The attacks have dealt a major blow to Saudi Arabia's efforts to establish itself as a destination for investment, tourism and business, pillars of its push to diversify its economy away from oil. The FII is a key platform for showcasing those ambitions to global investors and luring in billions of dollars of foreign capital.

The US-Israeli war with Iran has disrupted the Gulf's busy calendar of events, with several conferences cancelled, postponed or retooled in the UAE and Qatar in the first part of the year. The kingdom in recent weeks has faced an escalating war with Yemen's Iran-aligned Houthis, which have struck military, energy and civilian infrastructure across the country, including state oil company Aramco's headquarters and Riyadh's international airport, where strikes killed at least 18 people.

**Analysis:** Combined with last week's EU-GCC summit postponement, this represents a devastating one-two punch to MBS's Vision 2030 narrative. A kingdom that cannot host conferences cannot position itself as a global business hub. The Houthis are successfully demonstrating that Saudi Arabia cannot guarantee the security environment international business requires.

## Lavrov Visits Tehran as Russia Seeks Mediator Role [OFFICIAL]

Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov arrived in Tehran on October 19, 2026, for his first visit to Iran since the Islamic Republic's 40-day war with the US and Israel. Iranian media reported that Lavrov is scheduled to hold talks with Iranian officials regarding "regional tensions" and "preventing the resumption of war."

Lavrov landed in the Iranian capital on Monday afternoon on his first visit to Iran since February 2025. Araghchi said talks with Lavrov had focused on regional developments, including the US-Israeli war on Iran.

President Vladimir Putin told Pezeshkian earlier this month that Russia is ready to play a mediating role in ending the Middle East war. Lavrov's visit on Monday follows U.S. President Donald Trump's claims that Russia is "doing very little, if anything" to help Iran in the war with the U.S. and Israel.

Prior to arriving in Iran, Lavrov attended a strategic dialogue session in Bahrain on October 18, 2026, between Russia and the Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC). GCC members urged Lavrov to use Moscow's influence with Tehran to push for serious dialogue and restore shipping through the Strait of Hormuz.

**Analysis:** Lavrov's Tehran visit—bookended by his GCC meetings in Bahrain—positions Russia as the only actor with working relationships on all sides. Moscow benefits from high oil prices but also from being the indispensable mediator. Gulf states are actively pressing Russia to help de-escalate Iranian attacks, creating potential leverage for a face-saving off-ramp if both sides want one after the US midterms.

## Houthi Strike Kills 53+ at Shabwa Military Camp [VERIFIED]

A Houthi missile attack on the al-AIam military camp in southern Yemen has killed at least 53 people, according to local authorities. The Iranian-backed armed group launched three ballistic missiles on Monday at the camp in Shabwa governorate during a training exercise.

Officials for the internationally recognised government and medical sources initially said at least 18 people were killed and more than 30 wounded. Those numbers increased dramatically later on Monday, when the local authority in Shabwa said 53 people had been killed and 200 injured.

Those targeted were part of a newly established force called Rapid Intervention, which is tasked with defending the area. The missiles struck while recruits were lining up in formation for a training session.

The attack was carried out as fighting continued to rage across Yemen. The civil war between the Saudi-backed government and Houthis had eased after a 2022 truce, but the regional impact of this year's US-Israel war on Iran reignited hostilities.

An airstrike on Sunday blamed on Saudi-led forces killed at least nine people, including five children and two women, in Yemen's rebel-held northern province of Saada, the Houthi-run Health Ministry said. It was one of several that killed or wounded more than 30 people over the weekend.

**Analysis:** This is a significant escalation—the deadliest single attack of the Yemen conflict this year. The Houthis are fighting on multiple fronts simultaneously: attacking Saudi Arabia, striking Yemeni government forces, and maintaining pressure on Bab al-Mandab shipping. The 2022 ceasefire has definitively collapsed, and Yemen has become a primary escalation pathway tied to the broader Iran confrontation.

## Trump Endorses Netanyahu; IDF on Highest Alert [OFFICIAL/VERIFIED]

In a post on Truth Social on Sunday, Trump praised the Israeli leader for being an ally of the US in its war on Iran. He added that he was giving his "Complete and Total Endorsement" to Netanyahu.

Trump described the Likud Party leader as a "Great Warrior Prime Minister" and "an even greater Partner for Peace in combating the Islamic Republic of Iran." "If Israel has the wrong Leader, which would be anyone other than Bibi," Trump wrote, "America will have to do this work ourselves, and I am not inclined to do so."

Trump has backed Netanyahu in past elections. In recent months, however, Trump's remarks about the prime minister had been far more subdued, and he repeatedly demurred when asked whether he would support him.

Israel Defense Forces Chief of Staff Lt. Gen. Eyal Zamir placed the military on its "highest state of alert" ahead of next week's election and canceled soldiers' leave. Zamir held an assessment Monday morning with senior officers, during which he "stressed that the IDF's mission is to enable the democratic elections to take place on schedule and safely."

IDF Chief of Staff Lt.-Gen. Eyal Zamir announced a leave restriction for all units on Monday, effective Thursday, that could last up to six days, until the October 27 elections. In addition to the leave restriction, the IDF will also bolster its numbers across a wide range of units and brigades, particularly in the West Bank.

A Channel 12 poll finds 72% intend to vote, but among opposition voters the figure is 81%, while it is 74% among pro-Netanyahu voters.

**Analysis:** Trump's endorsement—after weeks of hedging—ties his administration explicitly to Netanyahu's re-election. The implicit threat that the US might scale back operations if Netanyahu loses represents direct interference in Israeli politics. The IDF's "highest alert" status and leave freeze reflect genuine concern about potential attacks timed to election day, though security officials have not identified a specific imminent threat.

## Iran's Seven-Day Hormuz Proposal Remains on Table [REPORTED]

Iran's offer to the United States to reopen the Strait of Hormuz within seven days is still on the table, Foreign Minister Abbas Araqchi told Iranian media outlets on Sunday. Iran presented its proposal in mid-September after an interim ceasefire collapsed. Tehran later acknowledged receiving a US response but Iran's security chief said last week negotiations have stalled because Washington failed to address all seven conditions for reopening the strait.

Iran's conditions include, among other demands, the lifting of the US blockade on its ports, the lifting of US sanctions on its oil exports as well as ending hostilities on all regional fronts. If Washington accepts Tehran's conditions, the strait could reopen within six days, with talks resuming the following day, Araqchi said. A central point of disagreement has been Washington's insistence on addressing Iran's nuclear programme from the outset.

Mohsen Rezaei, the head of Iran's Supreme National Security Council, said last week that the latest US response to Iran's proposal was "inadequate," though he said that Washington had accepted at least some of Tehran's conditions. Iran proposed a deal that would require the US to end its blockade of Iranian ports, waive some sanctions, and release some of Iran's frozen funds in exchange for Iran fully opening the Strait of Hormuz on the sixth day.

**Analysis:** The conditional nature of Iran's proposal—demanding sanctions relief and port access before any Hormuz reopening—makes near-term acceptance unlikely. However, the continued diplomatic signaling suggests Iran is keeping channels open for post-midterm negotiations. The core impasse remains: the US wants nuclear concessions upfront; Iran wants economic relief first.

## Israeli Election Remains Deadlocked [VERIFIED]

According to a new poll by Kan, Netanyahu's bloc receives 51 seats while his opponent's bloc gains one seat, receiving 52 seats. A new poll shows former IDF chief Gadi Eisenkot's bloc gaining a seat, while Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's bloc loses two.

Due to the boycott of Arab parties pursued by some parties in Eisenkot's bloc, the bloc is at 52 seats. Both Arab parties, Mansour Abbas' United Arab List and the Joint List, win a combined total of 13 seats. According to the poll, Yashar and Likud each gained one seat compared to last week: Eisenkot's party receives 23 seats, and Netanyahu's party 21.

The Maariv poll found that 48% of people think Gadi Eisenkot would make a better prime minister than Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, who had 41% support. The majority of Israelis would prefer a unity government to another election if the upcoming vote on October 27 doesn't produce a clear result.

**Analysis:** With eight days until voting, neither bloc has a path to 61 seats without Arab parties, which opposition leaders have ruled out. This creates a high probability of post-election deadlock, potentially requiring unity government negotiations or another election—either of which would complicate US-Israeli coordination timing on Iran.
