### **SAUDI ARABIA POSTPONES "DAVOS IN THE DESERT" TO 2027** [OFFICIAL]

Saudi Arabia on Monday postponed its big annual investor conference until 2027 as Iran-backed Houthi militants have escalated missile and drone attacks on the kingdom in recent weeks. The Future Investment Initiative, originally scheduled to take place in Riyadh from Oct. 26 to Oct. 29, has been postponed until 2027. Some of the biggest names in finance were listed as speakers for the conference, including JPMorgan's Jamie Dimon and Goldman Sachs' David Solomon.

Informally dubbed "Davos in the Desert," the state-backed annual event has showcased the kingdom's ambitious investment plans in a bid to attract billions of dollars needed to fund its economic transformation away from hydrocarbons towards tourism, sports and technology. The US-Israeli war with Iran has disrupted the Gulf's busy calendar of events, with several conferences cancelled, postponed or retooled in the UAE and Qatar in the first part of the year.

"The executives I've spoken to are very, very, very hesitant to travel to Riyadh for this month's conferences," a consultant who advises Saudi firms told AFP. "Companies won't put their employees in harm's way for the sake of an investment conference."

**[ANALYSIS]** Combined with last week's EU-GCC summit postponement, this represents a devastating one-two punch to Saudi Arabia's global positioning. Vision 2030's core promise—that Saudi Arabia can become a global hub for business and culture—is being directly undermined by the Houthis' missile campaign. Two flagship events cancelled in a week.

### **LAVROV VISITS TEHRAN FOR TALKS ON WAR AND MEDIATION** [OFFICIAL]

Russia's Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov has met his Iranian counterpart Abbas Araghchi in Tehran, to discuss the war with the United States and efforts to end it. Lavrov landed in the Iranian capital on Monday afternoon on his first visit to Iran since February 2025. Araghchi said talks with Lavrov had focused on regional developments, including the US-Israeli war on Iran.

Lavrov said Russia is committed to a 20-year strategic partnership agreement with Iran, which officially came into force on October 2. He said Russian specialists would gradually return to the Bushehr nuclear power plant after they left during the war.

President Vladimir Putin told Pezeshkian earlier this month that Russia is ready to play a mediating role in ending the Middle East war. Lavrov's visit on Monday follows U.S. President Donald Trump's claims that Russia is "doing very little, if anything" to help Iran in the war with the U.S. and Israel.

The meeting will be the fourth in-person meeting between the two countries' foreign ministers in 2026, in addition to 14 telephone conversations. The ministers were expected to discuss the situation in the Persian Gulf and ways to resolve what it termed the "acute international crisis triggered by the illegal US and Israeli aggression against Iran."

**[ANALYSIS]** Lavrov's visit—his first to Tehran since before the war—is significant for several reasons: (1) Russia signaling it's available as mediator, (2) the return of Russian technicians to Bushehr suggests Tehran is preparing for post-war reconstruction, and (3) Gulf states are reportedly pressing Moscow to help calm Iranian attacks. Russia may be positioned to broker a face-saving off-ramp if both sides want one after the US midterms.

### **HOUTHI MISSILE STRIKE KILLS 53+ IN YEMEN'S SHABWA** [VERIFIED]

A Houthi missile attack on the al-AIam military camp in southern Yemen has killed at least 53 people, according to local authorities. The Iranian-backed armed group launched three ballistic missiles on Monday at the camp in Shabwa governorate during a training exercise.

Those numbers increased dramatically later on Monday, when the local authority in Shabwa said 53 people had been killed and 200 injured. The attack was carried out as fighting continued to rage across Yemen. The civil war between the Saudi-backed government and Houthis had eased after a 2022 truce, but the regional impact of this year's US-Israel war on Iran reignited hostilities.

**[ANALYSIS]** This is one of the deadliest single attacks in Yemen this year. The Houthis are fighting a multi-front war—attacking Saudi Arabia while simultaneously pressing the Yemeni government. Their seizure of Red Sea coastal territory last month has dramatically raised the stakes for the Bab al-Mandab chokepoint.

### **IRAN AIR REVEALS WAR LOSSES: 20-30 AIRCRAFT DESTROYED** [OFFICIAL]

Iran Air chief says 20 to 30 commercial aircraft destroyed during US-Israeli war. Taher Abdolhai, Chief Executive Officer of Iran Air, has said at a news conference that Iran needs approximately 1,000 passenger aircraft, but currently only has around 300. Of those, between 130 and 140 aircraft were operational before the war, with 20 to 30 destroyed during the conflict.

Abdolhai also said between 70 and 80 aircraft were targeted during the war. He claims Israeli attacks caused particularly severe damage to Iran Air aircraft, saying some planes belonging to other operators were struck by single missiles, while one of his carrier's aircraft was completely destroyed.

**[ANALYSIS]** This is the first official Iranian acknowledgment of specific aviation losses. The destruction of 20-30 civilian aircraft—even if military-adjacent—illustrates the breadth of the air campaign's impact on Iran's infrastructure. Combined with sanctions preventing spare parts, Iran's civil aviation is in severe crisis.

### **VP VANCE CLAIMS US HAS "DECISIVELY WON"** [OFFICIAL]

U.S. Vice President JD Vance on Saturday said that in "some ways" the United States has "decisively won" the Iran war.

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi yesterday said Iran has "unfinished jobs" too and warned against the United States renewing attacks. Araghchi urged the United States to accept Iran's proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz within seven days as the deal is still on the table.

CENTCOM Commander Adm. Brad Cooper on Friday said Iran-backed militias in the region have been sending money back to the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps to relieve the economic pressure of the Iran war. Cooper said the U.S. naval blockade has cost Iran $25 billion in lost revenue and added that CENTCOM and the U.S. Treasury Department in recent weeks managed to freeze $350 million that Iran held in cryptocurrency.

**[ANALYSIS]** The dueling narratives are stark: Vance claims decisive victory while Araghchi says Iran has "unfinished jobs." CENTCOM's $25 billion figure is significant—this represents substantial economic damage to Tehran. But the stalemate persists: Iran cannot export oil, but the US cannot force Hormuz fully open.

### **TRUMP WAIVES RUSSIA SANCTIONS** [OFFICIAL]

President Donald Trump signed an executive order on Friday waiving new sanctions and tariffs on Russia required under the law named after the late Sen. Lindsey Graham, just two days before they were set to take effect. It's the second time in as many weeks that Trump has waived sanctions on Russia as he turns to Russian President Vladimir Putin to help relieve rising diesel prices.

Trump told Congress that the waivers issued Friday were in the national interest. The order now waives every operative section of the law pertaining to Russia, including a requirement to impose tariffs of up to 500% on all Russian goods, such as oil and gas, and the requirement to impose tariffs of up to 100% on the top five buyers of Russian crude oil.

Trump's executive order comes after he made a deal with Russian President Vladimir Putin by which the Kremlin agreed to eventually release millions of tons of diesel into the global market amid soaring prices.

**[ANALYSIS]** Trump is trading Russia policy for energy price relief two weeks before the midterms. This is having a modest cooling effect on oil prices. The move has sparked bipartisan backlash but reflects the administration's priority: managing domestic fuel prices over Ukraine policy.

## Assessment

**[ANALYSIS]** Three days of developments confirm the current stalemate while revealing its asymmetric impacts:

1. **Saudi Arabia is paying the highest price.** Two flagship international events cancelled in a week (EU-GCC summit, FII conference), airport closed since October 10, and 20+ killed in Houthi attacks. MBS's Vision 2030 narrative is crumbling—a kingdom that can't host conferences can't become a global hub.

2. **The economic war is biting both sides.** CENTCOM claims $25 billion in blocked Iranian exports; Iran Air admits 20-30 aircraft destroyed. But oil at ~$108 (up 75% YoY) is straining global economies and US voters—hence Trump's Russia deal.

3. **Russia is positioning itself.** Lavrov's Tehran visit, Putin's mediation offer, and Trump's sanctions waiver suggest a possible Russia-facilitated off-ramp being constructed. Moscow benefits from high oil prices but also from being the indispensable mediator.

4. **Neither side can force a conclusion.** Vance's "decisive victory" claim contradicts visible reality: Hormuz is effectively closed, Iran has "unfinished jobs," and no diplomatic breakthrough is in sight. The stalemate is exactly what my "war of attrition through 2027" base case describes.

5. **Israeli election in 8 days.** Netanyahu's airbase threats could be election positioning or genuine preparation for post-election action. With neither bloc at 61 seats, coalition math will determine Israel's regional posture.
