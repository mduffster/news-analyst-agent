## Key Developments Since Prior Report (July 23)

### 1. Trump "Close to Decision" on Massive Attack

**[OFFICIAL]** US President Donald Trump is close to making a decision on whether to launch a new major combat operation against Iran. "I am considering a massive attack. Bigger than ever before. I am close to making a decision. We are all set for it," he said.

**[OFFICIAL]** Trump added that Israel "would join in two minutes if I ask them to," but that there would be "consequences" to it joining the strikes, and that the United States didn't need any assistance.

**[VERIFIED]** In a brief interview, Trump acknowledged that such a decision would have consequences and stressed he hasn't made a determination yet. Trump didn't give a deadline for his decision. Two other U.S. officials confirmed no call has been made and no new orders have been given to the military.

**[OFFICIAL]** The president also said that Iranians "want to negotiate." Despite Trump's claims that Iran is eager to negotiate, he also said "they haven't received enough pain yet."

*Assessment: This is the clearest signal yet of imminent major escalation. The "bigger than Operation Epic Fury" framing suggests strikes on regime/nuclear targets. However, Trump has made similar threats before without following through immediately. The key variable is whether Iran's continued defiance triggers action.*

### 2. Iran Rejects US Ceasefire Proposal Delivered by Iraqi PM

**[VERIFIED]** Iran on Thursday rejected a ceasefire proposal from President Donald Trump that was carried to Tehran by the Iraqi prime minister, according to Iranian and Iraqi officials.

**[VERIFIED]** The officials said Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi's trip to Iran followed his recent visit with Trump at the White House. The details of the ceasefire proposal were not immediately clear. But Iranian officials said that their government was not interested in a temporary deal that left the question of control over the Strait of Hormuz unresolved.

**[OFFICIAL]** Iranian FM Araghchi stated: "The problem is America's outlook, which is illogical, greedy and controlling."

*Assessment: Iran's rejection of diplomacy and insistence on Hormuz control as a precondition eliminates the near-term ceasefire path. This hardens the trajectory toward escalation.*

### 3. US Completes 13th Consecutive Night of Strikes; Four Killed Near Ahvaz

**[VERIFIED]** The U.S. military completed its latest round of strikes on Iran overnight, marking a 13th consecutive night of attacks, according to U.S. Central Command.

**[VERIFIED]** US forces completed strikes against Iranian military command centers, drone storage facilities, communication networks, coastal surveillance sites, and maritime capabilities — the first published target list of the July campaign to lead with command-and-control nodes.

**[REPORTED]** Iranian media reported blasts in Khorramabad, Jask, Ahvaz, and Bandar Abbas and on Qeshm Island, and the Tasnim news agency, citing Khuzestan provincial authorities, reported four people killed and five wounded in a US missile strike near Ahvaz.

*Assessment: Strike campaign continues without pause. The expansion to command-and-control nodes represents escalation in target selection. This is the second consecutive night with fatalities reported inside Iran.*

### 4. Hormuz Crossings Collapse to Lowest Level Since May 7

**[VERIFIED]** The number of tankers crossing the Strait of Hormuz fell to just one on Thursday, the lowest level since May 7, according to shiptracking data. Only one tanker crossed the Strait of Hormuz on July 23, compared with three the day earlier, data from analytics firm Kpler showed.

**[VERIFIED]** Very large crude carrier New Giant, loaded with 2 million barrels of Iraqi Basrah crude, exited the strait on Thursday and is due to arrive at China's Rizhao port by mid-August. No ships entered the Strait of Hormuz on July 23.

**[VERIFIED]** Effectively no, it is closed to commercial shipping as of 23 July 2026. 15 ships transited on July 19 vs ~88/day normal.

*Assessment: Hormuz is functionally closed. Zero inbound traffic confirms commercial shipping has halted. The IRGC's "completely closed" declaration is now operational reality.*

### 5. Oil Retreats From $100, But Remains Elevated

**[VERIFIED]** Brent crude retreated after hitting $100 a barrel in the previous session on concern over widening risks to Middle East supplies. The global benchmark fell as much as 4.3% on Friday, having reached triple digits the day before for the first time in two months.

**[VERIFIED]** At 5:35 a.m. Eastern Time today, oil was priced at $97.04 per barrel with Brent serving as the benchmark. That's a drop of $1.45 compared with yesterday morning and around $27.50 higher than the price one year ago.

**[VERIFIED]** Brent rose to 100.65 USD/Bbl on July 23, 2026, up 7.00% from the previous day. Over the past month, Brent's price has risen 36.49%.

*Assessment: Oil pulled back from $100+ but remains ~35% above pre-conflict levels from early July. The retreat appears driven by profit-taking rather than fundamental improvement in supply outlook.*

### 6. Israeli Cities Open Bomb Shelters; Iran Threatens Tel Aviv Strike

**[VERIFIED]** Ramat Gan, Eilat, and Karmiel opened all their public bomb shelters on Thursday after municipal assessments found that the risk of Iranian missile fire over the weekend had increased and was no longer negligible. Kiryat Gat followed shortly after.

**[REPORTED]** The precautions come as the United States sharply escalates its own military campaign against Iran, now in its 13th consecutive night of strikes. Two Iranian officials cited by The New York Times said the next phase of the escalation would include a strike on Tel Aviv if President Trump follows through on threats to hit Tehran and its infrastructure directly.

**[OFFICIAL]** Defense Minister Israel Katz, during a security assessment on Thursday, said Israel is "prepared for every possibility" amid the tensions with Iran. "If Iran attacks Israel, it will suffer a crushing blow," he added.

**[VERIFIED]** Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu will convene top ministers and aides for a national security meeting tomorrow.

*Assessment: Israel is preparing for potential direct Iranian retaliation if Trump's "massive attack" proceeds. Iran's explicit threat to strike Tel Aviv links Israeli security directly to US escalation decisions.*

### 7. IRGC Commanders and Missile Equipment Flew to Yemen

**[REPORTED]** Iran flew Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) commanders, military advisers and missile- and drone-related equipment into Yemen this month, according to four sources, in a move that suggests Tehran is seeking to strengthen the ability of its Houthi allies to threaten Red Sea shipping. Four sources said Iran transferred the IRGC personnel and military-related equipment on a flight from Tehran to Yemen on July 13.

**[REPORTED]** The two Iranian sources told Reuters that between 10 and 21 IRGC personnel, including senior commanders, were on the Mahan Air flight.

**[VERIFIED]** The reported deployment came days before the Houthis announced a naval blockade of Saudi Arabia and later claimed attacks on oil tankers. Iran denied supplying the Houthis with missile capabilities, while the Houthis rejected claims that Iranian military experts were on board the flight.

*Assessment: This report, if accurate, confirms direct Iranian coordination of the Houthi Red Sea campaign. The timing (July 13, one week before the Saudi blockade declaration) suggests premeditated escalation.*

### 8. House Passes War Powers Resolution 214-208; Senate Effort Fails

**[VERIFIED]** The war powers resolution passed 214-208 with mostly Democratic votes. House Democrats on Thursday forced a second successful vote to end the war with Iran, but they did not pick up any new GOP support despite escalating tensions in the Mideast and the recent deaths of four U.S. service members.

**[VERIFIED]** The resolution passed on Thursday, nearly a week after three US military personnel were killed by an Iranian attack on a base in Jordan, with a 214-208 vote. Shortly after, another resolution in the US Senate failed to pass by a vote of 47 to 49.

**[VERIFIED]** Eighteen U.S. troops have died since fighting broke out in February, but four have died since July 17. U.S. forces have also suffered nearly 500 injuries, with 100 of those coming in the past two weeks, according to the Pentagon.

*Assessment: Congressional opposition is symbolic but growing. The resolution lacks force of law. However, casualty numbers (18 killed, ~500 wounded, with 100 wounded in past two weeks) show the human cost is accelerating.*

### 9. Saudi Aramco Rerouting Oil via Mediterranean

**[VERIFIED]** Saudi Aramco has offered additional crude cargoes for loading from Egypt's Mediterranean port of Sidi Kerir, according to five trading sources, as Houthi threats to Saudi shipping raise risks for southbound Red Sea exports through the Bab el-Mandeb strait.

**[VERIFIED]** The clean tanker Torm Innovation – loaded with about 500,000 barrels of naphtha bound for Asia – has made a slight turn for the Suez Canal exit instead of a typical Bab Al Mandeb exit. Rerouting ships via the Suez Canal to Asia instead of via the Bab Al Mandeb strait could make a voyage nearly three times as long.

*Assessment: Saudi Arabia is adapting to dual-chokepoint crisis by routing through SUMED pipeline to Mediterranean. However, capacity constraints (~2.5 million bpd) cannot replace full Red Sea/Hormuz volumes.*
