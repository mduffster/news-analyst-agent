## Critical Developments (September 10-11)

### 1. CRITICAL: Houthis Complete Takeover of Bab al-Mandab — Perim Island Seized [VERIFIED]

"Boats carrying armed Houthi fighters reached Mayyun Island after government forces withdrew from it yesterday," a local government official said, referring to a rocky volcanic island also known as Perim that divides the narrowest part of the strait into two shipping lanes.

"The Houthis have completed their takeover of the Bab el-Mandeb area and Mayyun Island, which is located in the middle of the strait," he added, speaking on condition of anonymity.

Earlier, two sources from the Saudi-backed, internationally recognized Yemeni government said its forces had withdrawn from Perim and that the Houthis had also taken the mainland Red Sea coastal town of Dhubab, which faces the island.

The seizure came just one day after the group captured the port city of Mokha, suggesting a coordinated offensive along Yemen's Red Sea coast rather than opportunistic skirmishing. Pro-government forces apparently retreated from the island without a prolonged fight, handing the Houthis a strategic prize.

Risk analyst Firas Modad noted the capture gives the Houthis "the ability to decide which ships can pass through the strait unhindered."

**Assessment:** [VERIFIED] This is the single most consequential strategic development since the war began. Iran-aligned forces now effectively control or threaten both the Strait of Hormuz AND the Bab al-Mandab — the two key chokepoints for global oil transit. Saudi tankers were already avoiding the strait before Perim's capture, and "now their position will be much, much worse."

### 2. MAJOR: Trump Rejects Saudi Request for US Strikes on Houthis [REPORTED]

The crown prince reportedly called Trump twice on Thursday to request strikes on the Houthis, but the American president declined. Washington will provide Saudi Arabia with intelligence on the Houthis and targeting data, and more than 200 US military personnel are already in Saudi Arabia providing "non-kinetic support."

Meanwhile, Saudi Foreign Minister Faisal bin Farhan spoke with his Iranian counterpart Abbas Araghchi about "the escalation of tensions and the growing insecurity in the region," according to Tehran's foreign ministry.

Trump's refusal to authorize strikes suggests Washington is seeking to limit direct U.S. involvement in Yemen while continuing to support Saudi operations through intelligence and targeting assistance.

**Assessment:** [REPORTED] Trump's refusal to strike Houthis even as they complete the Bab al-Mandab takeover is a major strategic decision. The administration appears focused on limiting the war's geographic scope, but this comes at the cost of ceding a critical chokepoint to Iranian proxies. The Saudi-Iranian ministerial conversation amid this crisis suggests possible back-channel accommodation.

### 3. MAJOR: East-West Pipeline Reportedly Struck — Unconfirmed but Alarming [UNCONFIRMED]

Saudi Arabia's East-West Pipeline corridor was targeted by Houthi rebels. Saudi Arabian authorities have not confirmed the strikes yet, but regional observers and experts have analysed satellite images. The images showed a large plume of smoke near the route of the Petroline toward Mahd adh-Dhahab.

"Multiple fire hotspots were detected at six points along the pipeline route at roughly the same time, around 17:56 UTC," reported OSINT analysts, while sharing satellite imagery.

Gulf News reported that a strike on a pumping station along the East-West pipeline reduced throughput by 700,000 barrels per day. The pipeline has become a key export route during the conflict, allowing Saudi Arabia to move crude to the Red Sea while flows through the Strait of Hormuz remain constrained.

**Assessment:** [UNCONFIRMED] If confirmed, this would be catastrophic. The East-West Pipeline is Saudi Arabia's only export route that bypasses the Strait of Hormuz. With Hormuz closed and Bab al-Mandab now Houthi-controlled, damage to this pipeline would effectively pressure ALL major Saudi export routes. Saudi silence is concerning; the market is already pricing in disruption regardless.

### 4. MAJOR: US Diesel Prices Hit Record $6/Gallon [VERIFIED]

Diesel prices in the U.S. hit yet another record on Friday, soaring past $6 a gallon on average as Washington's war with Iran disrupts the world's flow of fuel. The national average of $6.05 is up from $5.85 last week and $3.70 this time last year, according to motor club AAA.

Diesel prices are up by 55% since the United States and Israel launched strikes on Iran in February. It is on pace to record the largest annual percentage increase of all time this year.

Brown University estimates higher diesel prices have cost U.S. consumers more than $46 billion since the war began. About 20% of that is in Texas and California alone.

"Diesel is the real lifeblood of the economy," noted Bob McNally of Rapidan Energy. Higher diesel prices are passed down to consumers in what they pay for food, consumer goods and energy. Diesel fuels the trucks, trains and ships that bring goods to market.

**Assessment:** [VERIFIED] This is a direct political cost of the war with 53 days to midterms. The Department of Energy sharply raised its 2027 diesel price forecast this week, acknowledging a painful higher-for-longer scenario. With diesel at $6, inflation looks like it's here to stay for a while.

### 5. MAJOR: Israel Destroys Hezbollah Ali al-Taher Tunnel Complex [VERIFIED]

Using 1,100 tons of explosives, the Israeli military said on Thursday that it had demolished a "strategic" underground facility belonging to the Hezbollah terror group beneath a mountainous ridge in southern Lebanon.

Lebanon reported a massive explosion, while the US Geological Survey recorded a magnitude 4.1 tremor nearby.

"The terror infrastructure and the command center on the ridge were designed to serve as the staging base for the conquest of the Galilee... Their destruction completes the achievement of operational control over the Ali Taher ridge — above and below ground," said Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and Defense Minister Israel Katz in a joint statement.

The destroyed infrastructure, built over two decades with Iranian funding, was intended as a staging base for a potential assault on northern Israel's Galilee region.

**Assessment:** [VERIFIED] Israel proceeded with the tunnel destruction despite earlier Iranian warnings. Iran has not (yet) retaliated — a notable silence that suggests Tehran's deterrence credibility has limits when it comes to proxy defensive positions, even as its overall strategic position has improved dramatically via the Houthi advance.

### 7. Iran Hails Houthi Victory — Calls for End to Saudi "Blockade" [OFFICIAL]

Mohammad Mokhber, an adviser to Iran's Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei, congratulated the Houthis on their "resounding victory." "Iran's might in the Strait of Hormuz and the homegrown resolve of the resistance have driven the US and its allies into a tactical and strategic impasse," he added.

Iran's Ministry of Foreign Affairs issued a statement calling for an end to Saudi Arabia's blockade of Yemen, and urging a resumption of negotiations. It was the first statement on Yemen issued by Tehran since the Houthi seizure of Mokha.

A member of the Houthis' politburo sought to allay global fears for international shipping, stating the group posed "no danger" to Red Sea cargo routes. The Houthis corrected this statement to say Saudi Arabia remains an exception. "Maritime navigation is safe for all companies except Saudi ships, which have already been banned," Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree said.

**Assessment:** [OFFICIAL] Iran is attempting to reframe the Yemen escalation as a diplomatic/humanitarian issue while its aligned actor has gained decisive leverage at the chokepoint. The Houthi statement suggesting "no danger" to international shipping (except Saudi) indicates they may prefer strategic control over immediate disruption — they gain more leverage by threatening attacks than by executing them.
