## US-Israel-Iran Conflict: Daily Update — May 14, 2026

### Executive Summary

**The Trump-Xi summit in Beijing produced agreement on Hormuz principles but no concrete commitments to pressure Iran. Israel-Lebanon talks have resumed in Washington at the third round, while BRICS meeting in Delhi exposed deep divisions with Iran accusing the UAE of direct military involvement. A new vessel seizure near the UAE coast signals continued Hormuz tensions. Oil dropped sharply to $107.82, falling $3.05 from yesterday's high of $110.87.**

**Bottom line:** The diplomatic arena is crowded but thin on substance. Trump and Xi agreed Hormuz "must remain open" and Iran "can never have a nuclear weapon" — positions both held before the summit. No Chinese commitment to pressure Iran emerged, and Rubio explicitly stated the US "didn't ask for anything." Meanwhile, Iran used BRICS to publicly accuse the UAE of military collaboration with Israel, and Israel continues operations in Lebanon even as talks resume.

### Critical New Developments (May 13-14)

#### Trump-Xi Summit: Hormuz Agreement, No Pressure Commitment
**[OFFICIAL]** President Trump and China's Xi Jinping met Thursday and agreed that the Strait of Hormuz "must remain open" and that Iran "can never have a nuclear weapon," according to the White House.

**[OFFICIAL]** Trump and Xi agreed that the Strait of Hormuz must remain open to restore energy flows through the critical waterway. Xi reiterated Beijing's opposition to the "militarization" of the energy artery and "any effort to charge a toll for its use." China also expressed interest in purchasing more U.S. oil to wean off its reliance on Middle Eastern crude. Both countries agreed that Iran can never have a nuclear weapon.

**[OFFICIAL]** Rubio told NBC News that the United States was not asking for Beijing's help with Iran. "The Chinese side said they are not in favor of militarizing the Straits of Hormuz, and they're not in favor of a tolling system, and that's our position."

**[OFFICIAL]** Trump raised the issue of Iran with Xi, but "he didn't ask them for anything," Secretary of State Marco Rubio said. "We're not asking for China's help. We don't need their help," Rubio said.

**[REPORTED]** Trump described talks as "extremely positive" in a toast at the start of a lavish state banquet, and invited Xi to the U.S. later this year. The two leaders will meet again tomorrow.

**Assessment:** The summit produced rhetorical alignment but no operational deliverables on Iran. Xi's restatement of opposition to Hormuz "militarization" could be read as criticism of both Iran's blockade and US naval operations. The key tell: Rubio's admission that Trump "didn't ask for anything" suggests the administration has abandoned hopes of Chinese pressure on Tehran.

---

#### Xi Issues Sharp Taiwan Warning
**[OFFICIAL]** Xi issued a stark warning about what could happen if the US crosses its so-called red line on the sensitive issue of Taiwan. If the issue is "mishandled," Washington and Beijing could "collide or even enter into conflict, pushing the entire China-US relationship into a highly dangerous situation."

**[REPORTED]** There was no mention of Taiwan in the American readout, which focused on trade and the Iran war.

**Assessment:** The asymmetry in readouts is telling — China emphasized Taiwan warnings, the US emphasized Iran and trade. Xi used the summit to extract rhetorical positioning rather than offer concrete Iran assistance.

---

#### Iran Allowed Chinese Tanker Through Hormuz
**[REPORTED]** China is one of the few countries whose ships are very occasionally permitted by Iran to pass through the Strait of Hormuz. On Wednesday, as Trump arrived in Beijing, a Chinese-registered super tanker carrying two million barrels of oil was allowed by Iran to transit after being stranded in the Persian Gulf since early March. It could be seen as a timely reminder from Tehran of the tangible benefits to Beijing of a close relationship.

**[REPORTED]** Tehran has allowed some Chinese ships to pass through the Strait of Hormuz after discussions with China's foreign minister and ambassador, Iran's semi-official state news agency Fars reported Thursday.

**Assessment:** Iran's selective opening of Hormuz for Chinese vessels on the eve of the summit was likely calculated to demonstrate to Xi the benefits of maintaining close ties with Tehran — a counter-pressure to any US request for Chinese leverage.

#### New Vessel Seized Off UAE Coast
**[VERIFIED]** A ship off the UAE coast near the Strait of Hormuz has been taken by unknown people and is now headed towards Iranian waters, a UK maritime agency said on Thursday. The vessel was "taken by unauthorised personnel whilst at anchor" 38 nautical miles or 70 kilometres northeast of Fujairah, and "is now bound for Iranian territorial waters."

**[REPORTED]** Several ships have been seized or attacked in or near the key waterway as Iran and the US push on with rival blockades. Iran has captured a number of vessels, including a tanker identified as the Ocean Koi last week.

**Assessment:** The seizure, coinciding with the Trump-Xi summit and BRICS meeting, demonstrates Iran's continued ability to project power in the Gulf regardless of diplomatic developments.

#### Israel-Lebanon Peace Talks Resume in Washington
**[VERIFIED]** Lebanon will demand Israel cease fire at face-to-face talks in Washington on Thursday, a senior Lebanese official said, as Iran-backed Hezbollah and Israel continued to trade blows despite a U.S.-backed ceasefire agreement.

**[VERIFIED]** The Washington meetings mark the highest-level contact between Lebanon and Israel in decades. Both Lebanon and Israel are broadening their delegations for this round. Lebanese Presidential Special Envoy Simon Karam and Israel's Deputy National Security Adviser Yossi Draznin will participate in the talks, as well as senior Israeli military representatives.

**[REPORTED]** Unlike the last round, which Trump brought to the White House, or the first round, neither Secretary of State Marco Rubio nor Trump will participate as the president is on a state visit to China.

**[REPORTED]** While Lebanon is demanding a permanent truce and a halt to the destruction of border villages, Israel seeks to retain full freedom of operations in Lebanon until the Hezbollah issue is resolved. It is unclear how the United States will bridge these positions.

**[REPORTED]** One of Washington's demands is that Beirut repeal the 1955 law prohibiting normalization with Israel. "The Americans are telling the Lebanese: it is time for Beirut to offer something in return as well. So far, Israel has maintained the ceasefire and has taken significant measures to improve the atmosphere, and without action from the Lebanese side, it will be difficult to continue the ceasefire."

**Assessment:** The third round features expanded delegations and military participation but faces fundamental gaps — Lebanon wants a permanent ceasefire, Israel wants freedom of operations. Trump's absence reduces the summit's weight.

#### BRICS Meeting: Iran vs UAE Tensions Escalate
**[VERIFIED]** Foreign ministers from the BRICS nations began a two-day meeting in New Delhi on Thursday as the expanding bloc faces divisions over the war in Iran, rising energy prices and growing global economic uncertainty. It comes as the war in Iran has disrupted global energy supplies and driven up oil prices and coincides with U.S. President Donald Trump's meeting with Chinese leader Xi Jinping in Beijing.

**[OFFICIAL]** At the meeting, Araghchi urged BRICS nations to condemn the U.S. and Israel over what he called their "unlawful aggression" against Tehran. He called on the bloc members and other countries to "take practical steps to stop warmongering." Araghchi also urged the bloc members to stop what he called the politicization of international institutions. "It is necessary for all of us to intensify our efforts to end this sense of superiority and impunity on the part of the United States."

**[REPORTED]** Iran's foreign minister accused the United Arab Emirates (UAE) of being directly involved in military actions against Iran during a BRICS meeting in New Delhi. Araqchi criticized the UAE for not condemning the attacks against Iran and warned that those collaborating with Israel would face consequences. He stated that the UAE's alliances with the U.S. and Israel did not guarantee their security.

**[REPORTED]** Iran's deputy foreign minister said Wednesday that disagreements within BRICS over the conflict had prevented the bloc from reaching a unified position. Kazem Gharibabadi told Press Trust of India that "one member country" had pushed for language condemning Iran. "One country is insisting on condemning Iran."

**[VERIFIED]** The UAE has also come under direct fire throughout the conflict, intercepting more than 2,800 Iranian drones and missiles since February 28.

**Assessment:** BRICS has failed to produce a unified statement on the war. Iran's public accusation of the UAE at the meeting represents a dramatic escalation of intra-BRICS tensions and may signal Tehran's willingness to target Gulf states more directly.

#### Oil Drops Sharply
**[VERIFIED]** By 8:30 a.m. Eastern Time today, oil had reached $107.82 per barrel, measured using the Brent benchmark. That's $3.05 less than it cost yesterday morning and about $41.50 above its price a year earlier.

**[REPORTED]** The US Energy Information Administration reported that crude oil and fuel flows through the Strait of Hormuz declined by nearly 6 million barrels per day in the first quarter after the Middle East conflict erupted in late February. The IEA also warned that the global oil market is likely to remain heavily undersupplied until October even if the conflict ends next month.

**[REPORTED]** Reports from Iran indicated that around 30 vessels had crossed the Strait of Hormuz in recent hours, while Tehran has reportedly begun allowing transit for some Chinese ships.

**Assessment:** The sharp $3 drop likely reflects market reaction to the Trump-Xi summit and reports of limited Hormuz transits. However, the IEA warning about undersupply through October suggests structural price pressure remains.

#### Lebanon Casualties Continue
**[VERIFIED]** The Lebanese health ministry said that Lebanese death toll since the start of the war increased to 2,896 including over 200 children.

**[REPORTED]** On May 14, Israeli airstrikes continued across southern Lebanon. Ministry of Health announced that one person was killed and another injured in an Israeli airstrike that targeted a car in Sidon. Multiple airstrikes hit towns including Sadiqin, Kafr, Mansouri, Nabatieh al-Fawqa, and others.

**[REPORTED]** A Hezbollah drone attack seriously wounded two Israeli civilians; Israeli strikes on highway south of Beirut kill 8, including 2 children.

1. **Iran-UAE BRICS Confrontation** — Araghchi's public accusation that the UAE is "directly involved in military actions" represents a significant escalation. This intra-BRICS rupture may have implications beyond the meeting.

2. **China's Strategic Patience** — Intelligence assessments suggest China is using the war to maximize advantage over the US rather than mediate. Tehran's selective Hormuz opening for Chinese vessels reinforces this dynamic.

3. **Leadership Vacuum in Iran** — Bessent's comments about "several layers of leadership" being "decapitated" and difficulty communicating with anyone who can speak for Iranian leadership suggests a more fragmented Tehran than publicly acknowledged.

4. **Hormuz "Selective Opening"** — Reports of ~30 vessels transiting and Chinese ships being allowed through may represent Iran developing a tiered access system rather than full blockade.

### Bottom Line

**Today's developments reveal the limits of diplomacy without pressure.** The Trump-Xi summit produced agreement on principles both sides already held — open Hormuz, no Iranian nuclear weapons — without any Chinese commitment to pressure Tehran. Rubio's admission that Trump "didn't ask for anything" confirms the summit was about trade and Taiwan, not Iran.

**The BRICS confrontation may prove more consequential than the Beijing summit.** Iran's explicit accusation that the UAE is militarily involved in attacks on Iran, combined with threats of consequences for Gulf state collaboration with Israel, could accelerate regional escalation. The UAE's admission of intercepting 2,800+ Iranian projectiles confirms the war has already expanded.

**The ceasefire remains on life support.** With no breakthrough in sight and fundamental gaps persisting in Israel-Lebanon talks, the probability of ceasefire collapse continues to rise. The IEA's warning of undersupply through October — "even if the conflict ends next month" — suggests even optimistic scenarios involve months more disruption.
