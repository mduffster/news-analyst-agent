### **SATURDAY ATTACK: DEADLIEST SINCE WAR BEGAN** [VERIFIED]

Saudi authorities say the latest attack on the international airport in the capital, Riyadh, has killed 12 people and injured over 300. Their statement says the dead on Saturday included four Saudi citizens as well as two Bangladeshi nationals, a Palestinian, a U.S. citizen, an Egyptian, a Jordanian, a Syrian and a Sudanese.

A Saudi-led coalition blamed Iran-backed Houthi rebels in neighboring Yemen. It was the deadliest attack in any Gulf Arab country since the start of the Iran war.

Secretary of State Marco Rubio confirmed a US citizen was among the dead, stating "The United States strongly condemns the recent attacks on the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia by the terrorist Houthis, including today's strike on King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh that took numerous innocent lives, including an American citizen."

### **FIFTH ATTACK: SUNDAY STRIKE KILLS ONE MORE** [VERIFIED]

### **STRAIT OF HORMUZ: TANKER ATTACKS SURGE** [VERIFIED]

Between 21:00 UTC on 8 Oct and 18:00 UTC on 11 Oct, 35 vessels transited Hormuz (20 inbound, 15 outbound): 27 on AIS and 8 dark (23%). Daily transits averaged 11.7, down 18% from 14.3 over the 3–5 Oct weekend. Only 19 vessels of 100 m or more crossed, and just 4 of the 13 transits on 10 Oct were that size.

New attacks: PETRO DOLPHIN (tanker, Nicaragua) hit by a drone off Sohar anchorage on Oct 11 while waiting to berth; crew evacuated, two Indian seafarers missing. VALTHERA (tanker, Marshall Islands) struck by unknown projectile on Oct 10 while outbound; fire on board, all 11 Indian crew safe.

The Strait of Hormuz remains under severe operational pressure, with The Maritime Executive reporting additional tankers struck as the IRGC broadens its threat posture against vessels it considers non-compliant. The Financial Times reports attacks have reached a wartime high and that Iran has extended strikes beyond the strait into the wider Gulf.
