## Critical Developments (July 27-28)

### 1. CRITICAL: Trump-Netanyahu Meeting Underway — "Scaling Down" War Top Agenda [OFFICIAL]

Prime Minister Netanyahu met with President Trump in the Oval Office on July 28, 2026. Joining Trump in the meeting are Vice President JD Vance, Secretary of State Marco Rubio, CIA chief John Ratcliffe and special envoy Steve Witkoff. Their meeting started about an hour ago and is ongoing.

Israel's UN envoy Ambassador Danny Danon said "number one" on the Trump-Netanyahu agenda will be scaling down the Iran war. Netanyahu "definitely wants to hear from President Trump what he wants to see in the coming days or weeks." Danon added: "I cannot tell you which direction it will go. There are different options."

The meeting comes as both face growing pressures at home. Netanyahu is up for reelection and is embattled in part because of his deteriorating relationship with Trump. Trump is under pressure to end an unpopular war that has wreaked economic havoc ahead of midterm elections this November.

The two leaders also plan to discuss the framework agreement the US and Israel signed with Lebanon over the Israel-Hezbollah war and the expansion of the Abraham Accords. "We have a little difference," Trump said Monday when asked if he and Netanyahu were on the same page with Iran. "But pretty close, yeah."

On February 28, Trump and Netanyahu went to war together and it looked like there was no daylight between them. But exactly five months later, their interests have diverged more than ever — and bad blood and frustration have deepened as they've grown suspicious of each other's intentions. Netanyahu's visit comes several days after Trump paused US strikes in Iran and announced his envoys are trying — again — to negotiate a deal with the Iranian regime. Last Friday, Netanyahu found himself holding marathon talks with his cabinet about the war — while largely in the dark about Trump's thinking and plans.

*Assessment: [VERIFIED] This is the first Trump-Netanyahu meeting since Operation Epic Fury began. The explicit "scaling down" agenda framing — combined with both leaders facing domestic political pressure — represents a significant de-escalatory signal. However, divergent interests and mutual suspicion complicate the path forward.*

### 2. CRITICAL: Strike Pause Extends to Day 4 — Both Sides Holding Fire [VERIFIED]

The pause in US-Iran fighting appeared to hold on Tuesday after two weeks of strikes. Trump hailed "good talks" — but Iran denied direct negotiations are taking place.

The combat hiatus began Friday, following nearly two weeks of US strikes against Iran in retaliation for attacks on ships in the Strait of Hormuz that shredded a temporary ceasefire that had already been repeatedly undermined. The cessation came as diplomats sought to give peace talks "some space."

Iran and the United States held their fire Monday, handing Gulf shipping and the oil industry a respite from missile strikes as Donald Trump's UN envoy said the president was "giving talks some space."

President Donald Trump said the US halted strikes on Iran at the request of the Iranian regime and warned the US would resume attacks if a new ceasefire deal weren't reached.

*Assessment: [VERIFIED] The strike pause has now extended to four consecutive days — the longest de-escalation since the ceasefire collapsed on July 7. Both sides maintain "attack for attack" posturing, but the active choice to hold fire while diplomacy proceeds represents meaningful restraint.*

### 3. CRITICAL: Oman Proposes "Middle Passage" with "Voluntary Fees" [REPORTED]

Oman presented a proposal to Iran for joint management of the Strait of Hormuz with "voluntary fees" that would ensure Tehran does not exercise sole control of the vital waterway. Under the Omani proposal, which reportedly has regional backing, Tehran won't have sole control of the vital waterway.

Negotiators from Iran and Oman are trying to reach an agreement to restart shipping through the Strait of Hormuz. Iran and Oman are continuing discussions after their officials met in Tehran over the weekend. A successful deal would then enable the Islamic Republic and the US to resume talks over ending their war. One proposal is to reopen the strait's so-called middle passage.

That middle passage has largely been avoided by ships since the start of the conflict in late February, with vessels taking either a northern route close to Iran's shores or a southern one that passes near the Omani exclave of Musandam.

Discussions over the future of the Strait of Hormuz continued on Tuesday as Iran's Foreign Ministry said it had held talks on the strategically vital maritime passage with Saudi and Omani counterparts. Iranian Foreign Minister Seyyed Abbas Araqchi held separate phone calls with Saudi Arabia's Faisal bin Farhan and Oman's Badr Al-Busaidi regarding bilateral and regional developments.

*Assessment: [REPORTED] The Omani "middle passage" proposal with "voluntary fees" is the most concrete Hormuz compromise framework to emerge. This would give Iran some face-saving role without granting sole control. The key unresolved issue remains Iran's earlier insistence on mandatory (not voluntary) payments. Saudi Arabia's direct involvement in these discussions is a significant new development.*

### 4. CRITICAL: Oil Collapses ~15% from Peak — War Premium Evaporating [VERIFIED]

Brent fell to 86.58 USD/Bbl on July 28, 2026, down 1.54% from the previous day. Over the past month, Brent's price has risen 17.14%, and is up 20.79% compared to the same time last year.

Global oil prices fell sharply Monday after a pause in fighting between the United States and Iran, posting their biggest decline since April 8. Brent futures closed at $85.87 per barrel, down 11.3%. US crude dropped about 7% to $82.61 a barrel.

Brent and WTI crude oil prices are sitting well below the highs touched only a few sessions ago, after one of the sharpest reversals crude has produced all year. Both benchmarks have fallen sharply from their late-July highs, with Brent approaching $100 and WTI reaching above $92, after signs of an easing Middle East conflict took the geopolitical premium out of the market almost as quickly as it arrived.

As of 7:15 a.m. Eastern Time on July 28, oil is trading at $89.08 per barrel, based on the Brent benchmark. That's $1.35 below yesterday morning's level and about $18.50 higher than where it stood a year ago.

*Assessment: [VERIFIED] Brent has collapsed from ~$100 on July 23 to ~$86-89 today — approximately a 13-15% drop in five days. The war premium is rapidly evaporating as markets price in significant de-escalation probability. However, Brent remains ~20% above pre-war levels (~$72), and any resumption of strikes would likely push prices back toward $100.*

### 5. SIGNIFICANT: Hormuz Remains Closed — 450 Vessels Anchored [VERIFIED]

Ship traffic through the Strait of Hormuz remains well below prewar levels. Fewer than 10 commodity ships passed through the waterway as of Monday morning, according to shipping data tracked by CNN.

After an almost daily back and forth over whether the strait is open or closed, blockaded or not, vessel traffic is nowhere near the hundred-plus ships a day that were traversing the strait before the war.

The Strait of Hormuz is effectively closed to commercial shipping. Live status, updated every 5 minutes.

AIS tracking shows 450 vessels anchored or stopped as of 11:52 UTC, and port arrivals across the Hormuz-area ports reached 671 in the most recent 24 hours.

Iran launched strikes on ships and demanded tolls from vessels transiting the crucial waterway in the wake of the US-Israeli attack on Feb. 28. This effectively shuttered a trade route through which some 20% of the world's oil and natural gas once passed.

*Assessment: [VERIFIED] Despite the four-day strike pause and active diplomacy, Hormuz remains functionally closed with fewer than 10 vessels transiting per day versus ~100+ pre-war. The 450 anchored vessels represent continued massive disruption to global trade. Reopening the strait remains the key unresolved issue.*

### 6. SIGNIFICANT: Saudi-Houthi Front Continues — Yemen Government Warns of Escalation [VERIFIED]

Yemen's government issued a warning to the Houthi rebels after they stepped up recent attacks on Saudi oil infrastructure and imposed a maritime blockade, raising fears the Iran-backed group's campaign could open a new front in the wider regional conflict and threaten shipping through the Red Sea. Yemen's internationally recognised government said it is ready for any escalation with Houthi rebels. Fears are growing that renewed fighting between Saudi Arabia and the Houthi rebels in Yemen may further destabilise the Arabian peninsula.

The Houthis declared a maritime embargo against Saudi Arabia on Monday, threatening to cut off the kingdom's oil exports through the Red Sea and the Bab el-Mandeb Strait. The Iran-backed Houthis claimed to have attacked two Saudi oil tankers in the Red Sea earlier in the week.

The Houthis have said they've imposed a blockade on Saudi Arabia. They've actually been clear they are not closing the entire strait at this point. Their goals are to put a lot of pressure on Saudi Arabia. They feel like this is a moment where they can extract concessions.

*Assessment: [VERIFIED] The Saudi-Houthi front remains active despite the US-Iran pause. The Houthi blockade of Saudi shipping and attacks on Aramco facilities represent a secondary conflict that could escalate independently. However, the Houthis have been clear they are targeting Saudi Arabia specifically, not closing the entire Bab el-Mandeb strait.*

### 7. SIGNIFICANT: Israeli-Lebanese Talks Continue in Parallel [VERIFIED]

Israeli and Lebanese officials will meet again in Rome next week to continue work on implementing a US-brokered agreement ultimately aimed at a comprehensive peace between the two countries. A US State Department official said the US-led talks will take place in Rome on August 4-6. The latest round follows the launch of the initial "pilot zones" in southern Lebanon, where the Israeli military said it handed over control to the Lebanese Armed Forces last week.

*Assessment: [VERIFIED] The Israel-Lebanon track continues separately from the Iran situation, representing potential for regional stabilization. The August 4-6 Rome talks could produce additional positive momentum if the US-Iran pause holds.*
