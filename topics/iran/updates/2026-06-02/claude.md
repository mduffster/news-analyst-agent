### Executive Summary

**MAJOR DEVELOPMENT: Diplomatic channels remain open despite confusion; IRGC commander declares war "inevitable"**

Today brings a mix of clarifying signals and concerning new threats. Negotiations appear to be continuing through intermediaries despite yesterday's dramatic Iranian suspension announcement, but a senior IRGC commander publicly stated that war resumption is "inevitable" absent Iranian surrender. Israel-Lebanon talks resumed at the State Department amid ongoing clashes.

### Key Developments Since Yesterday

#### 1. Negotiations Status: Clarification Emerges

**[OFFICIAL]** President Donald Trump pushed back on reports that talks had stopped, saying in a post on Truth Social that "conversations between us have been going on continuously," including over the past several days and "today." "Where they lead, one never knows," Trump wrote, adding that he told Iran, "It's time, one way or another, for you to make a Deal."

**[OFFICIAL]** The comments amounted to a striking shift in tone from the administration's position a day earlier, when Trump told CNBC he did not care if Iran talks were over.

**[OFFICIAL]** Secretary of State Marco Rubio said that although the new Supreme Leader of Iran Ayatollah Mojtaba Khamenei has not been seen or heard from publicly, "there are indications out there that he is increasingly engaging at some level" in negotiations with the U.S. Rubio added that all of his communications have been through writing and intermediaries.

**[OFFICIAL]** Rubio indicated that Tehran had "agreed to negotiate aspects of their nuclear program," but did not provide details on which aspects and noted any negotiations are "not a guarantee that ultimately it will lead to a deal that's acceptable to the Senate or acceptable to the American people."

**Assessment:** The picture has clarified somewhat — mediated contact appears to continue despite yesterday's Tasnim announcement. Rubio's testimony suggests Iran's new supreme leader is engaged, but the IRGC's parallel messaging (below) creates deliberate ambiguity about Iran's true position.

---

#### 2. IRGC Commander: "War is Inevitable"

**[OFFICIAL]** A senior Iranian military officer said Tuesday that a resumption of hostilities with the United States was inevitable, as negotiations between Tehran and Washington appeared to stall. "The United States demands our total surrender, and the Iranian nation will never surrender," said Mohammad Jafar Assadi, deputy head of Iran's central military command, Khatam al-Anbiya. "Without surrender, war is inevitable."

**Assessment:** This represents an escalatory public statement from within Iran's military establishment. It may reflect: (a) IRGC hardliners' genuine position, (b) negotiating posture to lower US expectations, or (c) internal regime disagreement between military and diplomatic factions. The contrast between this statement and Rubio's claims of engagement underscores the opacity of Iran's internal decision-making.

---

#### 3. Rubio's Congressional Testimony: Conditions Outlined

**[OFFICIAL]** Negotiations on Iran's nuclear program would be "highly technical" and could take months, US Secretary of State Marco Rubio said Tuesday. The top US diplomat said such a phase would be predicated on Iran reopening the Strait of Hormuz. "Phase 2 is they have to commit to very specific negotiations on … the disposition of the highly enriched uranium that still is buried deep in a mountain somewhere," Rubio told the Senate Foreign Relations Committee. "They have to agree on negotiating severe and long-term limitations, and/or cancellation of enrichment activity in their country."

**[OFFICIAL]** Rubio said Iranian negotiators would often need "three to five days to get a response" from Iranian leadership. "They're operating--probably using couriers and things of this nature. Part of it is internal fissures on exactly what they should agree to or not agree to."

**Assessment:** Rubio's testimony provides the clearest public articulation of the administration's sequencing: Hormuz first, then nuclear commitments, with detailed negotiations potentially extending months. This timeline is incompatible with "deal in a week" rhetoric.

---

#### 4. Israel-Lebanon State Department Talks Resume

**[VERIFIED]** High-stakes talks between Israel and Lebanon are taking place at the US State Department after Iran-backed militant group Hezbollah and the Israeli military continued to trade strikes overnight. Lebanese authorities said Hezbollah agreed to a US proposal for a ceasefire with Israel in which strikes on Beirut would stop.

**[VERIFIED]** The United States is planning for its military to train the Lebanese army to confront Hezbollah and ultimately disarm the terror group, the Kan public broadcaster reports, citing sources familiar with the matter. According to the report, the initiative has Israeli backing and was discussed during talks between Israeli and Lebanese delegations in Washington aimed at reaching a lasting ceasefire agreement and advancing Hezbollah's disarmament. Officials briefed on the meeting reportedly say the discussions are taking place in a positive atmosphere and have yielded some areas of agreement.

**[VERIFIED]** Israel and Hezbollah exchanged fire on Tuesday, just hours after US President Donald Trump said the two sides had agreed to halt hostilities. On Tuesday, the warring parties appeared to contradict Trump's announcement, with Israel conducting strikes on southern Lebanon and Hezbollah firing into northern Israel.

**Assessment:** Talks are proceeding at the diplomatic level, but ground reality contradicts ceasefire claims. The reported US plan to train the Lebanese Army against Hezbollah represents an ambitious but uncertain path.

---

#### 5. Hezbollah Demands "Comprehensive" Ceasefire

**[REPORTED]** Hezbollah has refused any partial ceasefire proposal and is demanding a comprehensive halt to hostilities across all Lebanese territory. Hassan Fadlallah, a member of Hezbollah's Loyalty to the Resistance bloc in parliament, told Al Mayadeen channel that the party's position is that any ceasefire must be total and cover all of Lebanon. Fadlallah revealed that the movement has rejected a US ceasefire proposal that tried to create a security arrangement demanding Hezbollah refrain from attacking northern Israel in exchange for Israel committing not to attack Beirut's southern suburbs.

**[REPORTED]** The first proposal outlined that Israel would not strike Beirut's Dahieh district, a Hezbollah stronghold, in exchange for the group not attacking Israel, Al Manar reported. After Hezbollah rejected that offer and threatened to keep attacking Israel, the United States proposed that, if the militant group did not attack the country, Israel would not attack Dahieh for multiple days. The station suggested that the second proposal calls for a "full inclusive ceasefire" to take effect after 48 to 72 hours.

**Assessment:** The gap between a limited Beirut-for-northern-Israel arrangement and Hezbollah's demand for "comprehensive" coverage of all Lebanon remains significant. This complicates Trump's ability to deliver Lebanon calm as a condition for Iran talks.

---

#### 6. Bab al-Mandeb Threat Escalates

**[OFFICIAL]** The commander of the Islamic Revolution Guards Corps Quds Force, Brigadier General Esmail Qa'ani, has warned that the Bab al-Mandab Strait will be subjected to the same traffic restrictions as the Strait of Hormuz if the Zionist regime continues its crimes in Lebanon and Gaza under American protection.

**[VERIFIED]** Located at the southern entrance of the Red Sea between Yemen and the Horn of Africa, the Bab al-Mandeb Strait is one of the world's most strategically important waterways. At its narrowest point, it measures just 29 kilometres across, yet nearly 15% of global maritime trade passes through it each year.

**Assessment:** This is a direct threat from IRGC Quds Force command — not just Iranian media. It signals Iran's willingness to extend economic warfare beyond Hormuz if Lebanon operations continue.

### Emerging Signals

**Internal Iranian divisions becoming visible:** The contrast between diplomatic engagement (Rubio's testimony) and IRGC hardline messaging (Assadi's "war inevitable") suggests genuine factional disagreement, not just negotiating posture. The new Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei appears to be managing both tracks — allowing talks while preserving military options.

**Lebanon as the pivotal variable:** Iran, the US, and Israel all acknowledge that Lebanon is the key obstacle. But the US cannot compel Netanyahu's compliance, Israel will not accept Hezbollah demands, and Hezbollah rejects partial arrangements. This triangle has no obvious resolution.

**Timeline disconnect:** Trump's "deal in a week" rhetoric vs. Rubio's "months of highly technical negotiations" vs. IRGC's "war is inevitable" represents three incompatible expectations operating simultaneously.
