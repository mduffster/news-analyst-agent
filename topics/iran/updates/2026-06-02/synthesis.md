## Critical Developments (June 2)

### 1. US-Iran Negotiations Status: Clarifying After Confusion

**[OFFICIAL]** President Donald Trump denied reports that the U.S. and Iran are no longer engaged in peace talks on Tuesday. Trump, writing on his Truth Social account, saying conversations with Iran continued through the weekend and into this week. "Fake News Reports that the Islamic Republic of Iran, and the U.S.A., stopped speaking a few days ago are false and erroneous. The conversations between us have been going on continuously, including four days ago, three days ago, two days ago, one day ago, and today."

**[OFFICIAL]** Secretary of State Marco Rubio said that although the new Supreme Leader of Iran Ayatollah Mojtaba Khamenei has not been seen or heard from publicly, "there are indications out there that he is increasingly engaging at some level" in negotiations with the U.S. Rubio added that all of his communications have been through writing and intermediaries. Rubio said Iranian negotiators would often need "three to five days to get a response" from Iranian leadership.

**[OFFICIAL]** "They have to commit to very specific negotiations on the disposition of the highly enriched uranium that still is buried deep in a mountain somewhere," Rubio told the Senate Foreign Relations Committee. "They have to agree on negotiating severe and long-term limitations and/or cancelation of enrichment activity in their country."

**[REPORTED]** The semi-official Mehr news agency reported that Iran's final draft proposal for an interim ceasefire agreement with the US is "still under discussion," indicating that talks are back on track.

**[REPORTED]** A regional source told CNN negotiations were back on track, hours after Iran suspended talks over Israel's offensive in Lebanon.

**Assessment:** The picture has substantially clarified since yesterday's confusion. Mediated contact appears to continue, Iran's new supreme leader is reportedly engaging through intermediaries, and both sides have walked back from the brink. However, Rubio's testimony establishes that nuclear negotiations would be "highly technical and could take months"—incompatible with Trump's "deal in a week" rhetoric.

### 2. IRGC Commander: "War is Inevitable"

**[OFFICIAL]** A senior Iranian military officer stated Tuesday that a resumption of hostilities with the United States was inevitable, as negotiations between Tehran and Washington appeared to stall. "The United States demands our total surrender, and the Iranian nation will never surrender. Without surrender, war is inevitable," said Mohammad Jafar Assadi, deputy head of Iran's central military command, Khatam al-Anbiya.

**Assessment:** This represents a significant hardline signal from within Iran's military establishment. The contrast between this statement and Rubio's claims of engagement underscores internal Iranian divisions—the new Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei appears to be managing both diplomatic and military tracks simultaneously.

### 3. Trump-Netanyahu Explosive Confrontation

**[REPORTED]** On the call, Trump called Netanyahu "crazy" and accused him of ingratitude, according to two of the sources. He also put the brakes on Israel's plan to strike Beirut. Driving the news: The U.S. official said Trump knew Hezbollah had been shooting at Israel and that Israel needed to defend itself, but felt in recent days that Netanyahu was escalating in a disproportionate way.

**[REPORTED]** Trump's anger appeared to be driven by the fact that Netanyahu's decision to escalate in Lebanon was threatening to implode his negotiations with Iran. After the call, Trump posted on Truth Social that the Iran talks were "continuing, at a rapid pace."

**[REPORTED]** The second U.S. official claimed that, in reality, Trump had "steamrolled" Netanyahu on the call. "Bibi said, 'OK, OK, just make sure everything is taken care of,'" according to the official.

**[OFFICIAL]** Israel no longer plans to strike Hezbollah targets in Beirut, an Israeli official told Axios.

**Assessment:** This is one of the most consequential US-Israel confrontations since the war began. Trump's intervention successfully halted the Beirut strikes that were threatening to collapse Iran negotiations. However, the underlying tension remains: Netanyahu faces domestic pressure to escalate while Trump needs Israeli restraint for his Iran deal.

### 4. Israel-Lebanon Talks Resume at State Department

**[VERIFIED]** A fourth round of talks will take place in Washington on 2 and 3 June, 2026.

**[VERIFIED]** On May 29, there was a military coordination meeting between Lebanese, Israeli and American military delegations at the Pentagon. The topics discussed were the Israeli army withdrawal, the Lebanese army takeover of Southern Lebanon, the financial support for the Lebanese army, the disarmament of Hezbollah, and the enforcement mechanism for the ceasefire.

**[REPORTED]** The United States is planning for its military to train the Lebanese army to confront Hezbollah and ultimately disarm the terror group, according to Israeli sources. The initiative reportedly has Israeli backing.

**[REPORTED]** "We will not accept a partial ceasefire," Hezbollah official Mahmoud Qomati told AFP in a written statement, adding that "the Zionist enemy should know that any aggression against the suburbs could lead to a deeper and stronger response" from the group.

### 5. Ground Fighting Continues Despite Ceasefire Claims

**[VERIFIED]** At least 3,468 people have been killed in Israeli attacks since March 2, the Lebanese Health Ministry said today, an increase of 35 people killed since yesterday's toll was released. Hours after Trump declared yesterday that Israeli forces would not move into Beirut, Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said Israel's military would keep striking southern Lebanon "as planned."

**[VERIFIED]** The IDF says troops of the Givati Brigade captured hundreds of weapons and directed strikes that killed some 20 Hezbollah operatives during operations north of the Litani River in southern Lebanon in recent days. The brigade has been operating in the towns of Zawtar al-Sharqiyah and Zawtar al-Gharbiya, west of the Beaufort Castle.

**[VERIFIED]** Trump said "all shooting will stop" between Israel and Hezbollah. But Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said later that his country's military would "continue to operate as planned in southern Lebanon," and there were clashes overnight between Israel and Hezbollah.

**Assessment:** The disconnect between diplomatic claims and ground reality remains stark. Trump's ceasefire announcement is contradicted by continued Israeli operations in southern Lebanon and Hezbollah's ongoing resistance. The Beirut strikes were halted, but operations elsewhere continue.

### 6. Strait of Hormuz: 94 Days of Near-Total Closure

**[VERIFIED]** Despite President Donald Trump's claims that the Strait of Hormuz will reopen soon, shipping executives remain unwilling to risk the vital waterway. Only a handful of vessels are passing through the strait daily compared to the normal 100 cargo ships that typically transit. The closure has cut off 20% of the world's oil supply for 94 days.

**[VERIFIED]** "Traffic still remains exceptionally depleted," Matt Smith, director of commodity research at Kpler, told CNN. "Barring a handful of tankers crossing each day, the strait remains essentially closed."

**[VERIFIED]** Short-term container shipping freight rates have jumped all over the globe due to the Strait of Hormuz blockade. "Compared to pre-conflict, average spot rates in June are expected to be 75 per cent more from China to the US East Coast, 51 per cent higher to North Europe, 45 per cent to the Mediterranean."

**[VERIFIED]** Ongoing US-Iran tensions have kept commercial traffic through the Strait of Hormuz at roughly 4-7% of pre-crisis levels as of early June, with over 1,500 vessels stranded amid a US naval blockade, reported mines, and sharply elevated insurance premiums. These conditions underpin the market-implied 78.5% probability against normalization by month-end.

**Assessment:** Despite both sides' stated desire to reopen the strait, practical conditions have not changed. Mine-clearing would take six months even after a deal is signed. Insurance remains at 8× pre-crisis levels. Commercial shipping will not resume at scale until a verified agreement is in place.

## Structural Analysis: The Trump-Netanyahu Breaking Point

**Today's most significant development is the revelation of explosive US-Israel tension over Lebanon.**

Three key implications:

1. **Trump is willing to override Netanyahu:** Trump "steamrolled" Netanyahu on the call, demonstrating he will use personal pressure to protect his Iran negotiating track. This is a significant shift from previous deference.

2. **Iran has effective leverage through Lebanon:** By conditioning talks on Israeli restraint, Tehran forced Trump to confront Netanyahu directly. This validates Iran's strategy of linking fronts.

3. **Netanyahu faces a political dilemma:** Trump forced Netanyahu into a difficult political dilemma at a critical moment: much of the Israeli public is demanding escalation in Lebanon exactly when Trump won't allow it.

**The US-Iran diplomatic track survived because Trump prioritized it over Israeli freedom of action in Lebanon.** This creates an implicit framework where continued negotiations depend on Israeli restraint—something Trump can enforce but which generates friction with a key ally.
