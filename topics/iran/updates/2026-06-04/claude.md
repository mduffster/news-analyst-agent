### Executive Summary

**KEY DEVELOPMENTS: Hezbollah formally rejects Israel-Lebanon ceasefire; UNIFIL peacekeeper killed; House passes Iran war powers resolution; Iran says "no progress" in talks**

The Washington-brokered Israel-Lebanon ceasefire agreement announced on June 3 is already in jeopardy. A Hezbollah official told NPR that Hezbollah officially informed the Lebanese president that it would not accept any ceasefire that did not begin with the withdrawal of Israeli forces from south Lebanon. Israeli Defense Minister Israel Katz said Israel was demanding the creation of what it called a de-militarized zone within Lebanon while being able to continue attacks against Iran-backed Hezbollah. He said Israel would not be withdrawing from the south.

Meanwhile, Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said Wednesday that "no tangible progress" has been made in negotiations to end the Middle East war. This directly contradicts Trump's claim that Iran was "pretty close" to signing a deal, suggesting it could happen over the weekend.

### Key Developments Since Yesterday

#### 1. House Passes Iran War Powers Resolution — First Successful Vote This Year

**[VERIFIED]** The war powers resolution passed by a vote of 215 to 208, with four Republicans joining Democrats in support. A bipartisan majority in the Republican-led House voted on Wednesday to end the war with Iran, the clearest rebuke yet of President Trump's handling of the conflict and the subsequent economic fallout.

**[VERIFIED]** Lawmakers voted 215-208, with four Republicans—Reps. Thomas Massie (R, Ky.), Brian Fitzpatrick (R, Pa.), Tom Barrett (R, Mich.), and Warren Davidson (R, Ohio)—breaking ranks to approve the resolution, which is a largely symbolic rebuke directing the President to end hostilities with Iran.

**[OFFICIAL]** Trump called the resolution a "meaningless vote" and the four Republicans who crossed party lines "grandstanders" — saying their actions were "unpatriotic." He said they "should be ashamed of themselves."

**[VERIFIED]** Massie said after the vote, "People are tired of this. They're tired of $5 gallon gas and $6 gallon diesel, and fertilizer we can't afford to put on our fields in Kentucky."

**Assessment:** This is the first successful House vote on an Iran war powers resolution since the conflict began. While largely symbolic (the Senate has not passed and Trump would likely veto), it represents growing bipartisan frustration with the war's economic impacts and lack of clear end.

---

#### 2. Hezbollah Formally Rejects Israel-Lebanon Ceasefire

**[REPORTED]** Lebanon and Israel provisionally agreed in Washington to a new ceasefire Wednesday. But hours later Israel continued attacks and the militant group Hezbollah said it rejected any ceasefire that did not start with the withdrawal of Israeli forces from Lebanese territory. The fighting appeared to jettison immediate prospects of a wider ceasefire between the United States and Iran.

**[OFFICIAL]** The United States convened the fourth high-level trilateral meeting between Israeli and Lebanese representatives on June 2 and 3, 2026. As a result of the U.S.-led negotiations, Israel and Lebanon agreed to the implementation of a ceasefire. The ceasefire is contingent on a complete cessation of Hizbollah fire and the evacuation of all Hizbollah operatives from the South Litani Sector.

**[OFFICIAL]** Lebanese President Joseph Aoun said Thursday that the ceasefire would come into force within 24 hours of all concerned parties approving it, especially Hezbollah.

**[OFFICIAL]** Iran has said it will not agree to a ceasefire with the U.S. and Israel unless there is one in Lebanon.

**Assessment:** The Lebanon track is critical to any broader US-Iran deal. With Hezbollah rejecting the agreement and Israel stating it will continue operations and maintain occupation, the ceasefire framework is already hollow. This directly complicates Trump's stated goal of a deal "this weekend."

#### 3. UNIFIL Peacekeeper Killed — Seventh Since March

**[VERIFIED]** A United Nations peacekeeper died on Thursday from wounds sustained the night before when shelling hit his base near Marjayoun in southeastern Lebanon.

**[VERIFIED]** Serbia's defence ministry confirmed that the peacekeeper was Serbian, specifying that he died from injuries caused by a missile strike on the UN base. "Senior Sergeant Milovan Jovanovic was given emergency medical care at a hospital inside the base after being wounded and then transported by helicopter to the University Medical Center in Beirut, where he died."

**[REPORTED]** UNIFIL, the U.N. peacekeeping operation for Lebanon, announced Thursday that one of its peacekeepers had been killed and others wounded when mortars hit their position near Marjayoun in southeastern Lebanon. A U.N. source said the mortars appeared to have come from Hezbollah.

**[OFFICIAL]** UNIFIL has detected an increasingly high number of trajectories and impacts in South Lebanon. The violence must end.

**[VERIFIED]** A total of seven UN peacekeepers have been killed in Lebanon since hostilities began on 2 March.

**Assessment:** The UNIFIL casualty underscores how hollow the ceasefire announcement is. Fighting continues in the immediate aftermath of Washington's diplomatic statement.

#### 4. Iran: "No Tangible Progress" in Talks

**[OFFICIAL]** Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said Wednesday that "no tangible progress" has been made in negotiations to end the Middle East war. Araghchi said lines of communication with the U.S. were still open but warned that any attack by Israel on the Lebanese capital Beirut as part of its campaign against Hezbollah would trigger a "full-scale resumption" of the U.S.-Iran conflict.

**[OFFICIAL]** Araghchi told Al-Mayadeen TV: "There is currently no formal process of negotiations between Iran and the United States, but messages are still being exchanged."

**[OFFICIAL]** Araghchi said both sides were reviewing exchanged texts and working on a final formula, stressing that any return to negotiations would be based on securing the rights of the Iranian people and ending the war against Iran, Lebanon and the region.

**vs. Trump's claims:**

**[OFFICIAL]** Trump suggested that a ceasefire between the US and Iran remains in place despite recent strikes in the region. He painted a rosy picture of negotiations to end the war and suggested that a potential advancement could happen as soon as this weekend. "The negotiation itself has gone very well — actually, very well — even if it happens, and it might not happen, but if it happens, it could happen like over the weekend."

**Assessment:** The gap between US and Iranian characterizations of the talks has never been wider. Iran explicitly says "no progress" while Trump claims a deal could come "this weekend." One or both sides is misrepresenting the state of negotiations.

#### 5. Israel Continues Operations Despite Ceasefire Announcement

**[OFFICIAL]** Israeli Defense Minister Israel Katz said on Thursday that Israeli attacks on Hezbollah will continue in southern Lebanon and that displaced Lebanese residents will not be allowed to return, despite Wednesday's agreement to implement a ceasefire in Lebanon. Katz said that the Israel Defense Forces maintains "freedom of action," including in Beirut.

**[OFFICIAL]** Israeli Defense minister, Israel Katz, said Israel's military would continue its operations as well as its continued presence in some zones of Lebanon without the return of the population.

**[OFFICIAL]** Israeli defense minister Israel Katz said Israel was demanding the creation of what it called a de-militarized zone within Lebanon while being able to continue attacks against Iran-backed Hezbollah.

**Assessment:** Israel is treating the "ceasefire" as permission to continue operations under the guise of self-defense. This hardline position makes Hezbollah acceptance impossible.
