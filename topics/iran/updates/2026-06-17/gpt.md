### 1) The deal text is starting to leak — and it materially raises the “implementation friction” stakes
- **Leaked copies of an interim U.S.–Iran agreement** are now being described in detail: **Iran would take steps to reopen the Strait of Hormuz after signing**, be **allowed to sell oil without restrictions**, and the document **gestures at major future financial support/sanctions changes contingent on a final nuclear deal**. **[REPORTED]** ([apnews.com](https://apnews.com/article/19652f4611b704c0a991bf1f5bc9a4b9?utm_source=openai))  
- Separately, more reporting is quantifying **how much Iran could gain** (oil sales, access to frozen funds, and longer-run “rebuilding” concepts) if implementation proceeds and a final deal is reached. **[REPORTED]** ([axios.com](https://www.axios.com/2026/06/16/trump-iran-deal-billions-frozen-funds?utm_source=openai))

**What changed / what it means:**  
This shifts the main vulnerability from “is there a deal?” to **“can leaders operationalize a high-dollar, high-politics bargain fast enough to prevent spoilers?”** The larger/clearer the economic upside, the stronger the incentives to hold fire—but also the stronger the domestic/political backlash risk (U.S., Israel, Gulf), which can translate into **delays, carve-outs, and compliance fights** during the 60‑day window.

### 2) Geneva (June 19) still matters, but the U.S. and Iran are now discussing moving up the signing / releasing text
- Axios reports **talks about accelerating the signing timeline and potentially releasing the agreement’s text**, while still keeping the **Friday, June 19, 2026** Switzerland meeting between delegations on track. **[REPORTED]** ([axios.com](https://www.axios.com/2026/06/17/iran-deal-signing-text-release?utm_source=openai))  

**What changed / what it means:**  
This is a notable tell that **process risk is being actively managed** (reduce the “two-day spoiler window” before June 19). If they move up signing or clarify terms publicly, it could reduce uncertainty for shipping/insurers. But it also increases the chance that **any mismatch between Iran’s and the White House’s public interpretations** becomes an immediate political crisis rather than a “quiet” implementation dispute.

### 3) G7 alignment: support for the deal, plus explicit concern about Lebanon and energy-route diversification
- G7 leaders are reported backing Trump’s plan while emphasizing **ceasefire dynamics (including Lebanon)** and **diversifying energy supply routes** to reduce exposure to Hormuz disruptions. **[REPORTED]** ([apnews.com](https://apnews.com/article/2b13227bfc63d5c7c92c64488e3e2753?utm_source=openai))  

**What changed / what it means:**  
This adds diplomatic ballast to de-escalation, but also codifies a key reality: **even allies treating the deal as real are hedging structurally against Hormuz risk.** That hedge reduces leverage Iran gets from future chokepoint threats, but in the near term it underscores how fragile “normal shipping” remains.

### 4) The nuclear file is being reframed as the real cliff-edge of the 60‑day window
- New AP analysis stresses the interim deal **opens a two-month period focused on Iran’s nuclear program**, but leaves major questions unresolved and time short. **[VERIFIED]** (AP reporting carried by multiple outlets) ([apnews.com](https://apnews.com/article/d8e5c8ada80c35446d4194201d9a7502?utm_source=openai))  

**What changed / what it means:**  
This makes the most likely medium-term failure mode clearer: not an immediate return to open U.S.–Iran hostilities, but **breakdown in nuclear talks** that then re-triggers maritime/proxy escalation as leverage.
