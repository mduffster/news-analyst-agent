## US-Israel-Iran Conflict: Daily Update — June 17, 2026

### Summary

**The 14-point MOU text has been leaked, revealing the full framework of the US-Iran deal including Lebanon as "part of the war on all fronts." Iran has now issued 84 specific violation claims against Israel since Sunday and threatens a "harsh response" if attacks continue. The first Iranian oil tankers crossed the Strait of Hormuz since the blockade began—a major milestone—though commercial shipping remains effectively closed. Trump defended the deal at his G7 closing press conference, saying continued bombing would have caused "economic catastrophe." Oil prices stabilized around $78-80, down nearly 40% from conflict peak. VP Vance says the administration is now pushing for official MOU text release today (Wednesday).**

### Key Developments Since June 16

#### 1. MOU Text Leaked — 14 Points Revealed

**[REPORTED]** On June 16, 2026, Bloomberg published the 14-point draft memorandum of understanding text between the US and Iran. Major Western news organizations, including the Wall Street Journal, Bloomberg, and Axios, confirmed the authenticity of the leaked 14-point agreement.

**[OFFICIAL]** Steven Cheung, the White House director of communications, publicly said "the supposed text" of the memorandum of understanding that was obtained by CNN "does not reflect the language of the actual MOU."

**[REPORTED]** Key provisions in the leaked draft include:
- The Islamic Republic of Iran and the United States, together with their allies in the current war, declare upon the signing of this Memorandum of Understanding an immediate and permanent end to the war on all fronts, including Lebanon.
- The 14-point draft circulated at G7 meetings sees Tehran committing not to build nuclear weapons while Washington pledges $300 billion for Iran's reconstruction.
- The United States undertakes, together with its regional partners, to create a comprehensive plan for the rehabilitation and economic development of Iran, while ensuring financing of at least $300 billion. The implementation mechanism will be formulated within 60 days.
- The memorandum requires the U.S. to lift a naval blockade of Iranian ports within 30 days. Iran has the same amount of time to ensure regular traffic in the strait, pending the removal of mines.

**[OFFICIAL]** The semi-official Iranian news agency Tasnim described leaked versions of the draft as inaccurate.

---

#### 2. Iran Claims 84 Israeli Violations, Threatens "Harsh Response"

**[OFFICIAL]** Iran's army says Israel has violated the ceasefire in southern Lebanon 84 times since the US-Iran deal was announced on Sunday, adding that the aggressor "should expect a harsh response" if the attacks continue.

**[OFFICIAL]** Following Israeli military strikes on southern Lebanon on June 16 that left four people dead, Iran's Khatam al-Anbiya Central Headquarters has threatened retaliation. The Khatam al-Anbiya Headquarters said in a statement that if Israel does not halt its attacks in southern Lebanon, it should expect a "harsh response" from the Islamic republic.

**[OFFICIAL]** Araghchi stated: "Any military attack by Israel against Lebanon from this point forward, as well as any continued occupation of Lebanese territory, will be regarded by us as a violation of the memorandum of understanding." He added: "In our view, the two parties to this memorandum of understanding are the United States and Israel on one side, and Iran and Hezbollah on the other."

---

#### 3. Trump Defends Deal at G7 Closing Press Conference

**[OFFICIAL]** Trump defends the MOU he reached with Iran, arguing that resuming bombing against Iran would have caused "economic catastrophe." "If we didn't do this deal, we could have dropped more bombs for another three weeks, two to four weeks, [but] you would never have the Hormuz Strait open. You would never have success, your market would have, instead of going up at levels that nobody's ever seen before, would go down at levels that nobody ever saw before."

**[OFFICIAL]** Trump knocked hawkish critics who oppose the deal, calling them "the tough guys that would drive the country right down the tubes."

**[OFFICIAL]** A joint G7 statement, notable for the U.S. signing on to it, praised Trump for the agreement with Tehran and also pledged tougher sanctions on Russia.

**[OFFICIAL]** Trump announced that he would send the peace agreement with Iran to the US Congress for review.

---

#### 4. First Iranian Oil Tankers Cross Strait of Hormuz

**[VERIFIED]** The first tankers carrying Iranian crude oil have left the Strait of Hormuz since the US naval blockade began two months ago, a ship-tracking website announced on June 17.

**[REPORTED]** Since the preliminary deal was announced on Sunday, only seven ships have passed through, according to shipping monitor MarineTraffic. Among these were a few tankers carrying Iranian oil which crossed the US blockade line—Iran's "first crude oil exports in two months," TankerTrackers reported.

**[REPORTED]** More than 550 ships remain stranded on either side of the strait, waiting to transit the Gulf waters.

**[VERIFIED]** The Strait remains effectively closed to commercial shipping as of June 17, 2026. 0 ships transited vs ~94/day normal.

---

#### 5. Oil Sanctions Waiver Takes Effect Friday

**[OFFICIAL]** The United States will allow Iran to immediately begin selling oil and fuel under the agreement, a senior U.S. official said. The provision for waiving sanctions on Iranian oil sales will take effect once the agreement is signed on Friday, and also will cover services including banking, transportation, and insurance to facilitate the sales.

**[OFFICIAL]** The provisions will take effect immediately once the MOU is signed, according to a senior US official. The official told Reuters that the arrangement is a "performance-based agreement," under which Iran will receive benefits only if it complies with its commitments, including having "no nuclear weapon," neutralizing enriched uranium, and not disrupting navigation through the Strait of Hormuz.

### Emerging Signals

**MOU explicitly includes Lebanon:** The leaked text phrase "on all fronts, including Lebanon" is significant—it confirms Iran's interpretation that the deal covers Hezbollah. This puts the U.S. in an awkward position vis-à-vis Israel.

**Iran counting violations systematically:** The "84 violations" claim suggests Iran is building a documented case rather than immediately retaliating. This may be leverage for Friday negotiations or groundwork for later action.

**Performance-based structure:** The oil sanctions waiver is conditional on compliance—this gives the U.S. leverage but also means Iran has strong incentive to perform initially.

**Congress review announced:** Trump sending the deal to Congress could create domestic political complications, though unlikely to derail given Republican control.
