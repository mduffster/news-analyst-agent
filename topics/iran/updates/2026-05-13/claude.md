### Critical New Developments (May 12-13)

#### Trump Arrives in Beijing, Downplays Need for Chinese Help
**[VERIFIED]** US President Donald Trump has now arrived in Beijing for two days of high-stakes talks with Chinese leader Xi Jinping. The visit is the first from an American president since Trump's last trip in 2017, and is taking place after years of intensifying rivalry between the world's largest economies and the US war with Iran, a close partner of China's.

**[OFFICIAL]** As he departed the White House, Trump said he expected a "long talk" with Xi about Iran, which sells most of its US-sanctioned oil to China. But he also downplayed disagreements, telling reporters that "I don't think we need any help with Iran" from China and that Xi had been "relatively good" on the topic.

**[REPORTED]** In a sign of Trump's focus on business, Nvidia chief Jensen Huang boarded Air Force One during a stopover in Alaska, with Tesla's Elon Musk also travelling on the presidential plane to China.

**[REPORTED]** Joining Trump in China are his son and daughter-in-law Eric and Lara Trump, Secretary of State Marco Rubio, Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth and several tech CEOs, including Tesla and SpaceX's Elon Musk, Apple's Tim Cook and Nvidia's Jensen Huang.

**Assessment:** Trump's statement that he doesn't need Chinese help on Iran is a notable shift from yesterday's framing of the summit as critical for the war. This may signal either genuine confidence, a negotiating tactic to maintain leverage with Xi, or an attempt to lower expectations. The heavy tech CEO presence suggests trade deals will be prioritized for domestic political wins.

---

#### China Urges Pakistan to "Step Up Mediation"
**[OFFICIAL]** Chinese Foreign Minister Wang Yi spoke Wednesday on the phone with his Pakistani counterpart Foreign Minister Mohammad Ishaq Dar and urged Pakistan to "maintain confidence," and "step up mediation efforts" between the U.S. and Iran. They spoke just before President Trump touched down in Beijing for a summit with President Xi Jinping.

**[REPORTED]** So far China has taken a nominally neutral stance, given its robust ties with both Iran and Sunni-majority Arab states. China will be represented by its Ambassador to India Xu Feihong to fill in for their Foreign Minister Wang Yi who is unlikely to travel with US President Donald Trump visiting Beijing this week.

**Assessment:** Wang Yi's call to Pakistan hours before Trump's arrival suggests Beijing is working behind the scenes to keep the mediation track alive, even as Xi prepares to host Trump. China's absence from the BRICS meeting (represented only by ambassador) limits the parallel diplomatic track's potential.

---

#### Iran Sets Five Preconditions for Resuming Talks
**[REPORTED]** Tehran has set five preconditions for resuming negotiations with Washington to end the 75-day war, according to Iran's semi-official Fars news agency, including the lifting of sanctions and compensation for war-related damages. The report said Iran's government had rejected the most recent U.S. 14-point proposal for a peace deal as a "demand for surrender," calling it an attempt to secure through diplomacy what Washington had failed to achieve on the battlefield.

**Assessment:** Iran's framing of the US proposal as a "demand for surrender" and the imposition of preconditions (sanctions lifting, compensation) signals Tehran is hardening its position rather than softening. This makes a near-term breakthrough unlikely.

---

#### IDF Crosses Litani River in Major Operation
**[VERIFIED]** Israeli troops crossed the Litani River on the outskirts of the southern Lebanon town of Zawtar al-Sharqiyah, around 10 kilometers from the border with Israel, during a recent operation against Hezbollah, the military announces.

**[VERIFIED]** The weeklong raid was led by the Golani Brigade's Reconnaissance Unit and included other forces. The IDF says the troops achieved "operational control" over the area and worked to clear it of Hezbollah infrastructure. The soldiers did not cross the line demarcating the IDF's new security zone in southern Lebanon, referred to by some in the army as the "Yellow Line."

**[VERIFIED]** The week-long operation was one of the boldest and most significant since the start of ground operations in Lebanon, as it effectively shattered the "Litani barrier," viewed as a strategic line in the conflict. The operation took place at one of the deepest points IDF forces have advanced so far.

**[REPORTED]** The town lies north of the village of Kantara, which Division 36 captured nearly a month ago and where the IDF destroyed one of the largest tunnels it had ever encountered. In a separate update, the IDF said that since the start of the ceasefire understandings, the Air Force has struck more than 1,100 Hezbollah targets in southern Lebanon and eliminated more than 350 militants.

**Assessment:** The Litani River crossing is a significant escalation of Israeli ground operations in Lebanon, coming just one day before the May 14-15 peace talks in Washington. The IDF's engineering work to "enable easier crossing" in the future suggests this is not a temporary incursion but preparation for sustained presence north of the river.

#### Lebanese Death Toll Continues Rising
**[VERIFIED]** Over the past day, 14 people have been killed and 56 wounded in Israeli strikes in Lebanon, the country's Health Ministry said. The ministry added that 2,896 people have been killed and 8,824 wounded since the current round of fighting began on March 2.

**[REPORTED]** A fierce Israeli airstrike targeted Deir Zahrani in southern Lebanon. Four consecutive airstrikes on Jebchit were followed by artillery shelling of the town.

**[REPORTED]** Israeli Channel 12: Israeli army forces crossed the Litani River with heavy armored vehicles and troop carriers and took control of the area.

**Assessment:** The death toll has increased from 380+ since the ceasefire to 2,896 total since March 2, with another 14 killed in the past 24 hours. The pace of operations is intensifying rather than de-escalating ahead of tomorrow's talks.

---

#### Iran's Foreign Minister Arrives for BRICS Meeting
**[VERIFIED]** Iran's Foreign Minister Seyed Abbas Araghchi is due in New Delhi on Wednesday for a two-day BRICS foreign ministers' meeting, as the expanded grouping seeks to address escalating West Asian tensions and their impact on global energy security.

**[REPORTED]** The U.S.-Israeli war on Iran is set to cast a shadow over a two-day meeting of the foreign ministers of the BRICS grouping that kicks off in New Delhi on Thursday, testing the bloc's ability to reach a unified position and produce a joint statement. The grouping now includes Egypt, Ethiopia, Indonesia, Iran and the United Arab Emirates.

**[REPORTED]** The latest round could be tense after reports that UAE and Saudi Arabia carried out military strikes on Iran in retaliation for Iranian strikes. Indian Foreign Ministry spokesperson Randhir Jaiswal had said in March that some BRICS members were involved directly in the conflict, due to which it had been "difficult for us to forge a consensus."

**[OFFICIAL]** Iran had urged India, the BRICS chair for 2026, to use the BRICS platform to build a consensus condemning U.S. and Israeli actions in the Gulf conflict.

**Assessment:** The BRICS meeting creates a parallel diplomatic arena where Iran can seek support from Global South nations. However, internal BRICS divisions — particularly between Iran and the UAE, both members — make a unified statement unlikely. Russia's Lavrov attending while China sends only an ambassador limits the meeting's weight.

#### Oil Stabilizes Just Above $110
**[VERIFIED]** At 9 a.m. Eastern Time today, the price of oil sits at $110.87 per barrel, using Brent as the benchmark. That's an increase of 44 cents since yesterday morning and roughly $44 more than at this time last year.

**[VERIFIED]** The oil market will take until 2027 to normalize if the Strait of Hormuz stays blocked beyond mid-June, Saudi Aramco CEO Amin Nasser warned Monday.

**[REPORTED]** "We're in a stalemate, a frozen conflict," Amos Hochstein, who served as a senior energy advisor to former President Joe Biden, told CNBC. "In the meantime, the straits are closed so we're in a no war, no oil, no straits condition."

**Assessment:** Oil prices have stabilized after yesterday's jump, holding just above $110. Aramco's warning that normalization could extend to 2027 if Hormuz remains blocked underscores the long-term economic stakes.
