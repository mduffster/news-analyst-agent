### 2. MAJOR: Houthi Attacks Wound 73 in Saudi Arabia — Energy Facilities Halted [VERIFIED]

Operations at some energy facilities in Saudi Arabia, the world's top oil exporter, were halted on Tuesday following attacks by Yemen's Iran-aligned Houthis that wounded more than 70 people, Saudi authorities said. Fires erupted at the sites, and several people were injured as firefighting and other emergency teams rushed to the scenes to contain the blazes and assess the damage, the Saudi energy ministry said.

Yahya Saree, the rebels' military spokesman, said they fired dozens of ballistic missiles and drones at oil and economic facilities, as well as an air base in southern Saudi Arabia. He said they attacked Jazan City for Primary and Downstream Industries, a major industrial and logistics hub on Saudi Arabia's southwestern Red Sea coast. The rebels also attacked Aramco facilities in Najran and Abha, as well as King Khalid Air Base in Khamis Mushait.

The attacks early Tuesday hit "civilian and economic facilities" in the Saudi cities of Abha, Jazan, Najran and Khamis Mushait, said Maj. Gen. Turki al-Malki, a spokesperson for the Saudi-led coalition fighting in Yemen. Al-Malki said the attacks wounded 73 people, including women and children. The southern region houses major oil facilities, including a 400,000-barrel-per-day oil refinery in Jazan, which is one of Saudi Arabia's largest.

Yemen's Iran-backed Houthis attacked four cities in the south of U.S. ally Saudi Arabia on Tuesday, wounding more than 70 people and setting oil installations ablaze in what appeared to be a major expansion of the six-month-old Middle East war. Saudi authorities described blazes at the sites and said women and children were among the 73 people hurt. The reports of such large numbers of wounded suggested the attacks were among the biggest carried out against Saudi Arabia since the United States and Israel launched their war against Iran in February.

**Assessment:** [VERIFIED] This represents a significant escalation in the proxy dimension of the conflict. The 73 wounded (including women and children) and operational halts at facilities processing 400,000 bpd mark the most damaging Houthi attack since the war began. This extends the conflict's impact to Saudi territory, complicating US regional diplomacy and creating additional pressure on energy markets.

### 3. MAJOR: US Sanctions Entire Iranian Aviation Sector [OFFICIAL]

The US has placed sanctions on 27 Iranian commercial airlines as President Donald Trump's administration seeks to crank up its economic pressure campaign against Tehran. The airlines were among 36 targets the US named in sanctions on Tuesday over links to Iran's aviation sector, which Tehran uses to move weapons, personnel and illicit cargo.

The Trump administration took sweeping actions against Iran's airline industry on Tuesday as part of its pressure campaign on Tehran. The latest sanctions hit 27 Iranian airlines and nine service providers and sales agents accused of providing services to Mahan Air, which has been sanctioned since 2011. In addition, the US Treasury Department is "effectively today terminating all licenses that related to aviation," the official said, including "payments for overflights of Iranian airspace, authorizations that have allowed non-US airlines that were flying US export-controlled airplanes into Iran." "Essentially, we're taking the entire aviation sector of the Iranian economy out of the market," they said.

A Treasury official briefing reporters on condition of anonymity said "Essentially, we're taking the entire aviation sector of the Iranian economy out of the market." Also targeted were front companies and foreign intermediaries based in Turkey, Malaysia, Kazakhstan and the United Arab Emirates that allegedly helped Iran obtain US-origin aircraft and sensitive technology.

"Let this be a warning to anyone doing business with Iran's remaining airlines, all of which we sanctioned today: You are at risk of being cut off from the global financial system," Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said in a press release.

**Assessment:** [OFFICIAL] The aviation sanctions represent another major escalation in the economic pressure campaign ("Operation Economic Outcast"). By targeting all remaining Iranian airlines plus third-country facilitators, the US is closing additional economic pathways. This reinforces the "prolonged regional entanglement" trajectory — economic warfare tends to be persistent and creates its own escalation dynamics.

### 4. Iran-Oman Hormuz Corridor Said Imminent — Would Route Traffic Through Iranian Waters [OFFICIAL]

Iran will announce a new restricted maritime zone near the Strait of Hormuz and unveil a new shipping corridor agreed with Oman in the coming days, according to Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council. Rezaei also said Tehran and Muscat had reached an agreement on a new shipping corridor through the Strait of Hormuz, with the entry and exit points to be under Iranian control. He did not provide details on when the route would become operational or how it would affect international commercial traffic.

OFAC warned US and non-US parties about dealing with three Iranian bodies involved in managing passage: the Persian Gulf Strait Authority, the Persian Gulf Marine Insurance Company and the Hormuz Safe Marine Services Authority. The strait authority was created in 2026 to run Tehran's permit system for passing ships. It is an Iranian institution, not an international maritime body, and Iran's asserted jurisdiction over international shipping there is contested. OFAC says accepting insurance or other services, or even answering information demands in exchange for guarantees of safe passage, can create sanctions risk.

**Assessment:** [OFFICIAL] The corridor arrangement would give Iran de facto control over shipping through Hormuz by routing vessels through Iranian territorial waters. However, US sanctions on the Persian Gulf Strait Authority and related bodies mean shipping companies using the corridor face secondary sanctions exposure. This creates a sanctions trap — vessels can potentially transit but face financial system isolation for doing so.

### 5. Restricted Zone Still "Coming Days" — Not Yet Implemented [OFFICIAL]

Iran is set to declare a restricted zone near the Strait of Hormuz and announce a new shipping route agreed with Oman "in the coming days", a top Iranian security official has said. Iran's Supreme National Security Council Secretary Mohsen Rezaei said the new restricted zone "will start from the US Navy's blockade line and include areas of the Persian Gulf".

In an interview with state-run IRIB TV, Rezaei said that the zone would extend from "the line where the U.S. naval blockade begins to the Strait of Hormuz region and inside the Persian Gulf on the other side (of the strait)." Ships that enter the zone without coordination with Iran, he added, would face sanctions affecting their insurance coverage and future passage through the waterway. Rezaei framed the move as part of a broader strategic shift in Iran's approach to war and diplomacy, aiming at increasing pressure on Washington.

**Assessment:** [OFFICIAL] The restricted zone announced September 6 has not yet been formally implemented. Iran's timeline of "coming days" means implementation could occur within the next 24-72 hours. Once implemented, this creates additional escalation tripwires — enforcement incidents could rapidly escalate.

## Emerging Signals

**Proxy War Dimension Escalating:** The fighting between Saudi Arabia and the Houthis threatens to squeeze shipping through the Bab al-Mandeb strait, a critical route of the Arabian Peninsula for oil and global trade. The conflict is now affecting both Hormuz and the Red Sea/Bab al-Mandeb chokepoints.

**Sanctions Trap on Hormuz Corridor:** The Iran-Oman corridor creates a potential passage route, but US sanctions on Iranian passage authorities mean shipping companies face secondary sanctions for using it — effectively a lose-lose situation for commercial vessels.

**SPR Depletion Limits US Options:** With the SPR at its lowest level since 1982 and continuing to draw down, the US has less capacity to moderate prices through reserve releases if the crisis extends.
