### 2. MAJOR: Houthi Offensive Wounds 73 in Saudi Arabia — Operations Halted

**[VERIFIED]** Operations at some energy facilities in Saudi Arabia, the world's top oil exporter, were halted on Tuesday following attacks by Yemen's Iran-aligned Houthis that wounded 73 people, in what Saudi authorities described as a dangerous escalation. Fires erupted at the sites and several people were injured as firefighting and other emergency teams rushed to contain the blazes.

**[VERIFIED]** The Houthi military spokesperson announced what he described as a broad offensive against the de facto OPEC leader, targeting Saudi oil giant Aramco facilities in Abha, Najran and Jizan, as well as an air base in Khamis Mushait, with ballistic missiles and drones. On Monday, the Financial Times reported Aramco's Jazan oil facilities were hit in new strikes.

**[REPORTED]** Thick plumes of smoke are visible from the skies over Jazan, Saudi Arabia, resulting from the burning of the Jazan refinery after it was targeted by the Yemeni army.

**[REPORTED]** The Associated Press is reporting that 73 people were wounded, per Saudi authorities. This includes children. Several energy facilities near Yemen halted operations after the strikes.

**[VERIFIED]** Jazan, near the Yemeni border, hosts a 400,000-barrel-per-day refinery and associated energy infrastructure.

*Assessment: This represents a significant escalation by the Houthis, extending the Iran war's proxy dimension to Saudi territory. The casualty count (including children) and operational halts at facilities processing 400,000 bpd mark the most damaging Houthi attack in weeks. This was the catalyst for oil breaking $100.*

### 3. Iran-Oman Hormuz Corridor Said to Be "Days Away"

**[OFFICIAL]** Iran said a deal with Oman to manage shipping through the Strait of Hormuz is imminent, a move that could tighten Tehran's control over the waterway and raise questions over how the U.S. would respond.

**[OFFICIAL]** Rezaei also said the strait would remain closed unless the United States takes practical steps to fulfill its obligations under a peace memorandum of understanding signed between Tehran and Washington in June. In the coming days, he added, a new international corridor designated by Iran and Oman for passage through the strait would be announced, emphasizing that the corridor would be under Iran's management.

**[OFFICIAL]** Iran will announce a new restricted maritime zone near the Strait of Hormuz and unveil a new shipping corridor agreed with Oman in the coming days, according to Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council.

**[UNCONFIRMED]** Inbound VLCCs are forced into Iranian waters under a temporary geometry that Iran can revoke. Corridor width stated at 7 miles with zero published waypoints, buoys, or IMO amendment.

*Assessment: Iran's promised Hormuz corridor with Oman is now described as imminent. The corridor would route tankers through Iranian territorial waters, giving Tehran de facto control over passage. Without proper navigation infrastructure, insurance and charter rates will remain elevated.*

### 4. Restricted Zone Still "Coming Days" — Not Yet Implemented

**[OFFICIAL]** Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of the Supreme National Security Council, announced on the evening of September 6 that "in the coming days, we will announce a new exclusion zone" in the Persian Gulf and the Gulf of Oman. Tehran wants to extend its enforcement mechanism beyond the Strait of Hormuz to include the area between the American blockade line and Iran's oil-loading terminals. According to his description, any vessel traveling on either side of Hormuz toward the strait without coordinating its passage with Iran could be placed on an Iranian sanctions list.

*Assessment: The restricted zone announced September 6 has not yet been formally implemented. Iran's timeline of "coming days" means implementation could occur by end of week.*
