## Key Developments Since Prior Report (September 14)

### 1. NOTABLE: Trump Signals Openness to Iran Negotiations — Iran Rejects

**[OFFICIAL]** A senior Iranian official rejected Monday what he called President Trump's "mixed signals" on openness to talks to end the war between the two nations, insisting "the stakes have changed" and ruling out any dialogue "until Iran's conditions are met." President Trump on Monday signaled for the first time in about a month a willingness to rekindle direct negotiations with Iran, saying he would personally "determine whether or not the U.S.A. will choose to engage — The concept of which we are open to."

**[OFFICIAL]** President Donald Trump stated on Monday, Sept. 14, 2026, that Iran wants to reach a peace agreement "fast and at any cost," and announced that the United States is open to negotiating an end to the ongoing military conflict with the Islamic Republic. "The failing nation of Iran wants to make a deal, fast and at any cost. I will decide if the United States chooses to negotiate, a possibility we are open to," Trump wrote on the social media platform Truth Social.

*Assessment: This marks Trump's first signal toward talks in about a month, but Iran's immediate rejection maintains the diplomatic impasse. The rhetorical positioning — Trump claiming Iran is desperate, Iran insisting on preconditions — does not suggest imminent breakthrough.*

### 2. UPDATE: Saudi-Houthi Fighting Continues — Coalition Promises "Firm" Response

**[VERIFIED]** The Saudi-led coalition, which backs Yemen's internationally recognised government, said early on Tuesday that Houthi attacks on Khamis Mushait, Abha and Taif caused minor to moderate injuries and damaged seven homes and two vehicles. The Saudi-led coalition fighting in Yemen has promised to respond "firmly" to missile and drone attacks by the Iran-aligned Houthi group that wounded 13 civilians across three cities in the kingdom.

**[REPORTED]** The attacks come after the Iran-aligned group said that it fired dozens of missiles and drones at Saudi Arabia's King Khalid airbase in Khamis Mushait, targeting hangars, radar systems, runways and ammunition depots, in retaliation for Saudi air raids across Yemen. The Houthis said in a statement on Monday that Saudi warplanes had carried out 300 air raids across Yemen over the previous five days.

**[VERIFIED]** Al Jazeera's correspondent reported the government forces are trying to recapture territory near the key city of Mocha from the Houthis. Saudi Arabia is carrying out air attacks, mainly against Houthi military advances. "There have been 129 air strikes on the areas that have recently fallen to the Ansar Allah, including areas in the north where the Houthi group is trying to make advances into the city of Marib."

*Assessment: The Saudi-Houthi exchange of strikes continues without decisive shift. Saudi coalition rhetoric ("firmly") signals potential escalation but no concrete new military action beyond ongoing airstrikes. The Houthis have consolidated gains; the Saudi counteroffensive has not retaken significant territory.*

### 3. UPDATE: Trump Administration Maintains No Direct Military Action Against Houthis

**[VERIFIED]** The Trump administration is offering significant intelligence and targeting assistance to Saudi Arabia but does not currently plan on launching direct military attacks against the Houthis, two U.S. officials told ABC News. Both officials cautioned that the administration's thinking could change, particularly if the threat to Red Sea shipping lanes escalates significantly.

**[REPORTED]** Another official said the Saudi government has communicated in recent days a willingness to launch a major military operation against the Houthis, even if it means going it alone, but that Riyadh had not yet made a final decision.

**[OFFICIAL]** Riyadh has turned to the U.S. for help, but, so far, President Trump has not answered a call for American airstrikes. And over the weekend, he said the U.S. has been talking to the Houthis directly. "The Houthis called us, and they don't want to fight with us. They let us know they don't want to fight with us."

*Assessment: Trump continues to signal that the US views the Saudi-Houthi conflict as separate from its war with Iran. The Houthi back-channel communication appears to be working from the US perspective — Houthis are focusing attacks on Saudi targets rather than US interests directly.*

### 4. UPDATE: Lebanon Front Quiet for Fourth Consecutive Day — Iran Still No Retaliation

**[VERIFIED]** The Lebanese front produced no reported fire in either direction for a third consecutive day. No Hezbollah attack on Israeli forces was reported, no Israeli strike in Lebanon was reported, and no Lebanese casualty was reported. This has now extended to a fourth consecutive day.

**[VERIFIED]** Troops operating in Israel's south Lebanon buffer zone located a Hezbollah fighting position, including an armed rocket launcher and a tunnel shaft, the military says. According to the IDF, the launcher, with 32 rockets, had been left behind by Hezbollah before the ceasefire in Lebanon took effect.

**[VERIFIED]** Iran did not fire at a host state for a fifth consecutive day.

*Assessment: The de facto pause on the Lebanon front continues. Iran has now gone nearly two weeks without responding to the Ali al-Taher tunnel destruction despite explicit warnings. Israeli forces continue operations in their "buffer zone" but without triggering Hezbollah response. This quiet is notable and may persist through Israeli elections.*

### 5. CONFIRMED: Strait of Hormuz Vessel Attack on September 12

**[VERIFIED]** UKMTO Warning 134-26: a vessel was struck by an unknown projectile while transiting the Strait of Hormuz near Limah, Oman, heading south. The strike was reported at approximately 2300 UTC on 12 September and sparked a fire, prompting a crew evacuation; no casualties were reported.

**[REPORTED]** Iranian state media has claimed an Iranian commercial vessel was struck in the Strait of Hormuz late Saturday, killing one person, amid an ongoing battle between the US and Iran for control over the crucial energy chokepoint. The Hormuz attack, which the state-run Islamic Republic of Iran Broadcasting (IRIB) said injured four, took place near Qeshm Island in the strait. Iran's official Islamic Republic News Agency (IRNA) quoted the Qeshm governor as blaming a "terrorist enemy" for the attack.

**[VERIFIED]** There have been numerous tit-for-tat attacks on shipping in the Strait of Hormuz in recent weeks. U.S. Central Command, or CENTCOM, said on Wednesday it destroyed 10 Iranian tankers the preceding week. On Saturday, CENTCOM said that its forces have redirected 100 commercial vessels over the past 60 days since it resumed a naval blockade against Iran.

*Assessment: The "tanker war" in Hormuz continues. Conflicting reports (UKMTO reports one vessel near Oman; Iran claims one of its own vessels hit near Qeshm) suggest multiple incidents or different characterizations of the same event. Either way, Hormuz remains a combat zone with regular attacks.*

*Assessment: Oil slightly off yesterday's highs but stable above $107. IEA's updated supply deficit estimate (1.7 million bpd, up from 1.3 million) suggests prices could push higher. The market is pricing in sustained disruption.*
