## Trump Rules Out Nuclear Weapons, Warns of Pickaxe Mountain Strike [OFFICIAL]

President Donald Trump said the US may have to target Pickaxe Mountain, a site linked to Iran's nuclear program, as he aims to ensure Tehran does not get a nuclear weapon. In an interview with Newsmax on Thursday night, Trump also said he would not use a nuclear weapon in the war on Iran.

He ruled out using a nuclear weapon or a tactical nuclear weapon. "I wouldn't use nuclear. We don't have to. We're the greatest nuclear power in the world," he said.

"Their nuclear is buried under millions of tons of mountain. Now we're looking at Pickaxe Mountain. We may have to go there. We'll take a shot at that one. We'll knock it out fast," Trump told the news outlet.

Trump reiterated he was not considering using US ground troops in Iran. He did say the MOAB bomb was not a nuclear bomb.

Trump has ordered the military to develop additional strike packages against Iranian targets that include "a lot of energy infrastructure" and the potential use of a MOAB, one CNN source said. The same source said the phrasing used was that this attack would come "prior to US retrograde in CENTCOM AOR (area of responsibility)," indicating the administration could launch the additional strikes, attempt to declare victory and quickly pull everyone out.

**Analysis:** This is a significant moment: Trump has now publicly named a specific Iranian facility as a potential target while simultaneously taking nuclear weapons off the table. The "strike and withdraw" concept—striking, declaring victory, and exiting—suggests planners are looking for a face-saving off-ramp rather than decisive victory. According to several sources, the MOAB likely would be unable to penetrate deep below the ground, where Iranian nuclear facilities, such as Pickaxe Mountain, are believed to be. This raises questions about whether post-midterm strikes would achieve strategic objectives or serve primarily political purposes.

## CENTCOM Confirms IRGC Projectile Sank GFS Pride [OFFICIAL]

"The United States and regional partners control the strait as commercial vessels continue to transit despite unwarranted IRGC aggression," CENTCOM said, rejecting the IRGC's assertion that it controlled the strategic waterway.

CENTCOM also disputed the IRGC's account of the sinking of the M/V GFS Pride, saying the Singapore-flagged vessel was struck by an IRGC projectile rather than a naval mine in the Strait of Hormuz on Wednesday. According to CENTCOM, the ship sank while being towed toward shore. The command added that more than 40 commercial vessels had transited the strait over the preceding 24 hours.

Singapore's Maritime and Port Authority confirmed that the GFS Pride was struck by an unidentified projectile on Oct. 13, caught fire and subsequently sank. All 21 crew members were safely evacuated, and no injuries were reported.

The authority also reported that a second Singapore-registered container ship, the GFS Giselle, was struck by an unidentified projectile on Oct. 14. The vessel sustained hull damage but reached a safe anchorage under its own power.

CENTCOM also rejected the IRGC's claim that it had killed hundreds of US service members in Jordan. "Three American service members were killed in action in July as partner forces defended against Iranian attacks," CENTCOM said.

**Analysis:** The projectile-versus-mine dispute has important implications. CENTCOM's attribution of the sinking to an IRGC projectile confirms deliberate Iranian targeting rather than indiscriminate mine warfare. The 40+ transits in 24 hours contrasts with earlier reporting suggesting sub-25% pre-war traffic levels; the truth is likely that escorted convoys get through, but overall volume and risk remain elevated.

## Maritime Attacks Hit Record Weekly Levels [VERIFIED]

Attacks on commercial shipping in and around the Strait of Hormuz have reached their highest weekly level since the US-Israeli war with Iran began, raising concerns over maritime security and the movement of energy supplies through one of the world's most important shipping corridors.

Twelve vessels were reported attacked between 5 and 11 October, according to UK Maritime Trade Operations (UKMTO) incident data, exceeding the 10 recorded during the previous week. Six vessels were reportedly targeted on 11 October alone, marking the highest number of attacks recorded in a single day during the period.

The incident highlights the growing risks facing merchant vessels as attacks, surveillance activity and radio challenges from Iranian forces continue. JMIC reported unmanned aerial vehicle overflights, targeted monitoring of commercial ships and occasional VHF radio hailing by the IRGC.

## Houthi Aden Strike Toll Rises to Five Dead [VERIFIED]

Updated casualty figures confirm the Aden presidential palace attack was more lethal than initial reports indicated.

Five people have been killed and 16 injured after the Houthis attacked the presidential palace in Aden, the seat of Yemen's Saudi-backed and internationally recognized government. Yemen's Houthis fire missiles at the presidential palace complex in the southern city of Aden killing five people, the government says.

"This is an indication that the escalation is going to intensify between both sides," Al Jazeera's correspondent in Sanaa said, adding that fears are now growing inside the country of "an all-out civil war" that could potentially drag in other regional powers besides Saudi Arabia.

The Houthis' military spokesman, Yahya Sarea, said the group had targeted what he called "the Saudi enemy's military operations headquarters in Aden." Health Minister Qasim Buhaibeh said the casualties included palace guards and civilian employees.

**Note:** This updates yesterday's initial figure of two killed. The escalation from two to five reflects either additional deaths from injuries or clarified counting as Yemeni authorities assessed the damage.

## UN Security Council Condemns Houthi Attacks [OFFICIAL]

In a joint statement shared by the Security Council's president, Greece's Evangelos Sekeris, the 15-member UN body "condemned in the strongest terms continued and escalatory Houthi attacks against the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia," as well as "continued Houthi attacks and threats against commercial shipping in the Red Sea and Bab al-Mandab (Strait)."

The statement also "affirmed the inherent right of the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia of self-defense in accordance with international law."

The council members demanded that the Houthis immediately cease their military escalation in Yemen, and emphasized that sustainable peace in Yemen cannot be achieved through violence, coercion or the use of force. They reiterated their support for a political settlement.

The Houthi war on Saudi Arabia widened: a missile strike on the presidential palace in Aden killed five, and the UN Security Council, China and Russia included, unanimously condemned the Houthi attacks on the kingdom.

**Analysis:** The unanimous condemnation—including China and Russia—is notable given their typical reluctance to criticize Iranian proxies. The affirmation of Saudi Arabia's self-defense right provides international legitimacy for potential escalation. Saudi Arabia welcomed the statement and reserved the right to "all necessary measures."

## Saudi Arabia: Houthis Rejected Oman Talks [OFFICIAL]

"At the beginning of this escalation, we asked them to meet in Muscat, Oman and they refused. This was the last communication which happened a few weeks ago," said Dr. Rayed Krimly, deputy minister for public diplomacy at the foreign ministry, during a virtual briefing with reporters.

The official added that Iran continues to arm, train, and support the Houthis, accusing Tehran of continuing to provide assistance to the group.

**Analysis:** This confirms earlier reporting that the Houthis have rejected Saudi diplomatic overtures. The closure of the Oman channel—historically the primary backchannel for Saudi-Houthi contacts—reduces near-term prospects for de-escalation on the Yemen front. Combined with the Aden attack, this signals the Houthis are prioritizing military pressure over negotiations.

## GCC-EU Summit Postponement: Strategic Setback [OFFICIAL]

The second Summit between the European Union and the Gulf Cooperation Council, originally planned for 24 October 2026 in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia, has been postponed. A new date will be announced in due course.

The closure created a regulatory cascade that made flying twenty-seven heads of state into the kingdom operationally indefensible, not merely inconvenient. The EU Aviation Safety Agency widened its Saudi airspace guidance on 9 October, Lufthansa Group suspended all Riyadh flights through at least 16 October.

The move is a setback for Saudi Arabia as it tries to project a sense of business as usual despite the Houthi campaign and seeks international support against the Iran-backed group.

Riyadh's main airport has been shut since October 10, when a Houthi missile strike killed 13 people and wounded more than 300.

## Saudi Airport Attacks: Comprehensive Tally [OFFICIAL]

From 6 to 12 October 2026, the Houthis in Yemen launched a series of ballistic missile and drone attacks on three airports in Saudi Arabia, killing at least 20 people and injuring more than 370 others.

It is the deadliest series of attacks in the country since the Qatif and Dammam mosque bombings in May 2015, and is the deadliest of the attacks on the Gulf states since the beginning of the 2026 Iran war.

The Houthis had fired 1,104 missiles and 1,135 drones at Saudi Arabia since this round of fighting began in July. Mr. Al-Malki also said the Houthis had targeted the King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh 20 times so far since early October.

## Israeli Election Remains Deadlocked [VERIFIED]

Poll of Polls shows Netanyahu bloc 54, Opposition 52, Arab parties 14—neither bloc reaching the 61-seat majority threshold.

Eisenkot's bloc stands at 50 Knesset seats, down from 52 last week, while the governing coalition rose from 51 to 53. Eisenkot's Yashar party loses one seat compared with last week but remains the largest party, with 22 seats. Likud also loses one seat, falling to 20.

The Maariv poll found that 48% of people think Gadi Eisenkot would make a better prime minister than Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, who had 41% support. The majority of Israelis would prefer a unity government to another election if the upcoming vote on October 27 doesn't produce a clear result.

**Analysis:** With 11 days until the October 27 vote, neither bloc has a path to 61 seats without relying on Arab parties, which opposition leaders have said they won't include in their coalition. This creates a plausible scenario of post-election deadlock, potentially affecting regional coordination timing.
