# US-Israel-Iran Conflict: Trump Resists Netanyahu Pressure as Diplomacy Shows Movement

## November 2, 2026 — Day 248

**Assessment:** The conflict entered a pivotal phase on the eve of US midterms, with two competing dynamics now shaping the near-term trajectory. Trump publicly declared he is in "no rush" to resume strikes, overriding Netanyahu's reported pressure for immediate post-election military action—a significant friction point between the allies. Meanwhile, Iran shortened its Hormuz reopening proposal from seven days to 48 hours and a draft proposal is circulating from three days of Doha talks, even as maritime attacks continued unabated with projectile strikes on a tanker and an offshore tug. Oil crashed 5-7% on diplomacy hopes, the largest single-day drop in months. China dispatched its Middle East envoy to Iran, Oman, and Saudi Arabia, signaling Beijing's growing frustration with the stalled conflict. In Israel, all major opposition leaders have now pledged to recommend Eisenkot to Herzog, with Arab parties' support putting him on track for 60+ seat endorsements when consultations begin Wednesday—potentially reshaping Israel's Iran posture.

---

## 1. Trump-Netanyahu Divergence on Post-Midterm Strikes [REPORTED]

A significant disagreement has emerged between President Trump and Prime Minister Netanyahu over the timing of renewed military action against Iran.

According to Axios, which cited an Israeli source and an American source, Netanyahu has been pressing Trump to launch another military offensive against Iran immediately following Tuesday's US midterm elections. In a Saturday phone conversation, the Israeli leader pressed Washington to resume military action against Iran shortly after the ballot, but Trump favoured allowing ongoing diplomatic tracks to play out, according to American and Israeli sources familiar with the exchange.

Netanyahu is "concerned" about Trump's approach, according to sources. The Israeli official characterised the discussion as "tough," while the US source described it as "direct." Trump, on Sunday, confirmed having a "very good talk" with Netanyahu but declined to divulge what they talked about, beyond confirming that Iran was among the topics.

Trump said Monday he was in no hurry to resume military strikes against Iran as Qatari mediators push a confidence-building plan. Rubio confirmed that Trump "hasn't made a decision" on whether to renew attacks on Tehran.

Trump told reporters in Palm Beach that the US wanted to destroy up to two more targets in Iran, explicitly mentioning "Pickaxe Mountain" (the Fordow facility), but coupled this with statements that negotiations were "going fine."

**Significance:** This is the most consequential development since the October 30 report. Netanyahu, politically weakened after losing his coalition majority, appears to be pushing for a decisive security move that could boost his domestic standing. Trump's resistance—preferring to test diplomacy first—introduces a significant friction point that could delay or moderate any post-midterm US action. The divergence suggests the "immediately after November 3" strike scenario is less automatic than previously assessed.

## 2. Doha Talks: Draft Proposal Circulating, Iran Proposes 48-Hour Timeline [REPORTED]

Three days of Qatari-mediated talks concluded with Iranian negotiators departing Sunday, but diplomatic channels remain active.

Iranian negotiators have left talks with Qatari mediators aimed at ending the war with the United States, and a draft proposal is being circulated with a follow-up meeting possible, an official briefed on the talks said on Monday, November 2. The negotiators departed on Sunday, November 1, though not because of difficulties in the negotiations, the official said.

Iran's foreign ministry spokesperson said the basis of the talks is a seven-step plan Iran drew up in September to reopen the Strait of Hormuz and end the US naval blockade on Iran, with Tehran now suggesting that proposal could be implemented on a faster timeline under a deal.

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said Qatari mediators had helped shorten Tehran's seven-day reopening proposal. Under the revised sequence, the U.S. would take steps on the first day, and shipping would resume on the second.

Iran's conditions include an end to the war across the Middle East, the unfreezing of its assets held abroad, the lifting of sanctions on its oil sector and an end to the US naval blockade of Iranian ports. Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi also called for a new transit arrangement for vessels passing through the strait, agreed between Iran and Oman, as well as a reduction in US troop presence in the region.

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said Monday that talks with mediators from Qatar over the weekend produced no major breakthroughs, but the diplomatic process is still ongoing.

**Significance:** The timeline compression (7 days → 48 hours) represents a tactical flexibility signal from Tehran, though the underlying conditions remain unchanged. The circulation of a draft proposal suggests more substantive progress than public statements indicate. However, Iran's preconditions—sanctions relief, frozen assets, blockade-lifting before nuclear discussions—remain structurally incompatible with Washington's insistence that nuclear issues be addressed from the outset. The diplomatic track is more active than a week ago, but a breakthrough remains distant.

## 3. Maritime Attacks Continue: Projectiles Replace Ambiguous Mine Claims [VERIFIED]

The Strait of Hormuz saw multiple attacks over the weekend, with UKMTO and maritime authorities confirming projectile-based strikes rather than the mine incidents Iran has claimed.

A tanker was struck by two unidentified projectiles in the Persian Gulf late Sunday, marking the latest in a series of attacks on commercial shipping in and around the Strait of Hormuz that have left vessels damaged, triggered onboard fires and forced the evacuation of 25 seafarers. According to UK Maritime Trade Operations (UKMTO), the latest attack occurred at 2347 UTC on November 1, approximately 72 nautical miles east of Ras Al Khafji, Saudi Arabia.

A Seabulk International offshore tug was reported to be partially sinking following an attack in the Strait of Hormuz on 1 November. Maritime security firm Vanguard Tech reported that the Panama-flagged tug Selat Storm was struck by an unidentified projectile in the Strait of Hormuz at around 15:00 UTC on 1 November.

JMIC maintained its SEVERE threat assessment for the Strait of Hormuz, warning that further deliberate attacks were highly likely. The center recorded 18 confirmed attacks in the Strait since October 6 as of its November 1 assessment, before the two latest UKMTO warnings were incorporated.

Iran continues pushing an alternative narrative: In a statement, the Navy's public relations office said the tanker had entered a "prohibited and unsafe" zone with its navigation systems switched off. The Navy warned tanker owners against attempting to cross restricted waters without coordination or with their identification systems disabled, saying such actions could result in mine strikes and fires.

**Significance:** The shift from ambiguous mine claims to UKMTO-verified projectile attacks represents an escalation in attributable risk. Projectile attacks from small boats or shore batteries are more directly traceable to Iranian forces than floating mines. The attack on an offshore tug—relatively rare in this conflict—and the geographic spread into the upper Arabian Gulf (72nm east of Ras Al Khafji) demonstrate expanding operational reach. The JMIC's "severe" threat assessment and 18+ confirmed attacks since October 6 indicate sustained Iranian maritime pressure regardless of diplomatic progress elsewhere.

## 4. Oil Crashes on Diplomacy Hopes [VERIFIED]

Markets responded sharply to diplomatic signals, reversing the strike-fear premium.

Brent fell to 105.86 USD/Bbl on November 2, 2026, down 6.67% from the previous day. Other sources show intraday trading in the $104-111 range. Brent futures fell in the past 24 hours by 3.90% to $109.03/bbl, as of November 2, 2026. Crude Oil fell to 96.29 USD/Bbl on November 2, 2026, down 7.52% from the previous day.

Over the last 52 weeks, Brent Crude has traded between a low of 58.92 and a high of 118.35 USD / bbl (daily closing prices). Brent Crude is up 71.5% year-to-date in 2026, moving from 60.75 at the start of the year to 104.19 USD / bbl as of 2026-11-02.

**Significance:** The ~7% single-day decline is the largest since the conflict began and represents a meaningful probability shift by markets. Traders are pricing in higher odds of diplomatic progress and lower odds of immediate post-midterm strikes—consistent with Trump's "no rush" posture and the draft proposal circulation from Doha. However, if talks fail to produce concrete results post-midterm, expect a sharp rebound toward the $113+ levels seen last week.

## 5. China Steps Up Middle East Diplomacy [OFFICIAL]

Beijing is deploying significant diplomatic resources to the region amid growing frustration with the conflict.

China will send Zhai Jun, a special envoy to the Middle East, to Iran, Oman and Saudi Arabia in early and mid-November, Chinese foreign ministry spokesperson Mao Ning said on Monday.

Chinese President Xi Jinping, during a meeting with Sultan Haitham bin Tarik of Oman in Beijing, emphasized that the Middle East and the Persian Gulf region must not become an arena for rivalry among major powers and geopolitical conflicts. Xi Jinping stated that China has consistently worked toward stopping the war and establishing peace, expressing hope to offer solutions to address the region's security challenges.

China is stepping up its diplomatic efforts in the Middle East, including over the US-Israel war on Iran, following a lapse in activity. Wang told Ali Bagheri Kani, deputy secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council, that dialogue and negotiation remained "the only viable path forward" as conflicts in the region "drag on and risk further spillover".

"The meetings on Monday appear to illustrate that China is growing impatient with the lack of progress. 'I think China is very frustrated, and it wants to be seen to enter [the process],'" Al Jazeera's correspondent reported.

China is considered one of the primary buyers of Middle Eastern oil, and any disruption by the Islamic Republic to oil exports from Arab nations could directly impact China's economy.

**Significance:** Beijing's diplomatic intervention is notable. China brokered the 2023 Saudi-Iran rapprochement and has direct economic interests in Hormuz stability. Xi's explicit statement that the region "must not become an arena for rivalry among major powers" is a veiled criticism of US military operations. While China is unlikely to displace Qatari mediation, Beijing's engagement adds pressure on all parties and could provide an alternative channel if US-Iran talks collapse.

## 6. Israel Coalition: Eisenkot Set for Mandate as Opposition Unifies [VERIFIED]

The opposition bloc has coalesced behind Gadi Eisenkot ahead of President Herzog's consultations Wednesday.

Avigdor Liberman and Yair Golan said Monday they will recommend Gadi Eisenkot to form Israel's next government, joining Naftali Bennett, the Jerusalem Post reported. The Democrats party announced on Monday that it would recommend Yashar leader Gadi Eisenkot to President Isaac Herzog as its candidate to form the next government. The announcement came after the two other party leaders in the bloc seeking to replace Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, Naftali Bennett (B'Yachad) and Avigdor Liberman (Yisrael Beytenu), said they would recommend Eisenkot.

Israel's Arab parties, which won a record-breaking 19 seats in last week's general elections, are set to back opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot's bloc, further trampling Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's chances of leading another government.

Biton indicated that Israel President Herzog and US President Trump could provide assurances to facilitate an agreement between PM Netanyahu and principal opponent Eisenkot, leader of the Yashar Party. A unity government with Trump and Herzog as potential guarantors remains under discussion.

Talks at the President's Residence will begin Wednesday, when Eisenkot may be the first candidate to receive the mandate from the president, giving him 28 days, with a possible 14-day extension, to form a coalition.

**Significance:** With Bennett, Liberman, Golan, and likely the Arab parties all backing Eisenkot, he is on track for 60+ seat recommendations versus Netanyahu's ~46. This doesn't guarantee government formation—coalition negotiations will be complex and Arab party inclusion remains controversial—but it significantly shifts the political landscape. A government led by Eisenkot, a former IDF chief known for more cautious military positions, could fundamentally alter Israel's approach to the Iran conflict. Netanyahu's fraud narrative and unity government discussions suggest he may accept a grand coalition rather than face opposition government formation.
