## Key Developments (Past 48 Hours)

### **IRAN PROPOSES 48-HOUR HORMUZ REOPENING TIMELINE** [OFFICIAL]

Iran on Monday said it was ready to reopen the Strait of Hormuz within two days if the US agrees to its conditions, having previously proposed a one-week timeline.

Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesperson Esmail Baghaei said the plan remained the basis for negotiations with Washington through mediators, despite the shorter implementation timeline. "This once again demonstrates Iran's good-faith approach," he added.

Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi told Al Jazeera that Iran would reopen the strait two days after the US lifts its naval blockade on Iranian oil, lets Iran get its frozen money back and drops sanctions on its oil.

Baghaei gave no indication that Tehran had changed its demands or that Washington had accepted its proposal.

**[ANALYSIS]** The shortened timeline (7 days → 48 hours) suggests Iran is seeking to demonstrate flexibility ahead of the midterms. However, the underlying conditions remain unchanged — this is a timing concession, not a substantive one. The US has not accepted the proposal.

### **DOHA TALKS: DRAFT PROPOSAL CIRCULATING; FOLLOW-UP POSSIBLE** [REPORTED]

Iranian negotiators left Doha talks on Sunday, but not due to difficulties in negotiations aimed to reach a deal to end the US-Israeli war on Iran, an official briefed on talks told Reuters on Monday.

A draft proposal was being circulated for feedback, and a follow-up meeting is possible, the official added. Iran's foreign ministry spokesperson Esmaeil Baghaei said deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi had been in Doha to facilitate the exchange of messages with Washington via Qatari intermediaries.

"Our demands have been articulated very clearly and transparently. These demands are reasonable and logical: no final agreement can be initiated, nor can the Strait of Hormuz be reopened or secured, unless the US naval blockade is brought to an end, Iran's demands are respected, and Iran's legitimate requirements are addressed," Baghaei said.

**[ANALYSIS]** The fact that negotiators left "not because of difficulties" and a draft is circulating represents cautious progress. However, Iran's public statements remain uncompromising on preconditions.

### **TRUMP-NETANYAHU CALL: TENSION OVER TIMING OF STRIKES** [REPORTED]

President Trump had a candid phone call with Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu Saturday about the state of U.S. negotiations with Iran and how quickly to return to combat, according to one Israeli and one U.S. source.

The sources said Netanyahu is pressing Trump to strike Iran immediately after the midterm elections on Tuesday. But Trump, for now, wants to let negotiations play out — a strategy Netanyahu is concerned about.

The Israeli source said the call was "tough," while the U.S. source described it as "direct."

Trump told reporters on Sunday that he and Netanyahu had a "very good" call the day before. He said they discussed Iran but gave no details.

Secretary of State Rubio said "He'd prefer to solve it diplomatically," adding that Trump has "incredible confidence" in military action if that is needed.

**[ANALYSIS]** This is a significant development. Netanyahu, weakened domestically after losing his coalition majority, is pushing for immediate post-midterm strikes. Trump is resisting, preferring diplomacy — at least for now. This divergence could delay or moderate any US action.

### **TRUMP: "NO RUSH" ON IRAN; "1-2 SITES" REMAIN** [VERIFIED]

US President Donald Trump on Saturday said that Washington could strike Iran's remaining suspected nuclear facilities in "10 minutes," signaling the possibility of further military action despite ongoing diplomatic negotiations. Speaking to reporters at his Mar-a-Lago estate in Florida, Trump claimed that US military operations had severely weakened Iran's armed forces and nuclear infrastructure, leaving only a few sites to be targeted.

"We still have to take care of, I would say, one or two sites, but we can do that in 10 minutes. So, we have that plan." "You probably could look at Pickaxe Mountain, you have to take care of that. We can do that all very quickly," he added, referring to a suspected Iranian nuclear facility.

President Trump has not made a decision about new strikes on Iran amid stalled peace talks, Secretary of State Marco Rubio said Saturday.

Trump said Monday he was in no hurry to restart military operations against Iran as Qatari mediators push a confidence-building plan.

**[ANALYSIS]** Trump is running a dual-track approach: signaling military capability ("10 minutes") while expressing patience on diplomacy. The explicit mention of "Pickaxe Mountain" (likely Fordow) suggests targeting options remain active.

### **CHINA DISPATCHES MIDDLE EAST ENVOY** [OFFICIAL]

BEIJING: China will send Zhai Jun, a special envoy to the Middle East, to Iran, Oman and Saudi Arabia in early and mid-November, Chinese foreign ministry spokesperson Mao Ning said on Monday.

Chinese President Xi Jinping, during a meeting with Sultan Haitham bin Tarik of Oman in Beijing, emphasized that the Middle East and the Persian Gulf region must not become an arena for rivalry among major powers and geopolitical conflicts. Xi Jinping stated that China has consistently worked toward stopping the war and establishing peace, expressing hope to offer solutions to address the region's security challenges.

China is considered one of the primary buyers of Middle Eastern oil, and any disruption by the Islamic Republic to oil exports from Arab nations could directly impact China's economy.

**[ANALYSIS]** China's diplomatic intervention is notable. Beijing brokered the 2023 Saudi-Iran rapprochement and has direct economic interests in Hormuz stability. This adds a new diplomatic track to monitor.

### **ISRAEL COALITION: HERZOG BEGINS CONSULTATIONS WEDNESDAY** [VERIFIED]

President Isaac Herzog plans to summon representatives of the parties elected to Israel's 26th Knesset to his official residence as early as Wednesday, November 4, even before the new parliament is sworn in, to begin the process of forming the country's next government. Herzog will hear which candidate each party recommends to lead the coalition-building effort.

President Isaac Herzog will begin consultations with representatives of the parties elected to the 26th Knesset on Wednesday, starting with the smallest factions in an unusual departure from previous rounds of coalition negotiations. Rather than beginning with the largest parties, Herzog will invite faction representatives in ascending order of Knesset seats.

Israel's Arab parties, which won a record-breaking 19 seats in last week's general elections, are set to back opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot's bloc, further trampling Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's chances of leading another government.

President Isaac Herzog: 60 endorsements expected for Eisenkot, 46 for Netanyahu.

**[ANALYSIS]** Arab party support for Eisenkot is a major shift. If Eisenkot receives the mandate, it could fundamentally change Israel's Iran posture — though coalition formation will take weeks and Netanyahu could still block or delay.
