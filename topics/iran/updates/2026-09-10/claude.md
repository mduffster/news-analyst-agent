## Key Developments Since Prior Report (September 9)

### 1. MAJOR: Houthis Capture Mokha Port — Critical Bab al-Mandab Position

**[VERIFIED]** Iran-backed Houthi militants have seized the strategic port city of Mokha on Yemen's western coast after days of fighting with pro-government forces, further expanding their control along the Red Sea and near the Bab Al Mandeb shipping route.

**[VERIFIED]** Mocha sits less than 70 kilometers (about 43 miles) north of the Bab al-Mandab Strait, the narrow passage linking the Red Sea to the Gulf of Aden.

**[OFFICIAL]** "Resistance groups have left Mokha and are now deployed to Dhubab (46km) south of the city," a Yemeni official told The National. Yemen's Presidential Leadership Council Vice Chairman Tareq Saleh said government forces, including the National Resistance, Giants Brigades and Nation's Shield forces, had suffered deaths and injuries during several days of Houthi attacks.

**[REPORTED]** Hundreds of people have reportedly been killed and thousands displaced since the Houthis launched an offensive in the south-western coastal region a week ago.

**[REPORTED]** Yemeni military analyst Col. Abdul Basit Al-Baher has warned that seizing Mokha would give the Houthis new positions to attack vessels and smuggle weapons, saying "whoever controls Mokha would control Bab Al-Mandab."

*Assessment: This is a strategic game-changer. The Houthis now control a key port overlooking the Bab al-Mandab strait — the alternate chokepoint for Gulf oil exports that shippers had been using to bypass the closed Strait of Hormuz. Combined with the Hormuz crisis, Iran-aligned forces now threaten both major oil transit routes.*

### 2. MAJOR: US Fighter Jets Damaged in Iran's Jordan Strike — First Confirmed Equipment Losses

**[REPORTED]** Multiple US military aircraft were damaged in Iranian strikes carried out overnight Tuesday into Wednesday local time on Muwaffaq Salti Air Base in Jordan. The sources reportedly said that approximately eight F-15 fighter jets suffered minor damage but were subsequently returned to service. An A-10 Thunderbolt, known as the Warthog, was also hit and reportedly lost a wing, according to the sources.

**[REPORTED]** According to the report, US forces deployed more than 30 Patriot missiles in an effort to defend the base against the Iranian attack.

**[VERIFIED]** No US fatalities have been reported in connection with the attacks in Jordan.

*Assessment: This is the first confirmed damage to US aircraft in the conflict. While the eight F-15s returned to service with minor damage, the A-10 losing a wing represents a more significant equipment loss. Critically, no US casualties were reported — maintaining the threshold that has prevented major escalation.*

**[VERIFIED]** By 8 a.m. Eastern Time today, oil had reached $105.20 per barrel, measured using the Brent benchmark. That's $3.15 more than it cost yesterday morning and about $37.30 above its price a year earlier.

**[VERIFIED]** Brent Crude Oil at $105.71 as of Sep 10, 2026.

**[REPORTED]** Oil may rally to as much as $120 a barrel if attacks on shipping in the Middle East increase, according to Goldman Sachs Group Inc. "Events over the last few days do suggest that the risk of shipping disruptions broadening and intensifying is an important one," Daan Struyven, co-head of global commodities research, said.

**[REPORTED]** American consumers spent an additional $100 billion on fuel between the start of the Iran war on Feb. 28 and Sept. 8, according to a tracker from Brown University. Higher gasoline prices accounted for about $55 billion of that amount, while diesel accounted for the remaining $45 billion.

*Assessment: Oil has surged ~$5 in 24 hours on the Mokha news and continuing tanker war. The Goldman $120 upside scenario is now in play. With Mokha fallen, both Hormuz AND Bab al-Mandab are now threatened — the two key oil transit chokepoints.*

**[OFFICIAL]** "I think war is going to end immediately after the election because they can't hold out any longer. They're desperate to try and affect the election," he told reporters before departing for the Republican National Convention in Dallas.

**[REPORTED]** US President Donald Trump said the Iran war would end "immediately" after November's midterm election, contradicting the analysis of experts and even some of his closest advisors. ### 5. Saudi-Houthi Fighting Intensifies — Emergency Alerts in Kingdom

**[VERIFIED]** Fighting has escalated across Yemen, with the country's internationally recognised government claiming the Houthis killed three children in an artillery attack, while Saudi Arabia accused the Iran-backed group of continuing to target the kingdom's infrastructure. The claims came as warnings of impending attacks sounded in several Saudi provinces early on Thursday, with the Houthis promising to respond after accusing Riyadh of launching 54 airstrikes over a 12-hour span.

**[REPORTED]** Pakistan warned on Tuesday that continued Houthi attacks could trigger the recently signed Mecca Joint Defense Agreement, a mutual security pact involving Saudi Arabia, Turkey, and Pakistan.

**[VERIFIED]** Saudi forces launched at least 15 strikes on Houthi targets between Sept. 3 and Monday. At least 276 people were killed over the course of five days.

*Assessment: The Saudi-Houthi front is now a parallel theater to the US-Iran tanker war. Pakistan's warning about the Mecca Joint Defense Agreement signals potential for broader regional involvement. The fall of Mokha represents a major Houthi advance despite Saudi airstrikes.*

### 6. IAEA Referral Analysis — Largely Symbolic but Notable

**[VERIFIED]** In the first such action in 20 years, the International Atomic Energy Agency's (IAEA's) Board of Governors voted to refer Iran's noncompliance with its nuclear nonproliferation obligations to the UN Security Council (UNSC) for possible countermeasures. The vote passed with 23 in favor, three against, and eight abstentions. China, Russia, and Niger opposed the resolution.

**[VERIFIED]** At the UNSC, Beijing and Moscow are not expected to support new penalties against Iran, such as sanctions. Even so, the IAEA action sends a clear political message: irrespective of U.S. and Israeli military strikes on Iran's nuclear facilities and the ongoing conflict, Iran is still required to meet its IAEA safeguards commitments.

*Assessment: The IAEA referral is the strongest multilateral rebuke of Iran since 2006, but Russian and Chinese vetoes will block any Security Council action. The timing — amid escalating conflict — signals Western intent to maintain diplomatic pressure on all fronts.*
