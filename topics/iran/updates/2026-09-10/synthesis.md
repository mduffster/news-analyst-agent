## Critical Developments (September 9-10)

### 1. MAJOR: Houthis Capture Mokha — Strategic Red Sea Port Near Bab al-Mandab [VERIFIED]

The Houthis' takeover of Mokha was confirmed to The Associated Press by Ahmed Baash, a commander with the National Resistance Forces allied with Yemen's government, and Hazam al-Assad, a member of the Houthis' political bureau.

Iran-backed Houthi militants have seized the strategic port city of Mokha on Yemen's western coast after days of fighting with pro-government forces, further expanding their control along the Red Sea and near the Bab Al Mandeb shipping route. "Resistance groups have left Mokha and are now deployed to Dhubab (46km) south of the city," a Yemeni official told The National.

Mokha represents the Houthis' biggest territorial gain since a truce signed in 2022 to end a civil war that lasted more than a decade. A return to full-scale civil war would be devastating for the Yemeni people and potentially world energy supplies.

The Bab el-Mandeb has become even more important for global energy supplies since shipping through the nearby Strait of Hormuz was disrupted by Iran after it was attacked by the United States and Israel earlier this year.

**Assessment:** [VERIFIED] This is a strategic game-changer. The advance could increase the threat to Saudi oil exports through the Red Sea as disruption in the Strait of Hormuz limits shipments from the Gulf. Iran-backed Houthis have captured Yemen's port of Mokha, strengthening the rebel group's grip near the Bab al-Mandab strait. Iran-aligned forces now threaten both major oil transit routes — a dual chokepoint crisis that explains today's $5 oil surge.

### 2. MAJOR: US Fighter Jets Damaged in Jordan Strike — First Confirmed Equipment Losses [REPORTED]

Multiple American military aircraft were damaged in Iranian strikes overnight Tuesday into Wednesday local time on Muwaffaq Salti Air Base in Jordan. One A-10 Thunderbolt, an attack aircraft known as the Warthog, was struck and lost a wing. Roughly eight F-15s sustained light damage and were put back into service. No U.S. deaths have been reported in the strikes on Jordan.

The damage occurred after the US said Tuesday that it struck five Iranian tankers in retaliation for Iran's targeting of a US Navy vessel.

**Assessment:** [REPORTED] This is the first confirmed damage to US aircraft in the conflict. The eight F-15s returned to service with minor damage, while the A-10 losing a wing represents more significant equipment loss. Critically, no US casualties were reported — maintaining the threshold that has prevented major escalation. Both sides appear committed to avoiding American deaths that might trigger uncontrolled escalation.

Brent Crude Oil at $105.71 as of Sep 10, 2026.

By 8 a.m. Eastern Time today, oil had reached $105.20 per barrel, measured using the Brent benchmark. That's $3.15 more than it cost yesterday morning and about $37.30 above its price a year earlier.

Oil may rally to as much as $120 a barrel if attacks on shipping in the Middle East increase, according to Goldman Sachs Group Inc. The Goldman Sachs $120 upside scenario is now increasingly plausible with Mokha's fall threatening both major chokepoints. Goldman's framing gives traders a clear range to work with: $120 a barrel if shipping attacks broaden and intensify, $80 if Gulf exports normalize.

### 4. Trump Predicts War Will End "Immediately After" Midterms [OFFICIAL]

President Donald Trump said the war with Iran would end "immediately" after the US midterm elections, as Iranian attacks reportedly damaged several US military aircraft at a base in Jordan.

"I think the war is going to end immediately after the election" on November 3, he told reporters. He suggested that Tehran was trying to prolong the conflict as a way of keeping fuel prices high in the run-up to the election.

"But right after the election, oil prices are going to be tumbling downward." The comments mark a shift from his earlier statements about the expected duration of the conflict. When he launched the war alongside Israel on February 28, he said he expected it to last only a few weeks.

Trump's approval ratings recently sank to a record low due to frustration from voters over the Iran war and deepening cost-of-living crisis. Midterm elections, due to be held on Nov. 3 this year, are typically a referendum on the occupant of the White House.

**Assessment:** [OFFICIAL] Trump's post-midterm prediction contradicts earlier projections and, according to one report, his own advisors who reportedly expect Iran to resist through January 2029. This messaging appears designed to reassure voters that relief is coming, but the administration simultaneously signaled no interest in resuming diplomacy. "We're not looking for it to be honest," Trump responded. "Originally I wanted to make a deal. We're so far down the line. We have very little country left."

### 5. Saudi-Houthi Fighting Intensifies — Dual Front Escalation [VERIFIED]

Yemen's Houthi rebels reported about 40 airstrikes launched by Saudi forces on Thursday in a fresh salvo following attacks by the Iran-backed group against the kingdom. "The Saudi enemy's air force conducted around 40 strikes in recent hours in the governorates of Taiz, Hodeida, Al-Jawf and Marib," Houthi news agency Saba said.

Saudi forces launched at least 15 strikes on Houthi targets between Sept. 3 and Monday. At least 276 people were killed over the course of five days.

The Houthis have launched major offensives against Yemeni government forces backed by Saudi Arabia, attacking Saudi territory and causing injuries. The ongoing conflict in Yemen has resulted in over 500 deaths, mostly among combatants.

**Assessment:** [VERIFIED] The Saudi-Houthi front is now a parallel theater to the US-Iran tanker war. Despite Saudi airstrikes, the Houthis achieved their largest territorial gain since 2022 with Mokha's capture. The Yemen track has transformed from a side theater into a strategic front directly threatening global energy routes.

### 6. Dual Chokepoint Threat Emerges [VERIFIED]

Neil Quilliam, a Middle East specialist at Chatham House, said Mokha's significance lay in its location rather than the port itself. "Control of Mokha gives the Houthis another platform from which to exert pressure on Red Sea shipping, not just at sea but from land."

The stakes are higher because shipping through the Strait of Hormuz has already fallen sharply during the widening Iran conflict. Preliminary tracking data cited by Reuters showed only seven vessels transited Hormuz Wednesday, while 28 commodity ships passed through Bab el-Mandeb. Saudi Arabia has increasingly relied on its Red Sea port of Yanbu to move crude oil around the disrupted Hormuz route. Houthi control closer to Bab el-Mandeb could place greater pressure on that alternative.

Saudi Arabia can bypass Hormuz by pumping crude across the country to the Red Sea port of Yanbu. However, Houthi attacks on ships and Saudi infrastructure have made that route more hazardous.

**Assessment:** [VERIFIED] Iran-aligned forces now effectively threaten both major oil transit chokepoints. This is an unprecedented strategic development that explains the sharp oil price surge. The Yanbu bypass route Saudi Arabia had been using to circumvent Hormuz is now itself under elevated threat.
