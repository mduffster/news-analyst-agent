## Executive Summary

**KEY DEVELOPMENT: Draft 60-Day MOU Reached But Awaiting Leadership Approval; IRGC Attacks Kuwait Base; Israel Escalates Lebanon Operations**

**US and Iranian negotiators have reached agreement on a draft 60-day memorandum of understanding to extend the ceasefire and reopen the Strait of Hormuz, but neither President Trump nor Iran's Supreme Leader Khamenei has given final approval. This diplomatic progress was overshadowed by the most significant military escalation since the ceasefire began: IRGC struck a US airbase in Kuwait overnight in retaliation for US strikes near Bandar Abbas, with Kuwaiti air defenses intercepting missiles and drones. Israel simultaneously intensified its Lebanon campaign ahead of Pentagon talks scheduled for May 29.**

**Bottom line:** The conflict has reached a pivotal inflection point. "The signing of the MOU would be the most significant diplomatic breakthrough since the war started, but a final agreement that tackles Trump's nuclear demands would still require further intensive negotiations." However, the overnight IRGC attack on Kuwait—the first direct Iranian strike on a Gulf state during the ceasefire—demonstrates that military escalation continues alongside diplomatic progress. The situation is becoming binary: either leaders approve the draft deal soon, or the pattern of tit-for-tat escalation could collapse the talks entirely.

## Critical Developments Since May 27

### 1. Draft 60-Day MOU Reached — Awaiting Leadership Approval

**[REPORTED]** US sources confirmed that US and Iranian negotiators have reached a deal, but it still needs Trump's final approval. The US officials confirm that the president has been briefed on the draft memorandum of understanding, and say it would see the Strait of Hormuz fully opened over the course of 60 days.

**[REPORTED]** Key elements of the reported framework:
- According to Axios, the proposed 60-day memorandum of understanding would guarantee unrestricted shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, though the deal still requires approval from President Donald Trump. The report also said Iran would remove all mines from the strait within 30 days.
- The MOU will include an Iranian commitment not to pursue a nuclear weapon.
- The first issues to be negotiated during the 60-day window will be how to dispose of Iran's highly enriched uranium and how to address Iranian enrichment. The US will commit to discuss sanctions relief and the release of frozen Iranian funds as part of the negotiations.
- The US would also issue some sanctions waivers to allow Iran to sell oil freely.

**[REPORTED]** The MOU would also state that the war between Israel and Hezbollah in Lebanon would end—an issue on which Trump and Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu have had at least one tense discussion.

**[REPORTED]** Critical caveats remain: Tehran has not confirmed its acceptance of the MOU. Both Trump and Iranian leadership have rejected deals in the late stages of negotiations before. The US officials also say the Trump administration is still concerned about the fractured Iranian regime's ability to coalesce behind any agreement.

**Assessment:** This represents the closest both sides have come to a formal agreement since the war began. The framework addresses most major issues—Hormuz, nuclear commitments, sanctions—but leadership approval remains the critical bottleneck. The Lebanon inclusion clause could become a major flashpoint given Israel's ongoing escalation.

### 2. IRGC Attacks US Base in Kuwait — Most Significant Ceasefire Violation

**[VERIFIED]** Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps at approximately 0150 GMT on 28 May 2026 claimed a retaliatory attack on a US airbase in Kuwait it identified as the source of the Bandar Abbas strikes, with the IRGC stating in a Press TV-carried statement that "this response is a serious warning to the enemy that any act of aggression will not go unanswered, and if repeated, our response will be even more decisive."

**[VERIFIED]** In a brief statement posted on X, Kuwait's military confirmed that its air defenses had been activated amid the rapidly worsening regional situation. "Kuwaiti air defenses are currently engaging hostile missile and drone attacks," the country's armed forces said.

**[VERIFIED]** Reports that an Iranian ballistic missile was fired from Khuzestan just before the air raid sirens went off in Kuwait. Explosions, likely resulting from successful interceptions by air defenses, heard in the sky to the northwest of Kuwait City.

**[VERIFIED]** Iran's formal active retaliation campaign remains suspended under the 8 April 2026 ceasefire, but the 28 May data cycle confirmed the most consequential public Iranian kinetic action of the post-ceasefire period.

**[OFFICIAL]** The sequence began with US defensive strikes: The CENTCOM target description of "four one-way attack drones" and a "ground control station that was about to launch a fifth drone" together document a sustained multi-day IRGC drone-launch tempo in the Strait of Hormuz littoral.

**Assessment:** This overnight exchange represents a dangerous escalation pattern. The IRGC's attack on Kuwait—even though intercepted—tests the limits of what constitutes a ceasefire violation and creates precedent for tit-for-tat responses. However, the calibrated nature of both sides' actions (targeting infrastructure rather than personnel directly) suggests neither wants full-scale war resumption.

### 4. Israel Escalates Lebanon Campaign Ahead of Pentagon Talks

**[VERIFIED]** At least 16 people have been killed and 58 wounded in Israeli attacks in southern Lebanon, according to Lebanese health authorities.

**[VERIFIED]** Israel's air force carried out an airstrike on a southern suburb of the capital, Beirut, Thursday afternoon, the Israeli military said. It was not immediately clear who was the target of the airstrike on the suburb of Choueifat. The last time Israel struck Beirut was on 6 May, killing a military official with Hezbollah's elite Radwan Forces.

**[VERIFIED]** The strike came amid an escalation in southern Lebanon, where Israeli troops have crossed the Litani River over the past days and issued a warning for residents to leave much of the area.

**[VERIFIED]** The Israeli military confirms that it has been conducting raids in recent days beyond its declared "forward defense line," which demarcates the IDF's security zone in southern Lebanon. According to a military official, the goal of the raids is to push Hezbollah farther from the border.

**[VERIFIED]** On Wednesday, the Israeli military also declared all areas south of Lebanon's Zahrani River, an area roughly 40 kilometres from the border, "combat zones" and told residents to evacuate ahead of attacks against Hezbollah.

**[VERIFIED]** At least 3,269 people have been killed in Israeli strikes since the start of the war, according to the Lebanese Health Ministry, with over 9,800 wounded.

**[REPORTED]** Israeli officials explicitly state their dual-track approach: "This effectively means that Israel has decided to continue the 'war against Hezbollah' while holding diplomatic talks with Beirut to put pressure on the Lebanese government ahead of expected talks in the Pentagon. 'I hope that we will have the talks in the Pentagon with the Lebanese government and allow war against Hezbollah at the same time,' said an intelligence official in Israel."

**Assessment:** Israel's escalation ahead of the May 29 Pentagon talks creates structural tension with any US-Iran deal that includes Lebanon. Hezbollah has dismissed the talks and instead endorsed its key ally Iran, which has made ending the war in Lebanon a condition for its own talks with Washington brokered by Pakistan. This disconnect could derail the broader framework even if US-Iran bilateral issues are resolved.

### 5. Pentagon Talks: Security Track Opens May 29

**[VERIFIED]** There will be a military coordination meeting between Lebanese, Israeli and American military delegations at the Pentagon on 29 May, 2026. The topics to be discussed will be the Israeli army withdrawal, the Lebanese army takeover of Southern Lebanon, the financial support for the Lebanese army, the disarmament of Hezbollah, and the enforcement mechanism for the ceasefire.

**[VERIFIED]** Following that round, it was decided to prolong the ceasefire in Lebanon by additional 45 days.

**[REPORTED]** Israel's continuous attacks on southern Lebanon are delaying attempts to end the war—and "this is exactly the intention of the Israeli government. Clearly, Netanyahu doesn't want this war with Iran to end."

**Assessment:** The Pentagon talks represent a test of whether the US can simultaneously manage Israel-Lebanon security arrangements and the US-Iran bilateral track. The Lebanese government is caught between Iranian/Hezbollah pressure and Israeli military operations, with limited agency to shape outcomes.

### 6. Strait of Hormuz: Non-Iranian Transits Increasing

**[VERIFIED]** The proportion of vessels not linked to Iranian ownership that are transiting the key Strait of Hormuz has risen, according to data shared today from maritime firm Lloyd's List Intelligence. This is despite Iran's blockade of the waterway since the start of its war with the US and Israel on February 28. "The last week we saw ships flagged with Singapore, UAE, South Korea, and also a Norway-flagged vessel going through the Gulf, specifically exiting."

**[VERIFIED]** Crossings by very large crude carriers not tied to Iran appear in particular to be picking up again.

**[VERIFIED]** However, Iranian television, quoting a military official: Four ships attempted to cross the Strait of Hormuz and enter the Gulf today without coordinating with security forces.

**Assessment:** The modest increase in non-Iranian transits suggests de facto loosening of the blockade, though Iran maintains its regulatory assertion over passage. This incremental progress supports deal optimism while highlighting the contested nature of Hormuz governance.

## Structural Analysis: The Lebanon Contradiction

**The most significant obstacle to a US-Iran deal may not be bilateral—it's Israel.**

The reported MOU includes a clause stating the Israel-Hezbollah war in Lebanon would end. However:

1. Israel has decided to continue the "war against Hezbollah" while holding diplomatic talks with Beirut.
2. Hezbollah has dismissed the talks and instead endorsed its key ally Iran, which has made ending the war in Lebanon a condition for its own talks with Washington.
3. Israel's continuous attacks on southern Lebanon are delaying attempts to end the war—and this is exactly the intention of the Israeli government.

**This creates an impossible triangle:**
- **US** wants a deal with Iran that includes Lebanon cessation
- **Iran** demands Lebanon inclusion as precondition
- **Israel** refuses to stop operations against Hezbollah

Even if Trump signs the MOU, Israel's escalation could prevent implementation of the Lebanon clause, giving Iran grounds to withdraw.
