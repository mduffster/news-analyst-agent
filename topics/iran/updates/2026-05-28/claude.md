### Critical Developments (May 27-28)

#### 1. Overnight Escalation: US Strikes Iran, IRGC Retaliates Against Kuwait

**[VERIFIED]** US and Iranian forces exchanged fire early Thursday. Hours before Washington said it attacked Iranian drones and a launch site around the crucial Strait of Hormuz, US President Donald Trump insisted his administration is still "not satisfied" with the terms of the deal.

**[OFFICIAL]** Day 90 of the Iran War 2026: US Central Command on 27-28 May conducts a second self-defense strike in three days against Iranian targets near Bandar Abbas, with CENTCOM shooting down four Iranian one-way attack drones near the Strait of Hormuz and then striking an Iranian ground control station that was about to launch a fifth drone.

**[OFFICIAL]** "Following this morning's aggression by the invading US military against a location on the outskirts of Bandar Abbas Airport using aerial projectiles, the American air base that served as the source of the attack was targeted at 4:50 am (0120 GMT)," the IRGC says, according to IRIB.

**[OFFICIAL]** "This response is a serious warning so that the enemy knows that aggression will not go unanswered, and if repeated, our response will be more decisive," the IRGC stated.

**Assessment:** This is the most consequential Iranian kinetic action of the post-ceasefire period. The back-and-forth challenges the fragile ceasefire between the US and Iran, four days after US President Donald Trump claimed a deal extending the truce by 60 days was nearly complete. However, both sides appear to be calibrating carefully—the IRGC targeted a base rather than personnel directly, and Kuwait successfully intercepted the attack.

---

#### 2. Draft Deal Reported: Negotiators Agree, Leaders Haven't Signed

**[REPORTED]** Both Trump and Iranian leadership have rejected deals in the late stages of negotiations before. The U.S. officials also say the Trump administration is still concerned about the fractured Iranian regime's ability to coalesce behind any agreement, but that high-level Iranian officials have indicated the regime can accept the terms that are currently on the table.

**[REPORTED]** U.S. sources confirmed that U.S. and Iranian negotiators have reached a deal, but it still needs Trump's final approval. The U.S. officials confirm that the president has been briefed on the draft memorandum of understanding, and say it would see the Strait of Hormuz fully opened over the course of 60 days.

**[REPORTED]** According to Axios, the proposed 60-day memorandum of understanding would guarantee unrestricted shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, though the deal still requires approval from President Donald Trump. The report also said Iran would remove all mines from the strait within 30 days.

**Assessment:** The gap between negotiators and leaders is the critical variable now. Negotiators believe they have a framework, but Trump has been publicly hardening his position, and Iran's leadership must overcome internal divisions. The overnight military exchanges could be either a deal-breaker or final posturing before signature.

---

#### 3. Trump Maintains Hard Line on Sanctions

**[OFFICIAL]** Trump appeared unwilling today to ease any of the economic pressure on Iran. "We're not talking about any easing of sanctions or giving money. No sanctions, no money, no nothing," he said.

**[OFFICIAL]** "The Iranian military's latest attempt to extort global maritime trade is proof that Economic Fury has left the regime desperate for cash," Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said. The sanctions target Iran's Persian Gulf Strait Authority.

**[OFFICIAL]** Trump added a new entry to his threat list on Wednesday, threatening to strike Oman if it tries to control the Strait of Hormuz along with Iran. "Oman will behave just like everybody else, or we'll have to blow 'em up," he said.

**Assessment:** The threat against Oman—until now a key mediator—is notable. This suggests either Trump is genuinely frustrated with the negotiation framework or is using maximum pressure tactics before final approval.

#### 5. Israel Escalates Lebanon Campaign, Strikes Beirut

**[VERIFIED]** The attack came as Israel expanded its bombardment across southern Lebanon, hitting residential areas, roads and civilian infrastructure. Israeli fighter jets also struck an apartment building in southern Beirut, marking the first strike on the Lebanese capital in three weeks.

**[VERIFIED]** The Israeli military confirms that it has been conducting raids in recent days beyond its declared "forward defense line," which demarcates the IDF's security zone in southern Lebanon. According to a military official, the goal of the raids is to push Hezbollah farther from the border.

**[VERIFIED]** The Lebanese Health Ministry said Israeli strikes killed at least 34 people and wounded 77 others on Wednesday, in one of the deadliest days since the ceasefire in Lebanon took effect back in April. At least six people, including two children, were killed in an Israeli attack in the town of Adloun.

**[VERIFIED]** The IDF announces that it has killed 2,500 Hezbollah operatives in Lebanon since hostilities escalated amid the war with Iran.

**Assessment:** The escalation came as United States-mediated talks between Israel and Lebanon are due to resume on Thursday. However, Israel has significantly intensified its military operations in the past two days, casting doubt over the prospects for diplomacy. Israel is clearly establishing facts on the ground before the Pentagon talks.

---

#### 6. Pentagon Talks Today: Lebanon-Israel Security Track

**[VERIFIED]** The security track will be launched at the Pentagon on May 29, with military delegations from both countries. To that end, the parties have agreed to a 45-day extension of the April 16 cessation of hostilities agreement to allow the security track to meaningfully improve the communication and coordination between Israel and Lebanon.

**[REPORTED]** This effectively means that Israel has decided to continue the "war against Hezbollah" while holding diplomatic talks with Beirut. "I hope that we will have the talks in the Pentagon with the Lebanese government and allow war against Hezbollah at the same time," said an intelligence official in Israel.

**Assessment:** Israel's dual-track approach—diplomacy with Lebanon's government, war against Hezbollah—creates tension with Iran's demand that any US-Iran deal must include Lebanon. This could become a deal-breaker if Iran insists on a comprehensive regional package.

### Emerging Signals

**IRGC attack pattern:** The Kuwait strike represents a shift from threats to action. IRGC explicitly named the attack as retaliation and warned "if repeated, our response will be more decisive." This establishes a new tit-for-tat dynamic that could accelerate.

**Deal structure becoming clearer:** The reported terms—60 days for Hormuz reopening, uranium transfer to third party, 30 days for mine removal—are increasingly specific. The White House denial yesterday was about the Iranian version's framing, not the fundamental framework.

**Israel's dual-track strategy explicit:** Israeli officials are now openly stating they intend to continue military operations against Hezbollah while negotiating with Lebanon's government. This creates a potential landmine for any comprehensive US-Iran deal that Iran expects to cover Lebanon.
