## Critical Developments (August 17)

### 1. CRITICAL: MOU Expires — No Extension, No Framework [VERIFIED]

The Memorandum of Understanding between the United States and Iran, designed to halt hostilities, is set to expire on Monday (August 17). The agreement, reached in June after high-level talks mediated by Pakistan, aimed to pause fighting, reopen the Strait of Hormuz, and lift a U.S. naval blockade of Iranian ports. However, both sides have accused each other of violating the terms, leading to little interest in extending the agreement.

But Iran has not discussed extending the ceasefire with the US because Tehran does not consider the 60-day agreement to have begun, a senior Iranian source told Reuters last week. The agreement began to unravel soon after it was signed, through a series of disputes over implementation and control of the Strait of Hormuz.

Trump deemed the MoU 'over' within a month of it being signed, as the two sides traded attacks despite a 'ceasefire.' While full-scale fighting has been paused for the time being, the war on Iran, which began on February 28, is far from resolved. The Islamabad-brokered MoU was supposed to kick-start a 60-day period for peace talks.

*Assessment: [VERIFIED] The MOU expiration is a formal milestone but functionally irrelevant — both sides abandoned the framework weeks ago. There is no replacement mechanism for negotiations, and direct US-Iran talks remain stalled.*

### 2. CRITICAL: Trump Threatens to Bomb Oman [OFFICIAL]

US President Donald Trump has threatened to bomb Oman if the Gulf country "gets in the way" of Washington's efforts to reach a peace deal with Iran, according to Fox News. Fox reporter Trey Yingst said he asked Trump about parallel talks between Iran and Oman over control of the Strait of Hormuz. According to Yingst, Trump responded: "If Oman gets in the way, we'll bomb the shit out of them."

President Donald Trump on Monday reportedly told Fox News the U.S. would attack Oman if it interferes with the U.S. naval blockade against Iran, marking a new threat against a Gulf State widely viewed as neutral and peaceful. Oman and Iran are in talks to manage shipping traffic through the Strait of Hormuz.

The prospects for peace in the Middle East suffered a fresh setback, with President Donald Trump reportedly saying he is in no hurry to end the war with Iran. Trump told Fox News in a phone interview that a US naval blockade on Iranian ports is putting pressure on the country and he has no timeline for resolving the conflict.

Trump threatened, referring to US efforts to enforce iron fist control over the Strait of Hormuz. Trump also claimed that the Iranians are "good poker players, but they're dying." Regarding US munitions used against Iran, Trump claimed that the weapons used so far "is peanuts."

*Assessment: [OFFICIAL] This is an extraordinary escalation — threatening to bomb a US partner nation that hosts US military facilities and has served as a neutral mediator. The threat appears directly aimed at preventing the Iran-Oman Hormuz arrangement from proceeding without US involvement.*

### 3. CRITICAL: Iran-Oman Reach "Understanding" on Hormuz Transit [OFFICIAL]

Iran and Oman have agreed to a route for ships transiting the Strait of Hormuz and are putting the final touches on arrangements for jointly managing the passage, Tehran's foreign ministry said. "The geographical coordinates of the route envisaged by the two sides have been agreed upon and, if certain third parties do not obstruct the process, the joint statement of the two countries, containing the main considerations and points of agreement, is also in the final review and drafting stage," foreign ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baqaei said.

Under the proposal, ships entering the Gulf would use a northern shipping lane through Iranian waters while vessels leaving the Gulf would travel through a southern lane in Omani waters coordinated with Iran.

But Iran says the United States must meet additional conditions before vessels can transit freely.

Iranian state television earlier played down the outcome of the Iran-Oman discussions, citing a person familiar with the matter as saying an agreement between Tehran and Muscat would not necessarily mean the strait would open immediately.

*Assessment: [OFFICIAL] The Iran-Oman agreement represents progress on a bilateral track that explicitly excludes the US. However, Trump's threat against Oman and Iran's insistence on additional US concessions mean the agreement cannot translate to reopened shipping without Washington's cooperation.*

### 5. SIGNIFICANT: Trump Confirms IRGC Backchannel — IRGC Denies [CONFLICTING]

The 60-day ceasefire deadline is expiring Monday, but President Donald Trump's resolve on ending Iran's nuclear weapons aspirations is not, he told Fox News on Monday morning, where he confirmed there is a backchannel for peace talks with the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps. "They should put up the white flag of surrender," Trump said in a phone interview, confirming reports of the IRGC backchannel talks that bypass mediators and Iranian political officials.

The person they tapped for the backchannel was Nechirvan Barzani, the president of the Kurdistan region in Iraq, who had something very few others do: the trust of both U.S. and IRGC leaders. So Trump administration officials did something unconventional — they went around Iran's negotiators and reached out directly to IRGC leadership. The episode, which was detailed by three sources with direct knowledge and has not been previously reported, shows how tricky it's been for the U.S. to negotiate with a country where Washington isn't sure who exactly is in charge.

Barzani asked Vahidi if he was aligned with the Iranian negotiators. The Iranian general said he was. "I fully support them and this is also the position of the IRGC. We prefer to solve this crisis through negotiations," Vahidi said, according to a source with direct knowledge.

Iranian officials did not reject the proposal outright but expressed concerns about their security, including fears that Israeli intelligence could target them in Iraqi Kurdistan or during their travel. The proposed Erbil meeting ultimately did not take place.

*Assessment: [REPORTED] The Axios reporting on the Barzani backchannel is credible (three sources with direct knowledge). The IRGC denial of current talks may be technically accurate (the Erbil meeting never happened) while Trump's confirmation reflects that the channel existed. The key takeaway: the IRGC signaled support for negotiations but direct talks never materialized due to Iranian security concerns.*
