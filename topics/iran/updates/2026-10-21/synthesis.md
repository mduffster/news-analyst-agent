## 1. Financial Times: Netanyahu Ordered October 10 Strikes — Trump Blocked Them [REPORTED/DISPUTED]

The Financial Times reported Wednesday that Netanyahu ordered the IDF to prepare a multi-day bombing campaign against Iran for the weekend of October 10, but the operation was shelved after Trump opposed restarting the war before the November 3 US midterms.

According to the report, Netanyahu ordered the IDF to prepare a multi-day bombing campaign for the weekend of October 10. The targets were to include Iranian nuclear facilities, power plants and other civilian infrastructure, as well as regime-linked sites. The operation was designed to be more intense than the joint U.S.-Israeli campaign launched in February.

One source said further attacks in the coming weeks could involve both countries. "There will either be a deal or an attack," the source said, referring to Washington's efforts to secure an agreement on Iran's nuclear program and control of the Strait of Hormuz.

**However, Israeli sources dispute this account.** Neither Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu nor Defense Minister Israel Katz, nor any other government official, had told the IDF to prepare to attack in the way that such orders were handed down leading up to the June 2025 and early 2026 conflicts with Iran. Sources have told the Post that the premise of the Financial Times report — that Trump blocked Israel from an attack on Iran which it was about to carry out — is wrong, because Israel was not about to carry out an attack. Sources have speculated that a leak of such a potential attack on Iran to the Financial Times could be designed to impact the Israeli election, with Netanyahu claiming that he was ready to attack Iran a third time, but was prevented by Trump.

**Analysis:** The FT-vs-Israel denial creates competing narratives, but the pattern is consistent: Netanyahu continues pushing for renewed strikes while Trump maintains the pre-election hold. The "deal or attack" framing from FT sources is the most consequential signal — it indicates the post-November 3 window is live for escalation regardless of which account is accurate.

## 2. Israeli Jets Flew to Iran's Border — US Suspects Provocation Attempt [REPORTED]

Dozens of Israeli fighter jets and refueling aircraft flew over Syria and Iraq toward the Iranian border earlier this week, following a flight path resembling an attack on Iran, Channel 12 reports, citing US officials.

The aircraft turned around shortly before reaching Iranian territory and returned to Israel. The flight took place more than 800 miles from Israel, a week before the Israeli elections and on the same day U.S. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth met Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu at Israel's Ovda Air Base.

Israel informed Washington of the sortie in advance, saying it was a military exercise and a show of force intended to deter Iran. However, several sources say they believe some Israeli officials may have hoped Iran would fire on the aircraft, providing Israel with justification to launch an attack.

The flight could have led to a miscalculation, prompted an Iranian response and reignited the war. U.S. officials said Israel knew Iran's air defenses would detect the aircraft.

**Analysis:** If US sources are correct that Israel hoped Iran would fire first, this suggests Netanyahu may be attempting to manufacture a *casus belli* that would force Trump's hand or remove US objections. The timing — six days before Israel's election — adds domestic political dimensions. This is the most alarming signal of intent since the conflict began.

## 3. Third Tanker Hit Near Hormuz; Mine Blockade Devastating Oil Throughput [VERIFIED]

An unknown projectile struck a tanker 11 nautical miles west of Oman's Bukha, which overlooks the Strait of Hormuz, the United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations said on Wednesday. UKMTO said a fire that erupted was extinguished and that the tanker was at anchor. It added that all crew were reported safe.

On Tuesday, October 20, the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) claimed that two tankers on the southern route of the Strait of Hormuz caught fire and came to a halt following the detonation of naval mines laid by its naval forces. The IRGC did not disclose the names of the tankers, their flags, or the status of the crews.

The mine blockade's economic impact is severe. Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps has established a mine blockade of the Strait of Hormuz that has cut daily tanker transits from 85 to 4, according to IMF PortWatch data for the week of 18 October. The IRGC claims three supertankers struck mines in the past week; Kpler shipping data puts the throughput decline at 86 per cent — from 20.8 million barrels per day on 8 October to 2.9 million by 11 October.

Vessels that switch off their AIS transponders — a SOLAS violation but a survival measure in a conflict zone — disappear from AIS-based tracking. The 86% collapse figure therefore reflects a combination of genuine transit cessation and a mass migration to AIS-dark operations.

**Analysis:** The three tanker attacks in 48 hours demonstrate sustained IRGC pressure. The 86% throughput decline is economically catastrophic for Gulf exporters and global energy markets, though some AIS-dark transits likely continue. October has become the busiest month for attacks on shipping since March.

## 4. Iran's Araghchi: Ready for Diplomacy — But US Must Lift Blockade First [OFFICIAL]

Iran is prepared to give diplomacy with the United States another chance if Washington demonstrates a sincere desire to build trust and reach a "just solution," Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said Wednesday. "If the US chooses war over diplomacy, it'll certainly result in an even greater strategic failure," Araghchi wrote on X, sharing an op-ed he authored for the Financial Times.

He wrote: "While Iran is firmly committed to seeking a dignified peaceful resolution to the conflict, we are also fully prepared to defend ourselves." He says the White House has shown no willingness to reach an understanding and that there was little reason to expect progress now.

Iran will not reopen the Strait of Hormuz until Washington accepts its terms. Indirect negotiations between Tehran and Washington continue through mediators Pakistan and Qatar. "Until an agreement or understanding is reached regarding this strait, it will not be open to everyone," he said.

Earlier, in an interview with Iraqi media, he urged the US to return to the Islamabad Memorandum of Understanding, which was signed in June before quickly breaking down. Under that deal, Iran agreed to re-open the Strait of Hormuz and the US was to lift the naval blockade, release frozen Iranian oil funds and remove sanctions. Since its collapse, Washington has ramped up economic pressure on Tehran and refocused on Iran's nuclear program, widening the gulf between the two sides.

**Analysis:** Combined with yesterday's Reuters revelation that Iran is deliberately prolonging talks to deny Trump a pre-election victory, Araghchi's op-ed is diplomatic theater. Tehran signals rhetorical openness while maintaining demands that make a breakthrough impossible before November 3.

## 5. France's Macron Makes Surprise "Solidarity" Visit to Saudi Arabia [OFFICIAL]

French President Emmanuel Macron landed in Riyadh in a surprise "solidarity" visit, making him the first Western leader to touch down in the kingdom since Yemen's Iran-backed Houthis intensified their attacks this month.

French President Emmanuel Macron landed in Riyadh on Wednesday, adding an unscheduled stop to his ongoing tour of the Gulf region. The president's office said the visit aimed to demonstrate "France's solidarity" with Saudi Arabia in the face of attacks launched by the Houthis against its territory.

The unannounced trip comes as Saudi Arabia agreed to supply extra diesel to France "for as many months as necessary", the Elysee Palace told the AFP news agency on Wednesday.

The diplomatic push follows days of rioting in France, fuelled by the cost of living, especially as the US-Iran war and Iran's blockade of the Strait of Hormuz have kept energy prices high.

**Analysis:** Macron's visit internationalizes support for Saudi Arabia beyond the UK's Typhoon deployment yesterday. France is now explicitly linking energy security to defense cooperation — the Hormuz crisis is driving Western powers closer to direct involvement in the Yemen theater.

## 6. Egypt's Parliament Vote on Yemen Intervention Postponed to October 26 [REPORTED]

Egypt's parliament was expected to vote this week on whether to authorise the armed forces to help defend Saudi Arabia against attacks by Yemen's Houthis if Riyadh requests its support. The vote has now been postponed until October 26.

The move would bring the Arab world's most populous country closer to direct military involvement in the widening conflicts unleashed by the U.S.-Israeli air assault on Iran in February. President Abdel Fattah al-Sisi requested the approval in a speech on Saturday, saying Egypt would "never, never forget our brothers in the Gulf" and telling Saudi Arabia's leaders that Cairo would "take any measure to protect you".

A deployment of Egyptian troops in Yemen itself is unlikely, military analysts say. More likely interventions might include air defence and naval support.

**Analysis:** Egypt's potential entry would mark a significant expansion of the anti-Houthi coalition. The postponed vote to October 26 — one day before Israel's election — could align with broader regional coordination decisions. Egypt's F-16s, Rafales, and naval assets would substantially augment Saudi air defense.

## 7. Israeli Election: Neither Bloc Reaches Majority, Stalemate Likely [VERIFIED]

Less than a week before the elections, the four latest Knesset seat polls painted an almost identical picture: Gadi Eisenkot and his Yashar party led Likud in every poll, but neither bloc comes close to a 61-seat majority.

The polling average put the Zionist opposition at 51.25 seats, compared with 51 for Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's bloc, a near-perfect tie that leaves the Arab parties and parties hovering around the electoral threshold in a pivotal position ahead of election day.

According to the poll, after Benny Gantz withdrew from the race, Eisenkot's Yashar party gained one seat, reaching 23. In contrast, Netanyahu's Likud lost one seat, falling to 19.

The Zionist anti-Netanyahu camp thus has 54 seats, with another potential 4 from the Reservists, for a total of 58. The pro-Netanyahu camp stands at 48. Arab parties Joint List and Ra'am have a total of 14.

**Analysis:** Neither bloc approaches 61 seats needed for government formation. A deadlock could extend coalition negotiations for weeks or months, potentially complicating US-Israel coordination on post-election Iran operations.
