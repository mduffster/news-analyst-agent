### **FINANCIAL TIMES: NETANYAHU PLANNED OCTOBER 10 STRIKES — TRUMP BLOCKED** [REPORTED]

On Tuesday, the *Financial Times* reported that Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu had directed his military to prepare for new attacks on Iran this month, before deciding to call off the operation. The IDF had planned for "a multi-day bombing campaign to take place on the week of October 10 that would have targeted civilian infrastructure such as electricity plants, as well as nuclear facilities and regime targets."

According to the FT, Netanyahu instructed the Israeli military to prepare a multi-day bombing campaign for the weekend of October 10, targeting Iranian nuclear facilities, power plants and other civilian infrastructure, as well as sites associated with the regime. The planned offensive was intended to surpass the intensity of the joint U.S.-Israeli military campaign launched in February.

According to the FT's sources, Netanyahu decided against the strikes because of the opposition of President Donald Trump, who wants to avoid reopening the war until after the U.S. midterm elections.

One person familiar with the discussions told the FT that another round of strikes in the coming weeks remained a realistic possibility, potentially involving both Israel and the United States.

**However, Israeli sources dispute this account.** Neither Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu nor Defense Minister Israel Katz, nor any other government official, had told the IDF to prepare to attack in the way that such orders were handed down leading up to the June 2025 and early 2026 conflicts with Iran. Sources have told the *Post* that the premise of the Financial Times report — that Trump blocked Israel from an attack on Iran which it was about to carry out — is wrong, because Israel was not about to carry out an attack.

**[ANALYSIS]** The FT-vs-Israel denial creates uncertainty, but the pattern is clear: Netanyahu is pushing for renewed strikes, and Trump is holding him back until after November 3. The "either a deal or an attack" framing from FT sources suggests the post-midterm window is live for escalation.

### **ISRAELI JETS FLEW TO IRAN BORDER — US SUSPECTS PROVOCATION ATTEMPT** [REPORTED]

Dozens of Israeli fighter jets and refueling aircraft flew over Syria and Iraq toward the Iranian border earlier this week, following a flight path resembling an attack on Iran, Channel 12 reports, citing US officials. The aircraft turned around shortly before reaching Iranian territory and returned to Israel.

Israel informed Washington of the sortie in advance, saying it was a military exercise and a show of force intended to deter Iran. However, several sources say they believe some Israeli officials may have hoped Iran would fire on the aircraft, providing Israel with justification to launch an attack.

Last week, Axios reported that Netanyahu and President Donald Trump discussed in early October the possibility of a major new operation against Iran before the Israeli and U.S. elections. Netanyahu told Trump he was concerned about Israeli intelligence indicating that Iran was taking troubling steps to reconstitute its nuclear program.

**[ANALYSIS]** This is deeply concerning. If Israel is actively probing to provoke an Iranian response — and if the US believes this — it suggests Netanyahu may be trying to create a *casus belli* that would force Trump's hand. The timing (6 days before Israeli election) adds domestic political dimensions.

### **THIRD TANKER HIT NEAR HORMUZ; MINE BLOCKADE CUTTING TRANSITS 86%** [VERIFIED]

An unknown projectile struck a tanker 11 nautical miles west of Oman's Bukha, which overlooks the Strait of Hormuz, the United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations said on Wednesday. UKMTO said a fire that erupted was extinguished and that the tanker was at anchor. All crew were reported safe.

Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps has established a mine blockade of the Strait of Hormuz that has cut daily tanker transits from 85 to 4, according to IMF PortWatch data for the week of 18 October. The IRGC claims three supertankers struck mines in the past week; Kpler shipping data puts the throughput decline at 86 per cent — from 20.8 million barrels per day on 8 October to 2.9 million by 11 October.

**[ANALYSIS]** The mine campaign continues to tighten the chokehold on Hormuz. An 86% throughput decline is economically catastrophic for Gulf exporters and global oil markets. Today's tanker strike follows yesterday's two tankers — the IRGC is maintaining sustained pressure.

### **ARAGHCHI FT OP-ED: IRAN READY FOR DIPLOMACY — BUT WON'T YIELD** [OFFICIAL]

Iran is prepared to give diplomacy with the United States another chance if Washington demonstrates a sincere desire to build trust and reach a "just solution," Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said Wednesday. "If the US chooses war over diplomacy, it'll certainly result in an even greater strategic failure," Araghchi wrote on X, sharing an op-ed he authored for the Financial Times.

"While Iran is firmly committed to seeking a dignified peaceful resolution to the ‌conflict, ‌we are ‌also fully ‌prepared to defend ourselves," he wrote. He said the ‌White House had shown no willingness to reach an understanding and that there was little reason to expect progress now.

Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian said Wednesday that threats and sanctions must be lifted in practice before conditions for negotiations can be established. In a post on X, Pezeshkian said it was impossible to intensify sanctions and pressure, close avenues of engagement and simultaneously call for negotiations. "Dialogue has no meaning in the language of threats and pressure," he wrote.

Iran will not reopen the Strait of Hormuz until Washington accepts its terms. Indirect negotiations between Tehran and Washington continue through mediators Pakistan and Qatar, though Iranian officials see little prospect of a diplomatic breakthrough.

**[ANALYSIS]** The Araghchi op-ed is classic diplomatic positioning: signal openness while maintaining demands. Combined with yesterday's Reuters revelation that Iran is deliberately delaying, this is theater — Tehran wants to look reasonable internationally while running out the clock to the midterms.
