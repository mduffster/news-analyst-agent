## September 28, 2026 — Day 213

### Key Developments (Last 72 Hours)

**TRUMP REJECTS IRAN'S 7-DAY PROPOSAL** [VERIFIED]

Donald Trump has rejected Iran's latest proposal to end the war and reopen the Strait of Hormuz. "I reject their proposal," Trump told reporters on Saturday, arguing that Iran wants an agreement because it is "losing so badly".

"They want to make a deal where they open the strait immediately because they are losing so badly. You don't see that in the fake news. We're winning tremendously, we have total control of the Hormuz Strait, massive amounts of oil are coming out of the Hormuz Strait, last night we had 29 ships come out."

Trump told Axios on Sunday that Iran wants an agreement, but not on terms acceptable to Washington. He said Tehran had "overplayed their hand" and described its proposal as something the United States might have accepted a year ago.

**TRUMP SIGNALS POST-MIDTERM MILITARY ACTION** [REPORTED]

The Wall Street Journal reported Friday that Trump has turned down the seven-day truce offer from Iran and told aides he expects to launch new strikes once November's midterm elections are finished.

US officials told Tehran and the mediators there is no plan to lift the blockade, calculating that continued economic strain will eventually force Iran into an agreement on Washington's terms. The report suggested that Trump has cycled between diplomatic outreach, military action and sanctions pressure throughout the seven-month war, and voiced doubt internally that Tehran will agree to dismantle its nuclear program.

The officials say that Trump is reluctant to resume major combat operations, with the scope of the envisioned strikes unclear, while noting his views could change and that he could also end up being swayed by the midterm results.

**TALKS TO RESUME DESPITE REJECTION** [VERIFIED]

Mediators are still working with Iran and the United States on brokering a deal to end the fighting and open the Strait of Hormuz — even after Trump publicly rejected Iran's latest proposal. The indirect talks were expected to resume on Monday and come as the conflict that's caused global economic damage and badly battered Iran enters its eighth month.

Qatari mediators are likely to hold separate talks with Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araqchi in New York and with the US side on Monday or Tuesday. The talks are expected to focus on an amended version of a seven-day proposal that Iran presented last week on the sidelines of the United Nations General Assembly.

Two officials confirmed that mediators are working with both sides on a proposal that would include reopening the strait and lifting the U.S. blockade, and then removing sanctions. According to the draft text being discussed, the strait would reopen in accordance with a separate deal that Iran has been discussing with Oman, and it would include no tolls — something Tehran has pursued.

**IRAN MAINTAINS CONDITIONS, EXPRESSES DISTRUST** [OFFICIAL]

Iran and the US appeared far apart on a new ceasefire deal or the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz, with Tehran saying it's sticking to a proposal US President Donald Trump has rejected.

President Masoud Pezeshkian told Al Jazeera that Iran no longer trusts negotiations with the United States after previous rounds of talks were followed by attacks and sanctions. "We have no trust in the American side and do not know on what basis an understanding can be reached with it."

Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said in an interview with NBC News that Tehran was prepared for further military confrontation. "We are fully prepared for the war to be resumed. We stand firm in the face of any new aggression, even if it comes to a doomsday war, we stand ready for diplomacy. It is up to President Trump to choose."

### RAF Fairford Terror Plot — New Development

**FIVE ARRESTED NEAR US-OPERATED UK BASE** [VERIFIED]

Five young British men arrested on explosives and terror charges near a U.S.-operated air force base in England were released on bail Monday, and counterterror detectives said they were investigating "multiple lines of inquiry," including whether there is a link to Iran. The men were detained early Sunday after three vans were spotted in the countryside near RAF Fairford.

Investigators are examining whether the suspected plot is linked to Iran. RAF Fairford has played a role in U.S. military operations during the war with Iran — American B-1 and B-52 bombers have operated from the base, including for strikes on Iranian missile sites. Iran's Revolutionary Guards warned Britain in July against allowing U.S. bombers to operate from Fairford, saying bases used for attacks on Iranian territory could become legitimate targets.

Iran on Monday denied any involvement in the suspected terrorist plot.

**[ANALYSIS]** If confirmed as Iran-linked, this would represent a significant escalation — Tehran bringing the war to European soil. However, investigators have not confirmed any connection; Russia and non-state groups are also being examined. The incident underscores the global reach implications of the conflict.

### Bab al-Mandab / Yemen Situation

The Bab al-Mandab Strait crisis escalated in September as part of the wider Yemeni offensives tied to the 2026 Iran war. Houthi fighters seized the historic Red Sea port city of Mokha, pushing further along the Yemeni coast toward the strait, with Houthi troops moving onto the Hanish Islands in the middle of the Red Sea.

By mid-September, the Houthis' rapid advance had brought them to within about 20 miles of the US military base in Djibouti. As of mid-September 2026, Houthi forces control Yemen's entire Red Sea coastline, Perim Island, and the Hanish Islands, giving them strategic control over the Bab al-Mandab Strait.

Fighting intensified in September as the Houthis launched a lightning offensive that saw them seize Yemen's entire Red Sea coast, including the Bab al-Mandab Strait. The Houthis have in parallel launched a blockade on Saudi energy exports. Despite Saudi backing, Yemen's government forces have been unable to repel the latest Houthi offensive.

A Saudi airstrike on Taiz in southwest Yemen killed seven people and wounded dozens more, Houthi media reported on Sunday. The strike killed seven and wounded 40 in a central marketplace in Taiz.

### Assessment

**[ANALYSIS]** The picture that emerged over the weekend is clearer but darker than the diplomatic optimism of September 25. Three key developments:

**1. The Pattern Repeats:** Iran's 7-day proposal strongly resembled the June MoU that collapsed. Trump rejected it for the same reasons: it would require upfront US concessions (blockade lifted, sanctions waived) before Iran acts. The WSJ report that Trump told aides he plans to strike after the midterms — while maintaining a quiet phase through the election — suggests Washington sees no path to a deal Tehran would accept and is preparing for renewed military pressure.

**2. The Midterm Pause:** The war remains in a quiet phase even as the U.S. midterm-election campaign enters its most contested period. Last week, President Donald Trump said he didn't expect a peace agreement until after the elections, scheduled for Nov. 3. Over the weekend there were fresh indications that Mr. Trump was holding out the possibility of ordering a new series of strikes after the midterms. The result is a clear tie between American foreign policy and the domestic arena.

**3. Asymmetric Expansion:** The RAF Fairford incident — if ultimately linked to Iran — would represent a qualitative change in the conflict. Tehran has previously warned that UK bases used for US strikes would become legitimate targets. Whether this was an IRGC-directed operation, a proxy effort, or unrelated remains under investigation.

The amended proposal mediators are reportedly working on — dropping Iran's toll demands, using the Iran-Oman bilateral framework — may narrow the gap, but the fundamental sequencing problem remains: Iran won't reopen Hormuz without US action first; Trump won't lift the blockade without Iranian action first.
