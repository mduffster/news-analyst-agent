# US-Israel-Iran Conflict: Trump Rejects Iran's Proposal, Signals Post-Midterm Strikes

## September 28, 2026

**Assessment:** The diplomatic window that appeared to open at UNGA has sharply narrowed. President Trump rejected Iran's seven-day Hormuz proposal over the weekend, told Axios that Tehran had "overplayed their hand," and signaled willingness to resume strikes after the November 3 midterms. Talks through Qatari mediators continue Monday despite the public rebuff, working on an amended proposal—but the fundamental sequencing impasse persists. Meanwhile, a foiled terror plot near RAF Fairford, where US bombers conduct Iran operations, is under investigation for possible Iranian state involvement. The Strait of Hormuz remains effectively closed to commercial shipping, while oil prices rebounded above $106/barrel on Monday following Trump's rejection. Iran's parliament is advancing NPT withdrawal legislation, creating a potential escalation path on the nuclear track independent of diplomatic negotiations.

---

## Trump Rejects Iran's Seven-Day Proposal, Expects More Talks

President Trump said Saturday he had rejected Iran's latest proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz and restart talks aimed at reaching a deal on the country's nuclear program. [VERIFIED]

"I expect more talks with Iran [this week]. They want to make a deal, but it is not the deal that I want to make. It is what we would have maybe agreed to a year ago. They overplayed their hand," Trump said. [OFFICIAL] Asked if he is considering resuming strikes against Iran, Trump said: "I am always thinking about it."

Trump asserted that US naval operations have restored significant oil flows through the strait. Trump said the US military is facilitating a lot of oil out of the Strait of Hormuz, claiming that over the weekend the largest amount of oil transited through the strait since the war began—"More than 20 million barrels." A US defense official confirmed Trump's assertion, saying that on Friday night 22 million barrels of oil were exported through the strait. [OFFICIAL]

**Critical assessment:** The 22-million-barrel figure, if accurate, would represent a dramatic single-day surge—roughly equivalent to pre-war daily flows through the entire strait. However, independent tracking data tells a starkly different story. The Strait of Hormuz remains effectively closed to commercial traffic, with IMF PortWatch recording 1 transit on September 20 against a pre-crisis baseline of 85 per day. [REPORTED] This 77% decline in transits since 2025 suggests the claimed oil volume may reflect US-escorted convoy operations rather than normalized commercial shipping. The discrepancy warrants scrutiny: escorted bulk transits of pre-positioned tankers differ fundamentally from restored maritime commerce.

## Talks Continue Despite Public Rejection

Despite Trump's rejection, diplomatic channels remain active. Two regional sources confirmed Trump's comment about more talks this week. They said they expect another round of indirect dialogue between the US and Iran as early as Monday. But it isn't clear if the gaps can be bridged. [REPORTED]

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi and Iran's negotiating team are expected to meet Qatari mediators in New York on Monday to discuss the latest proposals and views on the current state of talks with the United States, IRNA reported. [OFFICIAL] Over the weekend, the Qatari mediators have been going back and forth separately with Iranian and US negotiators on changes to the text. Qatari mediators are expected to meet separately with Araghchi and Witkoff as early as Monday.

Iran maintains its conditions while signaling continued diplomatic openness. Araghchi told NBC on Sunday that despite Trump's rejection of the Iranian proposal, Iran still wants a diplomatic solution. He said that the midterm elections aren't part of Iran's calculations. "We only care about our national interests. We are ready for negotiations," he said. [OFFICIAL]

Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian told CBS News in an interview that despite an "atmosphere of distrust," Iran would allow UN nuclear inspectors into the country as part of a potential long-term ceasefire deal. [OFFICIAL] This represents a potentially significant concession, though it remains conditional on broader agreement.

## Post-Midterm Strikes Under Consideration

The Wall Street Journal reported that Trump is planning military action after November elections. The Wall Street Journal said Trump is weighing more air strikes after the Nov. 3 midterms amid high fuel prices—that is a report of options, not an announced campaign. [REPORTED]

President Trump on Sunday said he hasn't ruled out more military strikes on Iran before the midterm elections in November. [OFFICIAL] Trump has said Iran will make a deal "right after the election" because "they're waiting to see how I do in the midterm election."

President Donald Trump said on Monday the United States would win its war with Iran "very soon", predicting that gas prices would tumble once the conflict ends. [OFFICIAL] Trump said talks with Iran are due this week and the war will end soon, signaling an effort to cool surging U.S. oil prices before the midterms.

**Analysis:** Trump's messaging reveals competing pressures. He is simultaneously talking down oil prices by projecting imminent resolution while maintaining leverage through strike threats. The combination of rejected proposals and post-midterm strike planning suggests the administration sees prolonged coercive pressure—not rapid de-escalation—as the path to terms it finds acceptable.

## RAF Fairford Terror Plot: Investigation Probes Iran Link

A foiled plot near a key US airbase has injected a new dimension into the conflict. Five young British men arrested on explosives and terror charges near a U.S.-operated air force base in England were released on bail Monday, and counterterror detectives said they were investigating "multiple lines of inquiry," including whether there is a link to Iran. [VERIFIED]

A resident of the nearby village of Whelford, Gloucestershire, told the Press Association she saw three white vans "blocking the road" and a "large group of hooded" men wearing balaclavas, prompting her to alert police around 12:45 a.m. Sunday. Police declared a major incident, and armed officers arrested the men, ages 23 to 25, on suspicion of explosives offenses and preparing a terrorist act. [REPORTED]

Laurence Taylor, head of Counter Terrorism Policing, confirmed that investigations into the five men are ongoing, "including that this may be actively committed by proxies or individuals either knowingly or unknowingly working on behalf of a foreign state." [OFFICIAL] Investigators haven't concluded whether the incident was state-backed. They are investigating a possible link to Iran, Russia or non-state militant groups.

The base's significance is clear. Since March, RAF Fairford has been used by the US military to launch air strikes on Iran as the war in the Middle East rumbles on. [VERIFIED] In July, Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps specifically noted Fairford's use by US bombers, warning the UK that "any base used for aggression against Iranian territory constitutes a legitimate target for our forces."

Iran earlier denied any involvement in the incident. Its embassy in London issued a statement Monday saying it "strongly condemns the recent unfounded and malicious speculations published by some British individuals and media outlets attempting to link Iran to arrest of five suspects in the vicinity of RAF Fairford base." [OFFICIAL]

**Second plot emerges:** British authorities are investigating two plots involving mostly Iranian nationals in the United Kingdom that appear at this stage of the investigation likely to be state-sponsored, according to a senior official briefed on the threat. Eight people have been arrested in London and in other parts of the UK over the weekend. Seven of them are Iranian nationals. [REPORTED] Officials said the two plots were separate, but the senior official briefed on the threat said British law enforcement is now trying to determine whether the same entity was directing both plots.

**Significance:** If an Iran link is confirmed in either plot, it would represent Tehran bringing asymmetric warfare to European soil—a qualitative escalation with implications for allied support of US operations.

## Iran's NPT Withdrawal Legislation Advances

A significant development on the nuclear track: Iran's parliament is considering a "triple-urgency" bill to withdraw from the nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty, First Deputy Speaker Ali Nikzad said Monday. Nikzad said lawmakers "do not trust" the UN nuclear watchdog or its director general, Rafael Grossi, accusing the agency chief of carrying out superficial inspections and issuing negative reports on Iran. He added that lawmakers would examine the bill while taking its "international implications" into account. [OFFICIAL]

A total of 130 Iranian lawmakers have signed a statement calling on parliament to initiate the legal process for the country's withdrawal from the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty (NPT), arguing that international mechanisms have failed to protect Iran's rights, security and peaceful nuclear infrastructure. [REPORTED]

On Sunday, as President Masoud Pezeshkian flew to New York for the United Nations General Assembly, a coalition of lawmakers filed an emergency bill demanding that Iran withdraw from the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty immediately, and calling, in the most explicit language put into formal legislation yet, for nuclear weapons tests to begin as soon as possible. [REPORTED]

**Analysis:** The NPT withdrawal bill—filed as Pezeshkian was signaling diplomatic openness in New York—illustrates the internal fissures in Tehran. Hardliners are positioning to constrain future diplomatic flexibility regardless of what the Pezeshkian government negotiates. An actual NPT withdrawal would dramatically escalate the nuclear crisis and could trigger Israeli or US preemptive action.

## Oil Markets React to Stalemate

Brent rose to 106.89 USD/Bbl on September 28, 2026, up 2.46% from the previous day. Over the past month, Brent's price has risen 18.12%, and is up 59.32% compared to the same time last year. [VERIFIED]

Brent crude climbed above $106 per barrel on Monday, recovering losses from the previous session after President Donald Trump rejected Iran's latest proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, raising concerns that the restoration of oil flows through the critical waterway could face further delays. Trump also said Tehran had overplayed its hand and expects negotiations to resume this week. Meanwhile, Iran said it is waiting for a definitive US response to its seven-day proposal.

Crude Oil rose to 95.11 USD/Bbl (WTI) on September 28, 2026, up 2.92% from the previous day. [VERIFIED] As of 11 a.m. Eastern Time today, oil sold for $104.46 per barrel (using Brent as the benchmark). That's $1.71 higher than the prior business day and approximately a $37.13 rise over the past year.

**Market interpretation:** Friday's 2% decline on diplomatic optimism has reversed. The pattern reflects a market pricing in prolonged uncertainty rather than imminent resolution.

## Yemen/Bab al-Mandab: Dual Chokepoint Crisis Structural

The Houthi position at Bab al-Mandab remains entrenched. By mid-September, the Houthis' rapid advance had brought them to within about 20 miles of the US military base in Djibouti. As of mid-September 2026, Houthi forces control Yemen's entire Red Sea coastline, Perim Island, and the Hanish Islands, giving them strategic control over the Bab al-Mandab Strait. [VERIFIED]

The IDF and Houthi authorities confirmed the death of the Houthi prime minister and several cabinet members during a September 28 Israeli strike in Sanaa. Prime Minister Netanyahu asserted: "The IDF eliminated most of the Houthi government and additional senior military officials… We did what nobody else has done before us." [VERIFIED]

**Note:** This September 28 date in the source appears to reference the August 28, 2025 Israeli strike that killed Prime Minister Ahmed al-Rahawi, not a new strike today. Independent verification shows the last Israeli strike on Yemen, on August 28, killed the prime minister of the Houthi government and at least 11 other ministers.

Iran's seven-day proposal notably omits Yemen, leaving the Bab al-Mandab chokepoint outside any prospective Hormuz deal—a structural gap that could undermine any agreement.

## Primary Outcome Assessment

**Horizon: Through November 23, 2026 (60 days from September 24)**

| Scenario | Probability | Change from Sep 25 | Reasoning |
|----------|-------------|---------------------|-----------|
| **Continuation at roughly current intensity** | 40% | -3% | Trump rejection combined with post-midterm strike signals reduces baseline stability; forces more probability into widening/de-escalation tails |
| **Sustained widening beyond current baseline** | 38% | +5% | Post-midterm strikes likely per WSJ; RAF Fairford plots suggest asymmetric escalation potential; NPT withdrawal legislation creates nuclear escalation pathway |
| **Durable ceasefire or sustained de-escalation** | 14% | -2% | Proposal rejected; sequencing impasse unchanged; Pezeshkian's inspection offer insufficient without matching US concessions |
| **Major ground operations or catastrophic escalation** | 8% | — | No change; neither side signaling ground war intent; costs remain prohibitive |

**What shifted:** The diplomatic optimism from September 25 has reversed. Three factors drive the adjustment:

1. **Trump's explicit rejection** of Iran's terms, combined with statements that Tehran "overplayed their hand" and offered a deal acceptable "a year ago," signals no near-term willingness to meet Iran's preconditions.

2. **Post-midterm strike planning** reported by WSJ creates a defined timeline for potential escalation after November 3. The administration appears to view the period through the midterms as a quiet phase before renewed pressure.

3. **Asymmetric escalation signals** from the RAF Fairford plot and NPT withdrawal legislation suggest escalation pathways independent of the diplomatic track—Iran potentially bringing the war to Europe; hardliners constraining Pezeshkian's flexibility.

Talks continuing through Qatari mediators keep a narrow diplomatic path open. The amended proposal reportedly drops Iran's toll demands and uses the Iran-Oman bilateral framework. But without US willingness to sequence concessions before Hormuz reopens, structural gaps persist.
