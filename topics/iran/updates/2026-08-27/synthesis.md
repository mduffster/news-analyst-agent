## Critical Developments (August 27)

### 1. IRGC Announces Revenue-Sharing/Tolls — MAJOR ESCALATION RISK [OFFICIAL/DISPUTED]

The Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps stated that Iran and Oman have agreed each country's share of the Strait of Hormuz and of its revenues. IRGC spokesman Hossein Mohebbi stated through the Sepah News agency on 26 August that agreements had been reached on the two states' shares of the strait's waters and on their shares of its revenues.

The geographic split described — inbound traffic entering through Iranian waters under Iranian coordination, outbound traffic leaving through Omani waters under Omani management — is the basis on which the revenue is to be divided. The joint statement issued by the two foreign ministries on 25 August did not mention fees or revenue. The announcement came from the Guard Corps rather than from either foreign ministry, and it converts a navigation arrangement into a toll regime.

[REPORTED] A senior Iranian source told Reuters yesterday that "An agreement with Oman over the Strait of Hormuz has not been finalised," adding that the talks were continuing on the details of an accord. That seemed to contradict a statement by Brigadier General Hossein Mohebbi, a spokesperson for the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps, who said Iran and Oman had "reached results that are acceptable to both sides" on controlling the strait and its revenues.

**Trump's position:** Trump rejected the idea of a permanent fee to pass through the critical waterway. "We want it open; we want it free," Trump told reporters. "We don't want tolls."

**Assessment:** [DISPUTED] The divergence between IRGC statements and foreign ministry positions suggests either internal Iranian disagreement or deliberate ambiguity. The toll regime violates international maritime law — "There is no international agreement where tolls can be introduced for transiting international straits. Any such toll will set a dangerous precedent," a spokesperson for the U.N.'s International Maritime Organization told Reuters. This creates a fundamental barrier to any deal framework the US might accept.

### 2. Rezaei: Iran Preparing "List of Conditions" for Hormuz Reopening [OFFICIAL]

Iran is preparing a list of conditions for reopening the Strait of Hormuz after mediators asked Tehran to set them out. Rezaei said Iran had agreed with Oman on a shipping corridor through the strait, with parts of the route in Omani waters and parts in Iranian waters. Ships would use a designated central channel if the U.S. met Iran's conditions, he said through an interpreter in an Al Manar TV interview.

"The US must take practical steps to meet Iran's conditions before Tehran reopens the Strait of Hormuz," Iran's semi-official Tasnim News Agency quoted Rezaei as saying. "The US must first take practical measures toward fulfilling Iran's conditions, after which Iran will move to reopen the Strait of Hormuz."

Rezaei outlined Iran's terms for reopening the strait, specifying an end to U.S. military actions, the release of frozen Iranian assets, and the cessation of conflicts elsewhere in the region, including Lebanon and Gaza.

Mohsen Rezaei stated that the Strait of Hormuz will remain closed until the United States changes its behavior and accepts Iran's conditions. However, he noted that any potential transit agreement between Iran and Oman regarding navigation through the strait would remain separate from the broader closure of the waterway.

**Assessment:** [OFFICIAL] Rezaei's conditions are sweeping — end the war, lift blockade/sanctions, release frozen assets, region-wide ceasefire including Gaza and Lebanon. None of these conditions are close to being met. This suggests the Iran-Oman corridor framework is more about Iran-Oman bilateral arrangements than an actual path to reopening for international commerce.

### 3. Second Tanker Attacked This Week — Violence Continues [VERIFIED]

A tanker traveling through the Strait of Hormuz has been reportedly struck by an "unknown projectile," sparking a fire onboard.

On Monday, another tanker was attacked while traveling through the strait off the coast of Oman. In similar fashion, an "unknown projectile" purportedly struck the ship, disabling it and causing the vessel to drift. All crew were reported as safe, though tug assistance was required.

The JMIC statement outlined recent activity by the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps, citing "attacks, attempted attacks, and/or harassment activity," such as drone "overflight, targeted surveillance of merchant shipping, and occasional VHF hailing." "These actions continue to demonstrate Iran's intent to assert presence along key transit lanes and maintain pressure on transiting vessels."

**Assessment:** [VERIFIED] Two tanker attacks in one week undercuts both Trump's claims that Hormuz is open and the Iran-Oman framework's viability. IRGC harassment operations continue regardless of diplomatic progress.

### 4. Qatar PM in Tehran — Highest-Level Mediation Since June [OFFICIAL]

Qatar has served as a back-channel negotiator for the warring parties and played a direct role in securing a June ceasefire that briefly led to a cessation of hostilities. The visit comes as the war nears its sixth month with fighting largely paused but no diplomatic breakthrough in sight. Both sides are at odds over control of the Strait of Hormuz, a chokepoint for global oil supplies that Tehran has used as leverage.

Qatar's Prime Minister Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al Thani held talks in Tehran with Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araqchi. The two discussed a proposed phased framework that includes establishing a temporary joint Iranian-Omani shipping corridor through the strait and carrying out a joint project to clear mines from the waterway, Qatar's foreign ministry said.

Tehran has said the strait will remain shut unless the US meets Iranian conditions agreed under the June interim ceasefire. Those conditions include an end to the US blockade on Iranian ports, compensation and removal of sanctions.

The White House said there were currently no negotiations happening between the United States and Iran as Tehran had not yet "come to the table in a meaningful way." Iran has repeatedly said it has never left the negotiating table.

**Assessment:** [OFFICIAL] Qatar's re-engagement at the prime ministerial level represents the highest-level mediation since June. Qatar's track record in securing the June MoU gives this diplomatic push credibility. However, the White House's denial of any active negotiations and Rezaei's sweeping conditions suggest the gap remains wide.

### 5. Oil Prices Stabilize After Three-Day Slide [VERIFIED]

By 7:15 a.m. Eastern Time today, oil had reached $89.68 per barrel, measured using the Brent benchmark. That's $2.27 more than it cost yesterday morning.

Brent fell to 86.93 USD/Bbl on August 27, 2026, down 1.03% from the previous day. Over the past month, Brent's price has risen 5.91%, and is up 27.88% compared to the same time last year.

President Donald Trump said 10 million barrels of oil had passed through Hormuz on Tuesday, while reiterating claims that mines in the waterway had been cleared. Oil prices have also come under pressure this week after fresh US economic sanctions on Iran proved less aggressive than markets had feared, with the White House so far sparing Iran's trading partners from tougher measures.

Brent is at $87.65 a barrel and West Texas Intermediate at $82.90, recovering modestly from Tuesday's settlement. Two sessions have taken more than seven per cent off crude since last Friday's close.

**Assessment:** [VERIFIED] Oil is stabilizing in the $87-90 range after the dramatic three-day slide from ~$94. The market has absorbed the Bessent sanctions disappointment and Iran-Oman corridor news. The tolls announcement and tanker attack are mildly bullish. The EIA forecasts Brent crude oil spot price to average around $85 per barrel in the third quarter of 2026.

### 6. Iran Domestic Fuel Crisis Deepens — Strategic Reserves at "Red Alert" [VERIFIED]

Long queues have formed at petrol stations across Tehran, with several closing or experiencing disruptions, particularly in eastern and southern areas. Panic buying is driving a surge in demand, with distribution running approximately 30 per cent higher than usual amid fears of tightening supplies. Similar fuel shortages have spread beyond the capital, with disruptions reported in Kerman and Mashhad. New sweeping US sanctions are intensifying pressure on Iran's energy infrastructure.

Iran produces approximately 120 million liters of gasoline per day, while daily consumption reaches around 135 million liters. The shortfall therefore stands at 15 million liters per day.

The Iranian government is reportedly considering three different options for dealing with the crisis: limiting available gasoline to the level of domestic production; reducing subsidized quotas and selling additional quantities at market prices; transferring fuel quotas from vehicles to individual citizens. Fear that an increase in prices could trigger new protests has pushed the government to consume the strategic reserves in order to buy time.

Gasoline remains heavily subsidised and among the cheapest in the world, a politically sensitive issue that has previously sparked unrest. Queues persisted into Wednesday following US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent's announcement of expanded secondary sanctions aimed at further isolating Iran's economy.

The rush comes as Donald Trump's administration outlined a new campaign of what it called "economic warfare" against Tehran, which the Energy Optimisation Organisation — an Iranian state body tasked with managing fuel resources — warned was facing a "critical" shortfall of petrol.

**Assessment:** [VERIFIED] Iran's domestic fuel crisis is becoming critical. The blockade is biting hard — Iran, a major oil producer, is running out of refined fuel for its own population. This pressure may explain Iran's urgency in the Oman negotiations but also raises internal stability concerns that could affect decision-making. Previous fuel price increases in 2019 sparked major unrest.

### 7. Strait Traffic Remains Near Zero [VERIFIED]

Immediate effects on shipping levels in the strait are unlikely. The latest update from ship tracking service Kpler shows that transits "remain subdued," with just five crossings on Tuesday, all of them through Iran's preferred route.

Strait traffic has been less than a tenth of prewar levels for weeks, while Trump continues to tout the US blockade and his administration promises new economic sanctions to cut off Iran from the world.

Eight vessels completed the Strait of Hormuz crossing in the 24-hour window from 21:00 UTC 25 August to 21:00 UTC 26 August — five outbound, three inbound.

The U.N.'s International Maritime Organization has reported 70 incidents in the Strait of Hormuz since February 28 that have killed 19 seafarers.

**Assessment:** [VERIFIED] Despite the Iran-Oman framework and diplomatic momentum, actual shipping traffic remains at ~5-8% of normal levels (~5-8 ships/day vs. ~85/day prewar). The attacks on tankers this week demonstrate why commercial shipping remains reluctant regardless of political agreements.

### 8. Bessent Bank Sanctions — STILL PENDING [OFFICIAL]

If it fails to do so, the U.S. will impose secondary sanctions. He said that a major financial institution will be sanctioned by the end of this week because it's conducting business with Iran.

He wouldn't spell out a timeline of when Iran's biggest trade partners — including China — could face secondary sanctions. "Why would I want to blow up the global financial system?" he said.

Bessent declined to say whether the US is willing to target Chinese banks with sanctions — a move that could jeopardize delicate relations between China and the Trump administration. China is Iran's main trading partner.

**Assessment:** [OFFICIAL] No announcement yet. With Friday approaching (tomorrow), this remains the key near-term escalation marker. Bessent's reluctance to "blow up the global financial system" suggests he will choose a smaller target rather than a major Chinese state bank, preserving the Xi visit.

### 9. Trump: "Not in a Hurry" to End War [OFFICIAL]

Trump told Al Jazeera he has "no time schedule" for when he expects Iran to return to the negotiating table. "I have no time schedule, none. I'm not in a hurry. I have no time schedule at all."

"We are winning very big. They've got massive inflation, their economy is falling apart, they're treating their people very badly, they are killing many, many protesters."

White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt told Fox News and NewsNation on Thursday morning that the administration remains focused on upping the economic pressure on Iran. "They are in a devastating place. Inflation is over 100%. They can't pay their police."

**Assessment:** [OFFICIAL] The administration's public posture is patience and maximum economic pressure rather than urgency to deal. This suggests the economic warfare approach will continue through at least the midterms.

## Emerging Signals

**IRGC-Government Split?** The IRGC announced revenue-sharing while foreign ministries stayed silent on tolls. This could indicate:
- IRGC testing US/regional reaction before official adoption
- Internal disagreement between hardliners and pragmatists
- Deliberate ambiguity to preserve negotiating room

**Toll Regime as Fundamental Barrier:** The international legal consensus is that tolls on international straits are illegal. Trump rejected the concept when he floated it himself in April. Iran's assertion of tolls — even if not immediately implemented — creates an unbridgeable gap with US positions.

**Iran's Domestic Clock:** The fuel crisis adds urgency to Iranian decision-making that wasn't present a week ago. This could accelerate either:
- **Escalation** (regime preservation through external conflict) 
- **Deal-making** (regime preservation through economic relief)

**Qatar's Return:** The PM-level engagement is the highest since June. Qatar's track record gives them credibility — but the conditions gap may be too wide to bridge.

**Maritime Attack Pattern:** Two tanker attacks in one week strongly signals coercive negotiation environment: attacks/harassment help Iran enforce leverage while codifying reopening conditions.
