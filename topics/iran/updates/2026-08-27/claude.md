## Key Developments Since Prior Report (August 26)

### 1. IRGC Announces Iran-Oman Revenue-Sharing Deal — ESCALATION POTENTIAL

**[OFFICIAL]** "Agreements have been reached regarding each country's share of the strait's waters as well as Iran and Oman's share of its revenues," the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps said on Wednesday, according to the state-run Sepah News agency.

**[VERIFIED]** The comments by the IRGC go further than a joint statement issued by the two countries' foreign ministries on Tuesday, which said they discussed an "interim framework" for resuming ship transits but stopped short of announcing a final deal and didn't mention fees.

**[OFFICIAL]** Iran went further, claiming the sides have reached a revenue-sharing agreement on future tolls. The developments could be new flashpoints with President Trump, who made threats in response to the ongoing talks, including a pledge to bomb Oman if that country "gets in the way."

**[REPORTED]** A senior Iranian source told Reuters yesterday that an "agreement with Oman over the Strait of Hormuz has not been finalized," after Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps said the countries had agreed on how to share the waterway and its revenues. The source added that talks were continuing on the details of an accord.

**[VERIFIED]** However, the deal will not result in the immediate reopening of the Strait of Hormuz. The IRGC said that the US must comply with Iran's conditions first.

*Assessment: The IRGC's announcement of revenue-sharing/tolls significantly escalates the diplomatic situation. The foreign ministries' joint statement didn't mention fees, but the IRGC is asserting one. This toll regime violates international maritime law and directly contradicts Trump's position. The divergence between IRGC statements and foreign ministry positions suggests internal Iranian disagreement or deliberate ambiguity.*

### 2. Tanker Attacked in Strait of Hormuz — SECOND ATTACK THIS WEEK

**[VERIFIED]** The ship was "hit by an unknown projectile causing fire to the vessel which has since been extinguished," the UKMTO said in its alert. There were no reported casualties or environmental damage, the UKMTO said.

**[VERIFIED]** On Monday, another tanker was attacked while traveling through the strait off the coast of Oman, the UKMTO reported on behalf of the JMIC. In similar fashion, an "unknown projectile" purportedly struck the ship, disabling it and causing the vessel to drift. All crew were reported as safe, though tug assistance was required.

**[REPORTED]** CNBC reports: "Tanker attack tests Trump's claim that Hormuz is a 'functioning strait'."

*Assessment: Two tanker attacks in one week undercuts both Trump's claims that Hormuz is open and the Iran-Oman framework's viability. IRGC continues harassment operations regardless of diplomatic progress.*

### 3. Qatar PM Visits Tehran — HIGH-LEVEL MEDIATION RESUMES

**[VERIFIED]** Qatar's Prime Minister Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al Thani met Iran's foreign minister in Tehran as Doha seeks to revive diplomacy after the US vowed to intensify economic pressure on Iran. The talks focused on reducing escalation and a joint project to clear mines from the Strait of Hormuz.

**[OFFICIAL]** Sheikh Mohammed, who also serves as Qatar's foreign minister, held talks with Iran's Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi in the first high-level Qatari visit to Tehran since the war began on 28 February. The talks focused on efforts to reduce tensions and create conditions for renewed dialogue between Iran and the US, according to Qatar's state news agency QNA.

**[VERIFIED]** Qatar, a Gulf neighbour of Iran and a US ally, has served as a back-channel negotiator for the warring parties and played a direct role in securing the June ceasefire that briefly led to a cessation of hostilities. The visit comes as the war nears its sixth month with fighting largely paused but no diplomatic breakthrough in sight.

*Assessment: Qatar's re-engagement at the prime ministerial level represents the highest-level mediation since Pakistan's efforts. Qatar's track record in securing the June MoU gives this diplomatic push credibility.*

### 6. Iran Domestic Fuel Crisis Deepens — STRATEGIC RESERVES AT "RED ALERT"

**[VERIFIED]** Long lines of cars stretched through parts of Tehran and the northeastern Iranian city of Mashhad on Tuesday and Wednesday as motorists queued for gasoline and diesel, with some lines blocking entire streets... Gasoline shortages have now reached Tehran, forcing some gas stations to suspend operations.

**[REPORTED]** The Iranian government is reportedly considering three different options for dealing with the crisis: limiting available gasoline to the level of domestic production; reducing subsidized quotas and selling additional quantities at market prices; transferring fuel quotas from vehicles to individual citizens. Fear that an increase in prices could trigger new protests has pushed the government to consume the strategic reserves in order to buy time.

**[VERIFIED]** Panic buying is driving a surge in demand, with distribution running approximately 30 per cent higher than usual amid fears of tightening supplies. Similar fuel shortages have spread beyond the capital, with disruptions reported in Kerman and Mashhad. New sweeping US sanctions are intensifying pressure on Iran's energy infrastructure.

**[REPORTED]** Iran fuel reserves may run dry within weeks, sources say, Iran International reports. Iran VP says current gasoline situation is unsustainable.

*Assessment: Iran's domestic fuel crisis is becoming critical. The blockade is biting hard — Iran, a major oil producer, is running out of refined fuel for its own population. This pressure may explain Iran's urgency in the Oman negotiations but also raises internal stability concerns that could affect decision-making.*

## Emerging Signals

**IRGC-Government Split?** The IRGC announced revenue-sharing while foreign ministries stayed silent on tolls. This could indicate:
- IRGC testing US/regional reaction before official adoption
- Internal disagreement between hardliners and pragmatists
- Deliberate ambiguity to preserve negotiating room
Worth monitoring for further divergence.

**Toll Regime as Flashpoint:** The international legal consensus is that tolls on international straits are illegal. Trump rejected the concept when he floated it himself in July. Iran's assertion of tolls — even if not immediately implemented — creates a fundamental barrier to any deal framework the US might accept.

**Iran's Domestic Clock:** The fuel crisis adds urgency to Iranian decision-making that wasn't present a week ago. This could accelerate either escalation (regime preservation through external conflict) or deal-making (regime preservation through economic relief).

**Qatar's Return:** The PM-level engagement is the highest since Pakistan's army chief visits. Qatar's track record in the June MoU gives them credibility both sides might trust.
