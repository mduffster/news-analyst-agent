## Key Developments (Past 24 Hours)

### **OPERATION YEMEN DAWN: SAUDI-LED OFFENSIVE LAUNCHED** [VERIFIED]

This is the most significant development since yesterday's report. On 5 October, the PLC announced the start of Operation Yemen Dawn, aimed at retaking Sanaa and ending Houthi control of Yemen. Around 100 Saudi aircraft were supporting the operation, striking hundreds of Houthi targets.

Yemeni government forces on Monday secured control of the Bab Al Mandeb strait, the Dhubab area overlooking the sea lane and the strategic port city of Mokha, as a Saudi-led coalition provided aerial support and carried out air strikes on Houthi targets.

Coalition spokesman Maj Gen Turki Al Malki said 324 "high-value" Houthi targets had been destroyed in the offensive, with 100 fighter jets taking part in the operation.

**Critical detail on US involvement:** According to CNN, over 100 American military advisers who were stationed in Saudi Arabia weeks earlier due to signs that Iran was increasing aid to the Houthis were providing intelligence support to the Saudi military during the offensives. The support included a targeting tool that was compared to Project Maven as well as geospatial intelligence for strikes against the militia. US president Donald Trump rejected a request by Saudi leader Mohammed bin Salman to strike the Houthis.

**[ANALYSIS]** This is the outcome of the Camp David meeting. While Trump declined direct US strikes on Houthis, the administration has clearly greenlit Saudi operations with substantial US intelligence support. The offensive's stated goal—Bab al-Mandab control—is directly linked to Iran war objectives: providing an alternative shipping route while Hormuz remains closed.

### **HOUTHIS STRIKE BACK AT SAUDI ARABIA** [VERIFIED]

Saudi Arabia's civil aviation authority has said at least three people were wounded in attacks on Jazan and Najran airports near the Yemen border, as fighting with Yemen's Houthis rages. "The attacks resulted in three minor injuries and material damage," the General Authority of Civil Aviation said.

Yemen's Houthis have said they attacked an Aramco facility in the Saudi capital, Riyadh, as fighting between the Iran-aligned group and Yemen's Saudi-backed internationally recognised government intensifies. Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree said the group had carried out a "military operation with ballistic missiles and drones" that targeted Aramco in Riyadh.

The Houthis have also claimed an attack on the King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh and the Aramco refinery in coastal Rabigh.

### **FLYDUBAI ATTACKER: "REVENGE FOR GAZA" MOTIVE CONFIRMED** [REPORTED]

The investigation has produced significant new findings. An ongoing investigation into last week's midair attack on a FlyDubai plane has found that the co-pilot who tried to take control of the flight intended to crash it into Israel's Ben Gurion International Airport, a person familiar with the matter told NPR.

Reports have revealed that the Omani co-pilot who reportedly has been indoctrinated with radical ideologies and associates himself with Al Qaeda and other terror outfits intended to crash the aircraft as a "revenge for Gaza". He has also told the investigators that he intended to "die as a martyr".

Israeli officials say the co-pilot, an Omani national, had planned to crash last Wednesday's flight into Ben Gurion International Airport near Tel Aviv. He had also operated a flight to Tel Aviv earlier this year and intended to hijack and crash the plane then, but opted not to.

**[ANALYSIS]** This appears to be an al-Qaeda-inspired lone wolf rather than Iranian state direction, confirming yesterday's assessment. The "Gaza revenge" framing rather than Iran war framing reduces the incident's utility as justification for escalation against Tehran. However, it raises serious aviation security concerns given the vetting failure.

## Assessment

**[ANALYSIS]** Today's dominant development is the launch of **Operation Yemen Dawn**, which represents the outcome of Friday's Camp David meeting. The key dynamics:

1. **The second front is now active.** The Saudi-led offensive with US intelligence support directly connects the Yemen conflict to the Iran war. The strategic objective—securing Bab al-Mandab—would provide an alternative shipping route while Hormuz remains closed. This is a coalition-level strategic play, not merely a Saudi initiative.

2. **Trump's "compromise" on Yemen.** The administration threaded a needle: declining Saudi requests for direct US strikes while enabling operations through intelligence sharing, which CNN compared to Project Maven. This gives Trump deniability while the Saudis bear kinetic risk. However, Houthi retaliation against Saudi targets could draw the US in deeper.

3. **The Houthis are not collapsing.** Despite claims of Bab al-Mandab gains, Houthi forces simultaneously captured At Turbah (a key road junction) and are striking Saudi airports and Aramco facilities. On Sunday, the Houthis captured the town of Turbah, which sits on a main road linking the provincial capital of Taiz with the port city of Aden, which serves as an interim seat of Yemen's government. This is a grinding multi-front war, not a rout.

4. **The Flydubai investigation is separating from Iran.** The "revenge for Gaza" and al-Qaeda ideological framing confirms this was inspired rather than directed terrorism. While tragic, it does not strengthen the case for Iran escalation.

5. **Hormuz remains contested.** Four tanker attacks Monday and explicit IRGC warnings to vessels show no easing. Iran's leverage through the strait closure is unchanged.
