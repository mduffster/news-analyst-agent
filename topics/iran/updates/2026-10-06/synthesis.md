# US-Israel-Iran Conflict: Yemen Theater Activated as Mecca Pact Mobilizes

## October 6, 2026 — Day 221

**Assessment:** The conflict reached a significant inflection point over the past 24 hours with Operation Yemen Dawn achieving claimed gains at Bab el-Mandeb while simultaneously triggering the Mecca Pact's first activation, bringing Turkish and Pakistani military commitments into the conflict. The Yemen front has transformed from a secondary theater into a potential decisive arena, with US intelligence support enabling Saudi operations but direct American strikes still off the table. Meanwhile, Hormuz shipping attacks continue at elevated tempo despite oil exports paradoxically nearing pre-war levels, and the FlyDubai investigation has confirmed an al-Qaeda-inspired lone actor rather than Iranian state direction. With 28 days until midterm elections, the administration's Camp David decisions are now materializing on the ground—not through expanded US kinetic action, but through enabled coalition operations across two maritime chokepoints.

## Operation Yemen Dawn: Claimed Gains and Mecca Pact Activation

The most consequential development is the intersection of the Saudi-backed offensive with unprecedented regional alliance mobilization.

Yemeni government forces on Monday secured control of the Bab Al Mandeb strait, the Dhubab area overlooking the sea lane and the strategic port city of Mokha, as a Saudi-led coalition provided aerial support and carried out air strikes on Houthi targets. [REPORTED]

The Southern Giants Brigade announced that it gained "full control over Dhubab Airport, severing the main supply line linking Mocha and Bab al-Mandab, and imposing a tight siege on the terrorist Houthi militia in the strategic Bab al-Mandab." These claims remain contested: The Houthis have dismissed the claims, with the group's deputy governor of Taiz, Fuad Sinan, issuing a video statement from Mocha city on Monday evening.

The Saudi-led Coalition supported Yemeni armed forces with 100 fighter jets. Maliki said on October 5 that the coalition had "destroyed 324 high-value targets with the launch of Operation 'Dawn of Yemen.'" [OFFICIAL]

The coalition expanded operations beyond coastal positions: The Saudi-led coalition also announced that "the naval forces of the Coalition carried out a military operation in al-Hudaydah Governorate." The province is north of Taiz along Yemen's west coast and has been the launching point for much of the Houthis' terrorism in the Red Sea. Maliki said that this attack "destroyed weapons depots, boat explosive storage facilities, and naval mines." [OFFICIAL]

**US Role Clarified:** More than 100 US military advisers are on the ground in Saudi Arabia providing intelligence and targeting support to the Kingdom in its military campaign against the Iran-backed Houthis in Yemen. The assistance comes as fighting on the peninsula intensifies and risks spilling into open war with the Houthis, threatening a second major shipping corridor out of the Middle East.

Sources told CNN that the current US support has involved helping Saudi Arabia use a targeting tool that lets military officers see in real time what is happening on the battlefield, similar to the Pentagon's AI platform Maven, and providing "geospatial" intelligence for strikes in Yemen. [REPORTED]

However, the administration drew a line: The US is sharing intelligence with Saudi Arabia—as it routinely does already—but it is not participating directly in strikes. The US is also not helping refuel Saudi warplanes or providing other operational support, as it has in past fighting with the Houthis. [OFFICIAL]

**Mecca Pact Activated:** Turkey and Pakistan will undertake a "rapid deployment" of troops to Saudi Arabia as part of a new mutual defence alliance, the three countries said Monday, as the Gulf kingdom faces escalating attacks from Yemen's Houthis. The three countries in August signed the Mecca Joint Defence Agreement committing them to come to each other's aid in the event one were attacked. [OFFICIAL]

The pact unites NATO's second-largest army, that of Turkey, with the Islamic world's only nuclear-armed state, Pakistan, and the leading Gulf power, Saudi Arabia. Their representatives held an emergency meeting in Riyadh on Monday where they noted recent attacks on Saudi Arabia, in particular ones targeting the holy cities of Mecca and Medina. Reuters reported last week that Pakistan sent four plane loads of weapons to the port city of Aden to support the Saudi-backed government. [REPORTED]

**Significance:** The Mecca Pact activation transforms the Yemen conflict from a Saudi-Yemeni government campaign into a multinational operation. Turkish and Pakistani deployments would provide air defense expertise, potential ground forces, and—critically for Iran's calculus—nuclear deterrence implications. This is the most significant regional alliance shift since the war began.

## Houthi Counter-Operations and Saudi Vulnerabilities

Despite claimed losses at Bab el-Mandeb, Houthi forces demonstrated continued offensive capability on multiple fronts.

Houthis seized the town of Turbah on Sunday. Turbah sits on a main road linking the provincial capital of Taiz with the port city of Aden, which serves as an interim seat of Yemen's government. The rebels' taking of Turbah will likely constrain government forces' ability to reinforce and resupply front-line positions across Taiz province and the provincial capital of Taiz, according to the Institute for the Study of War. [VERIFIED]

Iran-backed Houthis on Monday said they had struck Riyadh's international airport, an Aramco refinery and several other sites in Saudi Arabia, as the Saudi-led coalition in Yemen said they had destroyed Houthi targets on the Red Sea coast. Loud explosions rattled Riyadh on Monday evening as the fighting escalated. [REPORTED]

In Saudi Arabia, officials say three people were injured Monday as Houthi fighters from Yemen launched attacks on two regional airports. Riyadh often doesn't immediately comment on Houthi attacks. However, Saudi Arabia confirmed that two southern airports were damaged on October 5. [VERIFIED]

Operations were suspended on Tuesday at an airport in Saudi Arabia, a regional source with knowledge of the matter said, after Yemen's Iran-backed Houthis announced they had attacked the site. [REPORTED]

The Houthis also launched attacks on Saudi Arabia's King Khalid International Airport and an oil refinery belonging to the state-run oil company Saudi Aramco. The Houthi military spokesman warned: "We warn all international airlines operating in Saudi airspace to suspend their flights, as it has become a military operation zone of our armed forces." [OFFICIAL - Houthi statement]

**Assessment:** The battlefield picture is contested. Coalition forces claim coastal gains while Houthis demonstrate the ability to simultaneously conduct inland offensive operations (Turbah) and strike deep into Saudi territory. This suggests a grinding multi-front conflict rather than rapid coalition victory.

## Hormuz: Attacks Continue Despite Oil Flow Recovery

The paradox of recovering oil exports amid intensifying attacks became more pronounced.

At least seven incidents involving tankers have been reported in the past week, according to shipping intelligence service Marisks. The United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations (UKMTO) agency has reported at least one attack a day in the Strait of Hormuz or the Gulf of Aden since October 2. [VERIFIED]

Nearly 20 commercial ships, mostly tankers, have come under attack over the past month while sailing through Hormuz, the Persian Gulf or off the coast of Oman, according to the Joint Maritime Information Center. Iran attacked roughly two ships for every 100 vessels that crossed the strait in the third quarter. [VERIFIED]

A tanker turned back after being threatened by Iranian forces in the Strait of Hormuz on Monday, according to the U.K. navy's Maritime Trade Operations center (UKMTO). "A tanker transiting the Strait of Hormuz inbound was hailed by the [Iranian Revolutionary Guard Corps] and instructed to turn back or the vessel would be targeted." [VERIFIED]

Yet exports have recovered: Despite those attacks, oil exports from the region have risen dramatically. According to provisional data from Kpler, the seven-day moving average for Middle East crude exports reached 18.3 million barrels per day (bpd) on September 30, compared with an average of about 18 million bpd during the 12 months before the war. [VERIFIED]

Oil flows through Hormuz now depend on a major U.S. military commitment to protect tankers transiting along a southern route along the coast of Oman. With the security situation in Hormuz still dangerous, it is unclear how long the rebound in crude exports can be sustained in the absence of a negotiated settlement or capitulation by Tehran.

The IRGC continues asserting control: "Don't trust U.S. Navy and don't use south corridor at all and don't put your life in danger," a radio message transmitted on a public shipping channel and reviewed by The Wall Street Journal said. [VERIFIED]

**Significance:** The simultaneous recovery of oil flows and intensification of attacks creates an unstable equilibrium. Iran demonstrates it retains interdiction capability while coalition protection enables continued transit—but at high insurance, freight, and security costs. The fragility of this arrangement was underscored by the IRGC turning back a tanker despite the naval presence.

## FlyDubai Investigation: Lone Actor Profile Solidifies

The investigation produced important clarifications that reduce the incident's potential as an escalation driver.

An ongoing investigation into last week's midair attack on a FlyDubai plane has found that the co-pilot who tried to take control of the flight intended to crash it into Israel's Ben Gurion International Airport, a person familiar with the matter but not authorized to speak publicly told NPR. [REPORTED]

Israeli investigators believe he was acting as a lone wolf, people familiar with the matter told CBS News. [REPORTED]

Multiple sources familiar with the investigation told CBS News that the co-pilot's name is Hamam al Hamami. Al Hamami worked for a short time for Oman Air, where he entered new pilot training. Surveillance by the Omani government discovered that he was visiting radical websites. Oman Air then transferred him to an office job. [REPORTED]

The Wall Street Journal, citing sources familiar with the matter, reported that al-Hammami had "previously been banned from flying by Oman over concerns he had adopted radical ideological views." [REPORTED]

**Significance:** The emerging profile—Omani national with al-Qaeda ideological links who was previously flagged by Omani security and banned from flying—confirms inspired terrorism rather than Iranian state direction. This significantly reduces the utility of the incident as a casus belli against Tehran, while raising serious questions about aviation security screening that allowed a previously-flagged individual to gain employment at FlyDubai and access to Israel-bound flights.

## Diplomatic Track: Stalled but Not Closed

Negotiations remain in limbo with both sides positioning around the November 3 midterm elections.

Iranian officials signaled on Sunday that peace negotiations with the U.S. are continuing, even as Washington prepares for a potential escalation in the war later this year. Iran's Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi said in a statement that Iran is reviewing the U.S. response to the seven-day peace proposal delivered to Washington via mediators late last month. [OFFICIAL]

Last Thursday, in Denton, Texas, Donald Trump said Iran is "ready to fold up" and that the war would be won "right after the election"—or "maybe before". The same week he told TIME that new strikes after the vote were "possible", and the Pentagon sent the USS Theodore Roosevelt carrier group and the USS Makin Island amphibious group, about 9,000 sailors and Marines, towards the Gulf. [VERIFIED]

A week earlier, in New York, Iran's president Masoud Pezeshkian had set the opposite deadline: "We don't want it to get to the midterm elections." Both governments have circled the same date, 3 November, and each believes the calendar favours it. That is the most important fact about these talks, and it points towards a deal built to be announced rather than one built to last.

The fundamental barrier remains trust: The Iranians claim Trump cheated them twice when he attacked them in the middle of negotiations. Many inside the Iranian regime think any negotiations with the U.S. are just a ruse to mask preparations for a surprise attack. The U.S. side counters it was Iran that cheated when it violated the June 2026 memorandum of understanding, by attacking ships in the Strait of Hormuz days after signing the deal.
