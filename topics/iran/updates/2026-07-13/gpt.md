## Daily incremental update — US–Israel–Iran conflict  
**As of Mon, July 13, 2026 (ET)**  
**New since Fri, July 10, 2026 (ET)**

### 1) The U.S. has now moved from “unclear attribution” to explicit, escalatory policy signaling on Hormuz
- President **Donald Trump said the U.S. will “blockade” Iran in/around the Strait of Hormuz** and **charge a “20% toll” / charge ships for “safe passage,”** while also saying Iranian ships won’t be allowed through. **[REPORTED]** ([apnews.com](https://apnews.com/article/6c2c44cfdd089d6393d18fa5930ed620?utm_source=openai))  
- This comes amid a weekend sequence in which **Iran/IRGC attacked another commercial ship** and **declared the strait “closed,”** followed by **additional U.S. strike waves**. **[REPORTED]** ([apnews.com](https://apnews.com/article/0764d17c09370a8c5cf1e8197a8878ab?utm_source=openai))  

**What changed / what it means:**  
- The risk picture shifts from “strike-for-strike ambiguity” to **a de facto maritime enforcement strategy**—i.e., the conflict is increasingly being operationalized as **control of transit in Hormuz** rather than isolated retaliation. That makes inadvertent escalation more likely because enforcement requires repeated, observable actions (intercepts, escorts, strikes, rules-of-engagement incidents). **[REPORTED]** ([apnews.com](https://apnews.com/article/935364080d8083d6137d399bbeb9205f?utm_source=openai))  
- The “toll/safe passage” framing is also a political signal that the U.S. is preparing to **normalize a sustained presence and sustained coercion**, not quickly “wrap up” after a few strikes.

---

### 2) Operationally, the “southern route” concept has hardened into a U.S.-backed transit regime (not just advice)
- Reporting now consistently frames the **southern route along Oman** as the practical mechanism by which the U.S. is **loosening Iran’s chokehold** and keeping some traffic moving even as Iran claims closure. **[REPORTED]** ([apnews.com](https://apnews.com/article/0764d17c09370a8c5cf1e8197a8878ab?utm_source=openai))  
- U.S. DOT/MARAD has an active advisory posture emphasizing immediate reporting, coordination with **U.S. Fifth Fleet** and **UKMTO**, and voyage-planning specifics for Hormuz transits—another indicator this is being treated as a persistent operating environment. **[OFFICIAL]** ([maritime.dot.gov](https://www.maritime.dot.gov/msci/2026-004-persian-gulf-strait-hormuz-and-gulf-oman-iranian-attacks-commercial-vessels?utm_source=openai))  

**What changed / what it means:**  
- This increases the odds of a **prolonged “managed corridor”** scenario: the strait is neither fully open nor fully closed, but transits become more militarized and incident-prone (warning shots, drones, mines/boat swarms, mis-ID events).  
- It also creates a clearer “tripwire”: **any successful attack on a ship inside/near the escorted route** becomes a strong candidate for immediate U.S. escalation, because it directly challenges the enforcement concept. **[REPORTED]** ([apnews.com](https://apnews.com/article/0764d17c09370a8c5cf1e8197a8878ab?utm_source=openai))
