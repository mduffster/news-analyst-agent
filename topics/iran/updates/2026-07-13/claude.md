### Key Developments Since July 10

#### 1. Trump Reinstates Iran Blockade, Declares US "Guardian" of Hormuz

**[OFFICIAL]** President Trump said Monday that the U.S. will be "the guardian" of the Strait of Hormuz. "The U.S.A. will be, from this point forward, known as 'THE GUARDIAN OF THE HORMUZ STRAIT,' but as such, and as a matter of FAIRNESS, will be reimbursed, at the rate of 20% on all cargo shipped."

**[OFFICIAL]** He also said the U.S. is reinstating its blockade on Iranian ports and vessels, adding the blockade is "so named because it is only stopping Iran's ships or customers from entering or leaving." "All other countries will have fair and open use of the Strait," he said.

**[VERIFIED]** The renewed naval blockade has not yet come into effect due to a legal requirement to notify ship owners 24 hours in advance. A U.S. official said CENTCOM will announce the specific timing later on Monday.

**[REPORTED]** A senior Gulf source told Axios the U.S. hasn't discussed the issue of possible tolls for securing the Strait of Hormuz with its allies in the region.

*Assessment: This is an extraordinary reversal. Trump has condemned Iran for proposing transit fees while now demanding the US be "reimbursed" 20% on all cargo. The $32 million per supertanker fee cited would be far above any previous Iranian charge. This effectively ends any pretense of the MOU framework.*

#### 2. US Strikes Reach ~300 Targets in Three Nights — Deepest Since War Began

**[VERIFIED]** U.S. Central Command completed a third round of strikes this week against Iran, July 11. U.S. forces hit approximately 140 Iranian military targets with precision munitions. Targets included Iranian missile and drone sites, naval capabilities, ammunition storage facilities, communication networks, and coastal surveillance locations. During three nights of strikes this week, CENTCOM has struck more than 300 targets.

**[VERIFIED]** US Central Command says it used "one-way attack aerial drones, and one-way attack sea drones for the first time" in strikes against Iran.

**[VERIFIED]** Strikes have expanded beyond coastal areas, according to reports from Iranian media, as US allies came under fire from Iran.

**[VERIFIED]** Iranian state media is reporting that at least one person was killed and four others were injured in the latest wave of U.S. strikes against Iran. Those affected were at an agricultural water pumping station in Mahshahr city.

*Assessment: The pace and depth of US strikes has intensified significantly. The use of sea drones for the first time and expansion beyond coastal areas signals a new phase. 300+ targets in one week approaches the intensity seen at the war's start in late February.*

#### 3. IRGC Launches Broadest Single-Night Attack of the War

**[VERIFIED]** Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) has targeted US military facilities in Bahrain, claimed it has destroyed radar systems in Oman and hit Jordan and Kuwait in its latest round of overnight retaliatory strikes against the United States. Tehran's attacks on Monday came as a response to Washington's escalating strikes.

**[VERIFIED]** Jordan's military said on Monday that it intercepted and downed "four missiles that entered Jordanian airspace" and came from Iranian territory. The IRGC said it also targeted a US surface-to-surface missile base in Kuwait, "setting fire to two HIMARS missile launchers and missile-packed warehouses, completely destroying them".

**[VERIFIED]** The IRGC claimed a "heavy and surprise" attack on logistics support centres and refuelling platforms used by US aircraft carriers at the port of Duqm in Oman.

**[REPORTED]** Iran's Zolfaghar ballistic missiles, now guided by China's encrypted BeiDou-3 satellite navigation, have largely removed US electronic warfare jamming as a viable first line of defense — leaving Patriot and THAAD interceptors whose combined stockpiles are already at critical-depletion levels.

**[OFFICIAL]** The risk of a regional conflict has increased following US strikes on Iran this weekend, the spokesperson for Iran's top joint military command said. "The leaders of the countries in the region are warned that any cooperation with the United States or logistical support for its military will be regarded as an act of war against Iran's sovereignty and national security."

*Assessment: IRGC attacks now span six countries (Bahrain, Kuwait, Jordan, Qatar, Oman, UAE). The Oman strike is particularly significant — targeting Port Duqm's US logistics facilities. The BeiDou guidance issue, if accurate, represents a fundamental shift in the electronic warfare balance.*

#### 4. Iran Declares Strait of Hormuz Formally "Closed"

**[VERIFIED]** The US launched its third round of strikes on Iran this week as Tehran declared that it's closing the Strait of Hormuz "until further notice."

**[VERIFIED]** US Central Command said Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps "blatantly attacked" a Cyprus-flagged container ship transiting the strait, and one crew member is missing. The IRGC said it fired a warning shot at a vessel trying to use an unauthorized route to cross the waterway.

**[VERIFIED]** Iran's Persian Gulf Strait Authority declared "due to recent hostile actions by the US forces, passage through the Strait of Hormuz is currently unfeasible." It reminded vessels that in Iran's view, "the sole means of obtaining a passage permit" to transit the strait is through its website.

**[VERIFIED]** The Strait remains effectively closed to commercial shipping as of 13 July 2026. 34 ships transited vs ~88/day normal. Brent $79.29.

**[VERIFIED]** The number of vessels transiting the Strait of Hormuz has fallen to its lowest level in five weeks. Six ships sailed through the strait on Sunday, according to data from Kpler.

*Assessment: Iran's declaration of formal closure is now explicit rather than de facto. Traffic has collapsed from 34 ships (July 10) to just 6 ships on Sunday — effectively a complete shutdown.*

#### 5. Mojtaba Khamenei Still Absent — Tehran Memorial Scheduled Tuesday

**[VERIFIED]** The country's new Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei thanked the "tens of millions" of people he said attended the funeral ceremonies in a purported message released by Iranian media. He has yet to appear publicly since the killing of his father and predecessor.

**[VERIFIED]** Iran is set to organise a memorial ceremony for slain Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei next week. As per media reports, the memorial ceremony will be held on Tuesday from 5 pm to 7 pm local time in Tehran. Though there is no official clarification on whether Mojtaba will attend, there is a strong buzz that the current supreme leader will likely make a public appearance.

**[REPORTED]** Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) released a new image of Mojtaba Khamenei on Saturday. According to the IRGC, the photograph was published through the official website of the Supreme Leader's office and was intended to reaffirm that he is in "complete health." The image came days before the planned memorial ceremony.

*Assessment: Mojtaba's continued absence now exceeds 4 months since the February 28 strike. The Tuesday memorial in Tehran will be the next opportunity. The IRGC releasing a photo claiming "complete health" suggests they are managing expectations.*

#### 6. Saudi Arabia Bombs Sanaa Airport — Houthis Declare Ceasefire "Over"

**[VERIFIED]** The attack comes after tensions between the two sides flared earlier this month. Yemen's defense minister Gen. Taher al-Aqili said the airport's runway was struck Monday to stop an Iranian plane carrying the Houthi delegation returning from the funeral.

**[VERIFIED]** The Houthi foreign ministry accused Saudi Arabia of restarting the conflict. "Saudi Arabia has announced the start of the war and bears full responsibility for it and for any consequences of this step." The Houthis said the strike was carried out "without any justification" and described it as a violation of the 2022 ceasefire agreement.

**[VERIFIED]** The developments threaten to undo years of relative calm between Saudi Arabia and the Houthis following a UN-backed truce that sharply reduced cross-border attacks.

*Assessment: This is a significant expansion of the regional conflict. The 2022 UN-brokered truce between Saudi Arabia and the Houthis appears to be collapsing. Houthi retaliation against Saudi airports and infrastructure is now likely.*

#### 7. Oman Dual-Corridor Proposal — Potentially Dead on Arrival

**[REPORTED]** Oman has drafted a proposal to manage traffic in the Strait of Hormuz through two separately controlled routes, a source with knowledge of the talks told CNN. Under the agreement, which is yet to be finalised, both corridors would remain open.

**[REPORTED]** The Southern Corridor, through Omani territorial waters, would allow free navigation under pre-war conditions. Vessels transiting the Northern Corridor, through Iranian territorial waters, would require prior approval from Iran, although no tolls would be imposed.

**[VERIFIED]** Iran says the US is undermining talks with Oman on ensuring safe passage through the strait.

*Assessment: Trump's "guardian" announcement and 20% fee demand appear to directly undercut Oman's mediation efforts. The dual-corridor proposal may now be moot given escalation.*

#### 8. Oil Rebounds Sharply — Markets Re-pricing War Risk

**[VERIFIED]** Brent rose to 78.85 USD/Bbl on July 13, 2026, up 3.74% from the previous day.

**[VERIFIED]** Oil prices have jumped amid the latest outbreak of hostilities. Brent crude, the main international benchmark, rose more than 4 percent on Monday as the US and Iran traded attacks. Brent futures for September delivery stood at $78.82 a barrel as of 08:00 GMT, the highest since June 22.

**[VERIFIED]** Brent crude jumped by more than 3 per cent on Monday on fears of disruption to one of the world's most important energy shipping routes, although prices remained below the peaks reached earlier in the conflict.

*Assessment: Oil has reversed the diplomatic-optimism decline from last week, jumping from ~$76 to ~$79. Markets are now pricing in prolonged disruption rather than diplomatic resolution.*

### Emerging Signals

**Trump's 20% Hormuz Fee — Strategic Contradiction:** Trump has condemned Iran for proposing fees while now demanding the US be "reimbursed" 20%. At current prices, such a fee would amount to roughly $32 million for a supertanker, far above previous Iranian transit charges of up to $2 million. This undermines the US position on "free navigation" and may complicate relations with Gulf allies.

**BeiDou Guidance Shift:** Iran's Zolfaghar missiles have migrated to China's BeiDou-3 satellite navigation system. Unlike commercial GPS, the B3A signal uses frequency-hopping and encryption that maintains approximately 98 percent navigation reliability even under sustained EW jamming. If accurate, this represents a fundamental shift in missile defense calculations.

**Interceptor Depletion Concerns:** Reports suggest Patriot and THAAD interceptor stockpiles may be reaching critical levels after months of sustained IRGC attacks. This could force harder decisions about which targets to defend.

**Economic Pressure on Iran:** The IMF expects Iran's economy to contract by 6.1% in 2026, with inflation averaging 68.9%. This creates pressure for a deal but also desperation that could drive escalation.
