## 1. Trump Signals Decision Coming "Soon," Weighs Resumption of Strikes [OFFICIAL]

U.S. President Donald Trump spoke to reporters and said that he has yet to decide on attacking Iran. In remarks Thursday, Trump struck a characteristically threatening tone: "I would rather not destroy the rest of the country if possible, but maybe I will have to."

Trump met with his national security team on Iran on Thursday, their first discussion since the midterms. According to Fox News, options for resuming strikes as soon as November 9 are under consideration.

Trump acknowledged it was "largely true" the Iran war caused the midterms loss, after Vance comments. Yet he maintains he would act the same way if given another chance, a position that underscores the tension between political consequences and his stated security priorities.

**Significance:** Trump is keeping all options open while buying time. The "decision coming soon" framing delays commitments while his advisers remain split between the Ratcliffe/Bessent "let the blockade work" faction and the Vance-led restraint faction. The post-election window creates both opportunity and pressure—opportunity to act without electoral consequences, pressure from incoming Democratic majorities to de-escalate.

## 2. Rezaei Delivers Khamenei Message to Putin; Iran Reiterates Maximalist Demands [VERIFIED]

Russia's President Vladimir Putin met Iran's security chief Mohsen Rezaei in Moscow to discuss regional security as diplomatic efforts gather pace. During the meeting on Thursday, Rezaei also delivered a letter to Putin from Iran's Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei, according to Iranian state broadcaster Press TV.

Russian presidential aide Yuri Ushakov and Igor Kostyukov, head of Russia's military intelligence agency, the GRU, also took part in the meeting. This signals Moscow's serious engagement at the highest levels of security coordination.

Rezaei was explicit about Iran's position: "On the issue of diplomacy, we have announced seven conditions to the Americans. As long as they do not execute our seven conditions, we will neither open the Strait of Hormuz nor have negotiations."

According to Rezaei, Putin reiterated that Russia is ready to provide support to end the war. "During the meeting with Mr. Vladimir Putin, discussions were held regarding the construction of gas pipelines from Russia to Iran," Rezaei stated.

**Significance:** The Putin-Rezaei meeting serves multiple purposes: it demonstrates Iran is not isolated, explores alternative economic arrangements (the gas pipeline), and probes Russia's role as potential mediator. Putin's stated readiness to "help end the war" positions Moscow as a player in any settlement, though Russian interests may not align with rapid de-escalation given the benefits of sustained oil price pressure on Western economies.

## 3. Confirmed Fatality in Gulf Attack; IRGC Claims More Tankers Hit [VERIFIED/OFFICIAL — DISPUTED]

One seafarer was killed in an explosion believed to have been caused by a missile attack on an oil products tanker in the Gulf on Thursday. The Marshall Islands-flagged tanker MT Velos Sapphire was sailing unladen towards Bahrain when it was hit.

As of 6 November, the IMO had confirmed a total of 129 incidents in the Strait of Hormuz and the wider Middle East region, resulting in 25 seafarer fatalities. The US-led Joint Maritime Information Center (JMIC) recorded six incidents in the Strait of Hormuz and the Middle East Gulf since 1 November.

The IRGC continued its claims of tanker damage: The IRGC Navy announced that two oil tankers caught fire after striking sea mines near the Strait of Hormuz. This claim comes despite earlier assertions by the United States that the main shipping lanes had been cleared of mines.

CENTCOM previously rejected a similar IRGC claim regarding a supertanker striking a mine, stating that no vessel had hit a mine in the Strait of Hormuz. This verification gap persists—Iranian claims remain unconfirmed by independent Western sources, but the operational impact on shipping and insurance persists regardless.

Data from Britain's maritime security agency UKMTO suggested that attacks on oil-carrying ships are still running at close to a record pace.

**Significance:** The confirmed seafarer fatality underscores that maritime violence continues at lethal intensity despite post-election diplomatic signals. The discrepancy between IRGC "mine strike" claims and CENTCOM denials matters for attribution, but the sustained attack pace—129 incidents, 25 fatalities—represents the conflict's ongoing cost regardless of mechanism.

## 4. Hegseth Faces Likely Impeachment Vote Next Week [VERIFIED]

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth could face a House impeachment vote next week as Republican lawmakers criticize his role in the decision to attack Iran. The possible vote follows Republican losses in Tuesday's midterm elections.

Representative Thomas Massie, a Kentucky Republican and frequent critic of President Donald Trump, told Bloomberg News he will "definitely" reintroduce articles of impeachment when the chamber returns next week. Multiple GOP sources told CNN they expect it to pass.

The House is scheduled to return on November 9. While a vote in the House could prove which Republicans in the lame-duck Congress are willing to break with the administration, it would be a harder lift to trigger a two-thirds Senate vote, or 67 Senate votes, to convict him.

If the vote takes place after the new House takes shape in January, Democrats will not need any Republican support: The party is on track to amass the largest majority since at least the 2008 election, with 240 seats confirmed so far.

**Significance:** A lame-duck House impeachment of Hegseth would be symbolically significant but faces near-certain failure in the Senate. The real pressure point is whether enough Republicans break with Trump to pass it now. If it passes in the lame duck, it signals the war's political toxicity even among Republicans; if it fails, Democrats will pursue it in January regardless.

## 5. Yemen Market Airstrike Kills 20-25; Houthis Blame Saudi Arabia [REPORTED — ATTRIBUTION DISPUTED]

The Iran-backed Houthi rebels said airstrikes hit a market in southwestern Yemen on Friday, killing at least 20 people, and blamed Saudi Arabia for the strikes. It would be one of the deadliest attacks since fighting resumed between the Houthis and Saudi-backed government forces.

Houthi military spokesperson Yahya Saree said three airstrikes hit the al-Hadara market in al-Waziyah in the province of Taiz at around 7 a.m. The latest attack came just four days after at least eight people were killed in an airstrike on the al-Makh'af market in the al-Misrakh district of the same province.

Taiz province's government media office acknowledged the attack on the busy market and said that there were casualties, but didn't provide details on the actual toll.

**Significance:** This mass-casualty incident on the Yemen front increases risk of Houthi retaliation—potentially through Red Sea shipping attacks or missile strikes on Saudi targets. The escalating Taiz fighting represents a proxy dimension that can complicate any Hormuz-focused diplomatic track. Attribution remains contested, but the civilian toll fuels cycle of retaliation.

## 6. Oil Set for Historic Weekly Decline Despite Partial Rebound [VERIFIED]

Oil prices were set for a sharp weekly fall after the election losses of Trump's Republican Party raised hopes of de-escalation. The Brent contract is on track for a 17% decline this week, which would be its steepest weekly percentage fall since the start of the COVID-19 pandemic and its biggest in absolute terms going on LSEG data back to 1988.

Brent fell to 98.16 USD/Bbl on November 6, 2026, down 0.13% from the previous day. WTI crude traded similarly weak.

Brent traded near $100 a barrel after closing almost 4% higher in the previous session, extending a volatile stretch in which prices have swung within a $19 range since Monday. Despite the rebound, front-month futures are down 15% this week.

"Oil doesn't have to be at $117 to be expensive, and a fall to $93 doesn't mean the underlying problems have disappeared," said Matt Stanley from Kpler.

**Significance:** Markets are pricing in hope for de-escalation that has not materialized in operational terms. The 17% weekly drop reflects political expectations—incoming Democratic majorities, Vance's public break with Trump—but attacks continue at record pace. This gap between price and reality creates snap-back risk if diplomatic signals prove illusory.

## 7. Eisenkot Mandate Confirmation Expected Sunday [VERIFIED]

Herzog said parties representing 67 lawmakers recommended Yashar leader Gadi Eisenkot, while parties representing 46 lawmakers backed Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu.

President Isaac Herzog said on November 5 that factions totalling 67 members of the 120-seat Knesset had recommended Eisenkot, while 46 had backed Netanyahu. He said he would announce his choice on Sunday, November 8.

Whoever receives the mandate would have 28 days to form a government, with a possible 14-day extension. If that attempt failed, Herzog could hand the task to another lawmaker.

Despite the decisive margin, Eisenkot has a mandate to govern with Arab party support that he has publicly distanced himself from accepting into his actual coalition. This creates coalition-formation challenges even with a clear mandate.

**Significance:** Sunday's mandate announcement will formalize the post-Netanyahu political transition in Israel, though coalition negotiations remain challenging. An Eisenkot government would likely align with US restraint factions rather than pushing for escalation, potentially easing one source of pressure on Trump.

## Iran's Seven Demands — The Diplomatic Gap

Iran's negotiating position remains unchanged. Iran sent a nuclear proposal to the US through Qatar but insists Washington must first meet seven conditions, including lifting sanctions and paying war reparations.

The conditions, as reported: The seven conditions include an immediate halt to all American and Israeli military action in the Middle East; immediate and complete lifting of the U.S. naval blockade against Iran; immediate lifting of all U.S. sanctions. Additional demands include recognition of Iran's sovereignty over Hormuz and compensation for war damages.

Iran has demanded the lifting of US sanctions, the release of frozen Iranian assets and war reparations, among other conditions, while Washington has said nuclear issues must be part of any initial negotiations.

**Significance:** These maximalist preconditions have been consistently rejected by Washington. The proposal functions as positioning rather than breakthrough—Tehran signals willingness to negotiate while setting terms it knows the US cannot accept unilaterally. Any progress requires one side to modify its sequencing demands.
