### **TRUMP CALLS QATAR EMIR ON DE-ESCALATION** [VERIFIED]

US President Donald Trump on Friday called Qatari Emir Sheikh Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani to discuss efforts to end the US-Israeli war on Iran, amid a diplomatic push to avoid a new round of conflict. Sheikh Tamim received a call from Trump, according to his office the Amiri Diwan, during which the pair discussed "diplomatic efforts aimed at de-escalating tensions in the region" undertaken by mediator Qatar.

The US leader and Emir Sheikh Tamim Bin Hamad Al-Thani discussed Qatar's efforts to resolve the differences between the sides and secure a comprehensive agreement to end the conflict, the emir's office said on Friday.

**[ANALYSIS]** Trump's call to the Qatari mediator signals continued diplomatic engagement despite his "finish the job" rhetoric. However, with Iran's Rezaei reiterating that US proposals fail to meet Tehran's demands, the gap remains wide.

### **IRGC CLAIMS 2 MORE TANKERS HIT MINES** [OFFICIAL — DISPUTED]

Two more tankers caught fire after hitting mines on an unauthorised Hormuz route, the navy of Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) said on November 6. The IRGC said the strait would remain closed until Tehran's "seven conditions" were met.

"Two violating oil tankers – one east of the Strait of Hormuz and the other within the Strait itself – that attempted to transit via an unauthorized route struck mines," the IRGC said.

The Strait of Hormuz remains effectively closed to commercial traffic, with vessel throughput on the IMF PortWatch data standing at 2 transits recorded on October 25, against a pre-crisis baseline of 85 per day. Reuters reports tankers were hit in the strait as Iran keeps up pressure following the US midterm vote.

As of November 6, neither CENTCOM, JMIC, UKMTO nor any flag-state maritime authority has confirmed mine strikes on the tankers described in any of the IRGC's recent announcements.

**[ANALYSIS]** Iran is maintaining pressure despite diplomatic signals. The 2 transits vs. 85-per-day baseline shows Hormuz remains effectively closed. However, IRGC claims are unverified by Western authorities.

### **IRGC CLAIMS 2 US DRONES DOWNED OVER QESHM** [OFFICIAL — UNCONFIRMED]

Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) said Thursday that it had brought down two US MQM-170 reconnaissance drones over Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz, according to Iranian state news agency IRNA. In two separate statements, the IRGC said its Aerospace Force used advanced electronic warfare systems to disable the drones over the island, bringing the total number of MQM-170 drones it claimed to have downed to six.

**[ANALYSIS]** If confirmed, this represents a tactical escalation by Iran. However, US has not confirmed losses. Claims should be treated skeptically.

### **OIL SET FOR BIGGEST WEEKLY DROP SINCE COVID** [VERIFIED]

Brent traded near $100 a barrel after closing almost 4% higher in the previous session, extending a volatile stretch in which prices have swung within a $19 range since Monday. Despite the rebound, front-month futures are down 15% this week, on track for the biggest weekly loss since April 2020.

Brent reached $99.32, up 4.71% over 24 hours, as of 23:46 UTC.

**[ANALYSIS]** Despite continued attacks in Hormuz, oil has stabilized near $99 after the post-election crash. Markets are pricing in hope for de-escalation despite minimal concrete progress.
