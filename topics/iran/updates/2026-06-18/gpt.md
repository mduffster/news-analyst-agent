## Daily incremental update — US–Israel–Iran conflict  
**As of Thu, June 18, 2026 (ET)**  
**New since Wed, June 17, 2026 (ET)**

### 1) The interim U.S.–Iran MOU is now **in force** (signed early / remotely) — and the “60‑day clock” starts **today**
- Multiple reports indicate the U.S. and Iran **signed the memorandum electronically ahead of the planned June 19 Geneva ceremony**, and it is **now effective**. **[REPORTED]** ([axios.com](https://www.axios.com/2026/06/17/iran-deal-signing-text-release?utm_source=openai))  
- VP JD Vance said **Thursday, June 18** is **Day 1** of the **60‑day negotiating period** (time zones/signing timing cited). **[OFFICIAL]** ([apnews.com](https://apnews.com/article/e71ed869e09b20d26442418390feb9f3?utm_source=openai))  
- AP reports U.S. officials **read the MOU to journalists** (under anonymity) after days of secrecy; Pakistan’s PM (mediator) also said it was signed and entered into force. **[REPORTED]** ([apnews.com](https://apnews.com/article/8576fbe2be1309977e903463fbf57ee6?utm_source=openai))  

**What changed / what it means:**  
This is a material de-risking of the near-term “spoiler window” (between announcement and signature). But it *pulls forward* the real stress test: **implementation in week 1–2** (shipping, waivers, deconfliction) while simultaneously compressing the runway for the **nuclear endgame** into a clearly-defined **60-day sprint** that begins **June 18, 2026**.

---

### 2) First concrete implementation step: U.S. Navy **lifts blockade** on Iranian ports; officials claim higher oil flows through Hormuz
- AP: Vance said the Navy has **allowed more than a dozen ships** through to Iranian ports, effectively **lifting the blockade** as part of the deal, and claimed **more oil is now flowing** through the Strait of Hormuz (including a specific throughput figure for Wednesday night). **[OFFICIAL]** ([apnews.com](https://apnews.com/article/888fd5ad6543ed9ec4189e609d7c53b1?utm_source=openai))  

**What changed / what it means:**  
This is the first hard indicator that the deal is moving from paper to operational reality. It modestly lowers the odds of an immediate reversion to a broad U.S. air/sea campaign **if** maritime incidents don’t resume. However, it also increases exposure to “single-incident collapse risk” (mine/drone/fast-boat event, mis-ID, or proxy action) because **more traffic** means **more opportunities** for an escalatory accident.

---

### 3) Israel’s political reaction sharpens; Lebanon remains the biggest near-term tripwire
- Axios reports Netanyahu is **angry** and Israeli allies are **raging** over the deal; Israeli officials claimed they hadn’t been allowed to review the MOU as recently as Tuesday, and the most immediate Israeli concern is **Lebanon**. **[REPORTED]** ([axios.com](https://www.axios.com/2026/06/18/israel-reaction-trump-iran-deal-netanyahu?utm_source=openai))  
- AP reporting from southern Lebanon underscores uncertainty about what “ending the war in Lebanon” means in practice, amid continued displacement/returns and reports of Israeli strikes framed as targeting Hezbollah. **[REPORTED]** ([apnews.com](https://apnews.com/article/b8fe65c97e30afc1424b8f00c3bfc377?utm_source=openai))  

**What changed / what it means:**  
The center of gravity for “deal derailment” is increasingly **Israel–Hezbollah dynamics** rather than U.S.–Iran direct fire. If Israel interprets the MOU as constraining its Lebanon operations while Washington frames it as allowing Israeli retaliation, you get a high-probability **interpretation clash** that can spill back into U.S.–Iran compliance.

---

### 4) Markets: oil slips further on signature / reopening expectations
- Reuters reporting (via Investing.com) says oil fell in early trading after the U.S.–Iran signing, reflecting expectations of reopening Hormuz and sanctions waivers on Iranian oil. **[REPORTED]** ([au.investing.com](https://au.investing.com/news/commodities-news/oil-slips-again-as-us-iran-sign-peace-deal-4493862?utm_source=openai))  

**What changed / what it means:**  
Market pricing is reinforcing the “implementation is real” narrative, which can help politically (lower energy prices) but can also raise the cost of any renewed disruption—if something snaps back, the shock could be sharper because the risk premium is being removed.
