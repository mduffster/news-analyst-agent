## Key Developments Since Prior Report (August 28)

### 1. US Strikes Larak Island — IRAN RETALIATES — ESCALATION RESUMES

**[OFFICIAL]** The US military on Sunday struck Iran's Larak Island, where forces were observed preparing to launch rockets with mines into the Strait of Hormuz, according to a US official, marking the first US strikes in over a month. US forces struck two Iranian launchers on the island.

**[OFFICIAL]** IRGC forces were observed "preparing to launch rockets with sea mines into Strait of Hormuz." Iran's Revolutionary Guards said the attack killed and wounded several soldiers and citizens and would be met with a "response and punishment" from Tehran.

**[OFFICIAL]** "Last week, CENTCOM completed clearing sea mines from the strait's international shipping routes. U.S. forces are monitoring the area closely and remain prepared to protect the free flow of commerce through this essential waterway."

*Assessment: The US strike was preemptive, targeting mine-laying operations that would have reversed CENTCOM's announced clearing of shipping lanes. This breaks the month-long pause in direct hostilities and represents a significant escalation.*

---

### 2. Iran Retaliates — Attacks US Bases in Jordan and UAE

**[OFFICIAL]** Shortly after the US strikes, the IRGC said a combined missile and drone operation targeted the King Hussein and Al Azraq US military bases in Jordan, according to Iranian media. The strikes "destroyed the technical and repair infrastructure, as well as the enemy fighter deployment sites", causing significant damage, the IRGC said in a statement. Tehran dubbed the strikes on Jordan as "a legitimate exercise of self-defence" under the UN Charter.

**[OFFICIAL]** Iran's army said it attacked the United Arab Emirates' Al Minhad Air Base with drones early on Monday, Iranian state TV reported. The Iranian army said the attack targeted US forces and helicopters stationed at the base. Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps also launched a missile-and-drone attack on US airbases in Jordan early Monday.

**[OFFICIAL]** The United Arab Emirates says it has engaged a drone coming from Iran over its waters and denied reports of an attack on its Al Menhad airbase, after the United States and Iran resumed hostilities for the first time since July. The UAE says it intercepted a drone coming from Iran and rejected 'false' reports of an attack on its Al Menhad airbase.

**[OFFICIAL]** Jordan reported intercepting eight missiles soon after the IRGC announced the attack. "Air defence systems intercepted eight missiles that breached the Kingdom's airspace at dawn today, Monday," the Jordanian military said in a statement. No injuries were reported at either base.

*Assessment: Iran's retaliation was immediate and directed at US regional bases rather than directly at US forces in the strait. The fact that Jordan and UAE intercepted the attacks without reported casualties suggests Iran's response was calibrated for symbolic impact rather than inflicting mass casualties. However, this marks the end of the month-long de-escalation.*

### 3. Iran Claims MQ-9 Reaper Shootdown — UNCONFIRMED

**[UNCONFIRMED]** In a further development on Monday, the IRGC said it shot down a US MQ-9 Reaper drone over the Strait of Hormuz.

**[REPORTED]** Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps Aerospace Force says it brought down a US MQ-9 Reaper drone east of the Strait of Hormuz, with the aircraft reportedly crashing into Gulf waters. The claim, circulated widely by Iranian state media on August 31, 2026, marks yet another chapter in what has become a brutal year for American drone operations in the region.

**[VERIFIED]** Throughout 2026, Iran has made a series of claims about downing MQ-9 Reapers during a period of escalating military friction with the United States.

*Assessment: Iranian claims of MQ-9 shootdowns have often been accurate during this conflict, with Bloomberg reporting over two dozen Reapers lost since February. However, this specific claim remains unconfirmed by US officials.*

### 4. Bessent Sanctions Banque Misr UAE — DELIVERS ON PROMISE

**[OFFICIAL]** The United States has said it will cut off the UAE operations of Banque Misr from the US financial system after accusing Egypt's second-biggest bank of doing business with the Iranian government. "Treasury promised to sever every economic lifeline Tehran has left and finally end the threat of the Iranian regime," US Secretary of the Treasury Scott Bessent said.

**[VERIFIED]** Treasury intends to revoke the access of Banque Misr UAE to U.S. financial institutions. Banque Misr UAE processed about $1.8 billion over the past two years for some 100 companies that are potentially part of Iran's shadow banking network.

**[OFFICIAL]** "The CBE affirms that this measure is limited to the bank's branches in the UAE and only to its dollar transfers," Egypt's Central Bank said in a statement. It added that the measure does not affect any other Egyptian bank and does not extend to Banque Misr's operations inside Egypt.

*Assessment: Bessent delivered on his Friday deadline promise, but chose the path of least geopolitical resistance — a UAE branch of an Egyptian bank rather than a Chinese institution. This allows escalation of sanctions rhetoric without threatening the Xi summit.*

### 5. Bessent Announces Weekly Bank Sanctions at G20 — OPERATION ECONOMIC OUTCAST ACCELERATES

**[OFFICIAL]** The U.S. Treasury Department is likely to unveil weekly new secondary sanctions aimed at increasing economic pressure on Iran, with an initial focus on banks, U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told Reuters on Sunday. After imposing penalties on the United Arab Emirates branches of Egypt's Banque Misr on Friday, Bessent said the next step may be cutting off an institution entirely from the dollar-based financial system.

**[OFFICIAL]** Bessent spoke with The Associated Press ahead of Group of 20 meetings in Asheville, North Carolina, where he will meet individually with his counterparts from the world's major economies to encourage participation in the economic isolation of Iran. "This is going to be financial violence if we have to," Bessent said.

**[OFFICIAL]** Bessent told reporters the EU had expressed support for Operation Economic Outcast "in the strongest terms" and predicted Iran's economy could feel the effects of U.S. sanctions "within weeks or months." Bessent told reporters that the European Union had made known "in the strongest terms" its support for Operation Economic Outcast. "They made it known today in the strongest terms that they supported the plan."

**[OFFICIAL]** He disagreed with some critics who have said that Iran sanctions will not be successful without targeting Chinese companies for secondary sanctions. Most of China's Iran oil purchases have been curbed by the US blockade of Iranian ports, and the amount of Iranian oil stored in tankers is dwindling, he said, adding: "Problem solved."

*Assessment: Bessent is using the G20 summit to rally international support for Iran sanctions, claiming EU backing. His dismissal of the need to sanction Chinese banks suggests protecting the Xi summit remains a priority. The weekly sanctions cadence represents a significant escalation of economic pressure.*

**[VERIFIED]** Crude oil rose more than 3% to above $86 a barrel on Monday as tensions in the Middle East escalated, with the US and Iran exchanging strikes for the first time in roughly a month and new concerns emerging over shipping through the Strait of Hormuz.

**[OFFICIAL]** Iran's Ministry of Foreign Affairs condemned the US strike on Larak Island in a statement as a "direct violation of national sovereignty and territorial integrity".

### 9. CENTCOM Mine-Clearing Confirmed — CONTEXT FOR STRIKE

**[OFFICIAL]** Navy divers and SEALs working in conjunction with air support from the Army, Air Force, Marines and Navy removed the deadly weapons from the vital commercial waterway, U.S. Central Command leader Adm. Brad Cooper said.

**[VERIFIED]** As of Aug. 18, there have been at least seven attacks on ships transiting the Strait of Hormuz during the month. All told, 61 vessels have been damaged and 25 people injured or killed in attacks on ships in the Strait of Hormuz, the Persian Gulf and the Gulf of Oman between Feb. 28 and Aug. 20.

**[VERIFIED]** Despite the successful mine clearance, Cooper said that the U.S. blockade of the Strait of Hormuz remains in effect. More than 20 warships are participating and they have turned around 75 ships and disabled three vessels since the blockade was restarted in July.

*Assessment: The US strike was specifically to prevent Iran from re-mining shipping lanes that CENTCOM had just cleared. This context frames the escalation as enforcement of the "zero tolerance" policy Trump announced last week.*
