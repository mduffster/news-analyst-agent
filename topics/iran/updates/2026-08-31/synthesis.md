## Critical Developments (August 29-31)

### 1. US Strikes Larak Island — First Military Action in a Month [OFFICIAL]

The U.S. military struck Iranian military targets to prevent the deployment of sea mines in the Strait of Hormuz. This is the first known U.S. strike in Iran since late July when President Trump paused a two-week U.S. military campaign.

"I can confirm that earlier today U.S. forces struck two Iranian launchers on Larak Island. Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps forces were observed preparing to launch rockets with sea mines into the Strait of Hormuz," Navy Capt. confirmed. CENTCOM said Revolutionary Guard forces were preparing rockets carrying sea mines for launch.

The U.S. military last week finished clearing all mines from the strait's main shipping lane. Trump said at the time that Iran has been notified that any ship or boat placing new mines will be "immediately and systematically destroyed."

The IRGC statement said the attack would "result in punishment of the aggressor."

**Assessment:** [OFFICIAL] The US strike was preemptive, directly enforcing Trump's "zero tolerance" policy on mine placement. This represents a shift back to kinetic enforcement after weeks of emphasis on economic pressure. Iran's attempted re-mining directly challenged CENTCOM's cleared shipping lanes.

### 2. Iran Retaliates — Attacks US Bases in Jordan and UAE [OFFICIAL]

Iran on Monday struck back at the U.S., carrying out attacks on American military assets at bases in the United Arab Emirates and Jordan after U.S. forces fired at two Iranian rocket launchers over the weekend.

Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps launched drones and missiles at the King Hussein and Al Azraq military bases in Jordan. The IRGC targeted U.S. forces and helicopters at Muwaffaq Salti Air Base in the UAE hours later. The Iranian military described its attack in Jordan as a "necessary and lawful answer" to the U.S. attack on Larak Island.

Jordan reported intercepting 8 missiles. According to the IRGC, U.S. military facilities "King Hussein" and "Al-Azraq" in Jordan were attacked with ballistic missiles. The Iranian side stated that the targets were technical infrastructure and locations of military aircraft.

The UAE air force responded to a drone detected over territorial waters approaching from Iran, but denied that Al Minhad itself had been attacked, the Emirati defense ministry said.

Iran's foreign ministry said the US bases in Jordan had been used to launch and support an earlier attack on Larak Island that killed and wounded members of Iran's armed forces and civilians. It said Iran would respond decisively to further military aggression.

**Assessment:** [OFFICIAL] Iran's retaliation was swift and geographically dispersed — targeting Jordan and UAE rather than concentrating in the Hormuz theatre. This may be in part to keep Iran from angering several key Gulf countries. Iran doesn't want to anger Saudi Arabia after Saudi Arabia, Turkey, and Pakistan entered into a new pact. Tehran wants to get along with Qatar and Oman, so it prefers to target the UAE and Jordan. Jordan's successful interception and UAE's denial of base damage suggests Iranian strikes were either intercepted or calibrated for limited impact.

### 3. IRGC Claims Tanker Hit Mines — CENTCOM Denies [DISPUTED]

An emergency occurred in the strategically important Strait of Hormuz. In the southern part of the strait, a giant supertanker encountered sea mines and exploded. This was officially reported by Iran's influential Fars news agency, citing the press service of the IRGC.

The name of the vessel and its flag state were omitted from the official announcement. In the statement published by Iranian state media, the IRGC issued an explicit warning: "Such will be the fate of any vessel that violates security regulations in the Strait of Hormuz."

United States Central Command adamantly denied Iranian claims that a tanker was disabled after striking two mines. "This is FALSE. No ships have hit mines in the Strait of Hormuz."

Iranian media also reported that a tanker had struck two mines and that authorities seized a bulk carrier near Bandar Abbas, although US Central Command said no vessels had hit mines in Hormuz.

**Assessment:** [DISPUTED] The conflicting claims create fog around actual conditions in the strait. CENTCOM's flat denial contrasts with detailed IRGC claims. This may be information warfare from Iran to discourage shipping despite cleared lanes, or CENTCOM may be downplaying an incident to maintain confidence in the strait's navigability. No independent verification available.

### 4. Bessent at G20: Weekly Sanctions, EU Support Claimed [OFFICIAL]

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent is meeting with finance ministers of the world's largest economies this week at a time when the United States has disrupted the international order with aggressive tariffs on major allies and an unpopular war on Iran that is keeping gas prices high for consumers.

"You're going to see a lot more of these every week," Bessent said ahead of a Group of 20 finance leaders meeting.

Bessent said he intends to drive the message home to G20 finance ministers and central bank governors to cut economic ties to Iran, or face secondary sanctions. The Treasury launched the effort, dubbed Operation Economic Outcast, last week. "There can be no leakage," Bessent said. "You're either with us or you're with the Iranians."

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent opened the Group of 20 finance ministers summit in Asheville by claiming broad European backing for Washington's Iran sanctions campaign. Bessent told reporters that the European Union had made known "in the strongest terms" its support for Operation Economic Outcast. "They made it known today in the strongest terms that they supported the plan," Bessent said.

However, he said that he disagreed with some critics who have said that Iran sanctions will not be successful without targeting Chinese companies for secondary sanctions. Most of China's Iran oil purchases have been curbed by the U.S. blockade of Iranian ports, adding: "Problem solved."

Inclusion of CEOs in the G20 finance ministers meeting marks a first, according to a senior Treasury official. Executives in attendance include Goldman Sachs CEO David Solomon, JPMorgan CEO Jamie Dimon, incoming Truist Bank CEO Mike Lyons, Eli Lilly CEO David Ricks, Deere & Co. CEO John May, Medtronic CEO Geoff Martha, and 3M CEO Bill Brown.

**Assessment:** [OFFICIAL] Bessent's G20 diplomacy is proceeding despite the military escalation. His claim of EU "strongest terms" support for Operation Economic Outcast, if accurate, represents significant coalition building. His dismissal of the need for Chinese sanctions ("Problem solved") clearly protects the September 24 Xi visit. The weekly sanctions cadence institutionalizes economic warfare.
