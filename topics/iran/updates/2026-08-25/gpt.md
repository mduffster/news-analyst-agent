### 1) “Economic D‑Day” happened — but it’s now clearly a **slow-burn secondary-sanctions campaign**, not a one-shot shock
- The U.S. rolled out what Treasury called **“Operation Economic Outcast”**, paired with an expanded **secondary-sanctions** posture meant to pressure *non‑Iran* actors who keep Iran economically afloat. **[REPORTED]** ([cbsnews.com](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/bessent-press-conference-iran-sanctions-economic-d-day/?utm_source=openai))  
- Coverage converges that it included **dozens of Iran-linked designations** (reported “more than 60” / “nearly 60”), and widened the *categories* of secondary sanctions to areas repeatedly mentioned across reporting: **digital assets, gold, aviation, technology, and shipping**. **[REPORTED]** ([axios.com](https://www.axios.com/newsletters/axios-am-1e8a4fa0-9fd4-11f1-8c73-a9e5b414cf00?utm_source=openai))  
- A key tactical detail: Treasury messaging emphasized **warnings / time to unwind ties** rather than immediately slapping China/major partners with headline secondary sanctions—suggesting phased escalation and selective enforcement. **[VERIFIED]** (multiple mainstream outlets consistent on “ultimatum/slow squeeze” framing) ([apnews.com](https://apnews.com/article/e367634d8853c8fa4a341132cd577f31?utm_source=openai))  

**What changed / what it means:**  
- **Change:** The sanctions are now **real policy**, with a defined campaign structure (named operation + expanded secondary-sanctions categories + large designation batch), but the “D‑Day” branding appears **overstated versus immediate bite**. **[VERIFIED]** ([apnews.com](https://apnews.com/article/e367634d8853c8fa4a341132cd577f31?utm_source=openai))  
- **Meaning:** This tilts the conflict’s center of gravity further toward **prolonged enforcement/retaliation cycles** (shipping finance, interdiction, deniable attacks) rather than a discrete kinetic “phase” that cleanly ends.
