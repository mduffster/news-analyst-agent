## Critical Developments (August 4)

### 1. CRITICAL: Bessent Claims Hormuz Deal "Today or Tomorrow" — Markets React [OFFICIAL]

US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said a deal between the US and Iran over control of the Strait of Hormuz could come as soon as Tuesday, sending oil prices plunging. "We're in talks with the Iranians and I think there is a chance we may have a deal today or tomorrow to open the strait," Bessent said on CNBC.

Bessent said: "There is a chance we may have a deal today or tomorrow to open the Strait and move towards a more normalized position in this conflict." The Treasury Secretary said the deal would allow freedom of movement in the strait when asked whether Iran would be allowed to charge a toll.

Bessent said hundreds, possibly more than 1,000 ships are waiting to depart, with the disruption affecting not only energy shipments but also fertilizers, refined petroleum products, and industrial gases.

*Assessment: [OFFICIAL] This is the most concrete timeline yet from a senior US official. However, Bessent's insistence on "freedom of movement" with no tolls directly contradicts Iran's stated position. The gap between US demands and Iranian conditions remains the core obstacle to any deal.*

---

### 2. CRITICAL: Iran Hardliner Rezaei Threatens to Attack US Warships [OFFICIAL]

Mohsen Rezaei, described as a military adviser to Mojtaba Khamenei, stated in an interview with Islamic Republic of Iran Broadcasting (IRIB) on Monday evening, August 3, that the Iranian government will under no circumstances permit the creation of a second corridor in the Strait of Hormuz. He threatened that if U.S. warships or military forces entered the area, they would be targeted.

In an interview with Iran's SNN TV channel, Major General Rezaei said: "Even if they bring a warship, we will strike that warship. If they bring military forces, we will strike them." Rezaei said many ships now heed Iran's warnings and turn back, while vessels pushed by the Americans "suffer some damage and then turn back themselves."

"The US had accepted Iran's arrangements for the Strait of Hormuz under the [Islamabad] MoU, but later violated the agreement. The US not only breached the MoU but also launched attacks without first referring the dispute to the designated dispute resolution panel," Rezaee said, according to Press TV.

*Assessment: [OFFICIAL] This represents significant hardliner pushback from within Iran's military establishment. Rezaei's threats directly contradict the notion of an imminent deal allowing "freedom of movement." The hardliner-moderate split in Tehran remains a key obstacle.*

### 3. SIGNIFICANT: Cargo Ship Struck in Hormuz — Third Engineer Missing [VERIFIED]

On Aug 3, 2026, at around 10 p.m. UTC, the 'Minoan Pioneer', enroute from São Francisco do Sul to Fujairah, was struck by an unidentified projectile while sailing northbound through the Strait of Hormuz, approximately 20 nautical miles northeast of Khasab. The projectile struck the vessel's engine room, causing a complete blackout. A fire subsequently broke out in the accommodation block. The 3rd engineer was reported missing, as the crew attempted to contain the blaze and called for assistance.

The latest strike comes only one day after two other dangerous incidents involving tankers in Omani territorial waters. The vessels Egypt Prosperity and On Pride reported explosions or warning shots at different locations within the Strait, although they ultimately sustained no damage.

No group has claimed responsibility for the attack, which marked the 4th maritime security incident reported close to Oman's Musandam Peninsula within five days and underlined the dangers ships attempting to use the Omani side of the strait were facing.

*Assessment: [VERIFIED] Iran continues striking vessels in the very corridor it's simultaneously negotiating with Oman to keep open. This is the fourth incident near the Musandam Peninsula in five days. The pattern suggests Iran is maintaining maximum leverage during talks — or demonstrating that no route will be safe without Tehran's explicit approval.*

### 4. SIGNIFICANT: Oil Crashes Below $80 — S&P 500 Hits Record High [VERIFIED]

Oil slid as optimism grew that the US and Iran are moving closer to a deal to reopen the vital Strait of Hormuz chokepoint. Brent dropped more than 4% below $80 a barrel, reversing earlier gains.

Crude Oil fell to 76.66 USD/Bbl on August 4, 2026, down 4.58% from the previous day.

Wall Street's benchmark S&P 500 hit an intraday record high for the first time since early June on Tuesday, boosted by upbeat AI-driven forecasts from Caterpillar and Palantir and hopes for an imminent deal to end tensions in the Middle East. Recent gains on Wall Street have been driven by strong earnings from AI heavyweights such as Amazon and Microsoft. The blue-chip Dow was also trading at a fresh intraday record high.

The Dow soared 655 points, or 1.2%, extending gains into record territory after closing at an all-time high on Monday, its first since July 6. The tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite rose 1%, and is about 4% away from its last record high set in early June.

*Assessment: [VERIFIED] Markets are aggressively pricing in a deal. Brent has fallen from ~$92 last week to below $80 — a dramatic ~13% decline. The S&P 500 breaking June 2 records signals investor confidence. However, this represents hope, not reality. If talks fail, expect sharp reversals.*

### 5. SIGNIFICANT: Qatar Confirms Draft Agreements "Being Circulated" [OFFICIAL]

Efforts to resolve the US-Iran conflict are "in very progressive stages," Qatar's Foreign Ministry said, adding that drafts of a potential agreement are "being circulated" but that there are no direct talks between the two sides.

Qatar's Foreign Ministry says diplomatic efforts are ongoing to find a solution to the US-Iran conflict and all mediators are working closely to facilitate negotiations and exchange proposals between the countries.

The Trump administration is signaling optimism on a deal for the crucial waterway, with US Secretary of State Marco Rubio saying there has been progress "but not finality" on an agreement and US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent floating a potential consensus by "today or tomorrow." Shipping analysts are less upbeat.

*Assessment: [OFFICIAL] Qatar's emergence as an active mediator alongside Oman adds a second diplomatic channel. The "drafts being circulated" language suggests concrete proposals are in play — but the acknowledgment of "no direct talks" underscores the indirect, fragile nature of the process.*

### 6. SIGNIFICANT: US Missile Stockpiles "Virtually Depleted" [REPORTED]

Reuters reports that key stockpiles of Precision Strike Missiles and Army Tactical Missile Systems have "virtually all" been used up. Trump's decision last week not to launch new strikes on Iran was partly due to warnings from military advisers about the depleted missile stockpiles, as well as pressure from U.S. allies in the Persian Gulf.

The United States has depleted a significant portion of its stockpile of long range precision missiles during its five month military campaign against Iran, raising concerns over its preparedness for future conflicts with major powers such as China and Russia.

The 39-day bombing and air defense campaign against Iran depleted inventories of key U.S. munitions stockpiles, as a previous CSIS analysis detailed. The United States has enough munitions for any plausible scenario in the Iran war, but the depleted inventories have created a window of vulnerability for a potential Western Pacific conflict. The time needed to rebuild those inventories has thus become a major concern.

*Assessment: [REPORTED] This represents a significant material constraint on US escalation options. While sufficient for the current conflict, depleted stockpiles reinforce the shift toward diplomacy over sustained high-intensity strikes — and create strategic risk if a Pacific contingency arises.*

### 7. MODERATE: Houthis Strike Saudi Najran Airport [REPORTED]

Yemen's Houthi movement has claimed responsibility for a drone strike targeting Najran Airport in southern Saudi Arabia. According to a statement published on the official Telegram channel of the Ansar Allah movement, the attack was carried out using an unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV). The Houthis said the strike was launched in response to what they described as Saudi Arabia's use of drones to violate Yemeni airspace over the Saada and Hajjah governorates.

There was no immediate confirmation from Saudi officials. The Houthis' military spokesman Yahya Saree said the attack achieved a "precise strike" and was conducted in response to what the group said were Saudi drone incursions into Yemeni airspace over Saada and Hajjah governates.

*Assessment: [REPORTED — unconfirmed by Saudi authorities] The Saudi-Houthi front continues escalating despite the broader US-Iran diplomatic push. The 14-nation Red Sea coalition announced by Riyadh has not yet produced operational results, and Houthi claims require verification.*

### 8. MODERATE: Iran-Oman Negotiations Continue — Core Gap Remains [OFFICIAL]

Iran's foreign minister says negotiations with Oman on the Strait of Hormuz are entering their "final stages", as Tehran insists the vital waterway will never return to its pre-war status. In a post on Telegram on Sunday, Abbas Araghchi said that in a report he presented to the cabinet, he described the negotiations between Tehran and Muscat as "on the way to being finalised".

Foreign Ministry spokesman, Esmail Baghaei, said that Tehran and Muscat were close to reaching a deal on a new maritime route through Hormuz, separate from existing corridors. "We are now going to reach an understanding on a route acceptable to both sides – neither the northern route nor the southern route – but one that respects the sovereign rights of both sides and safeguards our national interests and security," Baghaei said.

Iran has shown no sign of allowing vessels free passage through the Strait of Hormuz, which Trump has demanded. Instead, Iran said it's talking to Oman about a new route and not about whether the strait as a whole will be open or closed.

*Assessment: [OFFICIAL] The Iran-Oman track continues advancing on its own terms. However, Iran's insistence that this has "nothing to do with whether the Strait of Hormuz is reopened or remains closed" creates tension with US demands for full "freedom of movement."*
