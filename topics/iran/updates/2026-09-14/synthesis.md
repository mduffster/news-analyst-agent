## Critical Developments (September 12-14)

### 1. CRITICAL: Houthis Complete Control of All Four Red Sea Archipelagos — Hanish Islands Seized [VERIFIED]

Yemen's Iran-backed Houthi rebels captured more strategic islands in the southern Red Sea, government and Houthi officials said Monday, strengthening their control of a major maritime shipping route. The capture of the islands of Greater and Lesser Hanish was the latest in the rebels' swift advance around the Bab el-Mandeb Strait.

The Houthis deployed on the Hanish islands, 160 kilometres north of Bab el-Mandeb, after hundreds of government-allied forces withdrew from the archipelago. The group officially known as Ansar Allah captured the archipelago after seizing the nearby mainland port city of Mokha and the island of Perim, inside the Bab el-Mandeb Strait.

The Hanish islands, newly captured by the Houthis, had been controlled by the UAE-backed allies. However, the UAE dismantled its military infrastructure there earlier this year.

**Assessment:** [VERIFIED] This completes Houthi control of all four strategic archipelagos in the Bab al-Mandab region (Zuqar, Perim/Mayyun, Greater Hanish, Lesser Hanish). The UAE's earlier dismantling of military infrastructure facilitated this collapse. Like Hormuz, which is located at the mouth of the Persian Gulf, the Bab el-Mandeb Strait — on the opposite side of the Arabian Peninsula — is a chokepoint that connects the Red Sea to the open ocean and Saudi Arabia's key Asian markets. This represents the most significant change to Red Sea geography since the 2015 Saudi intervention.

### 2. CRITICAL: East-West Pipeline Repairs Confirmed — 3 to 5 Weeks [VERIFIED]

A crucial Saudi Arabian oil pipeline attacked last week could remain mostly out of service for three to five weeks while repairs are carried out, two officials told the Associated Press. The officials said repairs would include work at a major pumping facility and that the pipeline could operate partially while repair teams work on the damage.

Yet another contingency plan for the global oil market – buffeted by more than six months of war between the United States and Iran – has come under serious threat. On Friday, Saudi Arabia announced that it had shuttered its East-West Pipeline, blaming drone attacks from inside Iraq that it said had caused "injuries and some damage."

Sources familiar with the incident told the Reuters news agency that repairs could take five to six weeks, while another source said operations could restart sooner. Saudi officials said drones hit the pipeline in two areas around Riyadh and Medina.

**Assessment:** [VERIFIED] This transforms what was "unconfirmed" on September 11 into a confirmed major disruption. The 3-5 week repair timeline means Saudi Arabia's primary Hormuz bypass will be degraded through at least mid-October — past the critical pre-midterm period. Last week, Iran-backed Houthi rebels in Yemen captured the Red Sea port city of Mocha and Perim, an island located at the mouth of the Bab al-Mandeb strait. The combination of the Houthis' tightening grip over shipping in the Red Sea and Friday's pipeline closure have ignited fears that the main escape valves for the region's oil are slamming shut.

### 3. MAJOR: Iran-Gulf Hormuz Talks Postponed — Saudi Arabia Requests Delay [VERIFIED]

Iranian officials say a meeting with Gulf states to discuss the Strait of Hormuz has been postponed, dealing a blow to efforts to reopen the strait.

Talks were set to take place in Oman on Monday but was delayed "for now" after a request from Saudi Arabia, according to Iran's foreign ministry. That was partly down to the kingdom's frustration over continued attacks on its territory by Iran-supported groups such as the Houthis, said one person familiar with the matter.

US media outlets Axios and CNN reported that Saudi Arabia had submitted amendments to the Iran-Oman proposal for Hormuz because it had concerns that the wording would establish a new status quo which would be unacceptable to Riyadh. It comes days after Saudi Arabia shut down its critical East-West oil pipeline – the kingdom's key route for energy exports – after drone attacks in the area originating from Iraq.

Trump administration officials have privately told those in the Gulf region that they want to refocus any future US-Iran negotiations on Tehran's nuclear program and not reopening the strait. "I'm not trying to force Iran to the bargaining table," Trump wrote on Truth Social. "I like our position now much better, with almost total control of the Hormuz Strait, and their economy totally collapsing."

**Assessment:** [VERIFIED] This is a significant diplomatic setback. The main near-term pathway to Hormuz reopening has been blocked. Saudi frustration with Iranian proxy attacks — the pipeline strike and Houthi advance — has hardened Riyadh's position. The Trump administration's focus on nuclear issues rather than Hormuz reopening suggests Washington is not prioritizing economic relief.

### 7. NOTABLE: Israel-Lebanon Rome Talks Delayed to October [VERIFIED]

The next round of negotiations between Israel and Lebanon in Rome have been delayed until October, a US official said. Israel and Lebanon had signed a framework peace deal after US-mediated talks in June.

The delay was prompted by several factors that made it impossible for key people to attend, a source familiar with the planning told AFP. Those included preparations for the upcoming U.N. General Assembly, Jewish holidays, and a Paris conference in support of the Lebanese Armed Forces.

"They don't want to discuss the pilot zones from now until the end of October, because of the elections. Everything is elections, elections," a Lebanese official said.

Its core provisions center on the disarmament of Hezbollah, a phased, gradual withdrawal of Israeli forces from southern Lebanon, and the deployment of the Lebanese army into that vacated territory. Since April, Israel and Lebanon have held seven rounds of direct negotiations under this framework, five in Washington and the two most recent in Rome.

**Assessment:** [VERIFIED] The Lebanon diplomatic track is effectively paused through Israeli elections in October. The Ali al-Taher destruction occurred amid this pause, suggesting Israel is using the diplomatic lull to create facts on the ground.
