## Key Developments Since Prior Report (September 11)

### 1. CRITICAL: Houthis Complete Control of All Four Red Sea Archipelagos — Hanish Islands Seized

**[VERIFIED]** Yemen's Houthi rebels have seized the strategic islands of Greater and Lesser Hanish in the southern Red Sea, bolstering the Iranian-backed rebels' ability to control a key maritime shipping route.

**[VERIFIED]** The Houthis deployed on the Hanish islands, 160 kilometres north of Bab el-Mandeb, after hundreds of government-allied forces withdrew from the archipelago. The group officially known as Ansar Allah captured the archipelago after seizing the nearby mainland port city of Mokha and the island of Perim, inside the Bab el-Mandeb Strait.

**[VERIFIED]** The Hanish islands, newly captured by the Houthis, had been controlled by the UAE-backed allies. However, the UAE dismantled its military infrastructure there earlier this year.

*Assessment: This completes the Houthi takeover of all four strategic island groups in the Bab al-Mandab region (Zuqar, Perim/Mayyun, Greater Hanish, Lesser Hanish). The ~2,000 government troops previously reported besieged have apparently withdrawn. The UAE's earlier dismantling of military infrastructure facilitated this collapse.*

### 2. CRITICAL: East-West Pipeline Repairs Confirmed — 3 to 5 Weeks Out of Service

**[VERIFIED]** On 10–11 September 2026, a series of drone strikes launched from Iraqi territory hit Saudi Arabia's East–West Crude Oil Pipeline amid the wider 2026 Iran war. Saudi Arabia shut down the pipeline as a precautionary measure following the attack, temporarily removing its main alternative route for exporting crude oil while the Strait of Hormuz remained effectively closed by Iran.

**[REPORTED]** Repairs to Saudi Arabia's 1,200-kilometre East-West Pipeline could take three to five weeks, including work at a major pumping facility, two regional officials briefed on the matter told The Associated Press on Monday. One official said the pipeline could operate partially while repairs are carried out.

**[VERIFIED]** Saudi Arabia's Ministry of Energy confirmed in a statement posted to its website Friday that the pipeline had been attacked and "was shut down as a precautionary measure." The Saudi Ministry of Foreign Affairs later released a statement condemning "the attack on the East-West Pipeline in the Riyadh and Madinah regions by several drones coming from Iraq, which resulted in injuries and some damage that is currently being addressed."

**[VERIFIED]** With Iran effectively shuttering the Strait of Hormuz amid the ongoing war between the United States and Iran, Saudi Arabia had rerouted about 5 million barrels of oil per day that would otherwise have moved through the Gulf, sending it instead via the 1,200 km (750 mi) East–West pipeline to the Red Sea port of Yanbu.

*Assessment: What was "unconfirmed" on September 11 is now officially confirmed with repair timeline. The 3-5 week repair estimate means Saudi Arabia's primary Hormuz bypass will be degraded through at least mid-October — past the crucial pre-midterm period.*

### 3. MAJOR: Iran-Gulf Hormuz Talks Postponed — Saudi Arabia Requests Delay

**[VERIFIED]** A meeting between Iran and several Gulf Arab nations over a temporary shipping lane through the Strait of Hormuz was postponed, highlighting tensions with the Islamic Republic. Talks were set to take place in Oman on Monday but was delayed "for now" after a request from Saudi Arabia, according to Iran's foreign ministry.

**[REPORTED]** That was partly down to the kingdom's frustration over continued attacks on its territory by Iran-supported groups such as the Houthis, said one person familiar with the matter.

**[VERIFIED]** Saudi Arabia submitted amendments because it had concerns that the proposed language could effectively establish a new status quo that would not be acceptable to it or the other Gulf Cooperation Council states.

**[OFFICIAL]** Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmail Baghaei said Monday Saudi Arabia had "demanded" that a meeting bringing together Iran with other regional countries to discuss the Strait of Hormuz be scrapped "for the time being." The spokesman said an agreement between Iran and Oman on the Strait of Hormuz has been finalized and it will be announced later.

*Assessment: This is a significant diplomatic setback. The Iran-Oman bilateral deal appears ready but cannot proceed without GCC buy-in. Saudi Arabia's frustration with Iranian proxy attacks is now directly blocking the main pathway to Hormuz reopening. The Houthi advance and pipeline attack have hardened Saudi positions.*

### 4. MAJOR: Oil Prices Surge Past $110

**[VERIFIED]** As of 9 a.m. Eastern Time today, oil sold for $110.42 per barrel (using Brent as the benchmark). That's 79 cents higher than yesterday morning and approximately a $43.30 rise over the past year.

**[VERIFIED]** Brent rose to 108.34 USD/Bbl on September 14, 2026, up 3.56% from the previous day. Over the past month, Brent's price has risen 19.22%, and is up 60.64% compared to the same time last year.

**[VERIFIED]** The events deepened the prolonged halt of tanker flows from the region that have driven major oil producers to halt their operations, with Saudi output recently dropping to its lowest since 1990.

*Assessment: Oil has broken the $110 barrier — a significant psychological and economic threshold. The 3-5 week pipeline repair timeline ensures continued price pressure through at least mid-October.*

### 5. SIGNIFICANT: China Calls Houthi Attacks "Unacceptable"

**[OFFICIAL]** "China is deeply concerned about the casualties of innocent civilians and the damage to energy facilities and other civilian infrastructures caused by recent escalation in Yemen and the Red Sea," Foreign Ministry spokesman Guo Jiakun told reporters in Beijing. He said attacks against civilian infrastructure that bears on people's livelihoods "are unacceptable."

**[OFFICIAL]** "The sovereignty and security of all countries should be respected, and the only way out is to resolve disputes through political and diplomatic means," Guo said.

*Assessment: This is notable Chinese diplomatic intervention. Beijing has significant oil import interests and reportedly maintains direct communication with the Houthis. However, this statement stops short of condemning Iran and does not threaten any concrete action.*

### 6. NOTABLE: Iran Has NOT Retaliated for Ali al-Taher Destruction

**[VERIFIED]** The Lebanese front produced no fire in either direction for a second consecutive day. No Hezbollah attack on Israeli forces was reported, no Israeli strike in Lebanon was reported, and no Lebanese casualty was reported. The party has not fired since Israel took the Ali al-Taher ridge on 4 September; it did not fire while eight of its tunnels running 5.4 kilometers beneath that ridge and the Beaufort Castle area were destroyed with more than 1,100 tonnes of explosives in a demolition that registered as a magnitude 4.1 tremor.

**[VERIFIED]** Iran has yet to retaliate over the latest developments in Ali al Taher.

**[VERIFIED]** Iranian leaders may also prefer a limited response that employs a small number of munitions due to the reported degradation of Iran's ballistic missile capabilities following repeated combined force strikes on Iranian missile production sites, stockpiles, and launchers since at least February 2026. ISW-CTP reported on September 2 that the roughly 50 percent reported failure rate of Iranian ballistic missiles fired at Jordan in recent days may reflect degraded longer-range missile capabilities.

*Assessment: Iran explicitly warned the US it would respond to an Ali al-Taher offensive with a "large-scale attack" on Israel. That attack has not materialized. This may reflect degraded Iranian missile capabilities, strategic calculation, or both. Either way, Israel proceeded with the tunnel destruction and Iran's deterrence credibility has taken a hit.*

### 7. NOTABLE: Israel-Lebanon Rome Talks Delayed to October

**[VERIFIED]** The next round of negotiations between Israel and Lebanon in Rome have been delayed until October, a US official said.

**[VERIFIED]** Those included preparations for the upcoming U.N. General Assembly, Jewish holidays, and a Paris conference in support of the Lebanese Armed Forces.

**[VERIFIED]** Its core provisions center on the disarmament of Hezbollah, a phased, gradual withdrawal of Israeli forces from southern Lebanon, and the deployment of the Lebanese army into that vacated territory, beginning with designated "pilot zones" meant to test whether the arrangement can actually hold before expanding further. Since April, Israel and Lebanon have held seven rounds of direct negotiations under this framework, five in Washington and the two most recent in Rome.

**[REPORTED]** "They don't want to discuss the pilot zones from now until the end of October, because of the elections. Everything is elections, elections," a Lebanese official said.

*Assessment: The Lebanon diplomatic track is effectively paused through the Israeli elections. The Ali al-Taher destruction occurred amid this pause, suggesting Israel is using the window to create facts on the ground before resuming negotiations.*

### 8. UPDATE: Houthis Continue Attacks on Saudi Arabia

**[VERIFIED]** The Houthis also launched a fresh barrage of missile and drone attacks on Saudi military targets on Monday. The Houthis said they fired dozens of drones and missiles at the kingdom — which backs the Yemeni government — prompting the Saudi Civil Defence to issue brief alerts in the neighbouring cities of Khamis Mushait and Abha.

**[VERIFIED]** At least 73 civilians were injured in recent attacks, according to the Saudi-led military coalition in Yemen.

**[VERIFIED]** The UN has estimated that more than 80,000 people have been displaced in the past two weeks, including over 2,000 people who fled by boat to Djibouti. The fighting threatens to reignite a war that for more than a decade brought disaster to Yemen, killing more than 150,000 people and driving the country to the brink of famine.

## Emerging Signals

**Complete Houthi Maritime Control:** The seizure of all four Red Sea island archipelagos gives Houthis optimal positioning across the entire Bab al-Mandab region. Houthi forces are now positioned just 20 kilometres (12 miles) from the African coast and essentially control the strategically significant Bab al-Mandeb strait. This represents the most significant change to Red Sea geography since the 2015 Saudi intervention.

**China's Intervention:** Beijing's statement calling Houthi attacks "unacceptable" is notable but likely insufficient to change Houthi behavior. Beijing opened direct talks with the Houthis after the July blockade and now clears its tankers one ship at a time, keeping Tehran informed. China has leverage but appears reluctant to use it.

**Iran's Strategic Patience:** Tehran's non-response to Ali al-Taher suggests either capability constraints or strategic calculation that proxy gains (Houthis, Iraq militias) compensate for Hezbollah losses. The Iran-Oman bilateral deal being "finalized" suggests Tehran is focused on diplomatic pathways rather than military escalation.

**Diplomatic Paralysis:** Both the Israel-Lebanon talks and Iran-Gulf Hormuz talks are now postponed. The October Israeli elections and November US midterms create a period where no major diplomatic breakthroughs are likely.
