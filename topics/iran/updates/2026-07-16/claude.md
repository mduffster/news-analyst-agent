### Key Developments Since Yesterday

#### 1. US Strikes Expand to Tehran Region for First Time

**[VERIFIED]** The US military has continued strikes against Iran, hitting targets further to the country's north as well as close to capital Tehran for the first time in the latest round of violence.

**[VERIFIED]** The US military said it completed another wave of overnight strikes, hitting Iranian command centres, air defence systems, missile and drone capabilities — and for the first time in the current round of fighting, targets around Tehran and further north in the country.

**[VERIFIED]** Air defences were activated in Tehran and neighbouring Pakdasht and Parchin on Thursday.

**[VERIFIED]** Iranian state-run media reported strikes near Tehran, Qeshm Island, the southern port city of Chabahar and Semnan province, home to key ballistic missile production facilities and the Islamic Republic's space programme.

**[REPORTED]** This was the second reported US attack on northern Iran within a week. "The main building of a civilian airport in Semnan was attacked by the Americans," Al Jazeera reported. "There has been minor damage at the airport. Also, in the same city, a storage facility was hit."

**[OFFICIAL]** Iranian authorities said there were no casualties in Pakdasht or at the Parchin military complex near Tehran. In the northern Iranian city of Semnan, the IRIB, citing a senior security official, said there were no deaths or injuries following an attack.

*Assessment: US strikes are now reaching deeper into Iran, hitting areas near the capital and Semnan province (home to ballistic missile facilities). This represents a significant geographic expansion of targeting — though still focused on military assets, not civilian infrastructure.*

#### 2. Iran Issues Explicit Threat on Regional Infrastructure

**[OFFICIAL]** The Khatam-al Anbiya Central Headquarters, the body responsible for coordinating and commanding the armed forces of the Islamic Republic during wartime, announced that if the United States attacks Iran's infrastructure, "all infrastructure in the region" will be targeted in retaliatory strikes. In a statement published early Thursday, July 16, the headquarters also emphasized that the Strait of Hormuz remains a "red line" for the Islamic Republic, and that the US will "under no circumstances and in no way" be permitted to interfere in the waterway. "Now that the enemy has targeted Iran's oil and gas export routes, if aggression against the country's infrastructure continues, all infrastructure serving the interests of the United States and its allies in the region will be crushed."

**[OFFICIAL]** Iranian military spokesman Zolfaqari said "If the recent threats by the president of the United States to target the infrastructure of the Islamic Republic are carried out... all infrastructure across the region that has so far remained intact solely because of Iran's restraint will be crushed under the strikes of Iran's armed forces." He added that Iran's response would not be "proportional" but "superior," warning that any retaliation would be "more severe, more extensive, and more devastating than ever before."

*Assessment: Iran has explicitly linked Trump's infrastructure threats to a counter-threat against "all infrastructure in the region" — signaling it will target Gulf state energy facilities, desalination plants, and other critical infrastructure. This is the clearest warning yet of potential economic warfare expansion.*

#### 3. Casualties Rise — Hospital Evacuated in Ahvaz

**[OFFICIAL]** At least 35 people have died, and more than 300 have been wounded in the latest wave of attacks that began last Wednesday after CENTCOM launched strikes on Iranian port cities close to the Strait of Hormuz.

**[REPORTED]** A hospital in Ahvaz was forced to evacuate 211 patients after reportedly being hit in the strikes.

**[REPORTED]** Iran's Ministry of Health said the attack hit areas near Boghayi Hospital 2, a facility dedicated to treating cancer patients. In a post on X, a ministry official said, "Moments ago, the areas near Boghayi Hospital 2 (dedicated to cancer patients) in Ahvaz were hit by a missile attack. Some patients and their companions, out of fear from the intense sound and severe tremors, have fled the hospital, and now only the most critically ill patients remain!"

**[REPORTED]** A doctor at the hospital described scenes of panic and desperation following the strike, saying the blast caused extensive damage and forced an emergency evacuation of vulnerable patients. "Some children still had IV lines attached. Some patients were taken out in wheelchairs, while others were supported under the arms and carried to safety by medical staff and attendants."

**[UNCONFIRMED]** Iranian officials specifically identified the affected medical facility as Boghayi Hospital 2, a cancer hospital. There has been no independent verification of the claims.

*Assessment: Iranian casualty figures have risen from 30+ to 35+ dead, with 300+ wounded (up from 260). The hospital evacuation — whether the hospital was directly hit or damaged by nearby strikes — is generating significant media attention and may fuel international pressure.*

#### 4. Vance Accuses Israeli Officials of Manipulation Campaign

**[OFFICIAL]** VP JD Vance said in his Joe Rogan interview: "I know beyond a shadow of a doubt that there have been people within the Israeli government who are trying to, like, actually shift us away from that policy because they want to continue the military campaign." He added: "There are some people within their system, we know beyond a shadow of a doubt, who are manipulating and trying to change American public opinion to keep the war going on indefinitely. Again, not towards any objective, but just indefinitely."

**[OFFICIAL]** Vance pointed to a Time magazine article outlining what he referred to as a "very discreet, extremely well-funded campaign to try to derail the negotiation and try to derail the deal." The article claimed that former Trump campaign adviser Brad Parscale had been paid millions by the Israeli government to astroturf opposition to Trump's attempts at peace among online influencers. "They're attacking me obsessively, saying that we should not be negotiating with Iran."

**[REPORTED]** The Wall Street Journal reported Israel allocated hundreds of millions of dollars, including a $45 million lobbying contract to Brad Parscale, to bolster US war support. Parscale denied allegations that funds were used to pay online influencers who criticized Vance.

**[OFFICIAL]** Vance claimed there is a divide in Iran's leadership between pragmatists who want to make a deal with the US and hardliners who do not. According to Vance, the hardliners "freaked out" after the MOU was reached because they saw how much oil the US was able to get out of the Strait of Hormuz and began targeting ships there as a result. "They've basically said, 'We're going to try to shut this thing down. We're scared about losing our leverage.' The pragmatists in their system are saying, 'This was a mistake. Let's keep on talking.'"

*Assessment: This is extraordinary public friction between the Vice President and Israeli officials. Vance's comments mark a significant escalation in the US-Israel dynamic and suggest deep internal tensions within the Trump administration about the war's direction.*

#### 5. Iran Releases American Woman — Trump Praises "Gesture of Goodwill"

**[VERIFIED]** US citizen Dena Karari has been released after being detained in Iran since December 2024 and is now on her way back to the United States, according to her lawyer, Jared Genser. President Donald Trump said in a Truth Social post that Iran allowed an American citizen who was "wrongfully detained" in December 2024 to leave the country.

**[OFFICIAL]** "The United States of America appreciates this gesture of Goodwill by Iran!" Trump said.

**[REPORTED]** Genser called it a "minor miracle" that she was able to leave Iran in the middle of a war, but also because of her ailing health. He told CNN the 53-year-old Karari had suffered a massive heart attack last week, and there were significant concerns about her condition moving forward.

**[REPORTED]** There are other Americans who remain detained in Iran, including two who have been designated as wrongfully detained: Reza Valizadeh and Kamran Hekmati. As of last month, the US government was tracking at least six Americans detained in Iran.

*Assessment: This is a notable diplomatic gesture amid the fighting. Iran may be signaling openness to de-escalation even as military operations continue on both sides.*

#### 6. Iran Continues Retaliatory Strikes on US Bases — Jordan Intercepts 8 Missiles

**[VERIFIED]** Iran responded to the US's barrage on military targets such as command centers and missile sites by firing upon American bases in Kuwait and Jordan. The Jordanian government said it intercepted eight missiles.

**[VERIFIED]** The Army of the Islamic Republic of Iran issued a statement early Thursday, July 16, announcing that it had targeted parts of US military positions and systems in Kuwait, Bahrain, and Jordan using missiles and drones. The strikes were carried out in retaliation for the US attack on the Bampur barracks in Sistan and Baluchestan Province.

**[OFFICIAL]** The IRGC said that, during the eighth wave of its retaliatory Operation Nasr 2, its Navy and Aerospace Force carried out a combined missile-and-drone operation in response to "the enemy's attacks last night on locations along the country's southern coastline and in southern cities."

**[OFFICIAL]** The IRGC said it carried out the Jordan attack in retaliation for a strike launched from the base that hit "the vicinity of a children's cancer hospital". US bases and facilities in Kuwait, Bahrain and Jordan were also targeted by Iranian drone strikes.

*Assessment: The retaliatory strike cycle continues unabated, with Iran now explicitly framing strikes as retaliation for alleged hospital damage.*

#### 7. Iraq Condemns Drone Attack on Erbil

**[VERIFIED]** Iraq's Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi has "strongly condemned" a drone attack in the city of Erbil in the Kurdistan region. "We affirm that we will not tolerate these sinful attempts, which in desperation seek to undermine the stability of our people and their confident path toward building the state and societal peace," he said.

**[REPORTED]** Several explosions were heard late on Wednesday near the US consulate in Erbil.

**[VERIFIED]** Iraqi officials did not say who launched them.

*Assessment: The Erbil attack — likely by Iran or Iranian proxies — extends conflict dynamics into northern Iraq. The Iraqi PM condemned it while in Washington for talks, suggesting pressure on Iraq to take a clearer stance.*

#### 8. US Disables Oil Tanker Attempting to Reach Iran

**[VERIFIED]** CENTCOM, in a separate statement on Thursday, said it was enforcing a naval blockade reimposed on Tuesday by disabling a "non-compliant" oil tanker that was attempting to sail towards Iran's Kharg Island using Hellfire missiles.

**[VERIFIED]** The US struck Iran for a fifth straight day overnight and hit a sanctioned oil tanker near the country's main export terminal, as tensions between the warring sides show little sign of abating.

*Assessment: The blockade is being actively enforced with weapons use. This is the first armed interdiction of a vessel since the blockade was reimposed.*

#### 9. Saudi-Houthi Tensions Escalating

**[VERIFIED]** The Iran-backed Houthi group in Yemen fired ballistic missiles and drones on Saudi Arabia, the worst attack in several years that threatens to draw the rebels into the wider regional conflict between Tehran and Washington. The Houthis claimed they targeted Abha Airport in Saudi Arabia's southwestern region Monday and warned aviation companies to avoid Saudi airspace.

**[VERIFIED]** The attack marks the first publicly claimed Houthi strike on Saudi territory since the informal truce that came into effect in March 2022. The latest exchange therefore represents the collapse of a four-year de-escalation between Riyadh and the Iran-aligned terror group.

**[REPORTED]** When asked about the possibility of the Bab al-Mandeb Strait being closed, a Houthi official expressed that all options are on the table. "The Bab al-Mandeb card is a strategic asset that Yemen has the luxury of utilising," al-Bukhaiti said. "We will use this card against nations that are actively transgressing on us."

*Assessment: The Saudi-Houthi truce has effectively collapsed, opening a second potential front. Houthi threats to close Bab al-Mandeb — the other critical chokepoint — would compound the Hormuz crisis.*

#### 10. Oil Prices Retreat Slightly

**[VERIFIED]** By 5:30 a.m. Eastern Time today, oil had reached $84.64 per barrel, measured using the Brent benchmark. That's $1.28 less than it cost yesterday morning and about $15.38 above its price a year earlier.

**[REPORTED]** Oil prices gave up early gains on Thursday after rising for four consecutive sessions, as investors booked profits while continuing to monitor escalating tensions in the Middle East. Although concerns over potential supply disruptions kept crude prices elevated, the absence of any immediate impact on physical oil flows limited further upside.

*Assessment: Oil is retreating slightly from recent gains as traders take profits. Markets appear to be pricing in sustained conflict but not yet pricing in infrastructure strikes.*

### Emerging Signals

**US-Israel Friction Goes Public:** Vance's explicit accusation that Israeli officials are running an influence campaign to keep the war going "indefinitely" is extraordinary. This suggests deep internal divisions about war aims and may signal the administration's frustration with Israeli pressure against negotiations.

**Infrastructure Escalation Spiral:** Both sides have now explicitly tied infrastructure strikes to counter-infrastructure strikes. If Trump follows through on power plant/bridge threats, Iran has pre-committed to attacking Gulf state infrastructure. This could trigger a regional economic catastrophe.

**Second Chokepoint at Risk:** With Houthi-Saudi truce collapsing and Houthis explicitly threatening the Bab al-Mandeb Strait, the global economy could face simultaneous closure of both major Middle East shipping chokepoints.

**Diplomatic Mixed Signals:** The release of Dena Karari while fighting continues suggests both sides may still see value in keeping some channels open. Trump's public thanks for the "gesture of goodwill" is notable amid otherwise hostile rhetoric.
