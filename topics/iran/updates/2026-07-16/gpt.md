### 2) Strike scope appears to be widening (northward), while chokepoint-island pressure continues
- AP says the U.S. **expanded strikes farther north in Iran** early Thursday. **[REPORTED]** ([apnews.com](https://apnews.com/article/f98ff56554de2336f0e85bb5fdcae769?utm_source=openai))

**What it means:** Operationally, this looks like movement from “coastal anti-shipping suppression” toward a broader **campaign to degrade enabling nodes** (C2, sensors, logistics, and dispersed systems). That typically makes a quick return to a narrow maritime-only fight less likely.

### 3) Iran escalates “red line” messaging and explicitly widens the retaliatory set to Gulf infrastructure
- Reuters reports Iran declared **Hormuz a “red line”** and warned that if the U.S. strikes Iranian infrastructure, Iran would **strike infrastructure across the Gulf region**. **[REPORTED]** ([investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/world-news/iran-warns-strait-of-hormuz-is-a-red-line-and-will-resist-until-the-end-4794822?utm_source=openai))  

**What it means:** Tehran is trying to deter U.S. escalation (especially against grid/industrial nodes) by raising the implied cost for Gulf states—this increases the odds of **regionalization** even if neither side seeks it.

---

### 4) New widening-front risk: Iran signals Houthis could move against the Red Sea oil route (Bab el-Mandeb)
- Reuters: Iran has **asked Yemen’s Houthis to stand ready to close the Red Sea oil route** if the U.S. hits Iranian power infrastructure (per multiple sources to Reuters). **[REPORTED]** ([sa.marketscreener.com](https://sa.marketscreener.com/news/iran-tells-houthis-to-close-red-sea-gateway-if-us-hits-power-network-sources-say-ce7f5ed3d98ef625?utm_source=openai))  

**What it means:** This is the clearest signal yet of a potential **two-chokepoint energy squeeze** (Hormuz + Bab el-Mandeb). Even if not executed, it pushes shipping, insurers, and governments to plan for a wider maritime security mission—raising the “entanglement” baseline.

**What it means:** Markets are pricing not only “headline war risk,” but **logistics/throughput impairment**. That tends to create pressure for either (a) harsher enforcement or (b) a diplomatic pause—both of which can be destabilizing in the short term.
