## Key Developments (Past 24 Hours)

### **ISRAEL ELECTION: HISTORIC TURNOUT, RESULTS PENDING** [VERIFIED]

Israel's election is seeing historic voter turnout, reaching 74.3% by 8 PM, surpassing all of Election Day 2022. The overall turnout measured by 6 PM was 66.7 percent, the highest ever recorded by that hour. If the current pace continues, final turnout could reach around 80 percent.

At 4 PM local time, 55.7% of voters had cast a ballot, much higher than the same hour in the 2022 elections, when turnout stood at 47.5%.

The final round of pre-election polls showed Netanyahu falling short of a clear victory, with his main rival, former military chief Gadi Eisenkot, also short of securing a clear victory and likely needing support from Israel's Arab parties to oust Netanyahu.

**[ANALYSIS]** High turnout historically favors challengers in Israeli elections. The record numbers increase uncertainty but could benefit Eisenkot's bloc if Arab turnout is also elevated. Results expected late tonight/early tomorrow.

### **TEHRAN TALKS: NO BREAKTHROUGH, HOUTHI-US LINKAGE EMERGES** [REPORTED]

A day after Saudi Arabia and its two defence-pact partners sent senior officials to Tehran, sources say the clearest outcome is a promise to keep talking, not a deal to de-escalate on any front, including the Houthi attacks on Saudi cities.

Pakistan's army chief Field Marshal Asim Munir and Ibrahim Kalin, director of Turkey's intelligence service, also attended Monday's meeting, which ended with Iranian state media saying the parties agreed to continue discussions. Sources briefed on the talks said they made no clear headway.

Iran claimed it lacked leverage over the Houthis to halt attacks on Saudi Arabia, despite being the rebels' primary outside supporter.

Reuters, citing a Pakistani official, reported that Munir presented a possible compromise intended to offer gains to both Washington and Tehran. Saudi Arabia, Pakistan, Turkey and Qatar could potentially act as guarantors if it were accepted. Its terms were not disclosed, and no government publicly confirmed acceptance.

Under a separate proposal, Iran would reopen the Strait of Hormuz to commercial shipping while Washington lifted its naval blockade of Iranian ports, with mediators also pressing Tehran for an initial concession on its nuclear program. The negotiations have been complicated by disagreements over which side must act first.

**[ANALYSIS]** Iran's claim that it lacks leverage over Houthis is strategically useful—it allows Tehran to maintain pressure on Saudi Arabia without taking responsibility. The linkage between Houthi attacks and US-Iran negotiations suggests Iran views the Houthi campaign as leverage, not something to be defused separately.

### **KHAMENEI: IRAN'S CONDITIONS MUST BE MET TO END WAR** [OFFICIAL]

Iran's Supreme Leader reiterated his country's conditions for ending the war with the US. Mojtaba Khamenei emphasized the need to implement "the conditions set out by the country's officials, with the aim of ending the war and the 'neither war nor peace' situation" in a meeting with national security chief Mohsen Rezaei.

Under Iran's September proposal, Tehran offered to end the war and reopen the Strait of Hormuz within seven days, provided Washington accepted its conditions.

According to market analysts, Iran's conditions include: lifting the US port blockade, easing oil sanctions, and reopening Hormuz within seven days of a deal. The issue is who moves first. Iran wants relief, while Washington demands nuclear concessions.

**[ANALYSIS]** Khamenei's statement signals no flexibility on Iran's position. The "neither war nor peace" framing suggests Tehran recognizes the current situation is unsustainable but will not capitulate. This hardens the diplomatic impasse.

### **EGYPT AUTHORIZES MILITARY SUPPORT FOR SAUDI ARABIA** [VERIFIED]

Egypt's parliament authorized President Sisi Monday to take all necessary measures to support and defend Saudi Arabia as attacks by Yemen's Iran-backed Houthis intensify. The House of Representatives approved the mandate by a two-thirds majority following closed-door sessions.

The measures, to be taken in coordination with Riyadh, are aimed at defending Saudi Arabia's "territory, sovereignty and holy sites against any external aggression."

The Egyptian president said last week that any military effort would not include attacks on Yemen and would focus on defending Saudi territory and borders.

Egypt has frequently condemned Iranian and Iranian-backed attacks on Gulf countries but has not publicly engaged in direct combat or joined the Mecca mutual defense pact between Saudi Arabia, Pakistan, and Turkey that was signed in August.

**[ANALYSIS]** Egypt's entry adds another regional military power to the anti-Houthi coalition. While Cairo frames this as defensive, it represents a significant widening of the conflict's participant base and increases pressure on the Houthi-Iran axis.

### **HOUTHI ATTACKS CONTINUE — SAUDI COALITION INTERCEPTS** [VERIFIED]

The Saudi-led coalition said on Tuesday that it intercepted three ballistic missiles and four drones launched by the Houthis towards Saudi Arabia. Major-General Turki al-Maliki said the missiles were aimed at the kingdom's Eastern Province and caused no casualties. He added that the drones were headed towards the kingdom's southern regions.

Earlier on Monday, the Houthis claimed another attack on the Riyadh airport, as well as on crude oil pumping stations in Saudi Arabia's west and east. The Saudi-led coalition said the missiles were intercepted and destroyed.

The coalition also said it launched retaliatory attacks, destroying five tunnels used by the Houthis to store ballistic missiles, four missile launchers and a missile depot.

**[ANALYSIS]** The Houthi campaign continues unabated despite regional diplomacy. The attacks on oil infrastructure (pumping stations) alongside airports indicate strategic targeting of Saudi economic assets.

### **WEST BANK: DISPLACEMENTS RISE, UN WARNS OF "ETHNIC CLEANSING"** [VERIFIED]

A total of 3,971 Palestinians from 619 families had been forced to leave their homes in the ongoing Hebron operation.

Israeli troops fired a stun grenade on Tuesday to disperse Palestinians who gathered to protest against an Israeli military operation that has displaced thousands of residents from a neighbourhood in Hebron.

UN human rights chief Volker Türk stated: "Collective punishment is a grave violation of international law. Threats to empty and erase entire neighbourhoods also raise serious concerns of ethnic cleansing."

The Israeli military said in a statement on Sunday that troops would carry out demolitions in the area. It said soldiers had not located any weapons in searches of the houses.

**[ANALYSIS]** The operation continues through election day as initially announced. Displacement figures have risen from 2,670 to 3,971 in 48 hours.
