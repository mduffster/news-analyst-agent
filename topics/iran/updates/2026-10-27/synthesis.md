## 1. Houthis Strike Ras Tanura — Saudi Arabia's Largest Oil Refinery [REPORTED]

Yemen's Iran-aligned Houthis said on Tuesday they had launched an attack on Saudi Arabia's Ras Tanura oil refinery, the biggest in the kingdom, located on the Gulf west of the Strait of Hormuz.

The group's military spokesperson Yahya Saree said in a statement that the ballistic missile attack caused a fire at the site. The Saudi authorities have not confirmed the attack.

Bloomberg News reported on Tuesday, citing a person familiar with the matter, that Saudi Arabia had contained a fire near the Ras Tanura oil terminal. Ras Tanura, which has a capacity of 550,000 barrels per day, was shut for a period in the early months of the Iran war as a precautionary measure.

Nearly all Saudi Arabia's oil exports flow through two main ports: Ras Tanura in the Gulf and Yanbu on the Red Sea, both of which the Houthis have attacked in the past.

The Houthis have also claimed attacks on crude oil pumping stations along Saudi Arabia's highly crucial East-West oil pipeline. Over the weekend, the Houthis attacked Saudi Arabia's state-owned oil company Aramco's facility in Yanbu, and targeted the Rabigh oil refinery, as well as pumping stations along the 1,200 km East-West onshore oil pipeline.

**Significance:** The strike on Ras Tanura represents the most strategically consequential Houthi target to date—a refinery processing over half a million barrels daily and serving as a critical export hub. While the fire was reportedly contained and Saudi authorities have not confirmed operational impact, the attack demonstrates the Houthis' capacity to threaten Saudi Arabia's core energy infrastructure despite weeks of coalition airstrikes. Coming hours after Tehran's quadrilateral summit, the attack signals that diplomatic engagement with Iran produces no reduction in proxy pressure.

## 2. Tehran Talks Produce No Agreement—Iran Links Houthi De-escalation to US Truce [VERIFIED/REPORTED]

Tehran talks ended without a deal, with sources saying the clearest outcome was a promise to keep talking rather than any concrete agreement. Iran's Supreme National Security Council secretary said discussions were "very good," adding that regional countries, not outside powers, should ensure security in the region. A day after Saudi Arabia and its two defence-pact partners sent senior officials to Tehran, sources say the clearest outcome is a promise to keep talking, not a deal to de-escalate on any front, including the Houthi attacks on Saudi cities.

Iran claimed it lacked leverage over the Houthis to halt attacks on Saudi Arabia, despite being the rebels' primary outside supporter. The Iranians asked Saudi Arabia, Pakistan and Turkey to persuade Washington to accept a two-week regional pause in hostilities in exchange for gradually reopening the Strait of Hormuz.

Iran's Supreme National Security Council Secretary stated: "We reached the conclusion that security in West Asia must be endogenous and that any external intervention must be prevented." A senior Pakistani security official told Reuters that the primary goal of the summit was to establish a regional mechanism to prevent military attacks between countries in the region.

According to the Pakistani official, talks between Tehran and Washington, attacks by Iranian-backed Houthis against Saudi Arabia, the situation in the Strait of Hormuz, and the US blockade of Iranian ports were among the topics discussed at the meeting.

**Significance:** Iran's claim that it lacks leverage over the Houthis—while simultaneously linking Houthi de-escalation to a US-Iran regional pause—reveals Tehran's strategic calculus: proxy pressure continues until Washington offers concessions. The Makkah Pact countries' direct engagement with Iran represented an extraordinary diplomatic gambit, but produced only procedural commitments rather than operational constraints. The absence of any verification mechanism or timetable leaves the conflict trajectory unchanged.

## 3. Trump Calls Ceasefire "Waste of Time"—Strike Decision Looms [OFFICIAL]

US President Donald Trump has described the summer ceasefire with Iran as a "waste of time," saying he agreed to the pause on the advice of experts despite his reservations about the arrangement.

"We did a little ceasefire, which I never liked very much," he said. "We wanna be nice, but it was just a waste of time as far as I'm concerned."

Trump defended ongoing military pressures by highlighting the success of the US naval blockade in the Strait of Hormuz, asserting that no unauthorized Iranian vessels have breached the perimeter. Expressing confidence in Washington's strategic positioning, Trump predicted that the conflict would conclude "very soon" while reiterating that Tehran would be permanently blocked from acquiring nuclear capabilities.

CNN reported, citing two US officials, that US forces in the Middle East could launch new and possibly major operations against Iran "within 96 hours of receiving" an order from Trump, and that enough forces are already in the region. The officials said these operations would probably go beyond limited strikes on radars and launchers. US Central Command and Pentagon leaders have drawn up a target list that includes energy infrastructure, military sites and nuclear facilities.

The posture rests on three statements: Trump's 25 October remark that there would be no strike that day, the White House position of 22 October that the United States "will not be attacking Iran at any time prior to the midterm elections," and CNN's report of 26 October that US forces could begin potentially major operations within 96 hours of an order.

**Significance:** Trump's dismissal of the June ceasefire as a "waste of time" marks a rhetorical hardening ahead of the November 3 midterms. Combined with the 96-hour strike readiness and target lists that include nuclear facilities, the comments suggest diminishing diplomatic runway. The post-midterm window (November 3-10) remains the likely decision point, but Trump's framing leaves little room for a negotiated outcome.

## 4. Israel Election: Record 74.3% Turnout, Results Pending [VERIFIED]

Historic turnout in 2026 election reaches 74.3% by 8 P.M., surpassing all of Election Day 2022. As of 8 P.M., 5,449,277 votes have been cast in Israel's election, some 655,000 votes more than the entire 2022 election.

The overall turnout measured by 6 P.M. was 66.7 percent, the highest ever recorded by that hour. If the current pace continues, final turnout could reach around 80 percent.

The final round of pre-election polls in Israel last week showed Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu falling short of a clear victory in the October 27 election.

Election night results expected at 10:00 PM local time (3:00 PM ET).

**Significance:** Record turnout historically favors challengers in Israeli elections, potentially benefiting Gadi Eisenkot's opposition bloc. High Arab voter turnout could prove decisive in an election where neither bloc was polling near 61 seats. The outcome will shape Israel's Iran policy during the critical post-midterm window—a clear Eisenkot victory might pause Israeli participation in imminent strikes, while a Netanyahu win would likely accelerate them. Coalition negotiations could extend for weeks regardless of outcome, complicating US-Israel coordination.

## 5. West Bank Displacement Surges to Nearly 5,000 [VERIFIED]

The Israeli army continued a large-scale offensive in the West Bank city of Hebron for a fifth consecutive day on Tuesday, forcing nearly 5,000 Palestinians from their homes. The Hebron municipality said in a statement that 4,969 Palestinians from 780 families have been forcibly displaced from the Deir Bahha and Al-Jilda areas since the Israeli military campaign began on Friday.

Among those displaced are 336 infants, 441 elderly people, 216 pregnant and nursing women, 44 people with disabilities and 312 people suffering from chronic illnesses.

Israeli troops fired a stun grenade on Tuesday to disperse Palestinians who gathered to protest against an Israeli military operation that has displaced thousands of residents from a neighbourhood in Hebron. The unrest, which follows the deaths of two Israeli soldiers shot by Palestinian gunmen at a checkpoint last week, unfolded as Israelis were voting in an election in which the country's posture in the West Bank has been a major political issue.

According to the municipality, approximately 86 acres of the city had already been destroyed or marked for demolition by Saturday afternoon. Israeli soldiers have taken over evacuated houses, spray-painted black Xs on their walls, and draped Israeli flags over balconies.

**Significance:** Displacement figures have nearly doubled since October 25 (from 2,670 to 4,969). The operation's scale—86 acres destroyed or marked for demolition—and timing (coinciding with Israeli elections) have prompted UN warnings of potential ethnic cleansing. The operation serves dual security and political purposes for Netanyahu's right-wing coalition.

## 6. Maritime Attacks Continue—Tanker Disabled in Hormuz [REPORTED]

Two new maritime security warnings issued Monday point to continued dangers for commercial shipping in the Middle East, with one tanker confirmed disabled by a projectile in the Strait of Hormuz and another vessel reportedly on fire in the northern Persian Gulf. UK Maritime Trade Operations (UKMTO) said it received a verified, time-late report of a tanker struck by an unknown projectile while transiting outbound through the Strait of Hormuz on October 23 at 0839 UTC.

The impact disabled the vessel, although all crew members were reported safe. UKMTO did not identify the tanker or provide details about the extent of the damage.

The Joint Maritime Information Center (JMIC) maintained its SEVERE threat assessment for the Strait of Hormuz, warning that further deliberate attacks were highly likely. The assessment also kept the threat level across the wider Persian Gulf at SUBSTANTIAL, citing five attacks inside the Gulf since October 7.

An oil tanker caught fire after striking naval mines east of the Strait of Hormuz, the Iranian Navy said Tuesday. In a statement, the Navy said the tanker had switched off its tracking systems and was attempting to pass through a "restricted and unsafe" area when it hit the mines.

**Significance:** The newly surfaced UKMTO report (time-late from October 23) confirms ongoing maritime attacks with reporting lags—a pattern that increases miscalculation risk. The JMIC's "SEVERE" threat assessment and 156 confirmed incidents since March 1 underscore that the Strait of Hormuz remains a persistent conflict zone regardless of diplomatic efforts.

As of 8 a.m. Eastern Time today, oil is trading at $115.13 per barrel, based on the Brent benchmark. That's 28 cents below yesterday morning's level and about $49 higher than where it stood a year ago.

**Significance:** Markets are pricing conflicting signals: diplomatic engagement at Tehran offset by the Ras Tanura attack and Trump's hawkish rhetoric. The modest pullback from Friday's $116.72 peak suggests traders see near-term strike probability as marginally reduced, but the 80%+ year-over-year increase reflects entrenched conflict premium. Analyst expectations of $120+ by quarter-end indicate sustained uncertainty.
