### Key Developments Since Yesterday

#### 1. US Strikes Six Bridges in Southern Iran

**[VERIFIED]** The US hit southern Iran overnight, striking six road bridges, according to Iranian state media.

**[VERIFIED]** Iran said those targets included six bridges in the Hormozgan province facing the key waterway, as well as power infrastructure.

**[VERIFIED]** Many of the bridges that were targeted were on routes connected to the city of Bandar Abbas, a coastal city on the Strait of Hormuz which is home to an Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps naval base.

**[VERIFIED]** A photo released by state broadcaster IRIB and geolocated by CNN showed a damaged Kahurestan Bridge, linking Bandar Abbas to the city of Shiraz, after it was reportedly hit by a US strike. At least seven people were killed and nine were wounded, reported multiple state media outlets.

**[VERIFIED]** Iran's energy ministry said power lines in Bandar Abbas and surrounding villages were damaged, according to semi-official Tasnim News Agency.

**[REPORTED]** There were separate reports of attacks on the southern town of Bushehr, which houses the country's only nuclear power plant, and the western province of Lorestan.

*Assessment: This marks the first time US forces have struck Iranian bridges in the current wave of fighting — Trump's "next week" infrastructure threat has materialized. The targeting of bridges connecting Bandar Abbas to major cities appears designed to isolate Iran's main Hormuz port and IRGC naval base.*

#### 2. Iran Launches Largest Retaliatory Wave Since Truce Collapse — Targets 6-7 Countries

**[VERIFIED]** The US hit multiple bridges in Iran while Tehran responded with what appeared to be the largest retaliatory strikes since their truce collapsed. Qatar, Kuwait and Jordan have come under Iranian fire, and Iran also claimed it targeted US military assets in Bahrain and Oman. The fresh reprisals by Iran appear to be the largest in the region since talks with the US collapsed.

**[OFFICIAL]** Iran said Friday that it targeted US military sites in six Arab countries in retaliation for recent US strikes on its territory, while Kuwait, Qatar, Bahrain and Jordan said they intercepted Iranian missiles and drones targeting their territories. The Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) said its Aerospace Force targeted a US military base in Kuwait as part of the 12th wave of what it described as a retaliatory operation. The IRGC said the targets included a missile defense detection and tracking radar, several weapons depots, two High Mobility Artillery Rocket System (HIMARS) launchers and missiles stored for the system. It claimed the strike triggered a large fire at the base.

**[OFFICIAL]** Iran targeted seven countries across the region late Thursday and early Friday, claiming for the first time an attack on a US base in Syria, as well as radar stations in Oman. The announcements by the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) suggest it is expanding retaliatory attacks after a week of US strikes. Syria: The IRGC claimed to have targeted a US base at al-Tanf close to the border with Iraq, in what it said was retaliation for deadly US strikes.

**[REPORTED]** A Syrian source and the US military deny Iran's claims of an attack on a US base in Syria.

**[VERIFIED]** The U.S. military said in February that it had completed the withdrawal of forces from the strategic al-Tanf military base.

**[OFFICIAL]** Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) on Friday said that its navy targeted radar sites in Oman, including a US air-control radar. Citing an IRGC statement, the news agency reported that a naval control radar on the Salameh Rocks and a US air-control radar in Oman's Ghanem region were targeted at dawn. The IRGC claimed both radar systems were destroyed.

*Assessment: Iran has dramatically expanded its targeting, now claiming strikes on US assets in at least seven countries (Syria, Kuwait, Bahrain, Jordan, Qatar, Oman, and possibly Iraq). The Syria claim is dubious given the US withdrawal from al-Tanf, but the breadth of targets suggests Iran is trying to maximize pressure across the region.*

#### 3. Iran Strikes Kuwait Desalination/Power Plant — Critical Infrastructure Escalation

**[VERIFIED]** An Iranian attack damaged a power generation and water desalination plant in Kuwait, the country's Ministry of Electricity, Water and Renewable Energy said Friday. The attack damaged the facility, sparked a fire and affected several electricity generation units.

**[VERIFIED]** Iran attacked Kuwaiti water and power plants in counterstrikes against the US, underscoring how the conflict in the Middle East is worsening and civilian sites are increasingly under threat. Kuwait's government said on Friday a desalination and electricity plant was hit, with many power-generation units sustaining damage.

**[VERIFIED]** In Kuwait, about 90% of drinking water comes from desalination, along with roughly 86% in Oman and about 70% in Saudi Arabia.

**[VERIFIED]** Kuwait's Electricity Water and Renewable Energy Ministry said via social media that it had extinguished a blaze triggered by the attack and was working to assess the damage and get the station working again.

*Assessment: This is a critical escalation. Iran has now made good on its threat to target "all infrastructure in the region" — attacking civilian infrastructure that provides drinking water to 90% of Kuwait's population. This marks a qualitative shift in targeting and sets a dangerous precedent for further infrastructure attacks on both sides.*

#### 4. Child Injured in Qatar from Missile Shrapnel

**[VERIFIED]** Iran carried out extensive attacks on Qatar again with two waves of ballistic missile airstrikes at 3:30 am and 5:45 am local time on Friday morning. Euronews journalists reported from across Doha that the air-raid mobile alerts woke the city, urging residents to shelter. The capital was shaken twice by the loud booms of the missile interceptions by Qatar's air defence and the ensuing shockwaves. Authorities reported that one child was injured by falling shrapnel in the city.

**[VERIFIED]** Qatar's military intercepted all incoming missiles which appeared to be targeting the Al-Udeid US airbase, the largest in the Middle East, which the US has been using to carry out its strikes in Iran over the last week. The strikes came at the end of Qatar's four-day national mourning period to commemorate the passing of former Emir Sheikh Hamad bin Khalifa Al Thani.

*Assessment: Iranian missiles striking Qatar during its national mourning period — and injuring a child — represents a significant diplomatic provocation against a key mediator. Qatar has been instrumental in US-Iran talks, making this targeting particularly fraught.*

#### 5. Casualties Rise — 38 Dead, 400+ Wounded in Iran

**[VERIFIED]** Iranian state media said at least seven people were killed and 20 wounded in the latest U.S. strikes on infrastructure. According to Iranian health officials, 38 people have been killed and over 400 wounded in the past week.

**[VERIFIED]** The overnight deaths bring the total number of people killed in Iran to 38 since the US restarted its strikes on the Islamic Republic in July, a spokesperson for the Iranian Health Ministry, Hossein Kermanpour, said on X. At least 400 people have been injured, Kermanpour said.

**[REPORTED]** Iran's Health Ministry said on Friday that at least 38 people had been killed and more than 400 wounded in US strikes on the country, mainly in southern provinces, since June 22. Hossein Kermanpour, the ministry's head of public relations, said three women and one teenager were among the dead, while 22 women and nine people under the age of 18 were among the injured. Forty-seven of the wounded remained in hospital.

*Assessment: Iranian casualties have increased from 35 dead/300 wounded yesterday to 38 dead/400+ wounded today. Civilian infrastructure strikes will likely accelerate the casualty count.*

#### 6. Satellite Images Show Damage Inside Bushehr Nuclear Complex

**[REPORTED]** Satellite images reveal strikes between July 7 and 12 on Iran's largest nuclear power plant. Satellite imagery reveals new damage inside Iran's Bushehr nuclear power plant complex following the latest wave of US strikes during renewed military escalation between Washington and Tehran. A comparison of European Sentinel-2 satellite images captured on July 7 and July 12 shows newly formed impact scars inside the Bushehr complex, along with another apparent strike site within nearby support facilities.

**[VERIFIED]** US Central Command (CENTCOM) said it struck about 90 military targets on July 7-8, including air defence systems, missile and drone storage sites, naval assets and military infrastructure along Iran's southern coast. The US did not identify Bushehr or any nuclear facility among the announced targets.

**[OFFICIAL]** Jahanian later denied reports that the nuclear power plant itself had been hit, saying the reactor remained unaffected and continued operating normally.

*Assessment: Independent satellite imagery now shows damage inside the Bushehr complex, even though the US did not announce nuclear facility targeting. This raises serious concerns about nuclear safety and could become a major international issue. The IAEA warned in May that strikes near the plant "pose a very real danger to nuclear safety."*

#### 7. Iran Kills 8 Kurdish Opposition Fighters in Iraq

**[VERIFIED]** Iranian strikes were also reported in Iraq's Kurdistan region, where at least eight opposition fighters were killed, according to the Kurdish Iranian opposition group.

**[VERIFIED]** Iran killed at least eight people in attacks on America's Kurdish allies in Iraq, the region's president reported. Nechirvan Barzani, the president of the Kurdistan Region of Iraq, condemned the strikes. "Targeting the region and the resumption of violence are a serious escalation and a blatant violation of Iraq's sovereignty," he said in a statement.

*Assessment: Iran is now striking targets in Iraq's Kurdistan region, killing members of Kurdish opposition groups. This extends the conflict into Iraqi territory more significantly than the earlier Erbil consulate-area drone attack.*

#### 8. Oil Prices Up ~12% for the Week

**[VERIFIED]** Oil prices rose about 12 per cent for the week, with Brent trading at $85.1 a barrel and WTI at $79.93 a barrel. Escalating US-Iran hostilities have disrupted oil flows through the Strait of Hormuz, with confirmed crude transit falling 62 per cent to 4.1 million barrels a day.

**[VERIFIED]** Global oil prices were on track to end the week higher after the US launched its sixth consecutive night of strikes against Iran. Brent crude, the global benchmark, was up 0.4% to nearly $85 a barrel by 3.37 a.m. ET, up from a closing price of $76 last Friday. West Texas Intermediate, the US benchmark, was up 0.9% to almost $80 a barrel by the same time, up from $71 a week ago.

**[VERIFIED]** Oil prices were higher on Friday morning, firmly on track to register bumper weekly gains. International benchmark Brent crude futures with September delivery advanced 0.5% to trade at $84.67 per barrel. U.S. West Texas Intermediate futures with August delivery gained 0.9% to trade at $79.66. Both oil contracts are up more than 11% so far this week and on track for their best weekly performance since late April.

*Assessment: Oil prices have surged 11-12% this week — the best weekly performance since late April. Markets are now pricing in sustained infrastructure escalation. Confirmed crude transit through Hormuz has fallen 62% to 4.1 million barrels per day.*

#### 9. Houthis Threaten Saudi Oil Facilities, Prepare Red Sea Closure

**[OFFICIAL]** The leader of the Houthis (Ansar Allah) threatened on July 16 to strike oil and other vital facilities in Saudi Arabia if Riyadh joins attacks on Yemen. "All Saudi oil facilities and vital installations will be targets for our missiles and drones if Riyadh gets involved" in striking Yemen, Houthi leader Abdul Malik al-Houthi said in a televised speech. "The real equation is… airports for airports, ports for ports, and a blockade for a blockade," he warned.

**[REPORTED]** Iran has also asked Yemen's Houthis to be ready to close the Red Sea oil route if the U.S. targets Iranian power infrastructure, Reuters reported Thursday, citing three unnamed sources.

**[REPORTED]** A source close to the Houthis said the group has completed preparations to attack shipping, having deployed missiles and drones near the Bab el-Mandeb strait.

*Assessment: The second chokepoint threat is becoming more concrete. Reports indicate Iran has asked Houthis to be ready to close the Red Sea if power plants are struck, and Houthi sources say deployment near Bab el-Mandeb is complete. With bridge strikes already happening, power plant strikes may be imminent — potentially triggering Red Sea closure.*
