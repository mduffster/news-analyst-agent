## Critical Developments (July 16-17)

### 1. CRITICAL: US Strikes Six Bridges in Southern Iran — Infrastructure Campaign Begins [VERIFIED]

At least six bridges were hit, including one that was under construction. A railway junction station just west of Bandar Abbas was also hit.

The United States expanded its airstrike campaign against Iran early Friday by hitting more bridges, energy sites and collapsing a tower at a key Iranian port, part of U.S. President Trump's threats to start striking infrastructure to pressure Tehran to ease its chokehold on the Strait of Hormuz.

The highway and railway bridge strikes appeared aimed at cutting off Bandar Abbas, Iran's main port, from roads leading toward Tehran, the capital. While other routes still are open, the U.S. strikes could expand further, potentially disrupting both the movement of military material and goods needed for Iran's 90 million people.

Iran said those targets included six bridges in the Hormozgan province facing the key waterway, as well as power infrastructure. Iran also acknowledged "attacks on power infrastructure" during the U.S. airstrikes for the first time Friday, with the Energy Ministry asking people in southern provinces to use less electricity.

**Casualties:** At least eight people were killed and 20 others injured in the strikes, Iranian state news agency IRNA reported.

*Assessment: [VERIFIED] This marks the first time US forces have struck Iranian bridges in the current wave of fighting — Trump's "next week" infrastructure threat has materialized. The targeting of bridges connecting Bandar Abbas to major cities appears designed to isolate Iran's main Hormuz port and IRGC naval base. Iran's acknowledgment of power infrastructure damage and request for reduced electricity use suggests broader infrastructure targeting may already be underway.*

### 2. CRITICAL: US Destroys Chabahar Port Surveillance Tower [VERIFIED/OFFICIAL]

US Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth shared an image showing damage to Chabahar port tower after strikes. A surveillance tower at Iran's Chabahar port on the Gulf of Oman appeared to collapse after repeated US strikes on the facility.

The American military confirms destroying a surveillance tower at Iran's Chabahar port on the Gulf of Oman during last night's strikes. US Central Command says it "destroyed the Chah Bahar Shahid Kalantari Port surveillance tower, part of a maritime surveillance network along Iran's Gulf of Oman coastline used for decades by the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) to track and target commercial vessels transiting the Strait of Hormuz."

The tower, located at the Shahid Kalantari Port section of the Chabahar Free Zone on Iran's southeastern coast, had already sustained damage in earlier American strikes on July 8 and July 9.

Chabahar is Iran's only major deep-water port with direct access to the Arabian Sea and Indian Ocean, allowing it to bypass the Strait of Hormuz entirely. The port also carries significant importance for India, which has invested in Chabahar as part of the International North-South Transport Corridor.

*Assessment: [OFFICIAL] CENTCOM has officially confirmed destroying the Chabahar surveillance tower. This is significant as it targets Iran's maritime monitoring capability and a port with strategic value to India. The stated intent is to "degrade IRGC's ability to coordinate attacks on innocent civilian crew members."*

### 3. CRITICAL: Iran Strikes Kuwait Desalination Plant — Civilian Infrastructure Escalation [VERIFIED]

Iran attacked Kuwaiti water and power plants in counterstrikes against the US, underscoring how the conflict in the Middle East is worsening and civilian sites are increasingly under threat. Kuwait's government said on Friday a desalination and electricity plant was hit, with many power-generation units sustaining damage.

Kuwait's Ministry of Electricity, Water and Renewable Energy said an attack on Friday damaged one of the country's power generation and water distillation plants. The ministry said the strike caused a fire and affected several electricity generation units, prompting the activation of emergency measures to contain the impact and maintain grid stability.

The incident has raised serious concerns, given Kuwait's heavy reliance on desalination for its water supply. Nearly 90 per cent of the country's drinking water is produced by converting seawater from the Gulf into potable water, making such facilities critical national infrastructure.

Kuwait Fire Force teams brought the fire under control, while technical teams began assessing the damage, securing the facility and working to restore affected units.

*Assessment: [VERIFIED] This is a critical escalation. Iran has now made good on its threat to target "all infrastructure in the region" — attacking civilian infrastructure that provides drinking water to 90% of Kuwait's population. This marks a qualitative shift in targeting and sets a dangerous precedent for further infrastructure attacks on both sides.*

### 4. CRITICAL: Iran Launches Largest Retaliatory Wave — Claims Strikes in 6-7 Countries [VERIFIED/OFFICIAL]

Iran's armed forces claimed on Friday to have struck US bases and facilities in Syria, Kuwait, Bahrain and Oman, in retaliation for fresh American strikes on Iran and its infrastructure.

The Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) said its aerospace forces had launched a surprise attack on a US military centre at Syria's al-Tanf base, killing a number of American personnel and destroying a radar system and several helicopters used for special operations.

Iran's Revolutionary Guard also said it attacked U.S. special forces in al-Tanf in Syria, a former U.S. military outpost, from which U.S. troops withdrew in February, claiming casualties and damage. There was no immediate response by the U.S. Central Command (CENTCOM) to Iran's claim.

Iran said Friday that it targeted US military sites in six Arab countries in retaliation for recent US strikes on its territory, while Kuwait, Qatar, Bahrain and Jordan said they intercepted Iranian missiles and drones targeting their territories. The Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) said its Aerospace Force targeted a US military base in Kuwait as part of the 12th wave of what it described as a retaliatory operation.

Iranian forces also struck a US base in Kuwait, targeting air-defence radar systems, weapons depots and other military equipment, while naval units hit American radar facilities in Oman.

*Assessment: [REPORTED] Iran has dramatically expanded its targeting, now claiming strikes on US assets in six to seven countries. The Syria al-Tanf claim is dubious given the US withdrawal in February, but the breadth of targets suggests Iran is maximizing pressure across the region. The IRGC is now on its 12th-13th "wave" of retaliatory operations since the truce collapsed.*

### 5. CRITICAL: Child Injured in Qatar — Two Missile Waves Target Doha [VERIFIED]

Iran carried out extensive attacks on Qatar again with two waves of ballistic missile airstrikes at 3:30 am and 5:45 am local time on Friday morning.

Euronews journalists reported from across Doha that the air-raid mobile alerts woke the city, urging residents to shelter. The capital was shaken twice by the loud booms of the missile interceptions by Qatar's air defence and the ensuing shockwaves.

A child was injured after an Iranian attack targeting Qatar on Friday morning, the country's Ministry of Interior said. The ministry said the child sustained injuries from falling shrapnel following interception operations and is currently receiving the necessary medical treatment.

Qatar's military intercepted all incoming missiles which appeared to be targeting the Al-Udeid US airbase, the largest in the Middle East, which the US has been using to carry out its strikes in Iran over the last week.

Qatar, together with Pakistan, is one of the key mediators in talks between the two sides.

*Assessment: [VERIFIED] Iranian missiles striking Qatar — and injuring a child — represents a significant diplomatic provocation against a key mediator. Qatar has been instrumental in US-Iran talks, making this targeting particularly fraught.*

### 6. CRITICAL: Houthis Positioned and Ready to Close Bab el-Mandeb [VERIFIED]

A source close to the Houthis said the group had completed preparations to attack shipping by deploying missiles and drones near Bab el-Mandeb strait, the gateway to the Red Sea, in Yemen's highlands overlooking Hodeidah and the Gulf of Aden and was awaiting the order to begin.

Iran has asked Yemen's Houthi movement to stand ready to close the Red Sea oil route if the United States strikes Iranian power infrastructure, three sources told Reuters.

Any threat to the Red Sea and its Bab el-Mandeb gateway risks hugely exacerbating the global energy crisis triggered by Iran's closure of the Strait of Hormuz. With the Hormuz strait already shut, any Houthi attacks on vessels or ports in the Red Sea would leave the Middle East's two main oil export routes disrupted simultaneously, opening a new front in both the energy crisis and Iran's wider conflict with the United States.

The European Union is ramping up its security missions in the Red Sea as the Iran-backed Houthis are reported to be preparing at Tehran's behest to close the Bab el-Mandeb strait.

Around 10-15% of global maritime trade passes through Bab el-Mandeb, including a significant share of Europe's oil and gas imports. A closure would force vessels to take the much longer route around the Cape of Good Hope, adding two to three weeks to journeys between Asia and Europe and sharply increasing shipping costs.

*Assessment: [VERIFIED] The dual-chokepoint threat has moved from rhetorical to operational. With bridge strikes already executed, power plant strikes appear imminent — which could trigger the Houthi activation order. The EU's emergency response to bolster Red Sea naval presence signals serious concern about this scenario.*

### 7. SIGNIFICANT: Iranian Casualties Rise — 38 Dead, 400+ Wounded [VERIFIED]

The overnight deaths bring the total number of people killed in Iran to 38 since the US restarted its strikes on the Islamic Republic in July, a spokesperson for the Iranian Health Ministry, Hossein Kermanpour, said on X. At least 400 people have been injured, Kermanpour said.

Iran's Health Ministry said on Friday that at least 38 people had been killed and more than 400 wounded in US strikes on the country, mainly in southern provinces, since June 22. Hossein Kermanpour, the ministry's head of public relations, said three women and one teenager were among the dead, while 22 women and nine people under the age of 18 were among the injured. Forty-seven of the wounded remained in hospital.

*Assessment: [VERIFIED] Iranian casualties have increased from 35 dead/300 wounded yesterday to 38 dead/400+ wounded today. Civilian infrastructure strikes will likely accelerate the casualty count.*

Brent rose to 85.95 USD/Bbl on July 17, 2026, up 2.04% from the previous day. Over the past month, Brent's price has risen 7.64%, and is up 24.07% compared to the same time last year.

Global oil prices were on track to end the week higher after the US launched its sixth consecutive night of strikes against Iran. Brent crude, the global benchmark, was up 0.4% to nearly $85 a barrel, up from a closing price of $76 last Friday. West Texas Intermediate, the US benchmark, was up 0.9% to almost $80 a barrel by the same time, up from $71 a week ago.

According to Reuters, tanker traffic through the Strait has fallen to two-month lows as the conflict has intensified. Only seven vessels transited the waterway on Wednesday, the first full day after the US reimposed its naval blockade on Iran, down from 13 the previous day.

*Assessment: [VERIFIED] Oil has surged 11-12% this week — the best weekly performance since late April. Tanker traffic through Hormuz has fallen dramatically.
