## Key Developments (Past 24 Hours)

### **RUBIO CLAIMS IRAN HAS "LOST COMPLETE CONTROL" OF HORMUZ** [OFFICIAL]

Secretary of State Marco Rubio said Wednesday that Iran's grip on the Strait of Hormuz has significantly weakened in recent weeks. "The Strait of Hormuz is open. There's almost as much oil flowing out now as there was before this conflict began," Mr. Rubio told reporters in Athens. "They've lost complete control of the strait."

Rubio reiterated that Iran cannot have a nuclear weapon, adding that it failed to take advantage of multiple opportunities to reach an agreement with the US. He said that as a result of "crippling sanctions," the Iranian economy was "about to reach a place few countries in the world have been."

### **IRAN THREATENS TO BLOCK "ILLEGAL ROUTES" THROUGH HORMUZ** [OFFICIAL]

In direct response to Rubio's claims, transit routes in the Strait of Hormuz deemed illegal by Iran will soon be closed, an adviser to the Revolutionary Guards' Commander told the semi-official Fars News Agency on Wednesday, referring to the southern pathway hugging Oman's coast.

Mohammadreza Naqdi said these "few routes" had been created by blasting and damaging some of the rocky passages in the strategic waterway and were used by "small boats smuggling oil and transferring it to tankers", challenging the narrative that Gulf oil exports are regaining pre-war levels.

Brig Gen Mohammad Reza Naqdi reiterated Iran's position that the strait remains closed and said Iranian armed forces have "full control" over it.

**[ANALYSIS]** The Rubio-Naqdi exchange represents a propaganda war over Hormuz. The reality is somewhere in between: oil flows have recovered significantly through US Navy-protected southern routes, but Iran retains the ability to attack vessels and is now threatening to escalate by closing these alternative passages.

### **VANCE SOFTENS NUCLEAR DEMANDS — SIGNALS DIPLOMATIC OPENING** [OFFICIAL]

US Vice President JD Vance said in an interview with Reuters that Iran must meaningfully reduce its uranium enrichment capacity to meet US demands and end the seven-month war, while saying Washington remained uncertain about how decisions were being made in Tehran.

"I think if they want to show a commitment to not building a nuclear weapon, they would do something meaningful on their enrichment capacity," he said. Vance said Washington remained open to an agreement but expected concrete nuclear concessions from Tehran. "We're not going to trade words for actions," he said.

This demand that Iran make a "meaningful" reduction in its uranium enrichment capacity may signal a softening of Washington's earlier insistence that Tehran abandon enrichment altogether.

Vance, a longtime skeptic of US involvement in foreign wars, has taken a leading role in negotiating a settlement to the conflict and brokered a provisional peace agreement in June that quickly fell apart.

**[ANALYSIS]** Vance's language represents a notable shift. "Meaningful reduction" is different from "complete elimination" of enrichment. Analysts suggest this may indicate room for compromise, though one analyst noted that if the Americans were confident the strait was open properly, then they'd be pushing for a harder deal.

### **OPERATION YEMEN DAWN: DAY 3 — AL-WAZIIYA OFFENSIVE LAUNCHED** [VERIFIED]

Forces aligned with Yemen's internationally recognised government have launched an operation to capture a strategic mountainous district from the Houthis as part of a wider push to retake the Red Sea coast and the Bab al-Mandeb Strait.

The offensive on the district of al-Waziiya in Taiz governorate on Tuesday came as Saudi Arabia, which backs Yemen's government, intercepted a Houthi ballistic missile targeting the city of Khamis Mushait.

The media centre of Yemen's army said the Homeland Shield Forces have begun "a large-scale operation" to retake al-Waziiya. "The armed forces have secured strategic hills overlooking the al-Mansoura area, amidst a mass retreat by the Houthi militia."

**Houthis deny losses:** Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree said the group still held all territory seized last month. Houthi fighters "have full control over all their advances on the ground", he posted on Telegram.

### **MECCA PACT ACTIVATED: TURKEY & PAKISTAN DEPLOYING TROOPS** [VERIFIED]

Turkey and Pakistan will undertake a "rapid deployment" of troops to Saudi Arabia as part of a new mutual defence alliance, the three countries said Monday, as the Gulf kingdom faces escalating attacks from Yemen's Houthis. The three countries in August signed the Mecca Joint Defence Agreement committing them to come to each other's aid in the event one were attacked.

The pact unites NATO's second-largest army, that of Turkey, with the Islamic world's only nuclear-armed state, Pakistan, and the leading Gulf power, Saudi Arabia.

Pakistan already has tens of thousands of troops in the kingdom. Reuters reported last week that Pakistan sent four plane loads of weapons to the port city of Aden to support the Saudi-backed government.

**[ANALYSIS]** This is a major regional development. The formalization of the Mecca Pact and rapid troop deployments creates a new anti-Houthi/anti-Iran coalition beyond the original Saudi-UAE framework. Turkey's participation is particularly notable given its complex history with Saudi Arabia.

### **SATELLITE IMAGERY: RENEWED ACTIVITY AT IRANIAN NUCLEAR WEAPONS SITE** [REPORTED]

New satellite images show "significant renewed activity" at the underground Minzadehei nuclear facility near Tehran, which the IDF bombed during the war with Iran this year, according to a report published Tuesday by the Institute for Science and International Security. "Recent satellite imagery indicates a return to regular vehicle activity around the site post extensive cleanup operation."

"Given what is known about this compound, this activity may be indicative of an effort by Iran to reestablish a nuclear weapons development capability and to reconstitute what was lost in the attack on the compound," the institute said.

Based on public reporting and statements released by the IDF, the nuclear weapons work carried out at this site likely involved research and development on nuclear warheads.

### **HORMUZ ATTACKS HIT RECORD WEEKLY HIGH** [VERIFIED]

In the week of September 28-October 5, there had been at least 12 attacks on oil, liquefied natural gas and liquefied petroleum gas tankers around the Strait of Hormuz, according to data from three maritime security sources. That was the highest number of attacks in a single week since the US war with Iran began on February 28.

Iran has increased the pace of attacks on tankers in the Strait of Hormuz in recent days, just as oil shipments through the world's most important energy chokepoint approach prewar levels. UK Maritime Trade Operations has reported nine attacks in the waterway already this month, half the total number it reported for all of September.

Although Gulf oil exports have recovered significantly, attacks on vessels continue. On Tuesday, 12 crew members on board a Panama-flagged tanker were injured by an unknown projectile while crossing the strait.

## Assessment

**[ANALYSIS]** Today's dominant theme is **the battle over the Hormuz narrative**:

1. **The Rubio-Naqdi information war.** The US claims the strait is effectively open with flows approaching pre-war levels. Iran insists the strait remains closed and threatens to shut down the alternative southern routes vessels have been using. Both claims contain truth: flows have recovered substantially via US-protected routes, but Iran retains interdiction capability and is attacking at record rates.

2. **Vance's diplomatic signal.** The shift from demanding Iran eliminate enrichment to requesting "meaningful reduction" is notable. It suggests the administration may be feeling pressure from energy markets and midterm politics. However, the gap remains wide—Iran has demanded sanctions relief and blockade resolution before nuclear concessions.

3. **Operation Yemen Dawn's second phase.** The expansion to al-Waziiya moves the offensive from coastal terrain to mountainous districts. This is harder ground—the Houthis have proven resilient in mountain warfare. The conflicting claims (government: "mass retreat"; Houthis: "full control") reflect the fog of war.

4. **The Mecca Pact's significance.** The public deployment of Pakistani and Turkish troops to Saudi Arabia carries symbolic weight across the region. This creates a multi-national coalition with NATO and nuclear dimensions. However, it also risks drawing more states into an already complex conflict.

5. **The nuclear wildcard.** The Minzadayi imagery raises concerns that Iran is attempting to reconstitute weapons development capability destroyed in March. This is occurring simultaneously with Vance's diplomatic overtures, creating a race between negotiation and reconstruction.
