## Critical Developments (June 23-24)

### 1. IAEA Chief Defuses Nuclear Inspection Dispute [OFFICIAL]

[OFFICIAL] The head of the UN's nuclear watchdog has given his clearest signal yet that inspectors will gain access to Iran's uranium enrichment sites, pushing back against contradictory statements from both Washington and Tehran that have muddied the terms of last week's framework deal.

[VERIFIED] The international nuclear watchdog responsible for verifying Iran's stockpile of near-bomb-grade uranium dismissed the conflicting signals from Tehran and Washington overnight and said it expects to resume full monitoring at some stage. "There's a war of words here," said IAEA Director General Grossi.

[VERIFIED] Those inspections are key for the deal, which calls for Iran's stockpile of uranium to be "downblended" from highly enriched levels. There was no immediate reaction from Iran.

[REPORTED] The IAEA has said it observed regular vehicle movement in satellite imagery around the entrance to an underground tunnel complex at Isfahan, where uranium enriched to 20% and 60% is believed to be stored, and has stressed the urgency of gaining access without further delay.

**Critical context:** Since Israel launched a 12-day war on Iran in 2025, the IAEA has been blocked by Tehran from visiting enrichment sites where the Islamic Republic is believed to store enough highly enriched uranium to potentially build as many as 10 nuclear weapons, should it choose to rush for the bomb. Iran long has maintained that its program is peaceful, though it is the only country in the world to have uranium enriched up to 60% purity without a weapons program.

**Assessment:** Grossi's intervention is the most significant diplomatic development since the inspection dispute erupted. By asserting inspections will happen while emphasizing that timing is "not essential," he provided a face-saving path for both sides. This does not resolve the fundamental disagreement — the US wants immediate access to bombed sites, Iran insists this comes only with a final deal — but it prevents the framework from collapsing over the dispute.

### 3. Trump Declares Iran Will Charge No Hormuz Tolls [OFFICIAL]

[OFFICIAL] President Trump said there would be no tolls, insurance costs or charges of any kind for tankers looking to pass through the strait.

**Trump also warned that any discrepancy between Iran's assurances and its actions could jeopardize ongoing talks, stating "If this is false information, negotiations would end immediately!"**

[REPORTED] Iran and Oman announced the start of discussions on a joint framework for managing Hormuz transit, including fee structures, raising concerns that Tehran could impose passage charges.

**Assessment:** Trump's statement establishes a clear red line while Iran's discussions with Oman on "fee structures" suggest ambiguity remains. The gap between Trump's categorical denial of tolls and Iran's claims about changed "administration" of the strait could become a future flashpoint.

### 4. Hormuz Traffic Normalizing — Oman Corridor Operational [VERIFIED]

[VERIFIED] The cadence of vessels transiting the Strait of Hormuz remained steady Tuesday afternoon. In the past 24 hours, nearly two dozen vessels transited the strait. At least seven cargo ships and seven tankers exited into the Gulf of Oman, and six cargo ships crossed into the Persian Gulf — including two sailing under the Iranian flag. Before the war an average of 110 vessels transited the strait daily.

[VERIFIED] Oman's National Hydrographic Office said a "temporary maritime corridor" is being made available in the Strait of Hormuz. The notice says the usual traffic separation scheme, or TSS, is "not safe for use at this time." Instead, ships may be routed via two temporary outbound lanes through the strait — one to the north of the existing TSS and one to the south.

[VERIFIED] The International Maritime Organization said it had received security assurances that could enable hundreds of vessels to leave the Persian Gulf via Hormuz. The IEA reported that UAE oil exports in early June rebounded to nearly 85% of pre-conflict levels, supported by the use of pipelines, storage facilities, and alternative shipping routes.

[VERIFIED] Shipowners are confidently transiting the chokepoint with active satellite signals following safety guarantees from the IMO. The IEA estimates the United Arab Emirates is exporting oil at nearly 85% of pre-war levels, selling roughly 60 million barrels from the Persian Gulf recently.

**Assessment:** The opening of the Omani evacuation corridor specifically addresses the central channel mine concern. Combined with active AIS signals and IMO safety guarantees, this represents a functional reopening of the strait even while its official status remains "contested." Traffic is still ~25-30% of pre-war levels but trending upward.

### 5. Israel-Lebanon Talks Continue — Day 2, No Breakthrough [VERIFIED]

[VERIFIED] Israeli Ambassador to the US Yechiel Leiter laid into the Trump administration over its willingness to include a ceasefire in Lebanon as part of the memorandum of understanding Washington inked last week with Iran. "We are in a train wreck," Leiter said in a Hebrew statement issued as the ambassador began the fifth round of direct talks with Lebanon at the US State Department. Those talks were set up by the Trump administration specifically to detach Lebanon from the ongoing Iran conflict.

[OFFICIAL] Lebanese President Joseph Aoun rejects Israel's presence in south Lebanon and other foreign interference. "We accept nothing less than an end to the Israeli occupation and at the same time, the fall of foreign tutelage, because our only option is our national sovereignty and our sole wager is on the Lebanese state," Aoun said.

[VERIFIED] The new joint statement includes the creation of a "de-confliction cell" aimed at ending Israel's military operations in Lebanon, alongside a high-level committee and direct communication channels. The de-confliction cell is intended to support efforts to "ensure the adherence of the termination of military operations in Lebanon." VP Vance said the US and Iran will establish "coordination mechanisms," one to oversee the ceasefire in Lebanon and one to de-mine the Strait of Hormuz.

**Assessment:** The "train wreck" dynamic remains, but talks continuing into Day 2 without collapse is marginally positive. The proposed US-Lebanon-Iran "deconfliction cell" is a structural innovation that could help stabilize the situation, though it also gives Iran formal standing in Lebanon ceasefire monitoring.

### 6. Lebanon Ceasefire Holding — No Deaths Since Tuesday [VERIFIED]

[VERIFIED] Israeli gunfire killed two people in southern Lebanon on Tuesday, prompting Iran-backed Hezbollah to accuse Israel of violating a ceasefire that has largely held since Sunday. The lull in fighting is the longest yet in the war that was ignited by the US-Iran conflict on March 2. The highway south was clogged with cars on Tuesday as the relative calm encouraged displaced people to return home, despite concerns about the ceasefire's solidity.

[VERIFIED] Israeli attacks in Lebanon have killed more than 4,100 people, including 773 women, children and healthcare workers, according to the Lebanese health ministry. The toll does not say how many combatants are among the dead. Israel's death toll from this round of hostilities with Hezbollah includes at least 32 soldiers and four Israeli civilians.

[VERIFIED] The military held a similar security strip from 1985 until 2000, before withdrawing after years of steady casualties, a toll that is accumulating once again. Over the weekend, five soldiers were killed from Hezbollah fire within 24 hours.

**Assessment:** No additional fatalities reported since Tuesday's two deaths represents continued improvement. The ceasefire is the longest sustained halt since the March 2 escalation. However, the fundamental tensions remain unresolved: Israel refuses to withdraw, Hezbollah rejects any deal without full withdrawal, and Iran has made Lebanon a condition of the US-Iran framework.

[OFFICIAL] Technical-level talks between the US and Iran are expected to resume early next week at the Bürgenstock resort in Switzerland.

## Emerging Signals

**Grossi as de facto arbiter on nuclear issues:** The IAEA chief has effectively inserted himself as a mediator between US and Iranian claims. His reading of the MOU as requiring inspections gives Washington a win while his flexibility on timing gives Tehran room. The economic consequences of this price signal — lower inflation pressure in the US, reduced revenue pressure on Iran if it doesn't comply — reinforce the diplomatic track.

**Partial implementation as the emerging path:** The pattern is increasingly clear: Hormuz reopens (functional if not formal), economic measures proceed (oil waiver, $12 billion asset release, UAE exports at 85%), humanitarian steps advance (seafarer evacuation), while nuclear verification scope and Lebanon remain contested. This "half-deal" may become the de facto outcome.

**US-Lebanon-Iran deconfliction cell is structural innovation:** This proposed mechanism would formally link the US-Iran track with Lebanon, giving Iran formal standing in Lebanon ceasefire monitoring — a significant diplomatic shift from the US position that Lebanon is a "separate" track.
