## Daily incremental update — US–Israel–Iran conflict  
**As of Wed, June 24, 2026 (ET)**  
**New since Tue, June 23, 2026 (ET)**

### 1) Inspectors/access: the IAEA chief is now signaling inspections will happen (partial re-stabilization vs. yesterday’s public dispute)
- The AP reports that **IAEA Director General Rafael Grossi indicated Wednesday that inspectors would visit Iranian nuclear enrichment sites**—framing inspections as a *key component* of the interim U.S.–Iran arrangement. **[VERIFIED]** ([apnews.com](https://apnews.com/article/875ee115cacd1f5923052b70f2be4124?utm_source=openai))  
- UN Geneva coverage from the IAEA/UN orbit continues to message that the agency is prepared to support “concrete measures such as verification,” reinforcing that a verification track is actively being built (even if politics remain noisy). **[OFFICIAL]** ([ungeneva.org](https://www.ungeneva.org/en/news-media/news/2026/06/119773/us-iran-deal-technical-work-can-begin-says-atomic-energy-agency?utm_source=openai))  

**What changed / what it means:**  
Yesterday’s headline dynamic was “the U.S. says yes; Iran says no.” Today’s shift is that **the IAEA is publicly leaning toward “access is coming,”** which reduces (not eliminates) the chance of an immediate verification-driven breakdown. The key caveat remains **scope and timing**: “enrichment sites” language is not the same as guaranteed access to **bombed/sensitive locations** or full continuity of monitoring.

### 2) Hormuz: the disruption is now big enough to trigger a UN/IMO evacuation operation for stranded seafarers
- Axios reports the **UN’s International Maritime Organization (IMO) plans to evacuate 11,000+ sailors** stranded in/around the Strait of Hormuz amid the war-related shipping disruption. **[REPORTED]** ([axios.com](https://www.axios.com/2026/06/23/iran-war-un-hormuz-strait-stranded-ships-evacuate?utm_source=openai))  

**What changed / what it means:**  
This is a **material escalation in the “civilian/shipping system stress” signal**—even if the interim deal keeps some traffic moving. It supports the view that the *center of gravity* of risk is shifting toward **maritime insecurity/insurance/route disruption** rather than immediate large-scale land escalation.

---

### 3) Deconfliction architecture: new detail on a comms line + deconfliction cell tied to Hormuz safe passage
- Anadolu (citing Pakistani officials) says the process now includes a **communication line to avoid incidents/miscommunication** and a **“de-confliction cell”** involving the U.S., Iran, Lebanon’s government, and mediators—explicitly tied to **safe passage in Hormuz** and a **60-day roadmap** to a final deal. **[REPORTED]** ([aa.com.tr](https://www.aa.com.tr/en/us-israel-iran-war/us-iran-to-have-communication-line-to-avoid-incidents-pakistan/3976346?utm_source=openai))  

**What changed / what it means:**  
This is the first concrete “plumbing” detail that plausibly reduces the probability of **accidental escalation at sea** (misidentification, boarding incidents, close encounters). It doesn’t solve intentional coercion, but it **improves crisis-management capacity**, which matters given how often Hormuz has been used for signaling.
