## Key Developments Since Prior Report (September 15)

### 1. MAJOR: US Officials Met Houthis in Oman Over Weekend

**[REPORTED — MULTIPLE SOURCES]** US officials met representatives of Yemen's Iran-aligned Houthis in Oman over the weekend, Reuters reported on Wednesday, citing five sources familiar with the matter. The previously undisclosed meeting was held at the US Embassy in Muscat, three of the sources told Reuters. Two said Oman, a longtime regional mediator, helped organize the talks.

**[REPORTED]** One source said the meeting took place on Sunday. US embassy personnel represented Washington, while the Houthi delegation included senior official Mohammad Abdulsalam, who is under US sanctions, and Abdelmalik al-Ajiri.

**[REPORTED]** Reuters reports the terror group pledged not to attack American or Israeli vessels.

**[OFFICIAL]** Washington has negotiated with them since, reaching a ceasefire in May last year after bombing the group for two months to halt a campaign of attacks on Red Sea shipping. Asked whether the US had met with the Houthis in Oman, a State Department spokesperson did not confirm the meeting, but said safeguarding freedom of navigation in the Red Sea and preventing the export of terrorism from the Middle East were core US interests.

*Assessment: This is the most significant diplomatic development in days. The US is actively maintaining a channel with Houthis while refusing Saudi requests for direct military intervention. This reinforces Trump's strategy of keeping the Houthi conflict compartmentalized from the broader Iran war.*

### 2. NOTABLE: Houthis Claim Attack on Aramco Yanbu Facility

**[UNCONFIRMED — HOUTHI CLAIM ONLY]** Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree said on Wednesday that the group had targeted an oil facility and military base in Saudi Arabia using missiles and drones in retaliation for strikes carried out by Riyadh against neighboring Yemen. He said the attack targeted a facility belonging to the Saudi oil giant Aramco in Yanbu, on the Red Sea, "causing fires and widespread destruction," and another targeted the Khamis Mushait military airbase.

**[VERIFIED]** Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree said on Wednesday that the group had attacked an airbase in Khamis Mushait as well as Aramco facilities in Yanbu. Saudi Arabia did not confirm either of the attacks.

**[REPORTED]** The Houthis said they carried out two military operations targeting a facility of state oil company Saudi Aramco in Yanbu in western Saudi Arabia and the Khamis Mushait air base in the country's southwest. In the first operation, the group said it struck the Aramco facility with dozens of ballistic missiles and attack drones. The Houthis said the strike caused a large fire and extensive damage, but they released no specific evidence. Yanbu is the terminus of a pipeline that runs east to west across Saudi Arabia and is a key hub for crude exports through the Red Sea. Its strategic importance is significant because it allows crude to be shipped without passing through the Strait of Hormuz.

**[UNCONFIRMED — HOUTHI CLAIM]** The Houthis also said they shot down a Saudi F-15 fighter jet over Marib province in northeastern Yemen the same day. They provided no photos or video to support the claim. The Saudi government and Aramco have not officially confirmed any damage.

*Assessment: If the Yanbu attack is confirmed, this represents a direct strike on the terminal of the East-West pipeline — the same infrastructure that's already offline due to the September 11 attacks. This would compound the already severe constraints on Saudi export capacity. However, without Saudi confirmation, the extent of damage remains unclear.*

### 3. UPDATE: Saudi Pipeline Repair Timeline — Conflicting Estimates

**[OFFICIAL]** Saudi Arabia's critical East-West crude oil pipeline will start operating again "very soon" after Iran-backed attacks forced its closure, U.S. Energy Secretary Chris Wright told CNBC in an interview Tuesday. "This will be a brief and temporary interruption. It will be measured in days," Wright told CNBC's Morgan Brennan in Houston where energy officials from G20 nations are meeting.

**[REPORTED]** There has been damage to the pipeline, including at one major pumping station along the pipeline route, satellite images released by Vantor and published via Getty Images showed. The repairs could take three to five weeks, two regional officials briefed on the matter told The Associated Press.

**[REPORTED]** An oil industry analyst stated: "Judging from the on-line pictures, it will take months to repair."

*Assessment: There's a significant discrepancy between the US Energy Secretary's "days" estimate and regional officials' "3-5 weeks" estimate based on satellite imagery. The truth is likely somewhere in between. Wright may be emphasizing political messaging or referencing partial restart capacity.*

### 4. UPDATE: Yemen Humanitarian Crisis — 100,000+ Displaced, Thousands Flee to Djibouti

**[VERIFIED]** UNHCR, the UN Refugee Agency, is warning that rapidly escalating hostilities along Yemen's western coast have displaced more than 100,000 people inside the country and forced thousands more to seek safety across the Bab el-Mandeb Strait in Djibouti.

**[VERIFIED]** After Houthi forces seized the strategic Yemeni island of Mayyun, also known as Perim, in the Bab al-Mandeb strait... More than 3,400 people fleeing renewed fighting in Yemen have landed along Djibouti's northern coast, according to Djiboutian authorities.

**[REPORTED]** Fighting between the Houthis and Saudi-backed Yemeni government forces is intensifying across several fronts in the most serious escalation since a U.N.-brokered truce halted large-scale fighting in 2022. The escalation has displaced more than 100,000 people inside Yemen, while thousands more have fled across the Red Sea to Djibouti.

**[VERIFIED]** A UN official stated: "But there may be a time, if they continue to come, when they may overwhelm the capacity of the country to house and accommodate all of them." Djibouti's finance minister, Ilyas Dawaleh, said the situation was "becoming increasingly difficult for our country, despite our unwavering commitment to humanitarian assistance."

*Assessment: The humanitarian situation in Yemen is deteriorating rapidly. The 100,000+ displacement figure and thousands fleeing to Djibouti represent the most serious humanitarian crisis in the conflict since 2022.*
