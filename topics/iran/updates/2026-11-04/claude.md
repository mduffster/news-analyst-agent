## Key Developments (Past 24 Hours)

### **DEMOCRATS SWEEP 2026 MIDTERMS** [VERIFIED]

Democrats won sweeping victories in the 2026 midterm elections on Tuesday and will take control of the House and Senate next year, CBS News projects, as voters repudiated President Trump and GOP control in Washington.

CBS News estimates Democrats will hold 251 to 268 House seats. Democrats have secured at least 54 seats in the Senate. Texas and Kansas elected their first Democratic senators in decades, and the party also flipped seats in Ohio, Iowa, North Carolina, South Carolina and Maine.

The party has flipped seven Senate seats: Iowa, Kansas, Ohio, Maine, North Carolina, South Carolina and Texas. Democrats have also flipped governorships in Texas, Georgia, Nevada, Ohio and Iowa.

**[ANALYSIS]** This is a historic rebuke of the Trump administration. The scope exceeds most historical midterm swings against incumbent presidents. The war on Iran is being cited as a central factor, alongside inflation exacerbated by the conflict.

### **VANCE BLAMES IRAN WAR FOR GOP DEFEAT** [VERIFIED]

Vice President JD Vance blamed the Republican Party's massive midterm losses on the country's connection to Israel and the war in Iran in a social media post early on Wednesday. "The real reason we lost is obvious to almost everyone," Vance wrote on X.

Vance has blamed the war on Iran for his party's withering midterms defeat, suggesting that the conflict may not have been worth ceding control of the US Congress to Democrats. "The reason we lost is the war."

Citing a pollster he describes as "one of the smartest people I know", Vance said he was told ending the Iran war and "changing our relationship with Israel" were two of the top four answers given by Americans surveyed who had voted for Trump in 2024 but were planning to vote for Democrats in the 2026 midterms.

Vance, a longtime vocal critic of US foreign interventionism, had reportedly been skeptical behind the scenes about the war on Iran that Trump and Israel launched in late February.

**[ANALYSIS]** Vance is positioning for 2028 by distancing himself from the war. This is a remarkable break with a sitting president from his own vice president. It signals deep fissures within the MAGA movement over foreign policy.

### **TRUMP: "IRAN WAS A PART OF IT" BUT NO REGRETS** [VERIFIED]

Trump today acknowledged that the Iran war played a role in his party's defeat in the midterm elections. Trump told Fox News that "I think a part of it was Iran," and separately told the Wall Street Journal "Iran was a piece of it."

US President Donald Trump said he has no immediate plans to launch strikes on Iran following midterm elections. "I'm ready, we're ready. We're locked and loaded if we need to go, but there may be no reason for it."

President Trump has said the war with Iran was partly responsible for the dismal midterm election results for his Republican party this week, but that he does not regret his decision to launch it in conjunction with Israel.

A political scientist noted: "The midterms just delivered a clear political signal against the war, but the next several weeks carry a high risk of military escalation before the new Congress is sworn in. After January, blame-shifting between the president and Congress on the Iran war is the most predictable outcome."

**[ANALYSIS]** Trump's "no immediate plans" is notably more cautious than pre-election rhetoric. However, the warning that the lame-duck period before January carries "high risk of military escalation" is significant — Trump has a window before Democratic Congress constrains him.

### **OIL CRASHES ~9% ON MIDTERM PEACE HOPES** [VERIFIED]

Brent fell $8.94, or 8.6%, to $94.66 per barrel at 1:06 p.m. ET on Wednesday, after touching its lowest level since September 4, with traders pricing an end to the Iran war after the White House tied Tuesday's midterm losses to the conflict.

Oil's drop over the last three trading sessions has been precipitous. On Friday, Brent oil traded as high as $118 per barrel but by Wednesday, its price was around $94.

"The oil market was moving lower on the expectation that Trump might move on from Iran, but bigger headlines on peace talks were needed to truly settle the market," said one analyst.

Both have now retreated more than 15 per cent this week.

**[ANALYSIS]** This is the sharpest weekly oil price decline since the war began. Markets are betting Trump will be forced to de-escalate. However, if no deal materializes and conflict resumes, prices could spike sharply again.

### **IRAN: 4 TANKERS DESTROYED BY MINES IN HORMUZ** [OFFICIAL — DISPUTED]

Iran's Revolutionary Guard today said four oil tankers were destroyed overnight after striking naval mines while trying to traverse a prohibited route south of the key waterway of the Strait of Hormuz. The elite corps said the tankers were still ablaze, and that two other vessels had been seized.

"Four non-compliant oil tankers attempting to pass through the prohibited passage south of the Strait of Hormuz were destroyed after striking naval mines and are still burning," Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guards Corps said in a statement quoted by state TV.

In recent weeks, Islamic Republic state media have repeatedly quoted the IRGC reporting that ships struck naval mines in the Strait of Hormuz. However, U.S. Central Command (CENTCOM) has repeatedly refuted these claims, dismissing reports of ships hitting naval mines as inaccurate.

**[ANALYSIS]** Iran's mine claims have previously been disputed by CENTCOM. Whether accurate or propaganda, these statements signal Iran is maintaining its blockade posture despite diplomatic signals. The mines represent a persistent threat that could reignite crisis at any moment.

### **KHAMENEI: EXPECT NO US POLICY CHANGE** [OFFICIAL]

Iran's Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei said Wednesday that anyone expecting a shift in American policy, after midterm elections dealt a blow to US President Donald Trump, was "seriously mistaken." "Should such aggressions be repeated, the war's scope will become much wider," said Khamenei.

Khamenei's statement came after Iran's control of the Strait of Hormuz had shattered the "myth of America's invincibility" and warned that any renewed US attack would draw a far wider response.

He said Washington seeks the "unconditional surrender of the Iranian nation." Referring to the US-Israeli war on Iran, Khamenei said the US had "suffered an irreversible decline" after a "self-made scandal," adding that "the process of its collapse has accelerated."

Khamenei adviser Mohammad Mokhber said Trump was "slapped in the face by his own people" on Tuesday night. "The true winner in this arena was Iran, which transferred the cost of the war into the United States," he said.

**[ANALYSIS]** Iran is taking a victory lap over the midterms while simultaneously warning against expecting concessions. Khamenei's "wider war" threat appears designed to deter any lame-duck escalation while maintaining pressure for sanctions relief.

### **SPEAKER JOHNSON STEPS ASIDE** [VERIFIED]

House Speaker Mike Johnson will be stepping aside from Republican Party leadership after the GOP's wipeout in the midterm election, declining to seek support to stay on as House minority leader in the new Congress.

Democrats captured at least 21 GOP-held seats Tuesday, enough to retake control of the chamber.

Steve Scalise, the House majority leader, is running for the leadership post.

Republicans are scheduled to hold their leadership elections on Nov. 10.

**[ANALYSIS]** Johnson's exit removes a close Trump ally from leadership. This sets up internal GOP battles that could further fracture the party on Iran policy.

### **ISRAEL: HERZOG CONSULTATIONS BEGIN** [VERIFIED]

President Isaac Herzog began consultations with political parties Wednesday to determine who should be tasked with forming the next government. Herzog will meet representatives of all parties elected to the 120-seat Knesset through Thursday evening before deciding which candidate has the best chance of assembling a governing coalition.

Yisrael Beiteinu recommended Gadi Eisenkot to form the next government during consultations. The United Arab List faction will recommend Yashar party leader Gadi Eisenkot to form the next government.

Netanyahu is now expected to receive 46 recommendations, while Yashar chairman Gadi Eisenkot is expected to receive 56.

Ra'am backs Eisenkot as some ultra-Orthodox factions withhold endorsements. Yisrael Beiteinu formally recommended Eisenkot during its consultations with Herzog, arguing that the former IDF chief had the best chance of forming a government.

Eisenkot pledged to bring "new leadership to Israel" and form a national emergency government as soon as possible, in a message addressed to the United States and the international community.

**[ANALYSIS]** Eisenkot is positioned to receive the mandate, likely tomorrow. With 56-67 expected recommendations vs. 46 for Netanyahu, Herzog should grant Eisenkot the first opportunity to form a government. This would mark a significant shift in Israel's Iran posture.
