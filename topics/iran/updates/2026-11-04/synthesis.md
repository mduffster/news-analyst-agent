# US-Israel-Iran Conflict: Democrats Sweep Midterms as Vance Blames War for GOP Rout

## November 4, 2026 — Day 250

**Assessment:** The conflict reached a political watershed as Democrats captured both chambers of Congress in a historic midterm rebuke, with Vice President Vance publicly blaming the Iran war as "the reason we lost." Trump acknowledged the war contributed to GOP losses but expressed no regrets, while telling the Financial Times the US has "no immediate plans" to strike Iran—a notably more cautious posture than pre-election threats. Senate Democratic Leader Schumer vowed to use the "power of the purse" to end the war. Oil crashed approximately 9% to around $99 as markets priced in de-escalation, while Iran's IRGC claimed four tankers were destroyed by mines in Hormuz. Supreme Leader Khamenei warned anyone expecting US policy to shift is "seriously mistaken." In Israel, Herzog consultations began with Eisenkot leading 56-46 in expected recommendations. The conflict now enters a dangerous interregnum: Trump retains full executive authority through January, but faces political winds strongly against escalation and a Democratic Congress preparing to constrain war funding.

## 1. Democrats Sweep Both Chambers in Historic Rebuke [VERIFIED]

Democrats won sweeping victories in the 2026 midterm elections on Tuesday and will take control of the House and Senate next year, CBS News projects, as voters repudiated President Trump and GOP control in Washington.

CBS News estimates Democrats will hold 251 to 268 House seats. Democrats have secured at least 54 seats in the Senate. Democrats seized control of the House and Senate in commanding fashion during Tuesday's midterm elections, flipping seven Senate seats and making history in multiple races in a widespread rebuke of the Trump administration and Republican candidates.

Democrats are projected to have flipped Senate seats from Republicans in Iowa, Kansas, North Carolina, Ohio, South Carolina and Texas, the Associated Press projected.

The decisive wins came as President Trump's approval rating hit 38%, historically low for a president in the midterms, in an election where 89% of voters said cost of living was a major or crucial factor in their votes.

**Significance:** This represents one of the most lopsided midterm performances in modern US history. The combination of flipping both chambers—including traditionally Republican strongholds like Texas and Kansas—signals a fundamental political rejection of both the war and the administration's handling of inflation. Starting in January, Democrats can block legislation, demand hearings, and leverage appropriations to constrain war conduct.

## 2. Vance Publicly Blames War for GOP Defeat [VERIFIED]

In a remarkable break from a sitting president, Vice President JD Vance blamed his party's losses on Americans' frustration over the U.S. war with Iran, which has driven up oil prices at a time when costs are already high.

Vice President JD Vance delivered a blunt assessment of Republicans' sweeping midterm losses Wednesday morning, pointing at the Iran war as the driving force behind the party's election-night defeats. "The reason we lost is the war."

Vice President JD Vance blamed the Republican Party's massive midterm losses on the country's connection to Israel and the war in Iran in a social media post early on Wednesday. "The real reason we lost is obvious to almost everyone," Vance wrote on X.

"If you'd like to argue it was worth it, that losing the Senate for the next 6 years was worth it, then do so," Vance wrote.

"The main problem is that many people who voted for Trump/Vance in 2024 voted for Democrats in 2026," he wrote.

**Significance:** Vice President JD Vance is blaming Republicans' midterm performance directly on the Iran war. "The reason we lost is the war," he said in an X post that distanced himself from Trump and his agenda as he eyes a 2028 run. This public break positions Vance for a 2028 campaign premised on foreign policy restraint—and signals deep fissures within the MAGA movement over interventionism. The vice president who was known for anti-war positions before February is now explicitly validating those views.

## 3. Trump Acknowledges War's Cost but Defends Decision [VERIFIED]

President Donald Trump on Wednesday said Vice President JD Vance's analysis that Republicans lost the midterms because of the war in Iran was "largely true," but that he would do it again because "it's the only answer."

"You can't let Iran have a nuclear weapon."

US President Donald Trump said he has no immediate plans to launch strikes on Iran following midterm elections in which the Democrats took control of Congress in a landslide win over his Republican party. "We seem to be getting everything we want," Trump said in an interview with the Financial Times about the conflict.

"I'm ready, we're ready. We're locked and loaded if we need to go, but there may be no reason for it."

Asked about Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's suggestions that another attack on Iran could come later this week, US President Donald Trump told the Financial Times on Tuesday that Washington has no immediate plans for new strikes.

**Significance:** Trump's posture has notably shifted from pre-election threats to end the war "one way or the other" to expressing satisfaction with the current standoff. The "no immediate plans" language is more cautious than expected, though Gulf allies have also reportedly been told to prepare for possible strikes after November 4. The window for action remains open, but the political signal against escalation is now unmistakable.

## 4. Schumer Vows Democrats Will Use "Power of the Purse" [VERIFIED]

Senate Democratic Leader Chuck Schumer (N.Y.) said there will be Senate Republican support for ending the war with Iran as soon as possible and threatened that Democrats will use the "power of the purse" to starve the Defense Department of funding if necessary to bring a swift end to the conflict. "We are going to end the war. We have some Republicans already who have joined us in ending the war, and the Founding Fathers had the power of the purse as one of the ways of deterring a president who does war without the consent of the American people," Schumer said.

"This war must end. It must end," Schumer told journalists on Wednesday, later noting that the "power of the purse" is "one of the ways of deterring a president who does war without the consent of the American people."

Democrats are threatening to block the annual Defense authorization bill and Defense appropriations measure as long as the nation is still at war with Iran.

**Significance:** But even if the Senate passes a war powers resolution that has the binding force of law, Trump could still veto it and Democrats don't have the power to override his opposition. Democrats' leverage is real but limited—they can constrain funding but cannot force Trump to end operations. The confrontation will likely play out through appropriations battles starting in January. Four Republican senators have already backed war powers resolutions, suggesting potential for bipartisan pressure.

## 5. Oil Crashes to Four-Week Low on Peace Hopes [VERIFIED]

Markets responded dramatically to the political shift:

The price for a barrel of Brent crude, the international standard, plunged 9.2% on Nov. 4 to $94.11.

The price for a barrel of Brent crude oil fell below $100 for the first time in nearly a month. Vice President JD Vance blamed the war with Iran, which caused a spike in oil prices, for Republicans' midterm losses.

Brent fell to 99.20 USD/Bbl on November 4, 2026, down 4.25% from the previous day. Over the past month, Brent's price has fallen 1.12%, but it is still 56.17% higher than a year ago.

The week from 30 October to 4 November took around 8 percent off the oil price without anything improving on the water.

**Significance:** The approximately 9% single-day crash represents the sharpest decline since the war began. Markets are pricing in either de-escalation or at minimum a prolonged standoff rather than imminent major strikes. However, four hits off Hormuz during the same week demonstrate that maritime threats persist regardless of political developments. If the lame-duck period produces renewed strikes, a violent price reversal is likely.

## 6. Iran Claims Four Tankers Destroyed by Mines; CENTCOM Disputes [OFFICIAL — DISPUTED]

Iran claimed Wednesday at least four oil tankers caught fire after hitting mines in the Strait of Hormuz. "Four non-compliant oil tankers attempting to pass through the prohibited passage south of the Strait of Hormuz were destroyed after striking naval mines and are still burning," Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guards Corps said in a statement quoted by state TV.

In a statement, the elite corps said the tankers were still ablaze, and added that two other vessels had been seized.

In recent weeks, Islamic Republic state media have repeatedly quoted the IRGC and the Iranian Army reporting that ships struck naval mines in the Strait of Hormuz. Central Command (CENTCOM) has repeatedly refuted these claims, dismissing reports of ships hitting naval mines as inaccurate.

The claims have not been independently confirmed.

**Significance:** Whether accurate or propaganda, Iran's sustained mine claims serve multiple purposes: maintaining deterrence, demonstrating continued control over Hormuz, and raising insurance costs for vessels attempting unauthorized transit. Mines are difficult to detect and clear, and can threaten any ship passing through an area. That would deter traffic and push up insurance costs, even for tankers with no link to the conflict. The blockade infrastructure remains in place regardless of diplomatic or political developments elsewhere.

## 7. Khamenei: Expect No US Policy Shift [OFFICIAL]

Iran is taking a victory lap over the midterms while simultaneously warning against complacency:

Iran's Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei said Wednesday that anyone expecting a shift in American policy, after midterm elections dealt a blow to US President Donald Trump, was "seriously mistaken."

In a written statement, Khamenei also warned that Iran's response to any renewed US aggression would be on a much greater scale.

Earlier, Iran's chief negotiator in talks with the US, Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, said Americans had voted against war. "If the US govt betrays their public will to serve international paymasters and blackmailers, it will learn our NO comes with a Nightmare," Ghalibaf said.

Before the vote, Iran offered to open the Strait of Hormuz within two days if Trump agreed to its seven conditions.

**Significance:** Iran's messaging is internally consistent: celebrate the political defeat, warn against assuming it means concessions, maintain blockade posture. The seven conditions remain non-negotiable, and Tehran is clearly preparing for both continued negotiations and potential renewed strikes. "Trump has been defeated both militarily and politically," Ejei said, adding that Iran would seek to inflict further defeats on the US president.

## 8. Israel: Herzog Consultations Underway, Eisenkot Leads [VERIFIED]

Herzog begins consultations with Knesset factions on forming next government.

Avigdor Lieberman's Yisrael Beiteinu recommended Gadi Eisenkot to form the next government during consultations with President Isaac Herzog at the President's Residence in Jerusalem on Wednesday.

The Arab-majority United Arab List faction will recommend Yashar party leader Gadi Eisenkot to form the next government. The decision brings Eisenkot's expected endorsements to 56, compared with 46 for Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu.

Neither Netanyahu's bloc nor the Jewish opposition parties secured a Knesset majority. With 19 seats between them, the two Arab parties could determine whether Eisenkot gets the first chance to form a government.

**Significance:** Eisenkot is positioned to receive the first mandate, likely by Thursday. His pledged "new leadership" for Israel signals a potential shift in the country's Iran posture, though coalition negotiations will take weeks. The anti-Israel sentiment only hardened during the Iran war, with segments of the US electorate viewing the joint US-Israel strikes as a war that Netanyahu sold to Trump. Even now, Netanyahu has been pushing to resume strikes on Iran.

## 9. Speaker Johnson Won't Seek GOP Leadership [VERIFIED]

Speaker Mike Johnson will not seek to serve as House minority leader in the next Congress. A source familiar with the speaker's decision confirmed that the Louisiana Republican would not seek the post after Tuesday's midterm elections saw Republicans lose their narrow majority in a Democratic tsunami.

Ahead of the elections, two other House GOP lawmakers — Majority Leader Steve Scalise (Louisiana) and Judiciary Committee Chair Jim Jordan (Ohio) — signaled plans to run for minority leader regardless of Johnson's intentions.

House Republicans are scheduled to hold leadership elections on November 10.

**Significance:** Johnson's exit creates a leadership vacuum at a critical moment. The fight to replace him pits the House GOP's current second-ranking leader, Steve Scalise of Louisiana, an establishment conservative, against Judiciary Committee Chairman Jim Jordan of Ohio, a combative hardliner. The outcome will shape how House Republicans position themselves on Iran policy going forward—and whether any faction aligns with Democratic war-ending efforts.
