## Executive Summary

**US overnight "self-defense" strikes in southern Iran inject fresh volatility into near-complete deal; Iran calls it a "gross violation" of ceasefire but negotiations continue in Qatar; Trump convenes rare Camp David Cabinet meeting tomorrow as decision point looms; Israel escalates in Lebanon with IDF pushing toward Litani River.**

**Bottom line:** The trajectory has shifted from "deal imminent" (May 25) to "deal still possible but endangered" (May 26). American forces had "committed a violation of the ceasefire in the Hormuz region over the past 48 hours," Tehran's foreign ministry said Tuesday. The U.S. said the strikes on boats and missile launchers were self-defense. Despite inflammatory rhetoric, Iranian negotiators remain in Doha, suggesting Tehran hasn't abandoned the diplomatic track. Tomorrow's Camp David Cabinet meeting is the key inflection point—it could produce a breakthrough announcement or signal a decision to resume military pressure.

## Critical Developments Since May 25

### 1. US Strikes Southern Iran — CENTCOM Claims "Self-Defense"

**[VERIFIED]** "The United States Central Command (CENTCOM) claimed it carried out the 'self-defense strikes' to protect US troops from threats posed by Iranian forces. However, it did not provide details about the strikes and their locations. Iranian media reported explosions in Bandar Abbas, some 70km (42 miles) from the Strait of Hormuz, in southern Iran."

**[VERIFIED]** A Telegram channel affiliated with Iran's powerful Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) identified four navy personnel it says were killed in overnight U.S. airstrikes in southern Iran. It named the four men as Abbas Eslami, Ghodrat Zarangari, Abdolreza Golzari, and Hossein Sotoudeh. Sotoudeh had been "due to hold his wedding ceremony in the coming days."

**[OFFICIAL]** The US military reportedly targeted missile launch sites and vessels suspected of attempting to deploy mines in southern Iran, with US Central Command saying the operations were aimed at protecting American troops in the region. At the same time, President Donald Trump said talks with Tehran were progressing well, though he warned that additional attacks could follow if negotiations broke down.

**Assessment:** This is not the first military action since the April 8 ceasefire—skirmishes have occurred throughout. But the timing is sensitive: strikes came hours after Iranian negotiators met with Qatari mediators in Doha, just as both sides were describing a deal as near-complete.

### 2. Iran Threatens Retaliation, Calls Ceasefire "Violated"

**[OFFICIAL]** "The United States committed a gross violation of the ceasefire in the Hormozgan region in the past 48 hours. … Iran holds the U.S. regime responsible for all the consequences resulting from these aggressive and unjustified actions."

**[OFFICIAL]** Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei also said that countries in the region could no longer be a shield for U.S. bases.

**[OFFICIAL]** "The Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps warns against any violation of the ceasefire by the aggressive US military, and considers its right to reciprocal response to be legitimate and certain," a statement by the group said according to Iranian state-affiliated media.

**[REPORTED]** Tehran's powerful Revolutionary Guard vowed to "respond decisively to any violation of the ceasefire." It claimed to have shot down an American MQ-9 drone and fired at another U.S. drone and F-35 fighter jet, forcing them to turn around, according to a statement carried by the semi-official Fars news agency.

**[REPORTED]** "The response to any new aggression will be different from what it was before," Iran's senior armed forces spokesman told Al Jazeera, adding that Iran's attacks would be "more severe, heavier and stronger" than before.

**Assessment:** Iran has not yet retaliated militarily despite the rhetoric. The presence of Tehran's top negotiators in Qatar offered another clue that there may be progress toward a deal to end the three-month war. Parliament Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf left Qatar on Tuesday after staying overnight for continuing talks. The key question is whether Tehran treats this as a deal-breaker or an irritant to be set aside.

### 3. Camp David Cabinet Meeting Tomorrow — Decision Point

**[OFFICIAL]** President Donald Trump and his entire Cabinet will meet at Camp David on Wednesday, a White House official told CNBC. The trip was revealed less than a day after new U.S. strikes in Iran raised concerns about an anticipated peace deal.

**[OFFICIAL]** President Donald Trump will convene a rare Cabinet meeting at Camp David Wednesday as negotiations with Iran enter a critical phase. All Cabinet members are expected to attend the meeting, including outgoing Director of National Intelligence Tulsi Gabbard. The gathering comes as the administration weighs next steps in high-stakes negotiations aimed at securing a broader agreement with Tehran amid a fraying ceasefire.

**[REPORTED]** On June 8, 2025, Trump hosted Cabinet members and senior officials at Camp David for discussions on Iran and Gaza. Weeks later, on June 22, 2025, Trump directed U.S. strikes on Iranian nuclear facilities.

**[REPORTED]** It remains unclear what Trump plans to do at Camp David. U.S. reports have not indicated that he intends to present an agreement or announce a breakdown in talks.

**Assessment:** The presidential property has historically been the site of some significant U.S. policy developments — including the 1978 Camp David Accords. The historical parallel (June 2025 meeting preceding strikes) is ominous but not determinative. The meeting could signal either a breakthrough announcement or a decision on escalation.

### 4. Deal Framework: Wording Disputes, Frozen Assets Remain Sticking Points

**[OFFICIAL]** Secretary of State Marco Rubio said Tuesday that a peace deal could take "a few more days" to finalize. "He's [Trump] going to make a good deal or no deal, so on that everyone should be assured," Rubio told reporters at Jaipur International Airport in India. "But that may take a little while, I mean, a few more days."

**[REPORTED]** The US and Iran are working toward a memorandum of understanding, but disputes over language concerning Iran's nuclear program and sanctions have held up a deal.

**[REPORTED]** In recent days, Trump said he was giving his envoys time to negotiate with Iran but would not sign a "bad deal." Overnight, however, Trump appeared to shift his position. After weeks of saying Iran's enriched uranium must be removed from the country, he did not rule out another option: destroying it inside Iran under the supervision of the International Atomic Energy Agency.

**[REPORTED]** Tasnim news agency said Iran wants $12 billion to be transferred immediately once an agreement is signed with the US. Another $12 billion would be paid within 60 days under the Iranian proposal.

**[REPORTED]** Iranian media reported possible progress on one of the main sticking points. Iran's Fars news agency, citing a source close to the negotiating team, said the "last serious dispute" between the sides — access to frozen funds — was "on the way to being resolved" through Qatari mediation.

**Assessment:** The framework structure remains intact: 60-day ceasefire extension, Hormuz reopening, gradual sanctions relief, deferred nuclear negotiations. The sticking points are language (nuclear commitments, frozen funds, Hormuz management), not structure. This suggests a deal remains achievable if both sides want it.

### 5. Israel Escalates in Lebanon — IDF Pushes Toward Litani River

**[VERIFIED]** Israel's military clashed with the Iranian-backed Hezbollah militant group Tuesday along a strategic river in Lebanon as Israeli troops tried to push farther north, just three days before Lebanese and Israeli military delegations are set to meet for direct talks in Washington. A previously reached ceasefire appeared more nominal by the day, complicating efforts at a broader peace in the Iran war, as Tehran wants an end to the fighting to include Lebanon. The Litani River has been a de facto boundary in Lebanon, with large areas to the south under Israeli military control.

**[VERIFIED]** One strike hit the eastern village of Mashghara, killing 12 people including several members of a family, Lebanon's state-run National News Agency said. Israel in recent days has intensified strikes in Nabatieh city and province, just north of the river. On Tuesday it warned city residents to leave.

**[VERIFIED]** At least 3,185 people in Lebanon have been killed in Israeli strikes since the start of the war, according to the Lebanese Health Ministry. Over 9,600 others have been wounded. Elsewhere, according to Netanyahu's office, 23 Israeli soldiers and a defense contractor have been killed in or near southern Lebanon, and two civilians have been killed in northern Israel, the vast majority by drones.

**[REPORTED]** An Israeli source told CNN that forces will "expand operations" in Lebanon and "renew operations" in the capital Beirut. The Trump administration has doubled down its support for the Israeli campaign. The US president told Netanyahu he backs the country's wish to "maintain freedom of action against threats on all fronts, including Lebanon."

**Assessment:** Israel is escalating in Lebanon even as the broader Iran deal takes shape. Israel, which has played a key role in Trump's decision-making on Iran, is strongly opposed to a deal that includes a stop to the war in Lebanon. This creates a potential contradiction: Iran has demanded an end to Lebanon hostilities as part of any agreement, but the US appears to be supporting Israeli operations.

### 6. Oil Prices: Extreme Volatility Continues

**[VERIFIED]** Brent rose to 98.11 USD/Bbl on May 26, 2026, up 0.89% from the previous day. Over the past month, Brent's price has fallen 3.52%, but it is still 54.34% higher than a year ago.

**[VERIFIED]** As of 9:15 a.m. Eastern Time today, oil sold for $100.20 per barrel (using Brent as the benchmark). That's 67 cents higher than yesterday morning and approximately a $35.30 rise over the past year.

**[VERIFIED]** Despite Tuesday's rebound, oil prices are still down more than 10% over the past week amid growing optimism surrounding US-Iran negotiations. Brent crude futures stayed near $97 per barrel on Tuesday after plunging over 6% in the previous session.

**Assessment:** Oil's move from ~$110 (May 20) to ~$97-100 represents the sharpest weekly decline of the crisis. The overnight strikes caused a partial rebound, but markets remain broadly positioned for a deal. A collapse in talks would trigger sharp reversal toward March highs ($126).

### 7. Tanker Explosion Near Hormuz — Fresh Incident

**[REPORTED]** An explosion 60 nautical miles east of Muscat, Oman, damaged a tanker near the eastern entrance to the Strait of Hormuz on Tuesday, according to the U.K. navy's Maritime Trade Operations Center (UKMTO). "The crew and vessel are safe, although the master reports some bunker fuel has discharged into the sea," UKMTO added. No further information on the incident was immediately available. Before agreeing to a ceasefire with the U.S. on April 8 — and several times after — Iran attacked commercial vessels in and around the Strait of Hormuz almost daily.

**Assessment:** The timing and cause of the explosion are unclear. If attributed to Iranian action, this could further complicate talks. If accidental, it underscores the ongoing danger in the strait even during ceasefire.

## Emerging Analysis: The Three Fragilities Amplified

**Yesterday's analysis identified three spoiler dynamics. Today's developments have amplified all three:**

1. **US-Iran Interpretation Gap → Active Friction**
   - The US called strikes "self-defense"; Iran called them "gross violation"
   - Both narratives are technically correct: mines were being laid, ceasefire was supposedly in effect
   - This creates "incident generator" dynamic where self-defense becomes violation ad infinitum

2. **Israeli Coalition Pressure → Active Escalation**
   - Israel in recent days has intensified strikes in Nabatieh city and province, just north of the Litani River. On Tuesday it warned city residents to leave.
   - IDF pushing north of Litani while direct Washington talks scheduled for May 29
   - Iran demands Lebanon included in any deal; Israel opposes

3. **60-Day Framework Risk → Structural Uncertainty**
   - Framework was supposed to create structured de-escalation
   - But kinetic incidents before signing suggest implementation will be contested
   - Each "self-defense" action gives other side grounds to claim bad faith

**The fundamental tension:** Both sides want a deal but neither will perform first. US wants Hormuz open before sanctions relief; Iran wants assets before Hormuz. Strikes-as-leverage strategy risks deal collapse.

## Bottom Line

**The situation has shifted from "deal imminent" to "deal possible but at risk."**

President Trump is set to make a rare appearance at Camp David on Wednesday to conduct a Cabinet meeting, just hours after U.S. strikes on Iran left a peace deal hanging in the balance.

The overnight strikes were characterized as defensive, but Iran has grounds to claim ceasefire violation. The IRGC "considers its right to reciprocal response to be legitimate and certain." However, Iranian negotiators remain engaged through Qatari channels, suggesting Tehran hasn't abandoned the diplomatic track.

**Tomorrow's Camp David meeting is the key inflection point.** Trump appears to be gathering his Cabinet for either a deal announcement or a decision on next steps if talks stall. The historical parallel is ominous but not determinative.

The most likely near-term outcome has shifted from "signed interim framework with unresolved fundamentals" to **"extended stalemate with episodic friction."** The probability of formal deal signing within 7 days has decreased from 15% to 10%. The probability of sustained air/sea campaign in the 60-day horizon has increased from 48% to 55%.

**Lebanon remains the underappreciated spoiler.** Tehran wants an end to the fighting to include Lebanon, but an Israeli source told CNN that forces will "expand operations" in Lebanon, and the Trump administration has doubled down its support for the Israeli campaign. This creates a structural contradiction that could derail US-Iran talks even if bilateral issues are resolved.
