### Critical Developments (May 25-26)

#### 1. US Strikes Southern Iran Overnight

**[OFFICIAL]** U.S. forces conducted "self-defense" strikes in southern Iran early Tuesday local time, with U.S. Central Command saying the military action was to "protect our troops from threats posed by Iranian forces." CENTCOM spokesman Tim Hawkins said targets included missile launch sites and Iranian boats attempting to emplace mines.

**[REPORTED]** Iranian media reported explosions in Bandar Abbas, some 70km (42 miles) from the Strait of Hormuz, in southern Iran.

**[REPORTED]** In Iran, the news website Tabnak, believed to be close to former Revolutionary Guard chief Mohsen Rezaei, identified four dead Guard troops it said had been killed in American strikes on boats.

**Assessment:** This is not the first military action since the April 8 ceasefire—skirmishes have occurred throughout. But the timing is sensitive: strikes came hours after Iranian negotiators met with Qatari mediators in Doha, and just as both sides were describing a deal as near-complete.

---

#### 2. Iran Threatens Retaliation, Calls Ceasefire "Violated"

**[OFFICIAL]** American forces had "committed a violation of the ceasefire in the Hormuz region over the past 48 hours," Tehran's foreign ministry said Tuesday. The U.S. said the strikes on boats and missile launchers were self-defense.

**[OFFICIAL]** "The Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps warns against any violation of the ceasefire by the aggressive US military, and considers its right to reciprocal response to be legitimate and certain," a statement by the group said according to Iranian state-affiliated media.

**[OFFICIAL]** Iran's foreign ministry accused the U.S. of a "gross violation" of the fragile ceasefire between the two nations that has been in place for nearly seven weeks. Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei also said that countries in the region could no longer be a shield for U.S. bases.

**[REPORTED]** Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) claimed in a statement that its forces downed an MQ-9 Reaper drone and opened fire on an F-35 fighter jet and another drone that had entered Iranian airspace. In a statement, the IRGC did not reveal when the incidents took place.

**Assessment:** Iran has not yet retaliated militarily despite the rhetoric. "Optimism is still elevated that an agreement can be made to end the war," Deutsche Bank analysts wrote. "Last night's targeted action (by the United States) is clearly a warning shot that the ceasefire is fragile though." The key question is whether Tehran treats this as a deal-breaker or an irritant to be set aside.

#### 3. Oil Prices Volatile — Rebound After Monday Plunge

**[VERIFIED]** Brent decreased to 96.31 USD/Bbl, the lowest since April 2026. Over the past 4 weeks, Brent Crude Oil lost 5.18%.

**[VERIFIED]** Brent crude futures, the international crude benchmark, gained more than 4%, to $100.40 a barrel by 11 a.m. ET compared to its Monday close.

**[VERIFIED]** Brent crude gained 3% to $99/bbl while WTI fell 4% to $92.60 after U.S. strikes on Iranian missile sites and naval assets, a rare divergence from their usual correlation. The split reflects structural differences: Brent tracks seaborne supply risk tied to Hormuz, while WTI is insulated by domestic U.S. production.

**Assessment:** Oil's Monday plunge (Brent to ~$96) has partially reversed to ~$100 on the overnight strikes. This 10% swing over 48 hours reflects extreme uncertainty. If a deal materializes this week, expect renewed downside pressure; if talks collapse, prices could spike toward March highs.

#### 4. Trump Convenes Rare Camp David Cabinet Meeting Tomorrow

**[OFFICIAL]** President Donald Trump and his entire Cabinet will meet at Camp David on Wednesday, a White House official told CNBC on Tuesday, hours after new U.S. strikes in Iran raised concerns about an anticipated peace deal.

**[OFFICIAL]** Director of National Intelligence Tulsi Gabbard, who announced last week that she would resign in late June, will attend the meeting.

**[OFFICIAL]** President Donald Trump will convene a rare Cabinet meeting at Camp David Wednesday as negotiations with Iran enter a critical phase. The gathering comes as the administration weighs next steps in high-stakes negotiations aimed at securing a broader agreement with Tehran amid a fraying ceasefire.

**[REPORTED]** It would mark his first visit to the presidential retreat since June 2025 and only his second visit there since the start of his second term.

**[REPORTED]** On June 8, 2025, Trump hosted Cabinet members and senior officials at Camp David for discussions on Iran and Gaza. Weeks later, on June 22, 2025, Trump directed U.S. strikes on Iranian nuclear facilities.

**Assessment:** The symbolism of Camp David—site of the 1978 Camp David Accords—is notable but could signal either a breakthrough announcement or a decision point on military escalation. The historical precedent (June 2025 meeting preceding strikes) cuts both ways.

#### 5. Israel Escalates in Lebanon — Netanyahu Orders Intensified Strikes

**[VERIFIED]** As of 26 May, at least 3,185 people have been killed and 9,633 wounded by Israeli strikes in Lebanon during the war. Internal Hezbollah sources said that over 1,000 Hezbollah fighters had been killed in the war while the Israel Defense Forces (IDF) said that it had killed 1,700 fighters.

**[VERIFIED]** An Israeli airstrike on a village in eastern Lebanon killed 12 people, state media said Tuesday, as an Israeli official said the military had called up more troops to Lebanon. It came after Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said that he had authorized more intensive strikes targeting the Hezbollah militant group across Lebanon.

**[VERIFIED]** Israel's military clashed with the Iranian-backed Hezbollah militant group Tuesday along a strategic river in Lebanon as Israeli troops tried to push farther north, just three days before Lebanese and Israeli military delegations are set to meet for direct talks in Washington. A previously reached ceasefire appeared more nominal by the day.

**[REPORTED]** The US is also negotiating a lasting end to hostilities with Iran, which has reportedly demanded an end to Israeli operations in Lebanon as part of any agreement. On Monday, an Israeli source told CNN the IDF was poised to step up operations against Hezbollah's drone infrastructure, in moves coordinated with the US.

**Assessment:** Israel is escalating in Lebanon even as the broader Iran deal takes shape. This creates a potential contradiction: Iran has demanded an end to Lebanon hostilities as part of any agreement, but the US appears to be supporting Israeli operations. The May 29 Pentagon talks on Lebanon withdrawal may clarify whether these tracks can be reconciled.

#### 6. Austria Intelligence Report: Iran Nuclear Program "Well Advanced"

**[REPORTED]** Austria's domestic intelligence service believes Iran is actively pursuing a nuclear weapons program and expanding its missile capabilities. The Federal Office for the Protection of the Constitution said in a newly released report that Iran is seeking to rearm as part of a broader strategy. The report describes Iran's nuclear weapons development as "well advanced."

**[REPORTED]** Fox News, which reviewed the report, said it contradicts the current US intelligence assessment. In March, Director of National Intelligence Tulsi Gabbard told the Senate Intelligence Committee that the United States believes Iran is not building a nuclear weapon.

**[OFFICIAL]** Iran terminated all IAEA access to its nuclear facilities on February 28, 2026. As of May 25—86 days later—the IAEA "cannot provide any information on the current size, composition or whereabouts of the stockpile of enriched uranium in Iran," according to the Board of Governors report.

**Assessment:** The Austrian report complicates the diplomatic picture. If Iran's weapons program is more advanced than US intelligence assesses, the proposed 60-day negotiation period may be insufficient. However, IAEA's lack of access since February means no independent body can verify either assessment.

#### 7. Deal Framework: Wording Disputes, Not Structure

**[OFFICIAL]** Secretary of State Marco Rubio said Tuesday that a peace deal could take "a few more days" to finalize. "He's [Trump] going to make a good deal or no deal, so on that everyone should be assured," Rubio told reporters at Jaipur International Airport in India. "But that may take a little while, I mean, a few more days."

**[OFFICIAL]** The strikes came hours after Iranian negotiators met with Qatari mediators in Doha for talks in coordination with the US. The US and Iran are working toward a memorandum of understanding, but disputes over language concerning Iran's nuclear program and sanctions have held up a deal.

**[REPORTED]** After weeks of saying Iran's enriched uranium must be removed from the country, Trump did not rule out another option: destroying it inside Iran under the supervision of the International Atomic Energy Agency, the UN nuclear watchdog.

**[REPORTED]** Iranian media reported possible progress on one of the main sticking points. Iran's Fars news agency said the "last serious dispute" between the sides—access to frozen funds—was "on the way to being resolved" through Qatari mediation.

**Assessment:** The framework appears intact: 60-day ceasefire extension, Hormuz reopening, gradual sanctions relief, deferred nuclear negotiations. The sticking points are language (nuclear commitments, frozen funds, Hormuz management), not structure. This suggests a deal remains achievable if both sides want it.
