### 1) U.S. “self-defense” strikes in southern Iran inject fresh volatility into the near-deal window
- The U.S. military said it conducted **“self-defense” strikes in southern Iran** against **missile launch sites** and **boats it said were placing mines** (near the Strait/Hormuz area), even as talks continue. **[VERIFIED]** ([apnews.com](https://apnews.com/article/01a13e9a63ece786a0a7fa4933dbf09b?utm_source=openai))  
- **Iran publicly condemned** the strikes as **“bad faith and unreliability,”** called them a ceasefire violation, and warned the U.S. would bear responsibility for consequences (without detailing an immediate response). **[VERIFIED]** ([apnews.com](https://apnews.com/article/47980a4d87c63c0adb873d306f9b932c?utm_source=openai))  

**What it means:** This is a tangible **deal-fragility signal**: even if negotiators are close on paper, operational incidents around **mining / air-defense / force protection** can quickly become political blockers. It also increases the risk of **retaliatory tit-for-tat** that doesn’t fully “break the talks,” but makes a clean stand-down less likely.

**What it means:** This strengthens the “**two-track**” problem from yesterday’s report: even if a U.S.–Iran arrangement advances, the Lebanon front is becoming a **direct spoiler channel**—especially if Iran frames Lebanon as part of the package and Israel insists on continued freedom of action.
