### 2) Iran–Oman “Hormuz committee” becomes the visible mechanism; Iran’s messaging trends toward “coastal-state control”  
- Iran and Oman held their **first meeting of a joint Hormuz committee** created under the interim framework. **[REPORTED]** ([washingtonpost.com](https://www.washingtonpost.com/world/2026/06/29/trump-says-us-iran-will-meet-negotiate-after-days-strikes/?utm_source=openai))  
- Separate reporting highlights Iranian officials **talking up oversight/administration with Oman**—a signal Tehran is pushing an interpretation of the arrangement that implies **greater Iranian (and Omani) say over passage management**, not just “reopening.” **[REPORTED]** ([japantimes.co.jp](https://www.japantimes.co.jp/news/2026/06/30/world/iran-control-hormuz-new-talks/?utm_source=openai))  

**What changed / what it means:**  
This is a *structure* development: a bilateral Iran–Oman channel is now being operationalized alongside Qatar mediation. That can help reduce miscalculation—but it also **institutionalizes the dispute** over who sets transit conditions. If Iran insists on a “coastal-state control” frame, the U.S./partners may respond with **persistent presence/coordination** to keep passage “effectively open,” which is consistent with a longer maritime-security posture.
