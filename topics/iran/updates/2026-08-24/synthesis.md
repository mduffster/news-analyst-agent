## Critical Developments (August 24)

### 1. "ECONOMIC D-DAY" — TODAY'S MAIN EVENT [OFFICIAL]

"At dawn begins an economic D-Day — the single greatest financial offensive ever marshalled against an adversary," Bessent said in an opinion piece he authored in The Financial Times on Sunday.

U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent will hold a press conference at 1 p.m. EDT (1700 GMT) on Monday amid promises to reveal even more severe measures on a country that has endured near-continuous economic sanctions since the Islamic Revolution of 1979.

The U.S. will use its "full might" against anyone who does not comply. "We are going to them and saying you are either with us or against us," Bessent told CNBC. "It is time for our allies and the rest of the world to make a decision, and we are going to squash the economy of this murderous regime."

Bessent will unveil details of a plan to more effectively isolate the Islamic Republic later Monday, building on already extensive sanctions that have been in place for decades. It's unclear what will be announced, but the move may impact some of Iran's largest trading partners, including China, Turkey and India.

Bessent said the administration intends to target the remaining commercial and financial links sustaining Iran, including countries and entities involved in purchasing and transporting Iranian petroleum, moving money through exchange houses and free-trade zones, maintaining ship registries and facilitating other financial activity.

*Assessment: [OFFICIAL] Today's 1 PM EDT announcement will determine whether this represents incremental pressure or a genuine escalation of economic warfare. The key question remains whether major Chinese banks (particularly PBoC) will be targeted — which would significantly complicate the September 24 Xi visit.*

### 2. CRITICAL: Iran Threatens Complete Gulf Oil Shutdown [OFFICIAL]

Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council, on Sunday suggested economic retaliation. "If the economic war continues, not a single drop of oil will be exported, neither through the Strait of Hormuz nor from anywhere in the Persian Gulf," Rezaei wrote in a social media post.

"Iran will regard any country's participation in or support for America's economic war against the Iranian people as an act of war."

Iran's head of national security says Tehran will "retaliate in a seismic manner" to any sanctions and will consider any country supporting sanctions to be committing an act of war.

*Assessment: [OFFICIAL] Rezaei's threat represents a potential escalation trigger beyond current operations. If Gulf states (UAE, Saudi Arabia) formally join the sanctions regime, Iran may attempt to halt ALL oil exports from the region — not just Iranian exports. This maximalist threat is designed to deter regional compliance with US demands.*

### 3. CRITICAL: Iranian Leadership Publicly Fractures [VERIFIED]

Pezeshkian, along with his Parliamentary speaker and chief negotiator Mohammed Bagher Ghalibaf, could be growing weary of a prolonged scenario of no war and no peace, where the Islamic Republic remains under strenuous economic pressure without a proper resolution to the conflict.

"No matter how much military power we have, if the people are hungry and there is no financial circulation, economic growth, or national production, we will not last," Ghalibaf said.

On Friday, President Masoud Pezeshkian openly called for an end to the war, stating "It is better that we bring the war to an end now as we are in a position of power and dignity."

Ghalibaf, who has close ties to hardline IRGC senior leaders and is far more influential than Pezeshkian, seconded the call for compromise, stating: "No matter how much military power we have, if our people are struggling and the country lacks financial circulation and economic growth, we will not achieve progress…As someone who has experienced war, we understand the true value of peace."

Pezeshkian and economically focused officials appear more willing to accept negotiations and limited compromise if that can end the war and reduce economic pressure. Hardline security figures appear more willing to tolerate economic pain in pursuit of broader strategic concessions from Washington.

According to the Iranian government agency, the Statistical Centre of Iran, inflation is now at almost 90 percent.

*Assessment: [VERIFIED] The Pezeshkian-Ghalibaf joint messaging is significant — Ghalibaf has ties to the IRGC and is considered more influential than Pezeshkian. Their public break with the hardliner position suggests economic pressure is creating real policy debates. However, the IRGC/security establishment appears to still dominate decision-making, limiting the diplomatic opening.*

### 4. SIGNIFICANT: China Rejects Sanctions Demands; Xi Visit Looms [OFFICIAL]

At a Foreign Ministry press briefing on August 21, Lin Jian said China opposes "illegal unilateral sanctions" that lack a basis in international law or authorization from the UN Security Council. He urged all parties to act responsibly and remain committed to political and diplomatic efforts.

While the US Treasury Department sanctioned independent Chinese refineries that have been importing Iranian crude, Beijing ordered its companies to ignore those restrictions.

Beijing reportedly invoked a 2021 blocking statute that reportedly prohibits Chinese firms from complying with foreign sanctions deemed illegitimate. The diplomatic standoff occurs at a delicate time, given US officials are trying to finalize an agenda for Chinese President Xi Jinping, who is expected to visit Washington in the last week of September.

Any major US moves against China could also risk provoking countermeasures from Beijing. China holds a trump card in rare earths — critical minerals necessary for making many items, from smartphones to electric vehicles and fighter jets.

Analysts agree the Trump team is likely to calibrate its use of the new measures against China, particularly in advance of the visit of China's paramount leader, Xi Jinping, to Washington on September 24. To date, the U.S. Treasury Department has sanctioned independent Chinese refiners but has avoided the steps that would most antagonize Beijing, particularly penalizing major Chinese banks that finance that trade, including the People's Bank of China (PBoC).

*Assessment: [OFFICIAL] China's invocation of a blocking statute creates a structural ceiling on the economic campaign. The September 24 Xi visit constrains aggressive secondary sanctions on major Chinese entities. Today's announcement will reveal how the administration is threading this needle — aggressive rhetoric may exceed actual enforcement against Chinese banks.*

### 5. SIGNIFICANT: Diplomatic Activity Intensifies — Oman, Pakistan, Syria-Israel [REPORTED]

**Oman:**
A top Omani official is set to head to Tehran on Tuesday to hold discussions over the future of the Strait of Hormuz, which has been closed since the start of the regional war in February. Iran's foreign ministry said Omani FM Badr al-Busaidi will visit Iran tomorrow to discuss a deal on a new way to allow ships to pass through the Strait of Hormuz.

**Pakistan:**
Pakistan's army chief, Field Marshal Asim Munir, will visit Tehran on Monday to hold talks with senior Iranian officials, Iranian state-run media reported.

"Qatar is leading the political mediation and remains the essential communication bridge between Washington and Tehran," analyst Andreas Krieg told Al Jazeera. Munir's contribution was narrower, a "military-to-military channel into an Iranian system in which the IRGC and wider security establishment have become increasingly important to decisions about war and peace".

**Syria-Israel:**
Syria and Israel held high-level, U.S.-mediated talks in Jordan on Sunday aimed at easing tensions following recent Israeli strikes on Syrian territory, Syria's state news agency SANA reported. The talks focused on practical mechanisms to halt Israeli strikes, arrests and targeted operations, and on reviving negotiations towards a security agreement.

Syria had cut off contact entirely after the Israeli attack on Syria's Abu al-Duhur airbase last week. Al-Shaibani said the two sides had agreed on paper to "almost everything" around the beginning of the year, including arrangements for security zones in southern Syria and a buffer zone where only UN forces would be present.

*Assessment: [REPORTED] The diplomatic flurry — Oman visiting Tehran Tuesday, Pakistan's army chief today, Syria-Israel talks Sunday — suggests multiple offramp negotiations are being tested simultaneously. The Oman-Iran Hormuz talks are particularly significant as a potential face-saving arrangement for partial reopening.*

### 6. SIGNIFICANT: Hormuz Traffic Remains at ~20% of Pre-War Levels [VERIFIED]

Traffic through the Strait of Hormuz has increased fourfold over the last fortnight. According to the UK Maritime Trade Operations Centre, nearly 200 ships crossed the Strait, up from 150 the week before, and 40 weeks ago. That's still only a fraction—20%—of the pre-war traffic.

Iran is allowing some Iraqi oil tankers to pass through the strait, Reuters reported Saturday, citing the Iranian state-run Islamic Republic News Agency, but shipping traffic through the strait remains low. At least six vessels transited the waterway over the past 24 hours, including three tankers and two cargo ships entering the Oman Gulf, according to MarineTraffic data.

The Strait of Hormuz is a critical waterway through which one-fifth of the world's oil and LNG supplies were shipped before the US-Israel war on Iran began on February 28. Roughly 130 ships sailed through the strait each day, compared with the 236 in all that passed over 19 days in August 2026.

Tehran's targeting of Hormuz users and Washington's blockade of Iranian ports continue to suppress traffic volumes, although a small core group of operators remains active. Preliminary data show 73 transits between 10 and 16 August, down from 91 the previous week.

*Assessment: [VERIFIED] Data shows improvement from crisis lows — fourfold increase over two weeks — but traffic remains at only 20% of pre-war levels (~200 ships/week vs ~910 pre-crisis). Iran's selective enforcement (allowing Iraqi tankers) suggests managed rather than absolute closure. Rezaei's threat to halt ALL Gulf oil represents potential escalation beyond current state.*

### 8. MODERATE: Iraq Militia Deadline Shows Signs of Softening [REPORTED]

Now Iraq appears to be backtracking on the deadline for placing militia weapons under the control of the state. Iraq had initially indicated it would rein in the militias by September 30, when US troops who went to Iraq to back Baghdad against ISIS, are supposed to leave.

Zaidi set a September 30 deadline for independent armed groups to hand over their weapons to the state, but Tehran and its partners in Iraq are seeking to control any disarmament process. Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC)-Quds Force commander Esmail Qaani traveled to Baghdad to discuss disarmament with militia leaders and Shiite politicians on August 10. Reportedly, Qaani told militia leaders to hold onto their weapons as leverage with Washington while avoiding direct confrontation with Iraqi security forces.

A powerful Iranian-backed militia on Friday warned Iraq's government that using force to disarm armed groups before a September 30 deadline could result in internal conflict and set three conditions, including the withdrawal of US forces.

*Assessment: [REPORTED] The militia disarmament deadline is showing signs of softening from "complete by September 30" to "begin after September 30." This represents a partial Iranian victory following Qaani's visit. Violent confrontation risk has decreased slightly, but the underlying tension remains unresolved.*
