# US-Israel-Iran Conflict: Trump Rules Out Pre-Election Strikes as Yemen Front Intensifies

## October 8, 2026 — Day 223

**Assessment:** A dramatic policy shift dominated the day: President Trump announced Thursday that he has no plans to strike Iran before the midterm election, posting on Truth Social: "We will not be attacking Iran at any time prior to the Midterm Elections to be held in the United States on November 3rd," adding that the administration is "having productive discussions" with Tehran. This came hours after reports that the White House had requested pre-midterm strike options—a reversal that likely reduces near-term escalation risk but concentrates military decision-making into the post-November 3 period. Meanwhile, the Yemen theater continued its deadly expansion: Houthis struck Riyadh's airport for a second consecutive day while issuing an unprecedented warning for workers to evacuate Saudi oil facilities, and international airlines suspended hundreds of flights. Oil surged above $105 on these developments, erasing Secretary Rubio's "Hormuz is open" narrative as strait transits hit a two-month low.

## Trump's Pre-Election Strike Restraint: Context and Constraints

The president's pledge not to attack before November 3 represents a significant near-term de-escalation signal—but one bounded by political and military realities.

The Pentagon has ordered U.S. Central Command to prepare for further military strikes against Iran that could be launched before the midterm elections, according to The Atlantic. The outlet reported, citing unnamed administration officials, that the White House has asked for strike options that could be exercised before the elections to put more pressure on Iran and provide momentum for Republicans.

Administration officials linked the planning to Trump's desire to show progress in the conflict and address high gasoline prices ahead of the vote. Supporters of limited strikes believe an operation could help the president present a military success before the elections. However, even advocates acknowledged that such attacks alone would not secure an agreement with Tehran, restore safe passage through the Strait of Hormuz, or reduce gasoline prices before election day.

The reversal hours later suggests internal debate resolved—for now—in favor of restraint. The president's comments come after multiple media reports detailed the administration planning for potential new strikes before the elections—just 26 days away. Trump emphasized that he plans to keep up economic pressure on Iran, saying the blockade of Iranian ports "will remain in full force and effect."

**The political calculus is straightforward:** Officials know that flareups between the US and Iran have caused spikes in oil prices, aggravating cost-of-living concerns that have dragged down Trump's Republican allies. However, Trump said Wednesday at a campaign rally in San Antonio that he no longer wants a deal with Iran: "I think the deal isn't really something that I want to do, but they're willing to offer us anything to stop."

**What this means for the forecast:** The 26 days until November 3 now represent a constrained window with reduced probability of US-initiated escalation, though Iranian actions could still trigger a response. The post-election period, by contrast, becomes a concentrated decision point—especially given ongoing carrier deployments and strike planning.

## Riyadh Airport Under Siege: Airlines Suspend Flights

The Houthi campaign against Saudi civil aviation escalated dramatically, moving from casualties to sustained operational disruption.

Yemen's Houthis said on Thursday they targeted King Khalid International Airport in the Saudi capital Riyadh with a missile for a second time, as the group escalated its strikes on airports across the neighbouring kingdom. "Our armed forces targeted King Khalid Airport in Riyadh at midday with a ballistic missile that hit its target," said the Houthis' military spokesman Yahya Saree. [REPORTED - Houthi claim]

A drone attack on the same airport earlier this week killed one person, with another two killed in a separate strike on the airport in the southwestern city of Abha, Riyadh said. The attack on Abha airport killed Moroccan and Algerian residents and wounded 28 other people, while the attack on King Khalid airport killed a Sudanese national and wounded eight other people. [OFFICIAL]

The commercial impact proved substantial. Airlines are canceling hundreds of flights to Riyadh after Houthi militants struck two Saudi Arabia airports in recent days, killing three people and injuring dozens. Singapore Air postponed the launch of its services to the Saudi capital until March 28, citing the conflict in the Middle East. Indigo, Air India Ltd. and Philippine Airlines all halted flights to the city, the latter until at least Oct. 11. [VERIFIED]

44.7 percent of all scheduled departures from Riyadh were cancelled. Arrivals are also affected, with 124 of 369 scheduled inbound flights cancelled or not operating, equivalent to 33.6 percent. Saudia is experiencing the largest disruption by volume, with 97 of 140 scheduled departures cancelled—about 69.3 percent of its Riyadh departures. [VERIFIED]

Operations at King Khalid International Airport have shut down for several hours and restarted multiple times during the past few days as incoming attacks from Yemen make it unsafe for planes to land or take off.

**The Houthi warning expanded to include energy infrastructure:** Yemen's Houthis on Thursday called on staff at oil facilities in Saudi Arabia to leave to avoid being targeted as hostilities escalate. "Employees at all oil facilities must leave areas that are targets for our forces, so as not to endanger their lives," Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree wrote on X, while also issuing a warning "to airlines, airports, travelers and their staff". [OFFICIAL - Houthi statement]

**Significance:** The combination of civilian casualties, mass flight cancellations, and explicit warnings to evacuate oil facilities represents a strategic shift in Houthi targeting—from military objectives to sustained pressure on Saudi economic and civilian infrastructure. This increases the probability of expanded Saudi retaliation and creates pressure for Mecca Pact deployments to accelerate.

## Mecca Pact: Alliance Moves Toward "Rapid Deployment"

The October 5 activation of collective defense measures gained additional context as analysts assessed the alliance's scope.

Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan activated collective deterrence measures Monday and agreed to rapidly deploy forces and military capabilities to the kingdom, as Saudi-backed Yemeni troops advanced against the Houthis. The decision followed an emergency meeting of the Mecca defense pact's committee, which reaffirmed the three countries' solidarity in confronting attacks against Saudi Arabia and Islamic holy sites. [OFFICIAL]

The three signatories of the August Mecca Joint Defence Agreement met in emergency session in Riyadh and agreed to move immediately to "rapid deployment" of military forces, uniting NATO's second-largest army, the Islamic world's only nuclear state and the largest Gulf power.

Under the trilateral, NATO-style agreement, "any armed attack against any one of the three states shall be regarded as an attack against them all." Although military cooperation among the three Sunni Muslim-majority nations is nothing new, the timing of the pact's creation was particularly notable, as it came less than one month after Yemen's Houthi rebels began launching attacks on Saudi Arabia.

There were earlier news reports that in April, Pakistan had deployed about 10,000 desert warfare troops from the 25th Mechanised Division along the Saudi Arabia-Yemen border. This was in addition to the deployment of an 18-aircraft strong fleet of Pakistani fighters at Saudi Arabia's King Abdulaziz Air Base in March. [REPORTED - unverified force numbers]

After the airport attacks, the Saudi-led coalition said that it destroyed 82 Houthi targets across Yemen. [OFFICIAL]

## Hormuz: Transit Data Contradicts Recovery Narrative

Shipping data released Thursday directly challenged Secretary Rubio's Wednesday claim that Iran had "lost complete control" of the strait.

Crude flows through Hormuz fell 27% in a week as Iranian attacks on tankers hit a wartime high. The number of tankers traversing the Strait of Hormuz has dropped to the lowest in two months amid renewed attacks on vessels—right after tanker-tracking outlets said oil flows were back to pre-war levels. "Crude crossing the strait fell 27% from a wartime high the week before, to at least 10.1 million barrels a day," two Kpler analysts reported, as the firm counted only seven commodity carriers traversing the strait on Tuesday. [VERIFIED]

Shipping in the Strait of Hormuz is under fresh attack, disrupting transit, and the latest official PortWatch count is 4 vessels on October 4 at 5% of typical as the Petroline (East-West Pipeline) bypass runs at reduced capacity. On October 4, IMF PortWatch's most recent published day, commercial transit reached 5% of the pre-crisis vessel count: 4 vessels against a typical 85 per day. Iran says the Strait remains closed; reports say ships are still transiting. [VERIFIED - contested interpretation]

According to shipping data from Kpler, at least 16.5 million barrels left the region last month, matching the pre-war average, excluding Iran. However, about 40 percent of this is being exported via Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline or via ship-to-ship transfers by smaller boats travelling undetected through the strait.

**The reality:** Oil is moving, but through alternative channels—pipeline exports, ship-to-ship transfers in Omani waters, and dark transits with AIS disabled. The IRGC's threat to close these "illegal routes" remains a live escalation option.

## Putin-Pezeshkian: Russia Signals Continued Backing

The two leaders met in Turkmenistan on the sidelines of CIS and Caspian environmental summits.

Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian and Russian President Vladimir Putin met in Turkmenistan on Thursday and discussed expanding economic and strategic cooperation. The talks, held on the sidelines of a Commonwealth of Independent States summit. [OFFICIAL]

"Naturally, the topics to be discussed will include issues related to the development of bilateral cooperation and, of course, the tense situation in the Middle East," Putin aide Ushakov said. The meeting will be the fifth between Putin and Pezeshkian since the Iranian president took office and the second since the war between Iran, the United States and Israel began in February.

For the first time in the history of the CIS, Iranian President Pezeshkian was invited to the meeting in an expanded format.

**Significance:** Russia continues to provide diplomatic legitimacy to Tehran during the conflict. The unprecedented CIS invitation signals Moscow's commitment to supporting Iran's international standing, even as Iranian conventional military capability has been degraded.

## Diplomatic Track: Gap Remains Wide

Iran's formal response to Vice President Vance's "meaningful reduction" framework reiterated fundamental disagreements on sequencing.

Vice President Vance told Reuters the United States would accept a "meaningful" reduction of enrichment capacity. An Iranian official said American nuclear demands are "at odds with Iran's demands" and that the blockade of Iranian ports must end first. [OFFICIAL]

Complicating matters, Vance said Washington is uncertain who makes decisions in Tehran. He said the US was in talks with Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian and Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi. But it was unclear how much authority they wield within Iran's power structure after joint US-Israeli strikes at the start of the war killed Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei. "One of the things we picked up on is it's not totally clear how their country makes decisions," Vance said. [OFFICIAL]
