# US-Israel-Iran Conflict: Iran Expands Gulf Attacks to Kuwait; Israel Presses US for Renewed Strikes as Two Seafarers Now Missing

## November 18, 2026 — Day 264

**Assessment:** The conflict widened geographically today as Iran attacked a US air base in Kuwait for the first time since September — the fourth Gulf state targeted this week — while Israel's intelligence leadership lobbied Washington for resumed major strikes. The action-reaction cycle accelerated further: within 24 hours of the US striking Iran's Qeshm Island, Iran launched drones at Ali Al Salem Air Base in Kuwait and resumed attacks on Bahrain. Two seafarers are now missing from separate tanker attacks in Hormuz, and the US tightened its blockade with new restrictions on ship-to-ship cargo transfers. Despite this multi-front escalation, oil markets continued falling (Brent ~$92), and Secretary of State Rubio downplayed "dirty bomb" intelligence as not imminent. The simultaneous escalation on multiple fronts increases miscalculation risk, but both sides maintain calibrated limits.

## 1. Iran Expands Attacks to Kuwait — First Strike Since September

Iran's regular army claimed early Wednesday, November 18, 2026, that it targeted Kuwait's Ali Al Salem Air Base with drones. In a statement, Iran's army claimed its drones targeted U.S. forces' Patriot air defense systems as well as drone and missile launch pads at Ali Al Salem Air Base. [VERIFIED — attack confirmed; damage claims unverified]

The General Staff of the Kuwaiti Army announced that the sounds of explosions heard were caused by the interception of the attacks by the country's air defense systems. The country's Foreign Ministry condemned the repeated Iranian "criminal" attacks, saying they threatened Kuwait's security and stability.

Iran has struck Ali Al Salem air base at least 30 times during the war, damaging or destroying at least 15 aircraft hangars, numerous warehouses and 10 buildings near a residential area, according to an Associated Press analysis.

**Significance:** The resumption of attacks on Kuwait after a multi-week lull marks a geographic widening of Iran's campaign. Iran has now attacked all major Gulf states hosting US forces (Bahrain, Kuwait, UAE, Qatar) within the past week. The coordinated multi-country response pattern — striking Kuwait and Bahrain simultaneously following the US Qeshm Island operation — suggests Tehran is deliberately demonstrating its ability to threaten the full network of US regional basing.

## 2. Mossad Chief Lobbied Washington for Renewed US-Israeli Strikes

Roman Gofman, director of Israel's Mossad intelligence agency, advocated resuming joint US-Israeli strikes on Iran during meetings with US officials last week, according to two people familiar with the visit. The visit followed Donald Trump's November 9 pledge to refrain from new strikes after meeting Qatar's prime minister. [REPORTED — multiple sources citing unnamed officials]

Gofman met with Trump administration officials, including CIA Director John Ratcliffe, in Washington, the people said, adding that the Mossad chief briefed the officials on Israeli intelligence on Iran. One said the intelligence related to Iran's nuclear programme, a central focus of ongoing US-Iran diplomacy.

Around the same time Gofman was in the US, former Israeli minister Ron Dermer, a close Netanyahu confidant, was also in the US advocating for a joint attack in meetings with Trump administration officials. The two people said Gofman and Dermer's outreach to US officials was part of efforts by Netanyahu to convince Trump to launch a new attack on Iran.

**White House Response:** A White House official said Trump believed Washington's economic pressure campaign was so "devastating" to Iran that there was no need for "major combat operations."

**Significance:** This reveals a critical US-Israel split: Netanyahu's camp is actively pushing for major resumed strikes while the Trump administration prefers continued economic pressure. The timing — immediately after Trump's November 9 commitment to hold off — suggests Israel is trying to shift US policy before Democrats take control of Congress in January. The parallel Gofman and Dermer missions indicate a coordinated lobbying effort.

## 3. CENTCOM Chief Holds Trilateral Meeting with IDF, Mossad

U.S. Central Command chief Adm. Brad Cooper met Wednesday with Israel Defense Forces Chief of Staff Lt. Gen. Eyal Zamir and Mossad Director Roman Gofman in a trilateral session at IDF headquarters, the military said. [VERIFIED — IDF statement]

According to the IDF, the participants discussed regional developments and shared security challenges. They also discussed deepening "strategic and operational cooperation" and improving joint readiness to address "a range of scenarios."

The Yedioth Ahronoth reported Wednesday that Cooper and Zamir discussed recent developments and how prepared their militaries were to resume large-scale operations against Iran if necessary.

According to a CENTCOM assessment published by *Walla*, the blockade has prevented Iran from exporting more than $25 billion worth of crude oil.

**Significance:** The trilateral format — bringing together the US military commander, IDF chief, and Mossad director — signals high-level coordination amid the week's intensified exchanges. Media reports of discussions about readiness for "large-scale operations" suggest contingency planning is active, though a defense source noted such visits occur approximately monthly.

## 4. Second Tanker Attack Leaves Two Seafarers Missing Across Hormuz

The UAE's ADNOC L&S said on Wednesday that one crew member was missing and 11 injured after one of its vessels was attacked in the Strait of Hormuz on Tuesday. The company said the rest of the crew had been evacuated. "One crew member remains unaccounted for, with search and rescue operations ongoing in coordination with the relevant authorities." [OFFICIAL — corporate statement]

A NAVAREA IX navigational warning issued at 1500 UTC Wednesday identified *TARA GAS* as still burning and drifting southeast, approximately 5.7 nautical miles off Khawr Ghub Ali, Oman. India's foreign ministry confirmed that 24 of the vessel's 25 crewmembers had been rescued, including 21 Indian nationals brought safely ashore at Khasab. Two injured Indian sailors are being treated in hospital, while a search continues for the missing seafarer.

**Human Cost Rising:** Two seafarers are now missing from attacks this week — one from the Tara Gas (Indian national) and one from the ADNOC vessel. In July, two crude oil tankers operated by ADNOC L&S were struck by projectiles in the strait, killing one crew member and injuring others. At least 22 Indian nationals have been killed in Hormuz attacks since the war began.

**Significance:** The rising human toll — with casualties and missing crew becoming regular features of Hormuz shipping — increases pressure on affected countries (India, UAE) to respond diplomatically. India's Ministry of External Affairs condemned the attacks as "deeply worrisome," signaling potential diplomatic escalation.

## 5. US Tightens Blockade — Targets Ship-to-Ship Transfers

The US military will treat ships that receive cargo at sea from vessels loaded in Iran as attempting to evade its blockade of Iranian ports and could take action against them, according to an advisory issued by US Central Command (CENTCOM) on Wednesday. [OFFICIAL]

"Any ship that receives cargo via a ship-to-ship transfer with a vessel subject to the blockade and carrying cargo loaded at an Iranian port or coastline may be considered to be evading the blockade, even if it has not entered Iranian ports or the Iranian coastline," CENTCOM said. "Such ships may be stopped, boarded, diverted, captured, or directed to proceed to the Iranian port where the cargo originated."

The US naval blockade on Iran-related oil exports was first imposed on April 13 and lifted in June after Washington reached a draft understanding with Tehran. It was reimposed on July 14 after the agreement collapsed.

Iran has managed to export some liquefied petroleum gas and jet fuel since July, despite the US naval blockade, according to shipping intelligence and security sources. Iran's crude exports, however, have remained near a standstill for months, shipping sources say.

**Significance:** This closes a loophole Iran had exploited to export some energy products. The advisory explicitly authorizes force against non-compliant vessels, escalating the legal and operational scope of the blockade.

## 6. Knesset Elects Eisenkot Ally as Speaker — 66-52 Vote

The Knesset elected Matan Kahana of Gadi Eisenkot's Yashar party as its 26th speaker, replacing Amir Ohana, from Netanyahu's Likud party. Kahana received the support of 66 lawmakers. [VERIFIED]

Kahana secured the speaker role on Tuesday with the support of the 48 Knesset members of Eisenkot's bloc, which includes the Yashar, B'Yachad, Yisrael Beyteinu and Democrats parties. Although the Arab-majority parties supported Kahana's appointment as Knesset speaker on Tuesday, Eisenkot's path toward forming a government with their involvement would be a formidable task.

Kahana's election gives Eisenkot's bloc greater influence over the Knesset's legislative agenda as Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu remains in office and coalition talks continue.

**Significance:** This is a concrete demonstration that Eisenkot's bloc plus Arab party support = reliable majority. Control of the speakership gives Eisenkot significant leverage over the legislative process and fast-track capabilities if he forms government. Deadline for initial mandate: December 6 (18 days remaining).

## 7. "Dirty Bomb" Intelligence — Administration Downplays

US Secretary of State Marco Rubio said Tuesday he did not believe there was an imminent threat to the United States, responding to questions about a CNN report concerning a possible attack involving Iran and a dirty bomb or chemical weapons in Washington. "I don't think there's any imminent threat," Rubio told reporters during President Donald Trump's trip to Asia. [OFFICIAL]

New US intelligence indicates that Iran is developing plans to target senior Trump administration officials by detonating a so-called dirty bomb or a chemical weapon in Washington, DC, even as officials continue to debate whether Iran can or will attempt to execute such a plot. But American officials have viewed the intelligence as a signal of an alarming willingness to escalate by Iran as it faces intense pressure from ongoing US military strikes and a punishing economic blockade. [REPORTED]

An analyst added that a dirty bomb attack would "hand-deliver the political rationale" for a major US war on Iran, including a ground invasion, with bipartisan support even after Democrats take control of Congress in January.

**Significance:** The administration is walking a careful line — acknowledging threats without creating panic or justifying further escalation. Rubio's downplaying suggests the intelligence is being treated as contingency planning rather than imminent threat, though the report signals Iranian desperation under blockade pressure.

## 8. Oil Markets Continue Decline Despite Escalation

Brent fell to 91.68 USD/Bbl on November 18, 2026, down 1.57% from the previous day. Over the past month, Brent's price has fallen 16.31%, but it is still 44.35% higher than a year ago. [VERIFIED]

Oil prices declined on Wednesday as a substantial increase in US crude and fuel inventories reinforced concerns over supply, although escalating military tensions between the United States and Iran and disruptions to shipping through the Strait of Hormuz limited the losses. US crude stockpiles increased by 5.28 million barrels in the week ended November 13, according to figures from the American Petroleum Institute (API).

**Significance:** Brent has fallen from ~$97 on November 15 to ~$92 on November 18 — a ~$5 decline coinciding with the week's most concentrated escalation. Markets have concluded that the conflict is economically sustainable at current intensity, reducing pressure on both sides to seek resolution. This paradoxically enables continued operations.
