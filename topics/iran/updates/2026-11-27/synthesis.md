# US-Israel-Iran Conflict: US Disables Sixth Blockade Runner; Eisenkot Signs Two Coalition Deals as Abbas Set to Declare Hamas Terror Group

## November 27, 2026 — Day 273

**Assessment:** The conflict reached two distinct inflection points on Day 273. The U.S. moved from blockade deterrence to kinetic enforcement, disabling the Palau-flagged M/V Pacific Archer with precision munitions after it attempted to run the naval blockade — the sixth vessel disabled since the blockade resumed in July. Meanwhile, Israeli domestic politics accelerated dramatically: Prime Minister-designate Gadi Eisenkot signed coalition agreements with both Yisrael Beiteinu and Democrats, while Ra'am chairman Mansour Abbas is set to publicly declare Hamas a terror group Saturday evening — a historic shift that could unlock an Arab party's path into an Eisenkot coalition. Trump's Thanksgiving messaging emphasized "victory at hand" while naming Pickaxe Mountain as the next strike target if operations resume, maintaining dual-track pressure. The divergence between operational intensity at sea and emerging political transitions on both the Israeli and American sides defines the current phase.

## 1. US Disables M/V Pacific Archer — Kinetic Blockade Enforcement Intensifies

The military disabled the Palau-flagged M/V Pacific Archer on Thursday by firing "precision munitions" at the ship's stern from a military aircraft. "The ship's propulsion was ultimately disabled and the civilian crew was unharmed," U.S. Central Command said in a statement. [OFFICIAL — CENTCOM]

The Pacific Archer is at least the 15th disclosed vessel to be disabled by the U.S. military since the blockade began in the spring and the sixth since it resumed this summer.

The Pacific Archer was the first commercial vessel CENTCOM reported disabling since Iranian officials publicly backed breaking the blockade last week. On November 24, Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council, said the armed forces and state institutions were determined to break the blockade. The Pacific Archer left Bandar Abbas two days later.

CENTCOM said Friday that 158 compliant commercial vessels have been "redirected," while six others that did not comply with the blockade were "disabled." "All mariners operating in the Gulf of Oman and approaches to the Strait of Hormuz are urged to heed blockade warnings issued by CENTCOM forces," it said.

Iran's Revolutionary Guard said on Friday that two tankers attempted to transit the Strait of Hormuz through an "unauthorized" route, claiming that one of them struck a naval mine while the other was forced to turn back.

**Significance:** This represents a meaningful escalation in enforcement intensity. The U.S. has moved from deterrence and escorts to kinetic interdiction against commercial shipping attempting to breach the blockade. Iran's vow to break the blockade — followed two days later by the Pacific Archer's departure — suggests an emerging cycle of test-and-enforcement that increases miscalculation risk around Hormuz.

## 2. Trump: "Victory at Hand" — Threatens Pickaxe Mountain Strike

President Trump said Thursday that the U.S. has a "victory at hand" in the war against Iran. Trump said Thursday the U.S. would strike Iran's Pickaxe Mountain if it renewed attacks, while telling U.S. troops on Thanksgiving that Tehran still has a chance to reach a deal. [OFFICIAL — Trump statement]

"We would absolutely hit Pickaxe Mountain, but we haven't seen any real activity there," Trump told reporters at Mar-a-Lago in Florida after his holiday calls.

Trump said he would give Iran "every chance to make a deal and save a lot of lives," though he questioned whether Tehran would take the offer. He told reporters that earlier strikes had wiped out Iran's air force, navy, and air defenses and that Tehran had been unable to resume work at nuclear sites the U.S. had struck.

President Trump told the troops, "You will continue your deployment," emphasizing, "We are on the verge of victory." He added, "We are giving them every opportunity to reach a deal and save many lives," while noting, "I am not sure if they will take advantage of that opportunity."

President Donald Trump threatened to strike Pickaxe Mountain, an underground nuclear facility located 1.5 kilometres south of the Natanz complex in Isfahan province, Iran. The mountain's hard granite provides natural protection for the site, which Iran claims is for centrifuge production but inspectors have not fully verified.

**Analysis:** Trump's dual messaging — claiming near-victory while threatening Iran's most hardened nuclear site — maintains leverage for either outcome. The conditional framing ("if we did anything") combined with "we haven't seen any real activity there" suggests the administration is positioning for either negotiations or future escalation while keeping options open through January.

## 3. Eisenkot Signs Two Coalition Deals — Vows Government Within 8 Days

Israel's prime minister-designate Gadi Eisenkot vowed Friday to form a new government within eight days, announcing the signing of two coalition agreements despite a lack of progress in negotiations with Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's Likud party. [VERIFIED — multiple sources]

Gadi Eisenkot has secured a coalition agreement with Israel's left-wing Democrats, in a further step towards assembling a government to replace Benjamin Netanyahu.

Under the agreement, the Democrats will receive four ministerial portfolios, including an expanded internal security ministry, as well as labour and welfare, communications, and culture and sport. The Democrats said the agreement included a commitment to "fully repeal the judicial overhaul" and safeguard judicial independence.

The left-leaning Democrats are the second party to agree to a coalition deal with Eisenkot's Yashar after the right-wing Israel Beiteinu party did so on Tuesday, marking a further step towards forming a government.

However, Naftali Bennett's Beyachad party has yet to formally agree to join an Eisenkot-led coalition. Bennett favours a national unity government under a rotation agreement that would see Eisenkot serve as prime minister first, followed by Netanyahu.

**Coalition Mathematics:** Eisenkot, Liberman and Golan have a combined total of 36 lawmakers — far short of the 61 needed for a parliamentary majority. The bloc also includes the Beyachad party of former prime minister Naftali Bennett which has 12 seats but is yet to sign a formal coalition agreement with Yashar.

## 4. Abbas Set to Declare Hamas Terror Group — Historic Shift for Arab Party

Ra'am chairman Mansour Abbas is set to publicly recognize Hamas as a terrorist organization Saturday at 8 p.m., potentially removing one of the biggest obstacles to his party joining a government led by Yashar chairman Gadi Eisenkot. Hebrew media reported Friday that Abbas is expected to state publicly that he recognizes Hamas as a terror group in an interview on Channel 12. [REPORTED — Israeli media]

Openly declaring Hamas a terrorist organization is understood to be a prerequisite in the Gadi Eisenkot-led bloc's coalition agreement.

Thus far, a major obstacle to that goal has been Abbas's refusal to explicitly define Hamas as a terror group. While he has condemned acts of terror, including Hamas's October 7, 2023, onslaught, he has said that refraining from designating the organization as a terror group allows him to be a "bridge," and also shields his family from danger.

Asked whether his upcoming declaration could cost him his life, Abbas responded: "I've reconciled myself to it."

Crucially, Abbas wants Ra'am to become a full member of the governing coalition rather than merely provide parliamentary support from outside it.

Abbas "is familiar with 'taqiyya,'" Ben-Gvir said, referring to an Islamic concept that the far Right minister describes as "lying being permissible for the sake of destroying the State of Israel." Abbas "is a liar. He cannot be trusted."

**Significance:** If Abbas follows through Saturday evening, this would be a historic shift for an Arab party in Israeli politics. Combined with Ra'am's reported pledge of "full coalition discipline" on security matters, this represents the kind of political concession needed to bridge the secular-religious-Arab coalition that Eisenkot is attempting. The path to 63 seats (Yashar + Yisrael Beiteinu + Democrats + UTJ + Ra'am) becomes substantially clearer.

## 5. Saudi Air Defense Performance Revealed — 90% Intercept Rate

Saudi Arabia has intercepted nine out of every 10 missiles and drones launched against it during five months of sustained attacks mainly by Houthi rebels, an intelligence assessment passed to The National has revealed. The kingdom's air defence network, which includes Patriot and Thaad interceptors, destroyed 252 of at least 278 hostile munitions fired from Yemen and Iraq that were registered between June 27 and November 27. [VERIFIED — intelligence assessment]

The Saudi-led coalition in Yemen said early Thursday that its air defenses intercepted and destroyed two ballistic missiles launched toward Saudi Arabia's capital Riyadh.

Al-Maliki later said that coalition air defenses also intercepted and destroyed two drones over Saudi Arabia's Eastern Province.

**Analysis:** The 90% intercept rate sounds impressive, but the 10% that penetrate — 26+ successful strikes over five months — have caused significant damage including the Jazan refinery fatality, Ras Tanura strike, and Riyadh tower hits. The continued attacks demonstrate that Houthi pressure remains sustainable, while Saudi Arabia seeks U.S. support in strengthening its defenses.

## 6. Iran Confirms Mediation Continues — Demands Unchanged

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi has said Qatar and Pakistan are continuing mediation efforts between Iran and the United States, with both countries putting forward new proposals. [OFFICIAL — Iranian government]

"We are examining ideas that could open new paths, but we insist on implementing our demands related to reopening the Strait of Hormuz," Araghchi said.

He stressed, however, that Iran was focusing on strengthening its economic resilience in the face of sanctions rather than relying exclusively on diplomatic negotiations. Araghchi added that Tehran was exploring multiple channels to secure its requirements and counter efforts to impose an economic blockade and additional sanctions on the country.

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said on Thursday that Tehran is negotiating with the United States through mediators in Qatar and Pakistan, contradicting previous Iranian assertions that no peace talks could move forward until the U.S.

**Analysis:** This confirmation follows Kushner's acknowledgment of direct talks on November 26. Iran's "seven demands" remain maximalist, but both sides are keeping channels open. Combined with Trump's "giving them a chance to be smart" framing, there's a diplomatic track running parallel to military pressure — though no breakthrough appears imminent.
