## US-Israel-Iran Conflict Update — June 3, 2026

### Executive Summary

**MAJOR DEVELOPMENT: Escalation ruptures ceasefire — Iran strikes Kuwait airport killing 1, injuring 63; US responds with Qeshm Island attacks**

The most serious exchange of fire since the April ceasefire began overnight. The United States and Iran traded strikes overnight in one of the most intense bouts of conflict since the increasingly tenuous ceasefire between the two countries began in April. U.S. forces targeted Qeshm Island and Iran fired missiles and drones at Bahrain and Kuwait, killing one person and causing significant damage to Kuwait International Airport, which suspended flights after the attack.

Key takeaways:
- **Iranian strike on civilian infrastructure:** Kuwait airport Terminal 1 hit, killing 1 (an Indian national), injuring 63
- **Kuwait expels diplomats:** 2 Iranian diplomats declared persona non grata within 24 hours
- **Trump-Netanyahu tensions confirmed:** Trump publicly acknowledged calling Netanyahu "f---ing crazy"
- **Rubio claims "war is over"** despite ongoing strikes — contradicted by IG review launch
- **Oil surges:** Brent hits ~$97-101 as uncertainty grows

### Key Developments Since Yesterday

#### 1. Iran Attacks Gulf States — Most Serious Strike Since Ceasefire

**[VERIFIED]** Iran fired 13 ballistic missiles and 17 drones in an air attack that began at dawn on Wednesday, Kuwait's Army said.

**[VERIFIED]** One of the Iranian strikes killed an Indian national at Kuwait's airport and injured more than 60 others, according to Kuwait's Foreign Ministry. The casualties were among the highest in an Iranian strike in the region since the war began.

**[VERIFIED]** The airport strike came as part of a broader IRGC barrage that also targeted the US Fifth Fleet headquarters at Mina Salman and Juffair in Bahrain — the third Iranian attack on those facilities since the war started — and followed a night of US CENTCOM strikes on Iran's Qeshm Island, where American forces destroyed a military ground control station. The IRGC framed the entire salvo as retaliation for a US strike on an Iranian oil tanker near the Strait of Hormuz.

**[REPORTED]** Before June 3, the IRGC had struck military airfields, ports, radar installations, desalination plants, refineries, factories, data centres, and — in the case of Kuwait's own airport on February 28 — Terminal 1's radar systems. What happened on Tuesday is categorically different: Terminal 1 was handling scheduled commercial flights from seven international carriers.

**Assessment:** This represents a qualitative shift in Iranian targeting. Striking a functioning civilian airport terminal marks a departure from previous attacks on dual-use or military infrastructure. Combined with the IRGC commander's "war inevitable" statement yesterday, Iranian military posture has hardened significantly.

---

#### 2. Kuwait Expels Iranian Diplomats

**[OFFICIAL]** Kuwait has declared two Iranian diplomats persona non grata and ordered them to leave the country within 24 hours in response to the latest Iranian attacks on its territory. The number of staff at the Iranian embassy in the emirate will also be reduced, the Kuwaiti Foreign Ministry announced on Wednesday. The chargé d'affaires of the Iranian Embassy in Kuwait was summoned to the Foreign Ministry.

**[OFFICIAL]** The deputy minister also denied Iranian claims that the United States launched strikes from Kuwait. He said the Iranian allegations against Kuwait are "baseless" and such claims could not justify attacks on Kuwaiti territory or civilian facilities.

**Assessment:** Kuwait's diplomatic response is significant — this is the strongest action a Gulf state has taken against Iran since the war began. The explicit denial of US basing claims may not satisfy Tehran.

---

#### 3. Trump Confirms Clash with Netanyahu

**[OFFICIAL]** In a wide ranging interview on Pod Force One taped Tuesday afternoon at the White House, President Donald Trump seemingly confirmed the strong language he used on his call with Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu earlier this week, saying he "wasn't angry," but was "a little bit perturbed" by Israel "constantly fighting with Lebanon."

**[OFFICIAL]** Trump in an interview with the New York Post's "Pod Force One" podcast confirmed reports that he told Netanyahu during a phone call "you're f---ing crazy. You'd be in prison if not for me."

**[OFFICIAL]** Trump indicated he still has not made a decision on taking a deal with Iran or resuming the U.S.-Israeli military campaign.

**[OFFICIAL]** Netanyahu brushed off a question about Trump crudely cursing at him during a phone call this week. "I'm not going to get into details," Netanyahu said when asked about Trump reportedly calling him "f---ing crazy."

**Assessment:** Public confirmation of the expletive-laden call is remarkable. Trump remains undecided between deal and resumed war, while Netanyahu is downplaying the friction. The US-Israel relationship is under visible strain but not fracturing.

---

#### 4. Rubio Claims "War is Over" as IG Review Launched

**[OFFICIAL]** Secretary of State Marco Rubio also said this week that the United States' war with Iran is "over," even though the US and Iran have continued to trade missile and drone attacks over the last several days.

**[OFFICIAL]** US Secretary of State Marco Rubio doubled down Wednesday on his claim that the war with Iran is "over" and said the United States has achieved "victory."

**[VERIFIED]** The Pentagon, State Department and USAID inspectors general have launched a joint review of the US war with Iran, announcing in a press release Wednesday that they are mandated by law to probe overseas military operations that exceed 60 days.

**[VERIFIED]** The announcement is significant because it indicates that the watchdogs believe that, legally, the war has lasted more than 60 days from its commencement on February 28. Under the War Powers Act, the president is prohibited from keeping US troops in active hostilities for more than 60 days without congressional approval.

**Assessment:** The contradiction is stark. Rubio is claiming victory while inspectors general are launching oversight because hostilities exceed legal limits. The administration's position — that the ceasefire "pauses" the clock — is being implicitly challenged by the watchdog agencies.

---

#### 5. Israel-Lebanon Talks Continue at State Department

**[VERIFIED]** US-mediated talks between Israel and Lebanon are underway at the US State Department for the second day, Secretary of State Marco Rubio said.

**[VERIFIED]** A fourth round of talks will take place in Washington on 2 and 3 June, 2026.

**[VERIFIED]** The latest round of fighting has killed 3,468 people in Lebanon and displaced about 1.2 million, according to authorities. Netanyahu's office said at least 27 Israeli soldiers and a defense contractor have been killed in or near southern Lebanon, along with two civilians in northern Israel.

**Assessment:** Talks continue despite the overnight US-Iran exchanges. The diplomatic and military tracks are increasingly disconnected — negotiations proceed in Washington while combat intensifies regionally.

---

#### 6. Gulf States Condemn Iran — Regional Coalition Solidifying

**[VERIFIED]** The United Arab Emirates has called for a unified response from Gulf nations to ongoing attacks by Iran. Early Wednesday, Iranian missiles and drones targeted Bahrain and Kuwait, which Tehran said was in response to US strikes against coastal installations in Iran. An Iranian strike aimed at Kuwait killed one person and injured others at the country's international airport.

**[VERIFIED]** Egypt, UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Oman, Bahrain, Jordan and Lebanon all condemned Iran's attacks across the Gulf.

**Assessment:** Iran's escalation is unifying Arab states against it. Even Qatar and Oman — which have maintained mediating roles — joined the condemnation.
