## Key Developments (Nov 21-24)

### **HORMUZ ATTACKS HIT RECORD LEVELS** [VERIFIED]

"Reported attacks on shipping in and around the Strait of Hormuz are at the highest levels since the Iran war began, maritime data shows, as Tehran seeks to re-exert its control over the critical waterway amid falling oil prices."

More than 25 tankers have been hit in the past week alone. The tempo escalated sharply Sunday night when three tankers were hit in a row, all within minutes of each other. The strikes occurred at 2025, 2032 and 2042 UTC while the three vessels were headed eastbound and about to exit the Strait.

Vanguard Tech identified the tankers as three vessels belonging to Sinokor, a Korean shipping company that has become the largest player in the contested trans-Hormuz oil trade.

Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps said three "offending" oil tankers "suffered a severe explosion and fire after hitting naval mines" in the Strait of Hormuz. The statement claimed the tankers were being escorted and protected by the US military, but US support "failed to prevent the incidents from occurring."

The attacks come as the maritime security threat level for the Strait of Hormuz remains at SEVERE. The Joint Maritime Information Center (JMIC), in its November 22 assessment, warned that further attacks on tankers using the Omani side of the strait were highly likely.

**[ANALYSIS]** This represents a major shift in Iran's campaign. The simultaneous strike on three tankers in convoy formation demonstrates coordination capability that could threaten any attempt to restore normal shipping flows. Iran appears to be responding to falling oil prices and the blockade's effectiveness with intensified harassment rather than de-escalation.

### **HOUTHIS STRIKE RIYADH'S PIF TOWER AGAIN — 11 INJURED** [VERIFIED]

"Yemen's Houthi fighters struck the tallest skyscraper in the Saudi capital Riyadh on Tuesday for the second time in less than a week, wounding at least 11 people as the group presses on with a campaign that has disrupted the Gulf's biggest economy."

Two Saudi nationals and nine foreigners were injured in the Houthi attack on the building in Riyadh's financial district.

US Secretary of State Marco Rubio held a call with Saudi Foreign Minister Prince Faisal bin Farhan Al Saud, condemning the "Iran-backed Houthi terrorist attacks on Saudi civilian and energy infrastructure."

The attacks have caused the cancellation of most international flights to Riyadh, and forced Saudi Arabia to postpone or cancel major international events, including soccer's AFC Asian Cup.

**[ANALYSIS]** Two strikes on the same high-profile target in five days — now with casualties — demonstrates the Houthis' ability to penetrate Saudi air defenses repeatedly. The escalation from property damage to injuries increases pressure on Saudi Arabia to respond more forcefully.

### **IRGC ATTACKS US BASES IN BAHRAIN** [OFFICIAL]

"The IRGC said it had carried out a combined missile and drone operation targeting US military positions at the al-Riffa and Awali bases in Bahrain, claiming to have destroyed two US Patriot air defence radars at the bases."

"In the wake of the criminal Iranian attacks suffered yesterday by the Kingdom of Bahrain… a Bahraini national with a disability was wounded. His condition is stable. A Pakistani resident was also wounded and is in critical condition."

The IRGC said the operation was retaliation for a US strike on a radar installation on Iran's Hengam Island.

Central Command (CENTCOM) called the IRGC's claim that it damaged a US F-16 on Friday "FALSE," saying all US fighter jets in the region are safe and accounted for. [OFFICIAL]

**[ANALYSIS]** Iran is broadening its target set beyond Hormuz shipping to directly strike at US military infrastructure in the Gulf. While the IRGC's claims of radar damage remain unconfirmed, the pattern of escalating attacks against US positions suggests Tehran is testing Washington's redlines.

### **EISENKOT SECURES FIRST COALITION AGREEMENT** [VERIFIED]

"Israel's Yashar party, led by former military chief Gadi Eisenkot, said on Tuesday it had agreed to sign a coalition pact with a right-wing opposition party as it seeks to form a government following last month's election."

"The agreement between Eisenkot, whose party won 21 parliament seats, and Lieberman, whose party won 7, was something of a surprise, since Lieberman had been vocal about his opposition to forming a government with support from Arab parties as well as to a unity government with Netanyahu."

Eisenkot's bloc now holds 53 of the Knesset's 120 seats. His centre-right Yashar party and its patchwork coalition partners hold just 48 seats. But they have potential allies in parties representing Israel's Arab minority, which hold 19 seats.

Eisenkot has until December 6 to form a government under a 28-day mandate but he could ask for a 14-day extension.

Talks are also ongoing between Eisenkot, some of his "change bloc" partners and Likud on forming a unity government.

**[ANALYSIS]** The Lieberman deal is a significant breakthrough — it brings a right-wing nationalist into Eisenkot's tent, reducing the perception that his government would depend exclusively on Arab parties. The path to 61 seats remains narrow but viable: 48 (current bloc) + Lieberman's 7 = 55, plus possible UTJ support or Arab party backing.

### **US STRIKE POSTPONED AT LEAST ONE WEEK** [REPORTED]

Ynet, citing US officials, reports a possible strike on Iran has been postponed by at least a week despite pressure from Netanyahu.

US Central Command (CENTCOM), meanwhile, remains at maximum readiness and favors a joint strike with Israel, i24NEWS reported Sunday, citing Israeli security officials.

US President Donald Trump appeared to push a decision on renewed major strikes against Iran into next year, extending a timeline that he had previously suggested could come to a head after this month's US midterm elections.

Iran's Parliament Speaker Mohammed Ghalibaf said Iran rejects a prolonged "neither war nor peace" situation, and that it will be settled on the battlefield or the US must accept Iran's conditions.

**[ANALYSIS]** Despite the surge in Hormuz attacks and IRGC strikes on Bahrain, Trump is holding to his "wait until 2027" position. This suggests the administration believes economic pressure is working and that major escalation is unnecessary. However, the CENTCOM readiness posture keeps options open if Iran crosses a clearer redline.

### **OIL CONTINUES THREE-MONTH DECLINE** [VERIFIED]

"Global crude oil prices continued to decline for a second consecutive day on Tuesday, November 24, 2026, approaching their lowest levels in three months. Brent crude oil futures fell by $0.66, or 0.7%, to $88.71 per barrel in morning trading."

Oil is trading at $90.42 per barrel, about $27 higher than a year ago but down significantly from recent highs. Oil price one month ago was $117.47 — representing a 23% decline.

The main factor behind the price decline was an increase in global crude oil supply and expectations of a market surplus. According to Deutsche Bank estimates, global inventories of crude oil and other liquid petroleum products increased by 52 million barrels in October.

**[ANALYSIS]** Markets continue to price in expectations of limited escalation despite record Hormuz attacks. The 23% monthly decline validates the economic pressure strategy — Iran is losing revenue while oil prices aren't spiking enough to cause major Western economic pain.

## Assessment

**[ANALYSIS]** Day 270 reveals a paradox: escalating violence with declining pressure on markets.

**The Hormuz Surge:**
25+ tankers hit in a week — including a coordinated triple strike — represents Iran's most aggressive maritime campaign since the war began. The JMIC's "SEVERE" threat assessment and 40% traffic reduction demonstrate Iran retains meaningful chokepoint leverage. Yet oil prices continue falling, suggesting markets believe: (a) the blockade will hold, (b) alternative supply will compensate, and (c) major escalation remains unlikely.

**The Bahrain Escalation:**
IRGC attacks directly targeting US Patriot systems in Bahrain mark a geographic broadening of Iran's campaign. Combined with the F-16 shoot-down claim (denied by CENTCOM), Tehran appears to be testing whether strikes on US military assets will trigger the major response Trump has delayed.

**The Coalition Breakthrough:**
Eisenkot's deal with Lieberman is strategically important. It brings a nationalist right-winger into the tent, undermining the narrative that any Eisenkot government would be "Arab-dependent." With 12 days until his mandate expires (extendable to 26), the path to 61 remains narrow but more viable than a week ago. This reduces but doesn't eliminate Netanyahu's incentive to pursue a unilateral strike that could derail coalition talks.

**The Trump Paradox:**
Despite record Hormuz attacks, IRGC strikes on US bases, and Israeli pressure for action, Trump continues deferring any major strike decision to 2027. This confirms the "max pressure without escalation" strategy remains dominant. The question is whether Iran's accelerating attacks will eventually force his hand.
