## Key Developments (Nov 20-24)

### **EISENKOT-NETANYAHU COALITION TALKS COLLAPSE** [VERIFIED]

Talks between the parties of caretaker Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot to form a unity government have ended without a deal.

Representatives of Netanyahu's Likud and Eisenkot's Yashar parties met in a Herzliya hotel on Sunday, where they remained split on who would lead the next government and whether far-right National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir would be part of it.

Channel 12 reported that former Prime Minister Naftali Bennett proposed a rotation arrangement, with Eisenkot serving first and Netanyahu succeeding him. However, Eisenkot has opposed a rotation, and Netanyahu has reportedly insisted on taking the premiership first, while Lieberman has ruled out joining any government that includes Netanyahu altogether.

If unity talks fail, Yashar plans to seek a confidence vote in the Knesset for a minority government before Eisenkot's mandate expires.

Negotiations are set to resume on Monday.

**[ANALYSIS]** The failure of Sunday's talks increases the likelihood of Eisenkot pursuing a minority government with Arab party support. With his mandate expiring around Dec 6, the pressure is mounting rapidly.

### **TRUMP SIGNALS DELAYS, THREATENS BLOCKADE RUNNERS** [OFFICIAL]

President Donald Trump said on Sunday that he was inclined to resume military strikes against Iran after the new year if no agreement is reached, as indirect talks with Tehran continued.

Trump told Israel's Channel 12 on Nov. 19 that he was "not rushing into any decision," adding, "We will see what happens after the beginning of the year."

On the potential Iranian convoy to breach the blockade, Trump was blunt: "I hope they do, they'll all be at the bottom of the sea like the rest of the navy," Trump told Fox News when asked whether Iran would attempt to breach the blockade.

Trump is reportedly inclined to wait until after the new year before authorizing further strikes if negotiations fail. Trump's advisers remain divided over whether another offensive would achieve objectives or whether Washington should continue relying on its economic blockade of Iran.

**[ANALYSIS]** This is a significant de-escalation signal — Trump is explicitly pushing major decisions into 2027, reducing lame-duck strike probability. However, the convoy threat creates a potential flashpoint that could override this timeline.

### **IRAN PREPARES TANKER CONVOY TO BREACH BLOCKADE** [REPORTED]

Iran has positioned more than 14 oil tankers at Kharg Island in a convoy poised to attempt to break the US naval blockade of its oil exports, satellite imagery and ship-tracking data show.

Crews have been assigned to the tankers, Iranian flags have been raised over them, and Tehran plans to send them in convoy toward the Sea of Oman exit in the coming days, the reports said.

Guards-linked media, lawmakers and Friday prayer leaders signaled this week that Iran is preparing to send tankers through the US naval blockade, and warned that any attack on them would bring strikes on oil facilities across the Persian Gulf.

Iran should announce it officially, Fars said, and warn that any attack on the convoy would bring strikes on the region's energy infrastructure, "especially UAE oil," and on communications networks in Dubai.

However, there's uncertainty about timing: Maritime intelligence company Windward reported on November 21 that 16 tankers were anchored near Kharg Island, but none had moved since November 13. Its analysis found no evidence of an organized convoy heading toward the American blockade line as of November 20.

No Iranian government announcement has formally confirmed that a convoy operation has been authorized.

**[ANALYSIS]** This is the most dangerous development of the week. If Iran launches a convoy and the US fires on it, Iran has explicitly threatened to attack UAE oil facilities and Dubai infrastructure. This could rapidly escalate despite Trump's stated preference for delay.

### **HORMUZ ATTACKS AT RECORD HIGH** [VERIFIED]

Reported attacks on shipping in and around the Strait of Hormuz are at the highest levels since the Iran war began, maritime data shows, as Tehran seeks to re-exert its control over the critical waterway amid falling oil prices.

As of Sunday, at least 22 attacks were reported in November, the second highest figure since the war began, according to a CNN analysis of UKMTO data. The reporting area covers the Strait of Hormuz, the Persian Gulf and the Gulf of Oman.

Strait of Hormuz shipping stayed about 65% below pre-conflict levels with 263 transits in the week to November 18, as UKMTO reported six tanker attacks.

On Saturday, a tanker sailing outbound through the Strait of Hormuz was hit on its stern by an unidentified projectile about 11 nautical miles north of Oman's Khasab, the UKMTO reported.

UKMTO later reported another incident involving a tanker hit in its engine room by an unidentified projectile 17 nautical miles east of Khasab.

### **IRAN ESCALATES BAHRAIN ATTACKS** [VERIFIED]

Iran launched two waves of drone and ballistic missile attacks on Saturday night against four US bases in Bahrain, home to the US Fifth Fleet headquarters, the Iranian media reported.

Bahrain said Sunday that two people were wounded in an Iranian strike the previous day on "a civilian industrial site" in the Gulf kingdom, which has faced several attacks this week.

Iran's Revolutionary Guards have repeatedly said they targeted US facilities in Bahrain, where Iranian strikes resumed on November 13 after a lull since early September.

Iran's Revolutionary Guards Corps claimed that it struck 2 Patriot defence batteries and a radar system. Bahrain and the US military did not confirm the claims.

Saudi Arabia on Sunday lambasted Tehran for its continued "brutal aggression" against Gulf countries.

### **US-ISRAEL CONFIRM JOINT STRIKE PLANNING** [OFFICIAL]

The Israeli army and US Central Command have agreed to develop joint plans for a possible escalation against Iran. Israel's Channel 13 reported that the Israeli army confirmed the existence of joint plans with US Central Command (CENTCOM) and warned that the deadline for war was "approaching."

The report said Israeli security assessments indicated that renewed war with Iran was no more imminent than in recent days.

US Central Command (CENTCOM), meanwhile, remains at maximum readiness and favors a joint strike with Israel, i24NEWS reported Sunday, citing Israeli security officials.

Israeli media reported that Gofman met CIA Director John Ratcliffe to discuss Israeli intelligence assessments and the possibility of renewed military operations against Iran. Meanwhile, leaders of Israel's "change bloc," headed by Gadi Eisenkot, accused caretaker Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu of pushing for another war with Iran.

Brent fell to 90.63 USD/Bbl on November 23, 2026, down 2.09% from the previous day. Over the past month, Brent's price has fallen 21.74%.

Brent Oil Futures extended a punishing multi-week losing streak, shedding 4.1% to close near $86.28 — its lowest level since August 2026 — as the structural narrative in crude markets shifted decisively from geopolitical scarcity to fundamental oversupply.

Brent crude fell to around $92 per barrel on Monday, entering a fourth week of declines as expectations of strong global supply offset concerns over escalating geopolitical tensions in the Middle East.

Prices have collapsed from $117+ in late October to ~$86-90 — a decline of approximately 25% in one month.

- Iran has explicitly threatened to strike UAE oil facilities and Dubai infrastructure in response
- This could rapidly escalate despite both sides' stated preferences

The collapse of Likud-Yashar talks increases odds of:
1. **Minority government with Arab support** — Eisenkot's most likely path forward
2. **Netanyahu attempts to derail via escalation** — Change bloc explicitly accused him of this
3. **December deadline pressure** — Every day that passes reduces Eisenkot's options

**Oil Market Paradox**
The 25% collapse in oil prices since late October is remarkable given:
- Record Hormuz attack levels
- Potential convoy confrontation
- Renewed Bahrain strikes

Markets appear to be pricing in that: (a) global supply is robust despite blockade; (b) major escalation is unlikely; (c) the conflict will remain "frozen" rather than intensify.
