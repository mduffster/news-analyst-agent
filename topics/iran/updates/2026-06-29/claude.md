## US-Israel-Iran Conflict: Daily Update — June 29, 2026

### Summary

**KEY DEVELOPMENTS: MAJOR ESCALATION OVER WEEKEND — US and Iran traded strikes Friday through Sunday after the IRGC attacked two commercial vessels in the Strait of Hormuz. Iran struck US military facilities in Kuwait and Bahrain, killing a Qatari citizen as collateral. Both sides have now agreed to halt attacks and will meet Tuesday in Doha. The Israel-Lebanon framework agreement signed June 26 is facing immediate implementation challenges as Defense Minister Katz says IDF will not withdraw "a millimeter" until Hezbollah is disarmed. Hezbollah warns of "internal conflict." Oil prices remain low (~$73-74), suggesting markets still expect a deal. Technical talks originally scheduled for June 29-30 have been shifted to Doha for Tuesday, focused on Hormuz. The IMO evacuation remains paused.**

### Major Developments Since June 26

#### 1. CRITICAL: US-Iran Exchange Strikes Friday-Sunday; Both Agree to Stand Down

**[VERIFIED]** On 27 June, the US military, under the commander-in-chief's direction, conducted strikes on Iranian military surveillance infrastructure, communication systems, air defense sites, drone storage facilities, and minelayer capabilities. This action was a retaliation for an alleged Iranian attack on a ship in the Strait of Hormuz.

**[VERIFIED]** On 28 June, Iran said that it launched ballistic missiles and drones at the US Ali Al Salem Air Base in Kuwait and the US Fifth Fleet headquarters in Bahrain, as a retaliation to American attacks on five Iranian coastal sites.

**[VERIFIED]** US Central Command said early Sunday that fighter jets struck 10 Iranian military targets in and near the Strait of Hormuz in retaliation for a drone strike on the Panamanian-flagged tanker, the M/T Kiku. The vessel was transiting the strait with more than two million barrels of crude oil.

**[OFFICIAL]** Washington and Tehran agreed to halt military strikes in the region on Sunday. "Technical talks are slated to continue on all areas of the MOU. Both sides will stand down for now and vessels can move freely," the White House told Fox News.

**[OFFICIAL]** Trump wrote on his Truth Social platform: "United States aircraft just struck Iranian missile and drone storage locations, and coastal radar sites, for violating the Cease Fire Agreement, AGAIN!" Trump's post ended with a vague threat: "There may come a point when we are no longer able to be reasonable, and will be forced to militarily complete the job... If that happens, the Islamic Republic of Iran will no longer exist!"

---

#### 2. NEW: Qatari Citizen Killed in "Military Operations"

**[VERIFIED]** On 28 June, Qatar confirmed that a Qatari national was killed and an Egyptian national was injured by shrapnel during 'military operations in the area' after their vessel went missing and was later found. This occurred on the same day that Iranian drones were launched against US sites in Kuwait and Bahrain.

**[REPORTED]** The death of the Qatari citizen complicates the role of Gulf states, which have tried to contain the crisis while avoiding becoming direct casualties. Qatar has been one of the key mediators in the diplomatic effort, but now finds one of its citizens killed in the same escalation those talks were meant to prevent.

---

#### 3. US-Iran Talks Shifted to Doha Tuesday — But Iran Sending Mixed Signals

**[VERIFIED]** Representatives from the U.S. and Iran will meet Tuesday in Doha, Qatar, for a new round of technical talks, a senior Iranian source told Reuters. The source said the meeting would focus on the management of shipping in the Strait of Hormuz, and how to deescalate the tension between the two countries.

**[OFFICIAL]** President Trump posted on Truth Social not long before the Reuters report to say that Iran had requested another meeting and that it would take place in Doha on Tuesday.

**[OFFICIAL]** However, Iran's deputy foreign minister, Kazem Gharibabadi, was quoted by Iranian media on Monday saying that while consultations continue with mediator Qatar, technical talks with the U.S. are not yet planned for this week and will be held only "when the conditions are met." He did not elaborate.

**[VERIFIED]** The report says Tuesday's talks were originally planned to take place in Switzerland, but the flareup has moved them to Qatar's capital and changed their topic to the Hormuz standoff.

---

#### 4. Israel-Lebanon Framework Signed June 26 — Now Facing Implementation Test

**[VERIFIED]** A framework agreement brokered by the United States was signed on 26 June 2026 in Washington, D.C. by representatives of Israel and Lebanon, which would implement security measures along their shared border. As part of the framework, the Israeli Defense Forces would withdraw from Southern Lebanon and be replaced by units of the Lebanese Armed Forces, who would then be responsible for the disarmament of Hezbollah.

**[OFFICIAL]** Defense Minister Israel Katz says that Israel has "no territorial ambitions in Lebanon," but the IDF will not withdraw "a millimeter" until the Hezbollah terror group is disarmed. Speaking to reporters, Katz says the Israeli military is unlikely to withdraw from additional areas beyond the two agreed-upon pilot locations. "People should not hold their breath wondering where the next place will be from which Israel will withdraw in Lebanon, because it will not happen until Hezbollah is disarmed."

**[OFFICIAL]** From Hezbollah's point of view, the deal is nonexistent, [Hezbollah leader] Kassem said. He called the agreement a "humiliation," adding that linking Israel's withdrawal to Hezbollah's disarmament was a "very dangerous suggestion." The deal prompted one of the group's officials, Hassan Fadlallah, to warn that it could result in civil war because Hezbollah won't give up its weapons.

**[VERIFIED]** An Israeli soldier was killed in southern Lebanon in a firefight with a Hezbollah gunman, the Israeli military said Sunday, two days after a new agreement was signed in Washington between the two countries.

---

#### 5. CENTCOM Commander Visits Israel and Lebanon to Discuss Framework Implementation

**[VERIFIED]** In Lebanon, [CENTCOM Commander Admiral Brad] Cooper and his staff met with President Joseph Aoun and Lebanese army chief Gen. Rodolphe Haykal. "The leaders discussed the path forward in implementing a historic framework agreement signed in Washington, DC, on Friday," CENTCOM says. In Israel, Cooper met with "senior civilian and military leaders."

#### 6. Oil Prices Remain Low Despite Weekend Escalation

**[VERIFIED]** As of 8:50 a.m. Eastern Time today, oil sold for $74 per barrel (using Brent as the benchmark). That's 15 cents lower than yesterday morning.

**[VERIFIED]** U.S. West Texas Intermediate futures rose 1.7% to $70.38 per barrel. The WTI contract settled below $70 on Friday for the first time since Feb. 27 — the day before the start of the Iran war. International benchmark Brent crude futures gained 1.1% to $72.80.

**[REPORTED]** Strategists at ING said energy market participants appeared to be too optimistic about the timeline for a recovery in Persian Gulf oil supplies. Developments over the weekend underscored the risks still facing the oil market. "This complacency is odd and clearly leaves significant upside risk if the supply recovery proves slow – or if we see significant re-escalation."

---

#### 7. IMO Evacuation Still Paused; No Resumption Announced

**[VERIFIED]** IMO pauses evacuation in Strait of Hormuz following attack (25 June). No resumption has been announced as of June 29.

**[VERIFIED]** Traffic through the Strait of Hormuz has partially recovered but remains well below pre-war levels. In the week following the ceasefire, 125 vessels passed through the strait, according to Lloyd's List Intelligence, the highest level of weekly transits since the war started in late February.

---

#### 8. Iran Warns Oman Over Strait Route Cooperation

**[REPORTED]** In a rare statement, Iran's adviser to the supreme leader on international affairs warned Oman about aligning itself with countries bordering the Persian Gulf, saying: "Any follow-through, even if it is due to external considerations and pressures, could tarnish Oman's positive and respectable image in the minds of the Iranian nation and sovereignty." He emphasized Iran's desire to collect tolls from ships passing through the Strait of Hormuz and suggested Oman agree to Tehran's demand.

### Emerging Signals

**Qatar's position complicated:** Qatar has been one of the key mediators in the diplomatic effort, but now finds one of its citizens killed in the same escalation those talks were meant to prevent. The death of a Qatari citizen in "military operations" — with no clear attribution to either side — puts Qatar in an awkward position as it hosts Tuesday's talks. Qatar also suspended all marine activities Monday, suggesting heightened concern.

**Iran internal signals mixed:** Trump says Iran "requested" talks; Iran's deputy FM says talks only "when conditions are met." This could reflect genuine internal debate, or deliberate ambiguity to extract concessions. US President Trump says a meeting with Iran will be held in Qatar's capital on Tuesday after Tehran "requested talks" following days of reciprocal attacks. However, a senior Iranian official says there are no planned meetings with US officials from "technical teams" in Doha this week.

**Market complacency:** ING strategists said: "This complacency is odd and clearly leaves significant upside risk if the supply recovery proves slow – or if we see significant re-escalation." Oil at pre-war levels despite weekend strikes and two ship attacks suggests markets are betting on successful de-escalation. This could change rapidly if talks fail.

**Israel-Lebanon framework structural problem:** The framework signed Friday ties IDF withdrawal to Hezbollah disarmament. Katz says he does not believe the Lebanese army will "suddenly become lions charging at Hezbollah," and therefore the IDF's presence in Lebanon will be "long-term." The defense minister confirms that Israel recently tried to get the Lebanese army to enter the Ali Taher Ridge area and clear it of terror operatives. However, "the Lebanese army refused to do it." This suggests permanent stalemate is the most likely outcome.

### Bottom Line

**The MOU survived its most serious test over the weekend — barely. US and Iranian forces exchanged strikes Friday through Sunday, Iran hit US facilities in Kuwait and Bahrain, a Qatari civilian was killed, and a supertanker was attacked. Both sides have agreed to stand down and will meet Tuesday in Doha, but the fundamental dispute over Hormuz control remains unresolved. The Israel-Lebanon framework signed June 26 faces immediate implementation challenges: Katz says no IDF withdrawal until Hezbollah disarms; Hezbollah says disarmament is non-negotiable and warns of civil war. Oil prices remain remarkably stable at ~$74, suggesting markets still expect a deal, but analysts warn this complacency may be premature. With July 1 two days away and the IMO evacuation still paused, the Hormuz "reopening" promised by the MOU is clearly not happening on schedule. We are now in damage-control mode: the question is whether the framework can survive rather than whether it will be fully implemented.**
