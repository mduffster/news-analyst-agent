- **Weekend escalation:** Reuters reporting indicates a **commercial tanker was hit in/near Hormuz**, followed by **fresh U.S. strikes on Iranian targets**, described as the sharpest escalation since the interim deal was signed roughly two weeks ago. **[REPORTED]** ([investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/world-news/us-carries-out-fresh-strikes-against-iran-after-tanker-struck-in-hormuz-escalating-hostilities-4764056?utm_source=openai))  
- **De-escalation push (claims of halting strikes):** Axios reports a **senior U.S. official** said the U.S. and Iran **agreed to stop “kinetic activity”** and plan **talks in Doha** focused on the Hormuz dispute. **[REPORTED]** ([axios.com](https://www.axios.com/2026/06/28/us-and-iran-agree-to-halt-strikes-and-meet-this-week-us-official-says?utm_source=openai))

The system is now in a classic “two-track” pattern: **military tit-for-tat + hurried diplomatic damage control**. That tends to *increase* the odds of **sustained air/sea enforcement** (escorts/ISR/retaliatory strikes) because each incident creates domestic and alliance pressure to “prove” deterrence—while talks become narrower and more crisis-management oriented.

**What changed / what it means:**  
Compared to June 26’s setup (where **IAEA modalities and June 29–30 Switzerland talks** looked like the near-term hinge), the immediate center of gravity is now **shipping security “rules”** and **incident prevention** (lanes, notifications, enforcement boundaries). That’s a sign the interim deal is being treated less like a durable framework and more like a **fragile armistice needing hotline-style management**.

### 3) Maritime risk signals are deteriorating: threat level reportedly raised after multiple incidents
- Trade reporting says the Joint Maritime Information Center (JMIC) **raised the Hormuz threat level to “SUBSTANTIAL”** after **two attacks on merchant vessels in 72 hours** (per a 28 June advisory update). **[REPORTED]** ([en.portnews.ru](https://en.portnews.ru/news/print/393448/?utm_source=openai))  
- IMO’s official position remains that the evacuation operation is **paused pending clarity/safety guarantees**. **[OFFICIAL]** ([imo.org](https://www.imo.org/en/mediacentre/pressbriefings/pages/statement-on-the-attack-in-strait-of-hormuz-evacuation-plan-pause.aspx?utm_source=openai))  

**What changed / what it means:**  
Even if volumes don’t crash again, **risk pricing** (insurance, routing, convoy demand) is likely to worsen when threat levels ratchet up. That directly increases the probability of a longer **maritime-security-centric entanglement** where the U.S. stays operationally committed even without a major new strategic objective.
