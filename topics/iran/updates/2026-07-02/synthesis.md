## Critical Developments (July 2)

### 1. CRITICAL: Doha Talks Conclude — No Breakthrough, But Implementation Steps Agreed [VERIFIED]

Iran and the United States concluded a round of indirect technical talks on Wednesday with no sign they had made headway towards a lasting peace. Instead, the two countries focused on issues that they said had been resolved when an interim agreement was announced two weeks ago.

The Doha indirect talks concluded after two days without a breakthrough but with implementation steps, including an arrangement to use part of the $6 billion in Iranian assets held in Qatar to purchase necessary goods for delivery to Iran and a communications mechanism to flag violations.

In Doha on Wednesday, Qatari Emir Sheikh Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani met US special envoy Steve Witkoff and Trump's son-in-law, Jared Kushner, and reaffirmed Qatar's continued mediation efforts, alongside Pakistan, to end the war in the Middle East.

Iran's Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi headed the country's technical team in the talks. Iran's top negotiators, Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi and Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, did not attend the talks.

Qatar says the next meeting will take place after funeral processions for Iran's former supreme leader.

**Assessment:** The Doha round produced limited but tangible progress — a frozen assets mechanism and communications channel — while failing to advance on the harder questions (Hormuz governance, nuclear issues). That negotiators spent two days relitigating issues "supposedly resolved" in the MOU confirms the interim agreement was more framework than settlement. The absence of top Iranian negotiators (Araghchi, Ghalibaf) signals Tehran is treating this as a technical implementation track, not strategic negotiation. The pause for Khamenei's funeral (July 4-9) delays progress by at least a week.

### 2. MAJOR: Hormuz Oil Flows Surge to 10+ Million Barrels/Day — "Caught Tehran Off Guard" [VERIFIED]

Commercial shipping through the Strait of Hormuz has surged over the past few weeks, with American military support helping boost oil flows to more than 10 million barrels per day, a US official said.

The increase since President Donald Trump signed an interim peace agreement with Iran represents a big jump in traffic since the war paralyzed flows. That has caught Tehran off guard, underscoring its now-limited ability to halt traffic through the corridor and helping spark recent attacks around the strait.

The United Arab Emirates restored its exports to over 3.9 million barrels daily, pushing total daily Hormuz flows past 10 million barrels. This surge, combined with ongoing emergency reserve releases and ad hoc Saudi sales to Asia, has created a market surplus.

With at least 10 million now getting through daily, combined with 5 million via alternative routes, flows are approaching normal levels.

The administration sees last week's attacks as evidence that Tehran is attempting to reassert control over the strait after realizing its ability to paralyze traffic is limited. Iran's limited capacity to monitor traffic far from its coastline has hampered its awareness of activity in the southern transit corridor.

**Assessment:** This is a potentially significant shift in the leverage calculus. Pre-war Hormuz flows averaged ~20 million barrels/day; if 10 million are now transiting (plus 5 million via alternative routes), the strait is operating at roughly 75% of normal capacity. This undermines Iran's core leverage — the ability to choke global energy supplies. The US claim that Iran was "caught off guard" by recovery suggests Tehran's situational awareness of southern corridor traffic is limited. However, Iran's fresh military warning (below) suggests it may attempt to reassert control.

### 3. CRITICAL: Iran Issues Explicit Military Warning — "Decisive Reaction" to US Interference [OFFICIAL]

Iran's joint military command warned Thursday that all oil tankers moving through the Strait of Hormuz must use its approved routes or face a "forceful response," again ratcheting up tensions over a waterway crucial for international energy supplies.

"Any attempt by the United States to interfere in security matters or any disruptive action in the Strait of Hormuz will be considered a threat to Iran's national sovereignty and will be met with a rapid and decisive reaction."

An effort by Oman and a United Nations agency to launch a new route near Oman's shore sparked attacks across the Mideast last weekend, highlighting the tensions.

The statement from the Khatam al-Anbiya military command, reported by Iranian state television, comes after both U.S. and Iranian diplomats met with mediators on Wednesday in Qatar. It wasn't immediately clear what sparked the threat from Iran.

**Assessment:** Iran's explicit threat — "rapid and decisive reaction" to US interference — represents an escalation in rhetoric following the Doha talks. The timing suggests Tehran may be responding to the surge in Hormuz traffic that bypassed its control. This creates a potential flashpoint: if ships continue using the southern Omani corridor while ignoring Iran's "approved routes," Tehran faces a binary choice between enforcement (risking escalation) or acquiescence (losing leverage). The threat may be calibrated to deter further traffic recovery during the funeral pause.

### 4. Grounded Vessel Narrative Exposed — Ship Actually Tied to Iran [REPORTED]

In a report apparently aimed at bolstering Tehran's claims to control the strait, Iranian state television said this week that a foreign ship got stuck in the waterway after ignoring instructions from Iran's paramilitary Revolutionary Guard. However, the vessel's shape, reported location and other details indicate the ship is tied to Iran and appears to have been stranded for months.

**Assessment:** AP's fact-check significantly undermines Iran's grounding narrative. Rather than a real-time enforcement example, the incident appears to be staged propaganda using a vessel with Iranian ties that has been stuck for months. This revelation weakens the credibility of Iran's "approved corridor" messaging but doesn't change the underlying dispute over route control.

### 6. Netanyahu: IDF Staying in Lebanon "For the Foreseeable Future" [OFFICIAL]

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu on Tuesday visited Lebanese territory occupied by the Israeli military, telling soldiers that Israel would not withdraw from the country's south as long as Iran-backed Hezbollah continued to pose a threat. It was the first visit by Netanyahu to occupied Lebanese territory since the Israeli and Lebanese governments reached a security agreement last Friday.

"Our insistence is that we will not leave southern Lebanon until the threat is removed," Netanyahu declared. "And as long as Hezbollah remains here, armed and threatening us, we will remain here as well."

A security deal between Israel and Lebanon risks entrenching a stalemate rather than resolving Israel's underlying conflict with Hezbollah by tying Israel's pullout from southern Lebanon to the Iran-backed terror group's disarmament, a condition regional analysts and politicians say is unattainable.

Israeli attacks on Lebanon since then have killed over 4,000 people and have continued despite the U.S. ceasefire with Iran, which requires Israel to stop bombing Lebanon and withdraw its forces.

Iran responded on Tuesday by reiterating its long-standing position on Lebanon while emphasizing that Washington must honor its commitments to end conflict across the region. Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesperson Esmaeil Baghaei said the United States had explicitly committed to ending the war in Lebanon and should follow through. "The US has explicitly made a commitment to end the war in Lebanon and must fulfil its commitment."

**Assessment:** Netanyahu's "foreseeable future" statement confirms the Israel-Lebanon framework is structurally deadlocked. The condition for withdrawal (Hezbollah disarmament) cannot be met, so withdrawal won't happen. Iran's response — demanding the US fulfill its commitment to end the Lebanon war — highlights the MOU's vulnerability: Iran can claim US non-compliance (failure to stop Israeli operations) as justification for its own non-compliance on Hormuz.

### 7. Khamenei Funeral Preparations — Six-Day Ceremonies Begin July 4 [VERIFIED]

Iranian officials announced that funeral and burial ceremonies for the former Supreme Leader will take place between July 4 and July 9, 2026. The elaborate ceremony will unfold across several major stations. Khamenei's body will lie in state at the Tehran Mosalla on July 4 and 5, with a funeral procession through Tehran's streets on July 6.

Ceremonies will then move to the holy city of Qom on July 7, before part of the proceedings shifts to Iraq on July 8, including processions in Najaf and Karbala, after which the body will be returned to Iran. The final burial will take place on July 9, 2026, at the shrine of the eighth Shia imam in Mashhad.

According to official estimates, between 15 million and 20 million people are expected to take part.

Mystery continues to surround Khamenei's successor and son, Mojtaba Khamenei. Mojtaba was elected as the new Supreme Leader on March 8, 2026, but has rarely appeared in public since his appointment, with several senior US officials, including Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, claiming he is in a coma. It remains unclear whether he will personally preside over the ceremonies.

**Assessment:** The funeral creates a diplomatic and potentially military pause (July 4-9). Iran is unlikely to initiate major operations during the procession. However, the ceremonies also serve as a domestic consolidation event — "a call for revenge and a show of force" per Israeli analysis. Mojtaba Khamenei's potential appearance (or absence) will signal regime stability. The Iraq leg (Najaf, Karbala) underscores Iran's regional reach.

### 8. Iran Bars IAEA Inspectors from Nuclear Sites [OFFICIAL]

On 2 July, Iran announced that IAEA inspectors would be denied entry to the Fordow, Natanz, and Isfahan nuclear sites under any circumstances, citing a parliamentary law and a decision by the Supreme National Security Council following damage from the US bombings.

**Assessment:** This is a significant hardening of Iran's nuclear posture. Barring IAEA inspectors "under any circumstances" — citing war damage and parliamentary law — removes international verification of Iran's nuclear activities during the negotiation period. This complicates any path to a comprehensive deal that includes nuclear provisions and may be Tehran's leverage play: no inspections until sanctions relief and full MOU implementation.
