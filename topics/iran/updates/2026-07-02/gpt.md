- **Iran’s joint military command warned oil tankers they must use Iran’s “approved routes,” threatening an “immediate and forceful response” for deviation/noncompliance.** **[OFFICIAL]** ([apnews.com](https://apnews.com/article/de981ef87afe8da617076fe494c37482?utm_source=openai))  
- **AP “Fact Focus” reporting adds a complicating wrinkle:** the widely circulated “foreign ship ran aground after ignoring IRGC instructions” narrative appears tied to **a vessel with links to Iran** (and may have been in the area for months), suggesting the episode may have been used as **information/route-control messaging** as much as (or more than) a real-time enforcement event. **[REPORTED]** ([apnews.com](https://apnews.com/article/d42c19cab5cfb5e4318732408550b7d6?utm_source=openai))  

**What changed / what it means:**  
This is a notable ratchet: Iran moved from insinuation/PR about “approved routes” to an **explicit deterrent threat**. Even if no boarding/seizure follows immediately, the statement increases the chance that the next “route dispute” becomes a **live-fire or interdiction trigger**, especially if U.S./partner naval escorts encourage transits that Iran labels “noncompliant.”
