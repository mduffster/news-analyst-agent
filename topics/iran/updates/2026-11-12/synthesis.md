# US-Israel-Iran Conflict: Trump Declares No Deal Necessary as Netanyahu-IDF Clash Over Strike Preparations Intensifies

## November 12, 2026 — Day 258

**Assessment:** Day 258 marks a significant rhetorical pivot in U.S. policy: President Donald Trump said on Thursday the US no longer needed a deal with Iran, claiming that "those days have long since passed." The President's comments marked a shift from recent weeks, when he had repeatedly said the war would end after the midterm elections and that a deal with Iran remained possible. Meanwhile, Israel's security cabinet has ordered attack preparations against Iran, but reports indicate the IDF itself sees no immediate urgency for strikes — creating dangerous civil-military tensions during an already fraught political transition. Iran hardened its negotiating posture further, with Parliament Speaker Ghalibaf declaring that the Strait of Hormuz will "never return" to its pre-war status. The confluence of Trump's deal rejection, Netanyahu's push for military action, and Iran's maximalist positioning suggests the conflict is settling into an indefinite stalemate rather than moving toward resolution or escalation.

## 1. Trump: "We Don't Need a Deal With Iran" — Vance Redirected to Domestic Issues [OFFICIAL]

US President Donald Trump said Thursday that he was directing Vice President JD Vance to focus completely on domestic anti-fraud efforts and not negotiations toward a deal to end war with Tehran. "We don't need a deal with Iran. Those days have long since passed. We've got their money, Hundreds of Billions of Dollars, and we've got their Strait!" Trump wrote on his Truth Social platform.

The directive threw cold water on ongoing back channel talks and dampened prospects for a diplomatic breakthrough to end the war. It also appeared to undercut one of Trump's main war objectives: to prevent Iran from obtaining a nuclear weapon.

Polls leading up to Election Day and since have shown that a majority of voters, and a sizeable chunk of Trump's Republican base, felt the Iran conflict was a mistake. Trump's announcement Thursday could represent his latest way to try assuaging GOP voters, including members of his "Make America Great Again" base.

This represents a stark reversal. On the eve of the election, Trump wrote on Truth Social, "Oil Prices are DROPPING LIKE A ROCK, down almost $10 a Barrel, A RECORD, because people feel that there will not be a War with Iran. Iran wants a settlement, as soon as possible." "I believe we'll make a deal right after the election because it doesn't make sense for them not to," Trump said.

**Significance:** Trump's statement codifies "economic strangulation without resolution" as the explicit policy framework. Combined with redirecting Vance away from negotiations, this signals the administration is accepting indefinite conflict rather than pursuing either military escalation or diplomatic settlement. The pattern suggests Trump believes he can satisfy his base by claiming victory ("we've got their Strait") without actually ending the war.

## 2. Israeli Security Cabinet Orders Attack Preparations — IDF Reportedly Sees No Urgency [VERIFIED/CONFLICTING]

Israel has reportedly ordered its military to step up readiness for possible strikes on Iran as Tehran warned it would expand the war beyond the region if attacked. Israel's security cabinet, chaired by Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, has ordered the military to step up preparations for an attack on Iran, Israeli media reported on Wednesday evening.

Public broadcaster KAN said the directives issued by the security cabinet could lead Israel to carry out activities that could trigger a war, and KAN said the directives were made public after opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot told Netanyahu not to deploy the military "without a direct and immediate threat" until the next government is formed.

However, military assessments appear to contradict the political push: Channel 13 news reports that the IDF position presented to Eisenkot during the briefing was that there is no immediate urgency for Israel to strike Iran.

The cabinet is scheduled to meet Sunday, Israel's Yedioth Ahronoth daily reported.

Eisenkot warned against deploying security forces without a direct and immediate threat requiring urgent action, saying this could have devastating consequences for Israeli democracy and public trust in state institutions. He urged Netanyahu to exercise restraint and avoid placing security chiefs in a constitutional dilemma over which orders they should follow.

**Significance:** This creates a dangerous divergence between political direction and military judgment. Netanyahu is ordering attack preparations while the IDF reportedly assesses no immediate threat justifies action. The Sunday security cabinet meeting is the key near-term flashpoint, with potential for constitutional crisis if Netanyahu attempts to use military force over military objections.

## 3. Trump-Netanyahu "Shouting Match" Reports — Daily Calls to Resume War [REPORTED]

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and President Trump held a contentious phone call over the weekend concerning strategy in the Iran war that devolved into a "shouting match from hell," according to reports. Sources cited by the Yedioth Ahronoth newspaper described the match as particularly confrontational. The two leaders apparently disagreed over when, or if, to restart strikes on Iran after a multiweek pause.

Mr. Netanyahu's office denied the characterization of the conversation reported by Yedioth Ahronoth, calling it "fake news." The report of the contentious phone call comes just days after another report in the Financial Times said Mr. Netanyahu calls Mr. Trump almost daily to urge him to restart military action against Iran.

The Financial Times reported that Netanyahu has been calling US President Donald Trump every day to urge him to resume military action against Iran, as Gulf leaders press Washington to choose negotiations over a new offensive. "Netanyahu calls him every day [to urge him to resume the war]."

Netanyahu has consistently argued that Washington should resume strikes and actively pursue regime change within the Islamic Republic, while Trump has favoured maintaining economic pressure to secure diplomatic concessions from Tehran.

**Significance:** Whether or not the call was a "shouting match," the underlying tension is clear: Netanyahu is lobbying intensively for military resumption while Trump is resisting. This rift is occurring precisely when Netanyahu faces losing power and may view military action as a way to trigger crisis conditions that could alter the political calculus.

Opposition circles fear Netanyahu could launch a strike on Iran to place the country under a state of emergency, potentially paving the way for a unity government.

**Significance:** The briefing partially defuses the immediate constitutional crisis over security access, but the underlying tensions remain. This bolsters concerns that Netanyahu's push is politically rather than security-driven.

## 5. Iran: Strait of Hormuz Will "Never Return" to Pre-War Status [OFFICIAL]

Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, Iran's parliament speaker and top negotiator, has told Al Jazeera that the Strait of Hormuz will "never return" to its status prior to the US-Israel war on Iran. "There are no 'free waters' [international waters] there, and the 1982 [UN Convention on the Law of the Sea] does not apply to the strait … The regulations within the Strait of Hormuz are governed by the laws of the two littoral states that border it," he said.

Ghalibaf said the Strait of Hormuz will "never return" to its pre-war status and that Iran-Oman have reached an understanding on overseeing it. He said Tehran sent Washington seven conditions for peace talks via Qatar mediator and is awaiting a US response, adding "the ball is in the American court."

As the United States-Iran war drags on and talks remain stalled, Tehran is determined to move beyond a state of "no war and no peace." Ghalibaf says Tehran remains open to a deal with the US to agree a ceasefire and the reopening of the strait, and has been waiting for Washington to respond to its terms for this to happen. "The ball is now in the American court," he said.

**Significance:** This represents Iran's clearest statement that it intends to permanently alter the Hormuz status quo, not merely use it as temporary leverage. Combined with Trump's "no deal" declaration, both sides are signaling they can accept extended stalemate. This framing of permanent Iranian control contradicts Trump's claim that "we've got their Strait."

## 6. Iran Demands Environmental War Compensation at COP31 [VERIFIED]

Iran's President Masoud Pezeshkian told the COP31 climate conference on Wednesday that his country wanted compensation for environmental damage caused by US and Israeli strikes, saying the issue is related to global efforts to fight climate change. International climate agreements currently include billions of dollars in wealthy-country finance to help developing countries adapt to climate change and pay for its impacts, but they include no provision covering the environmental impacts of military strikes.

Pezeshkian called on the international community to establish a genuine and effective connection between climate commitments made during peacetime and responsibilities assumed during wartime. He emphasized the need to document and assess environmental damage inflicted by military aggression. The Iranian leader also urged the creation of specific mechanisms to provide compensation for such ecological destruction.

**Significance:** Iran is adding a new demand vector — environmental reparations — to its already-rejected seven conditions. While this has no near-term diplomatic traction, it signals Tehran is expanding its grievance framework and seeking new international venues for pressure.

## 7. Two Iranians Convicted of Spying in UK — First Under National Security Act [VERIFIED]

Two Iranian men have been found guilty by a court in the UK of spying on dissident journalists in Britain on behalf of Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps, a case police said reflected increasing espionage activity by Tehran. Mostafa Sepahvand, 41, was convicted at Woolwich Crown Court of two offences and Farhad Javadi Manesh, 46, was convicted of one offence under Britain's National Security Act.

Police said the convictions marked the first time Iranian nationals had been convicted under the National Security Act, introduced in 2023 to counter threats from foreign states including Iran, which the authorities say have risen sharply.

Sepahvand was also convicted of planning a violent attack on one or more of their targets. Sepahvand said he was offered up to £150,000 to carry out a violent attack on at least one of their targets, which included two Iran International journalists and the CEO of UK Lawyers for Israel.

London's Metropolitan Police said this was the fourth case in recent years involving a direct threat to Iran International journalists. "The threat from hostile states to the U.K. is increasing and Iran continues to adapt its approach to using 'proxies'," said Vicki Evans, a deputy assistant commissioner.

**Significance:** Combined with Tuesday's DOJ indictments over the RAF Fairford plot, this represents coordinated Western legal action exposing Iranian covert operations across Europe and North America. The pattern demonstrates systematic Iranian targeting of perceived enemies on Western soil.

## 8. Maritime Attacks Continue — UKMTO Confirms Projectile Strike [VERIFIED]

In an updated warning, the UKMTO said it received confirmation that a tanker was hit by an unidentified projectile while passing through the strategic waterway. The agency reclassified the incident as an "attack" following the confirmation.

Another tanker transiting outbound through the Strait of Hormuz was reportedly struck by an unidentified projectile on November 7. In its November 8 advisory, the Joint Maritime Information Center (JMIC) maintained the threat level for the Strait of Hormuz at SEVERE, warning that further deliberate attacks were highly likely.

**Significance:** UKMTO's reclassification of the incident as an "attack" provides independent corroboration beyond Iranian claims. The ongoing pattern of maritime incidents undermines market assumptions of de-escalation and confirms that the Gulf remains an active conflict zone.

## 9. Houthis Target Saudi Pipeline, Yanbu, Aramco in Riyadh [REPORTED]

Yemen's Iran-aligned Houthis said on Thursday they had attacked a pumping station on the Saudi East-West oil pipeline, an oil facility in the Saudi Red Sea city of Yanbu, and an Aramco building in the capital Riyadh.

Houthi spokesman Yahya Saree said in a post on X that one operation "targeted Pumping Station No. 7 on the East-West Pipeline using a drone", while a second "targeted the Yanbu oil facility using a ballistic missile".

With the conflict in the Middle East disrupting oil flows through the Strait of Hormuz, Riyadh has rerouted about 4 million barrels per day of crude via the East-West Pipeline to Yanbu, equivalent to about 4% of global supply. The pipeline has spared Saudi Arabia, the world's biggest exporter, from the brunt of the impact of the wartime shutdown of the Strait of Hormuz.

Saudi authorities have not confirmed damage or casualties at Yanbu. They have reported intercepting missiles and drones aimed at multiple regions, adding to tensions around the kingdom.

**Significance:** The East-West pipeline is Saudi Arabia's critical bypass route around the closed Hormuz strait. Repeated Houthi attacks on this infrastructure demonstrate Iran-aligned proxies are systematically targeting alternatives to Hormuz transit, compounding global supply pressures.
