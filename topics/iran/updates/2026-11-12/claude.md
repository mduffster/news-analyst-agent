### **IRAN ADDS NEW DEMAND: ENVIRONMENTAL COMPENSATION AT COP31** [VERIFIED]

Iran's President Masoud Pezeshkian told the COP31 climate conference on Wednesday, November 11, his country wanted compensation for environmental damage caused by US and Israeli strikes, saying the issue related to global efforts to fight climate change.

"We expect the global community to establish a genuine and meaningful link between climate commitments in times of peace and responsibility in times of war, to document and assess the environmental damages caused by military aggressions and to develop compensation mechanisms," he said.

These demands come at a time when current climate agreements—which regulate the distribution of billions of dollars to support developing countries—lack any explicit legal provisions or financial coverage for the environmental consequences of wars, armed conflicts, and attacks on ships and energy transport.

**[ANALYSIS]** Iran is adding to its already-rejected demands. This environmental compensation claim is a new vector for diplomatic pressure, though it faces "complex political and legal obstacles." It's unlikely to gain traction but signals Iran is expanding its grievance framework.

### **TWO IRANIANS CONVICTED OF SPYING IN UK — FIRST UNDER NATIONAL SECURITY ACT** [VERIFIED]

Two Iranian men have been found guilty by a court in the UK of spying on dissident journalists in Britain on behalf of Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps, a case police said reflected increasing espionage activity by Tehran.

Mostafa Sepahvand, 41, was convicted at Woolwich Crown Court of two offences and Farhad Javadi Manesh, 46, was convicted of one offence under Britain's National Security Act.

Police said the convictions marked the first time Iranian nationals had been convicted under the National Security Act, introduced in 2023 to counter threats from foreign states including Iran, which the authorities say have risen sharply.

Prosecutors said the men had been carrying out surveillance on journalists working for Iran International, a Persian-language television station which is critical of Iran's government, between August 2024 and March 2025. This was being carried out on behalf of Iran's foreign intelligence service in order to attack them "as retribution for their criticism of the Iranian regime," prosecutors said.

**[ANALYSIS]** Combined with yesterday's DOJ charges on RAF Fairford, this demonstrates coordinated Western legal action against Iranian covert operations. Iran's asymmetric threat to Europe is being systematically exposed and prosecuted.

### **HOUTHIS HIT SAUDI OIL INFRASTRUCTURE — PIPELINE, YANBU, RIYADH** [VERIFIED]

Yemen's Iran-aligned Houthis said on Thursday they had attacked a pumping station on the Saudi East-West oil pipeline, an oil facility in the Saudi Red Sea city of Yanbu, and an Aramco building in the capital Riyadh.

The Saudi-led military coalition fighting the Houthis said later on Thursday it had intercepted and destroyed two drones launched by the group, one in the Riyadh region and another in the kingdom's oil-rich Eastern Province.

Saudi-led coalition forces said they had intercepted three cruise missiles launched towards Riyadh, along with drones launched towards Mecca and Saudi Arabia's southern regions. Saudi Arabia's Civil Defence Authority said debris from the interception of two ballistic missiles fell in the village of Sabuhah in Mecca province.

The Arab League strongly condemned continued attacks by the Houthi militia on Saudi Arabia, involving the launch of ballistic and cruise missiles and UAVs toward areas including Makkah, Riyadh, Eastern and the southern regions. The attacks mark a dangerous escalation and reflect the militia's persistence in threatening Saudi Arabia's security and stability.

The pipeline is a vital route for Saudi oil exports due to Tehran's restrictions in the Strait of Hormuz following the US-Israel war against Iran.

**[ANALYSIS]** The East-West pipeline is critical because it bypasses Hormuz — Houthi attacks on it compound pressure on global oil supplies. Arab League condemnation and Saudi intercepts suggest defenses are working, but the tempo of attacks continues.

### **IRAN: HORMUZ "WILL NEVER RETURN" TO PRE-WAR STATUS** [VERIFIED]
