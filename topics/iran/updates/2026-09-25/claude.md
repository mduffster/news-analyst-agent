# Daily Update: US-Israel-Iran Conflict
## September 25, 2026 — Day 210

### Key Developments (Last 24 Hours)

**IRAN'S 7-DAY HORMUZ PLAN DETAILED** [REPORTED]

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said Thursday that Tehran proposed a seven-day plan to U.S. negotiators this week to end the war, one that would reopen the Strait of Hormuz, ease sanctions and begin talks on his country's nuclear program. Speaking at a press conference on the sidelines of the U.N. General Assembly in New York City, Mr. Araghchi said the Iranian proposal appeared very similar to the Islamabad memorandum of understanding, which both countries signed in June.

Iran has offered to reopen the Strait of Hormuz and resume talks on its nuclear program in seven days if the United States lifts its naval blockade of Iranian ports, waives sanctions on Iranian oil sales and observes a ceasefire that would include Lebanon. Iran's Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi detailed the proposal in a private gathering on Thursday on the sidelines of the U.N. General Assembly in New York.

**PEZESHKIAN SIGNALS OPENNESS TO NUCLEAR INSPECTIONS** [REPORTED]

Pezeshkian told journalists on Thursday that Iran was "open" to allowing inspections of its nuclear facilities, and he denied that the regime is seeking to assassinate Mr. Trump or any members of his family. The diplomatic overture came as Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian told Fox News that Tehran had no ambition to acquire nuclear weapons and was willing to accept international inspections and dilute its enriched nuclear material.

**TRUMP-XI SUMMIT WRAPS IN WASHINGTON** [VERIFIED]

President Donald Trump said Friday that he and Chinese leader Xi Jinping discussed the war in Iran at their meeting at the White House. "We did," Trump said in response to a question from CNN, during his tour with Xi at the National Archives. When asked if he and Xi made progress on the Strait of Hormuz, Trump responded: "I think we're going to do great."

Four months after their last meeting, President Donald Trump and Xi Jinping traded niceties during the Chinese leader's first visit to the US capital in over a decade. The two leaders extended a trade truce, exchanged gifts and compliments, and acknowledged the possibility of working together to contain the dangers of AI development. For a historic summit meant to address rising tensions between the world's largest economic and geopolitical powers, the Washington trip was relatively uneventful.

**TALKS ENTER "TECHNICAL PHASE"** [REPORTED]

Iran-U.S. negotiations in New York have moved beyond initial diplomatic contacts into a more detailed technical phase, with sources in Tehran describing the atmosphere as increasingly positive, Al Jazeera's bureau chief in Tehran reported Friday. The Qatari-government owned network said technical experts had joined the discussions.

### Iran Internal Dynamics

**HARDLINER BACKLASH INTENSIFIES** [REPORTED]

Iran's leaders have sought to project unity, but some hard-liners want to continue or escalate the war to secure bigger U.S. concessions and have criticized Pezeshkian and Araghchi's diplomatic steps this week. Firebrand lawmaker Ebrahim Rezaei wrote on X that merely meeting with "the aggressor enemy" showed weakness.

Iran's Assembly of Experts, a panel of Shiite clerics who appoint the supreme leader, said "there is no path other than resistance."

**GULF MEDIATION STALLED** [REPORTED]

Earlier this month, Gulf leaders planned to gather in Oman to discuss an emerging agreement between Oman and Iran on the Strait of Hormuz, which runs between the two countries. But the meeting was canceled after Saudi Arabia called for amendments, only some of which were accepted by Iran.

### Bab al-Mandab / Yemen Situation

The Saudi-led coalition says it shot down 6 ballistic missiles launched by Houthis. Hours later, Yemen's Houthis said they had launched missile and drone attacks on what they described as "a sensitive target" in Saudi Arabia's capital Riyadh and on Saudi Aramco facilities in Yanbu.

At least 150 Houthi fighters and government troops have been killed in the past 24 hours, military sources said, with clashes on hilltops overlooking the strategically important Bab Al-Mandab Strait at the mouth of the Red Sea. As the Houthi attacks targeted Saudi Arabia on Thursday, battles raged on the ground in Yemen.

### Assessment

**[ANALYSIS]** The diplomatic picture sharpened significantly in the past 24 hours. Iran's 7-day Hormuz plan — essentially an accelerated version of the failed June MoU — provides Washington with something actionable: a compressed timeline, a stated willingness for IAEA inspections, and a formula that addresses both immediate (Hormuz) and longer-term (nuclear) concerns.

The question is whether this is a serious opening or a tactical maneuver. Two readings:

1. **Serious opening:** Pezeshkian and Araghchi are under pressure to deliver something before the U.S. midterms. The nuclear inspection language and the 7-day timeline suggest they want a quick win that relieves economic pressure while keeping the hardliners at bay.

2. **Tactical play:** The proposal closely mirrors the June MoU that collapsed within weeks. Iran is asking for the same preconditions (blockade lifted, sanctions waived, Lebanon ceasefire) that Washington has already rejected. Tehran may be establishing a diplomatic paper trail to justify resumption of fire after Rezaei's deadline.

The market is reading it as a real opportunity — oil fell 2-2.5% today. But an analyst at Briefing.com said "It is a dubious offer considering Iran hasn't changed its conditions from before. We'll see how long the glow of Iran's offer lasts."

The Trump-Xi summit produced no visible deliverables on Iran despite confirmed discussions. China remains the underexplored variable; Beijing has leverage with Tehran but has not publicly deployed it.

The Bab al-Mandab situation is deteriorating. War-risk premiums for Saudi-linked tankers calling at the Red Sea port of Yanbu have tripled to around 3% of a vessel's value from less than 1% in early July, making the kingdom's workaround nearly as expensive as the route it was supposed to replace. The fighting for control of the strait is now the deadliest since the Yemen civil war resumed.
