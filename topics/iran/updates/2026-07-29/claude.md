## Key Developments Since Prior Report (July 28)

### 1. Iran Rejects Oman's 50-50 Hormuz Proposal — Major Setback

**[OFFICIAL]** Iran says it is negotiating with Oman over an interim shipping arrangement for the Strait of Hormuz but insists it must exercise effective oversight over key parts of both shipping lanes. Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi told Iranian state television Tuesday Oman had proposed a 50-50 arrangement under which the inbound route would be on one country's side and the outbound route on the other's. Tehran rejected that formula, he said, because it would not adequately address Iran's national security concerns.

**[OFFICIAL]** Instead, Gharibabadi said Tehran proposed placing one shipping lane entirely within Iranian territorial waters and part of the other within Iranian waters, citing national security concerns. "Our proposal is for one route to lie entirely within the territorial waters of the Islamic Republic of Iran, with part of the other route also passing through Iranian territorial waters, so that Iran can effectively exercise oversight over both inbound" lanes.

**[VERIFIED]** Tehran has ruled out Oman's proposal for regional joint management of the Strait of Hormuz, a senior Iranian official told Reuters on Wednesday, scuppering hopes for a resolution to the impasse that has choked off Gulf trade for months.

**[REPORTED]** He added that Tehran rejected Oman's 50-50 proposal and warned that if Muscat does not accept Iran's counteroffer, the Strait of Hormuz will remain closed. He also said Iran is prepared for the possibility of renewed conflict.

*Assessment: This is a significant setback. Iran's rejection of the "voluntary fees" and 50-50 arrangement — the most concrete proposal yet — and its counteroffer demanding control of both shipping lanes indicates Tehran is unwilling to compromise on Hormuz sovereignty. The diplomatic window that appeared to be opening is now narrowing.*

### 2. IRGC Intercepts 3 Oil Tankers — First Strikes Since Pause Began

**[OFFICIAL]** Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) Navy said early Wednesday that it "targeted and stopped" three oil tankers in the Strait of Hormuz after the vessels ignored its warnings. In a statement, the IRGC said the vessels were intercepted "a few hours ago" but did not provide details on the tankers' identities, ownership, locations or the nature of the alleged violations. "The IRGC Navy once again warns that the illegal interventions and orders of the US, a child-killing army, will not go unanswered by vessels in the region," it said.

**[VERIFIED]** The competing Iranian enforcement regime was more active: the IRGC turned back six vessels attempting transit with warning shots in the 24 hours to 26 July, and Tasnim reported on 29 July that the IRGC "struck and stopped" three oil tankers that it said "continued to move along an unsafe and illegal route, ignoring our warnings" -- the first claimed Iranian strikes on Hormuz shipping since the mutual pause began.

**[REPORTED]** No casualties from the tanker strikes were reported through the cutoff, and no independent confirmation of damage was available.

*Assessment: This is the first Iranian action against shipping since the strike pause began. It signals Iran is reasserting control over Hormuz regardless of diplomatic talks. Combined with rejection of Oman's proposal, this represents a hardening of Iran's position.*

### 3. Iran Launches "Surprise Attack" on US Forces; US-Saudi Joint Strikes on Iraqi Militias

**[VERIFIED]** The US military intercepted ballistic missiles fired by Iran in an attempted "surprise attack" on US forces in the Middle East, US Central Command said Tuesday. Oil prices jumped on the news. The attack came roughly a day after President Donald Trump said the US had halted strikes on Iran.

**[OFFICIAL]** US Central Command said the US military and Saudi Arabian forces conducted joint strikes "against Iran-aligned terrorists" in Iraq. Saudi Arabia's involvement is notable as the kingdom has largely resisted being pulled fully into the conflict.

**[OFFICIAL]** An Iran-aligned Iraqi militia said several of its bases across Iraq were targeted by US and Saudi airstrikes early Wednesday. "A number of" people were killed and wounded in the strikes, which also damaged several buildings, according to preliminary information, the Popular Mobilization Forces said in a statement. "We consider this targeting to be a highly dangerous escalation, a violation of Iraq's sovereignty, and an attack on its official security institutions," the PMF said.

*Assessment: Despite the "pause" in direct US-Iran strikes, both sides conducted military actions. Iran's missile attack — even if intercepted — and the US-Saudi joint strikes against Iraqi militias show the conflict is not de-escalating. Saudi Arabia's direct participation is a notable escalation.*

### 4. Trump-Netanyahu Meeting: Positive Rhetoric, Substance Unclear

**[OFFICIAL]** Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu visited the White House Tuesday for his first in-person talks with US President Donald Trump since the Iran war began in February, and later described their meeting as "excellent" and "one of the best we've ever had." The White House succinctly called the talks productive and positive.

**[VERIFIED]** The meeting took place behind closed doors in the Oval Office, with reporters not invited in and without a press conference afterwards. Netanyahu entered the White House through a side entrance that was only partially visible to reporters, and left without fanfare some 90 minutes later.

**[OFFICIAL]** Speaking on Instagram, the prime minister said they enjoyed "an understanding of the common goal of ensuring that Iran does not have nuclear weapons, and other goals as well."

**[VERIFIED]** The most important matters "coordinated" between Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and US President Donald Trump were not disclosed publicly after their 90-minute meeting ended. Neither the official briefing for the Israeli media nor the hints provided to the American press revealed the substance of the understandings. The outcome of the meeting will instead become apparent in the United States' next steps against Iran's ayatollah regime.

**[REPORTED]** Despite reports ahead of the meeting, Israeli officials said Netanyahu did not present intelligence intended to draw the US into any particular military strike. They described the atmosphere as relaxed and friendly.

**[OFFICIAL]** Asked on Fox News about Israeli intelligence concerning renewed Iranian activity at an underground nuclear facility called Pickaxe Mountain, Trump brushed aside Netanyahu's planned briefing. "I don't need Bibi to tell me that," Trump said.

*Assessment: The meeting produced positive rhetoric but no announced policy changes. The lack of a press conference and Netanyahu's low-profile entry/exit suggest both sides wanted to avoid public scrutiny. Trump's dismissive comment about Netanyahu's intelligence briefing indicates tension beneath the surface.*

### 6. Houthi-Saudi Conflict Continues

**[VERIFIED]** The missile strikes followed Houthi attacks on two Saudi tankers in the Red Sea after the group announced a blockade of Saudi shipping transiting the Bab al-Mandeb chokepoint. The Houthis claimed a further attack on a Saudi vessel Monday.

**[VERIFIED]** For the Saudis, who have re-routed much of their oil exports through Red Sea ports such as Yanbu as Hormuz remains paralyzed, the blockade represents a major escalation. They are planning a further expansion of oil exports via the Red Sea – to about 9 million barrels a day over the next three years.

**[VERIFIED]** Saudi Arabia spent seven years trying and failing to dislodge the Houthis from northern Yemen. But for now, the Saudi response is more likely to comprise air strikes. On Friday, the Saudi air force targeted the port at Hodeidah, the largest under Houthi control, in retaliation for the tanker attacks.

*Assessment: The Houthi-Saudi front is escalating in parallel with the main US-Iran conflict. Saudi Arabia now faces dual chokepoint exposure — Hormuz closed by Iran, Bab al-Mandeb threatened by Houthis.*
