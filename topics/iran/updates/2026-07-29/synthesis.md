## Critical Developments (July 28-29)

### 1. CRITICAL: Strike Pause Shatters — Iran Launches "Surprise Attack" on Jordan [VERIFIED]

Iran launched multiple ballistic missiles toward a US military base in Jordan on Tuesday in what US Central Command described as an "attempted surprise attack." All of the missiles were intercepted, CENTCOM said.

This marks Iran's first ballistic missile attack against a US base in the region since President Trump paused strikes against Iran on Friday to give diplomacy another chance. The attack threatens to unravel the fragile pause and could force Trump to decide whether to resume military action.

Jordan's military said it intercepted and brought down five missiles launched from Iran early Wednesday.

The IRGC "struck and stopped" three oil tankers that it said "continued to move along an unsafe and illegal route, ignoring our warnings" — the first claimed Iranian strikes on Hormuz shipping since the mutual pause began. No casualties from the tanker strikes were reported through the cutoff, and no independent confirmation of damage was available.

*Assessment: [VERIFIED] Iran conducted two separate escalatory actions within hours: ballistic missiles targeting a US base (all intercepted) and IRGC naval interceptions of three tankers. These actions effectively end the five-day strike pause and signal Iran is reasserting maximum pressure regardless of diplomacy.*

### 2. CRITICAL: Trump Vows Forceful Response — "Going to Get a Beating" [OFFICIAL]

President Trump is vowing a tough response after US troops in Jordan had to intercept a series of ballistic missiles from Iran. "We're going to beat the f***ing s*** out of them," Mr. Trump told Fox News. "We'll be hitting them hard. They're going to get a beating."

Trump said American forces had only minutes to respond and shoot down the incoming Iranian ballistic missiles. The president added that he reviewed video showing US personnel calling out coordinates in real time during the interceptions. US Central Command said all Iranian missiles were successfully intercepted.

Despite the latest escalation and strong Trump warning to Iran, he said talks with Iran would continue. "We're going to let them keep talking," Trump told Fox News.

*Assessment: [OFFICIAL] Trump's explicit threat of forceful retaliation while maintaining the diplomatic channel open creates a dual-track approach. However, the visceral language suggests imminent resumption of strikes on Iran.*

### 3. CRITICAL: US-Saudi Joint Strikes in Iraq — Saudi Arabia Enters War [VERIFIED]

Saudi Arabia carried out "limited, targeted strikes" with the US Wednesday against Iran-backed militias in Iraq that were linked to attacks on Saudi oil facilities. Earlier, the ministry said it had intercepted and destroyed several drones launched from Iraq targeting oil facilities in eastern Saudi Arabia.

This is the first time Riyadh has claimed responsibility for launching strikes inside Iraq since the start of the Iran-US war. Saudi Arabia has been repeatedly targeted by Iran throughout the war, but has largely refrained from a military response.

Iraq's Popular Mobilisation Forces (PMF) has said at least 20 of its members were killed and 32 wounded in joint Saudi-US strikes on its sites across the country. Saudi Arabia and US Central Command said early Wednesday that joint air strikes targeted Iran-backed militias in Iraq responsible for attacks on American troops and Saudi energy infrastructure. The strikes are the first major US military action in the Middle East since last Friday, when President Donald Trump suspended an intensive bombing campaign after 13 days.

Saudi Arabia justified the strikes citing drone attacks on its petroleum facilities, invoking Article 51 of the UN Charter's right to self-defence. Iraq's Popular Mobilisation Forces condemned the strikes as a "dangerous escalation" and a breach of the country's sovereignty.

Trump said the US-Saudi strikes on Iran-backed Shiite militias in Iraq were coordinated with the Iraqi government. He called the militias a "cancer on the world."

*Assessment: [VERIFIED] Saudi Arabia's direct participation in offensive strikes marks a major widening of the conflict. Riyadh has been pulled into active combat operations after months of trying to stay on the sidelines. The casualty toll (20+ killed, 32 wounded) is substantial.*

### 4. CRITICAL: Iran Rejects Oman's Hormuz Proposal — Demands Control of Both Lanes [OFFICIAL]

Tehran has rejected Oman's proposal for regional joint management of the Strait of Hormuz, dashing hopes for a swift diplomatic breakthrough in the war that has disrupted Gulf trade for months. Oman proposed a new regional deal aimed at resolving the conflict over the critical strait. However, Tehran ruled out Oman's proposal, saying it had no chance of success.

Iran's deputy foreign minister, Kazem Gharibabadi, stated that Tehran had rejected Oman's proposal to divide transit routes evenly between the two countries in the Strait of Hormuz. In an interview with Iran's state television, Gharibabadi noted that the proposal envisaged determining a route that would be 50 percent controlled by Iran and 50 percent by Oman.

"We stated that this does not address Iran's concerns," Gharibabadi said. According to the Deputy Foreign Minister, Tehran countered with a temporary proposal under which vessel traffic would follow a single route passing through Iranian territorial waters, with portions of both the inbound and outbound routes located within Iran's waters.

Gharibabadi added that Oman had also suggested allowing a third country to conduct mine-clearing operations in the southern portion of the Strait of Hormuz, a proposal that Tehran likewise rejected. He dismissed the possibility of negotiations with the United States, stating that over the past 15 days, the Islamic Republic had made no requests to engage in talks with Washington.

The US and Saudi Arabia are trying to pressure Oman to advance their "unrealistic plans" regarding the strategic strait, the Iranian official told Reuters.

*Assessment: [OFFICIAL] This is a critical diplomatic setback. Iran's rejection of the 50-50 proposal and insistence on controlling both shipping lanes represents a maximalist position that effectively forecloses near-term compromise. Tehran's counteroffer is unacceptable to the US and Gulf states.*

### 5. SIGNIFICANT: Trump-Netanyahu Meeting — Positive Spin, No Concrete Outcome [VERIFIED]

Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu visited the White House Tuesday for his first in-person talks with US President Donald Trump since the Iran war began in February, and later described their meeting as "excellent" and "one of the best we've ever had." The White House succinctly called the talks productive and positive. The meeting took place behind closed doors in the Oval Office, with reporters not invited in and without a press conference afterwards. Netanyahu entered the White House through a side entrance that was only partially visible to reporters, and left without fanfare some 90 minutes later.

The most important matters "coordinated" between Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and US President Donald Trump were not disclosed publicly after their 90-minute meeting ended. Neither the official briefing for the Israeli media nor the hints provided to the American press revealed the substance of the understandings. The outcome of the meeting will instead become apparent in the United States' next steps against Iran's ayatollah regime.

There were no final agreements, but the parties made progress during the meeting. "It was an extremely positive meeting," a senior Israeli source who was at the meeting said. "The prime minister and president held an excellent and comprehensive discussion on the key issues of the day, first and foremost Iran."

Asked on Fox News about Israeli intelligence concerning renewed Iranian activity at an underground nuclear facility called Pickaxe Mountain, Trump brushed aside Netanyahu's planned briefing. "I don't need Bibi to tell me that," Trump said. "Bibi is telling me that because he wants me to stay involved."

*Assessment: [VERIFIED] The meeting produced no announced policy changes and was deliberately low-profile. Trump's dismissive comment about Netanyahu's intelligence briefing on Pickaxe Mountain suggests tension beneath the positive rhetoric.*

### 6. SIGNIFICANT: Oil Rebounds as Escalation Resumes [VERIFIED]

At 5:05 a.m. Eastern Time today, the price of oil sits at $89.53 per barrel, using Brent as the benchmark. That's an increase of 45 cents since yesterday morning and roughly $16.84 more than at this time last year.

Oil surged as fresh fighting erupted in the Middle East, casting a pall over diplomatic efforts to end the war and threatening further disruptions to energy flows. Brent climbed more than 4% to near $88 a barrel, paring some of the 16% loss over the past three sessions that was its biggest such decline since 2020.

Brent crude oil fell to around $84 per barrel on Tuesday, touching its lowest level in more than a week, as renewed diplomatic efforts over the Strait of Hormuz improved the outlook for Middle East oil supplies.

*Assessment: [VERIFIED] Oil's trajectory tells the story: it fell to ~$84 on Tuesday on diplomatic optimism, then rebounded to ~$89.53 on Iran's actions. Markets are repricing escalation risk after briefly pricing in de-escalation.*

### 7. SIGNIFICANT: Houthi-Saudi Front Continues — Second Chokepoint Under Threat [VERIFIED]

Yemen's Houthis said yesterday that they had targeted another Saudi oil tanker with missiles. Saudi authorities have not confirmed the attack on the latest tanker, the NCC Ghazal, which is listed by a public maritime monitoring service as Saudi-flagged.

In a post on X, Yahya Saree, the Houthi military spokesman, said the group had "carried out a military operation targeting the Saudi oil tanker (NCC GHAZAL) for violating the maritime navigation ban" that the group had imposed on Saudi vessels. He added that the group had used several ballistic missiles and forced the vessel to retreat.

The Trump administration has been urging the United Kingdom and France to help lead a multinational naval mission to protect shipping through the Strait of Hormuz. European allies are reluctant to commit until a lasting ceasefire in the Iran conflict is in place.

*Assessment: [VERIFIED] Saudi Arabia now faces dual chokepoint exposure — Hormuz closed by Iran, Bab el-Mandeb threatened by Houthis. US efforts to build a multinational naval coalition are stalled by European reluctance.*
