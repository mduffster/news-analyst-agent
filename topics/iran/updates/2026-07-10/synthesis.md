### 1. MAJOR: First Strike-Free Night in 72 Hours — US Confirms Technical Talks Continue [VERIFIED]

Talks between the US and Iran over a permanent peace deal are continuing, according to an American official, despite two days of clashes that threatened to shatter an already fragile ceasefire. The US is still committed to finding a diplomatic solution with Iran, the official said Thursday, declining to be named discussing sensitive matters. The official described the ongoing discussions as technical talks.

A United States official has told Al Jazeera that despite two days of launching attacks on Iran this week, Washington remains committed to negotiations with Tehran and that technical talks for a lasting peace deal will continue.

**Strike-pause-talk pattern confirmed:**
The US has been striking and then pausing to avoid escalation as negotiators meet behind the scenes, a US official said.

**MOU status — Schrodinger's ceasefire:**
While neither side has officially called off talks or declared the MoU at an end, each has accused the other of violating it.

Amid the new violence, US president Donald Trump on Wednesday suggested the ceasefire was "over," but also said he would not prevent negotiations from going ahead.

**Qatari mediation active:**
Qatar is also reportedly taking steps with Iran to make sure talks remain ongoing. Two sources familiar have told me there is a Qatari delegation on the ground in Iran today trying to create the conditions for negotiations to continue. A lot of diplomacy moving over the last 24 hours after a tense two days of strikes exchanged.

**Assessment:** [VERIFIED] The overnight pause and US confirmation of continuing technical talks represent a meaningful de-escalation signal. The explicit statement that strikes were "foregone in favor of diplomacy" indicates deliberate US calibration. However, the MOU's status remains ambiguous — Trump says it's "over" while officials confirm talks continue. This pattern mirrors earlier cycles where public rhetoric diverged from back-channel reality.

### 2. MAJOR: Mojtaba Khamenei Memorial Announced — First Appearance Possible? [REPORTED]

Iran's Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei will hold a memorial ceremony for his predecessor and father Ali Khamenei on Friday, his office said, sparking speculation that the Islamic Republic's top official could make his first public appearance since the war. A three-day service will begin this evening at the Imam Reza shrine in the northeastern city of Mashhad.

The statement announcing the event did not specify whether Mojtaba Khamenei would attend or the nature of his role in it.

**Burial confirmed:**
Iran's former Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei was finally laid to rest early Friday at the Imam Reza shrine in Mashhad, Iran, according to a statement posted by his official office.

**Context — seven days of absence:**
Iranian officials told The New York Times that Iranian Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei was barred from attending the funeral due to Iranian fears of being assassinated or tracked down to wherever he may be hiding by Israel.

He has remained in hiding since the war began in late February, communicating with his supporters only through written statements shared by Iranian media and attributed to him.

**Assessment:** [REPORTED] The three-day memorial creates the first real opportunity for Mojtaba Khamenei to appear publicly since the war began. His presence would be an extremely significant signal of recovered capacity; continued absence would further confirm IRGC dominance over Iranian decision-making. Security concerns remain acute — the memorial being hosted at the same shrine where his father was just buried creates obvious targeting concerns.

### 3. SIGNIFICANT: Israel Shares Assassination Plot Intelligence — US Officials Skeptical [REPORTED]

Israel shared intelligence with the United States that Iran had recently devised a new plan to assassinate President Donald Trump, two sources familiar with the matter told CNN, adding another layer of tension as a ceasefire deal between the countries comes under strain. One of the sources said the warning came this week. Another source said the US had picked up a steady drumbeat of intelligence in recent weeks about possible plans to assassinate Trump, but the warning from Israel was new and concerned a specific plot. Other American officials suggested the Israeli report could be an effort to sway Trump's decision-making as he weighs whether to intensify American military action against Iran.

**US intelligence skepticism:**
But that source said that the Israeli report is viewed — in part — as piece of a broader Israeli effort to influence Trump's decision making on Iran. Some in the intelligence community are always skeptical of Israeli reporting, the source said.

However, two US officials told Channel 12 news that the information related to general dialogue between Iranian officials on potentially assassinating the US president, rather than a specific plot. The officials said that they believed Israel gave the information to the US to improve ties between Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and Trump and to try and influence Washington's policies on Iran.

**Trump's response:**
"They want to take out the US leader — me," Trump told reporters Wednesday. "I'm on whatever list. I saw this morning I'm on every single one of their lists. And so far, I guess I've been a bit lucky, but maybe that doesn't last very long."

**Netanyahu-Trump coordination:**
Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has voiced deep doubts toward Trump's efforts at diplomacy with Iran, and he has clashed with Trump over Israeli military action in Lebanon, which had complicated the talks. The two men spoke by phone Thursday, and Netanyahu is expected to visit Washington soon for talks with the president.

**Assessment:** [REPORTED] The timing of this intelligence sharing — amid Trump's diplomatic outreach to Iran — and US officials' explicit skepticism about Israeli motives represents a significant dynamic. Netanyahu has consistently opposed Trump's diplomatic approach and prefers continued military action. This intelligence sharing, viewed by some US officials as an influence operation, could complicate the diplomatic track if it hardens Trump's position.

### 5. CRITICAL: Strait of Hormuz Traffic Collapsed to Near-Complete Standstill [VERIFIED]

At least 13 commercial vessels transited the Strait of Hormuz in the past 24 hours, according to MarineTraffic data. Traffic had started to increase during the ceasefire when US-Iran negotiations were ongoing. But dramatically dropped off again this week following strikes on several commercial vessels.

**Breakdown of traffic:**
Broken down, a total of seven ships entered the Persian Gulf over the past 24 hours — five cargo vessels and two tankers. An additional six ships left the gulf, consisting of four cargo vessels and two tankers.

Before the war, about 110 ships a day transited through the vital waterway. GPS interference has also made a comeback in recent days, with elevated levels of spoofing visible in vessel and flight data around the Strait of Hormuz.

Shipping in the Strait of Hormuz has plunged amid renewed fighting between the United States and Iran, dealing a new blow to energy markets already reeling from the biggest supply disruption in history.
