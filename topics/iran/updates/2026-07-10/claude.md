## Daily Update: July 10, 2026

### Summary

**NO MAJOR NEW STRIKES OVERNIGHT — FIRST PAUSE IN 72 HOURS: Following two consecutive days of US-Iran strikes (~170 total US strikes), overnight July 9-10 saw no confirmed new attacks — the first pause since the escalation began Tuesday. Mojtaba Khamenei's office announced a three-day memorial ceremony beginning Friday evening, sparking speculation he could make his first public appearance. Israel shared intelligence with the US this week about an alleged new Iranian assassination plot against Trump; some US officials suspect it may be an Israeli effort to influence Trump's decision-making. Despite Trump declaring the MOU "over," technical talks continue, with a US official confirming Washington "remains committed to negotiations." Strait of Hormuz traffic collapsed to just 13 large vessel transits in 24 hours. Oil retreated sharply to ~$76-77 from yesterday's $79+ peak, suggesting markets are cautiously pricing in diplomatic efforts continuing.**

### Key Developments Since Yesterday

#### 1. First Strike-Free Night in 72 Hours — Diplomatic Window Opening?

**[VERIFIED]** On Thursday, another US official said diplomatic efforts with Iran are still underway behind the scenes. Multiple officials said there had been preparations for potential strikes if needed Thursday night, but they were foregoing them in favor of diplomacy.

**[VERIFIED]** A United States official has told Al Jazeera that despite two days of launching attacks on Iran this week, Washington remains committed to negotiations with Tehran and that technical talks for a lasting peace deal will continue.

**[OFFICIAL]** The US is still committed to finding a diplomatic solution with Iran, the official said Thursday, declining to be named discussing sensitive matters. The official described the ongoing discussions as technical talks.

**[VERIFIED]** The US has been striking and then pausing to avoid escalation as negotiators meet behind the scenes, a US official said.

*Assessment: The absence of overnight strikes combined with US officials confirming "technical talks continue" suggests a deliberate de-escalation attempt. This pattern — striking then pausing for diplomacy — mirrors the June escalation cycle.*

#### 2. Mojtaba Khamenei Memorial Announced — First Appearance Possible?

**[REPORTED]** Iran's Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei will hold a memorial ceremony for his predecessor and father Ali Khamenei on Friday, his office said, sparking speculation that the Islamic Republic's top official could make his first public appearance since the war. A three-day service will begin this evening at the Imam Reza shrine in the northeastern city of Mashhad.

**[VERIFIED]** The statement announcing the event did not specify whether Mojtaba Khamenei would attend or the nature of his role in it.

**[VERIFIED]** Iran's former Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei was finally laid to rest early Friday at the Imam Reza shrine in Mashhad, Iran, according to a statement posted by his official office.

*Assessment: This three-day memorial creates a potential opportunity for Mojtaba's first public appearance. However, security concerns and his confirmed injuries make an appearance uncertain. If he does appear, it would be an extremely significant signal of recovered capacity.*

#### 3. Israel Shares Assassination Plot Intelligence — US Officials Skeptical

**[REPORTED]** Israel shared intelligence with the United States that Iran had recently devised a new plan to assassinate President Donald Trump, two sources familiar with the matter told CNN. One of the sources said the warning came this week.

**[REPORTED]** Another source said the US had picked up a steady drumbeat of intelligence in recent weeks about possible plans to assassinate Trump, but the warning from Israel was new and concerned a specific plot. Other American officials suggested the Israeli report could be an effort to sway Trump's decision-making as he weighs whether to intensify American military action against Iran.

**[REPORTED]** However, two US officials told Channel 12 news that the information related to general dialogue between Iranian officials on potentially assassinating the US president, rather than a specific plot. The officials said that they believed Israel gave the information to the US to improve ties between Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and Trump and to try and influence Washington's policies on Iran.

**[OFFICIAL]** "I'm No. 1 on the kill list for Iran," Trump told reporters at the NATO summit. "I'm on every list. I saw this morning, I'm on every single one of their lists. And so far, I guess I've been a little bit lucky, but that maybe doesn't last very long," he said.

**[VERIFIED]** Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has voiced deep doubts toward Trump's efforts at diplomacy with Iran, and he has clashed with Trump over Israeli military action in Lebanon. The two men spoke by phone Thursday, and Netanyahu is expected to visit Washington soon for talks with the president.

*Assessment: The timing of this intelligence sharing — amid Trump's diplomatic outreach to Iran — and US officials' skepticism about Israeli motives suggests this may be an attempt to derail negotiations. Netanyahu has publicly opposed Trump's diplomatic approach and prefers continued military action.*

#### 4. Strait of Hormuz Traffic Collapses to Near-Complete Standstill

**[VERIFIED]** At least 13 commercial vessels transited the Strait of Hormuz in the past 24 hours, according to MarineTraffic data. Traffic had started to increase during the ceasefire when US-Iran negotiations were ongoing. But dramatically dropped off again this week following strikes on several commercial vessels.

**[VERIFIED]** Shipping in the Strait of Hormuz has plunged amid renewed fighting between the United States and Iran, dealing a new blow to energy markets already reeling from the biggest supply disruption in history. No large vessel has crossed the strait via the US-coordinated route while broadcasting their location since Tuesday, with traceable crossings via the Oman-hugging lane "effectively grinding to a halt", Lloyd's List Intelligence said on Thursday.

**[VERIFIED]** Effectively no, it is closed to commercial shipping as of 10 July 2026. 34 ships transited vs ~88/day normal.

**[VERIFIED]** Before the war, about 110 ships a day transited through the vital waterway. GPS interference has also made a comeback in recent days, with elevated levels of spoofing visible in vessel and flight data around the Strait of Hormuz.

*Assessment: Traffic has collapsed from yesterday's 34 transits to just 13 large vessels in the past 24 hours — the lowest since the MOU was signed. The "Southern Highway" (US-coordinated Omani route) is now completely dark.*

#### 5. Oil Retreats Sharply — Markets Pricing in Continued Diplomacy

**[VERIFIED]** At 6:15 a.m. Eastern Time today, oil was priced at $76.80 per barrel with Brent serving as the benchmark. That's a drop of $2.45 compared with yesterday morning.

**[VERIFIED]** Oil steadied at the end of a bumpy week, as talks between the US and Iran continued despite a flare-up in fighting. Brent traded near $76 a barrel after losing more than 2% on Thursday, while West Texas Intermediate was below $72.

**[VERIFIED]** Despite the latest turmoil in the region and the Strait of Hormuz, oil prices largely held steady on Friday. Brent crude stood at $76.58 per barrel as of 05:00 GMT, practically unchanged from its settlement price on Thursday and down about 2 percent from Wednesday.

**[REPORTED]** While the relative price stability reflects "confidence on the part of the market that the situation will stabilise" in the Middle East, the latest hostilities will exacerbate the upward pressure expected to build as oil inventories dwindle in the coming weeks, said Bart Melek, global head of commodity strategy at TD Securities.

*Assessment: Oil's sharp retreat from $79+ to ~$76-77 despite near-standstill Strait traffic suggests markets believe diplomacy will prevent full-scale war resumption. This is a significant shift from Tuesday's panic buying.*

#### 6. Iran Signals Defiance — Diplomatic Track Continues Through Mediators

**[VERIFIED]** Iran has indicated it is not willing to negotiate while US attacks are ongoing. The country's political leaders condemned the new wave of attacks this week and denounced the US strikes as a "war crime". They accused Washington of violating the MoU, which stipulates a ceasefire on all fronts. Tehran has also launched a formal complaint against the US with the United Nations Security Council and the UN Secretary-General.

**[VERIFIED]** While neither side has officially called off talks or declared the MoU at an end, each has accused the other of violating it.

**[VERIFIED]** Despite Iran's violations of the memorandum of understanding, "the United States is still committed to finding a resolution, and technical talks continue."
