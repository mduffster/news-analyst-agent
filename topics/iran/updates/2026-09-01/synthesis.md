## Critical Developments (August 31 – September 1)

### 1. US Launches Second Round of Strikes — Expanded Target Set [OFFICIAL]

The U.S. military says it's striking targets in Iran as hostilities flare again after a month without military action. U.S. Central Command said Tuesday that the strikes "follow recent attempted attacks by the IRGC against commercial shipping in the Strait of Hormuz and against American service members deployed to the region."

US forces began striking Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps targets in Iran at noon Tuesday, CENTCOM said, adding that the action followed recent attempted IRGC attacks on commercial shipping in the Strait of Hormuz and on American service members in the region.

Target locations included:
- **Bandar Abbas** — southern Iran's major port city
- **Chabahar** — southeastern Iran
- **Konarak** — southeastern Iran

**Assessment:** [OFFICIAL] This is the second US strike round within 48 hours, following Sunday's Larak Island attack. The expanded target set — hitting multiple cities including Bandar Abbas, Chabahar, and Konarak — represents a broader geographic footprint than the initial preemptive strike on mine launchers. CENTCOM's justification cites both shipping protection AND force protection ("American service members"), broadening the operational mandate.

### 2. Iran's Retaliation — Jordan Intercepts, UAE Denies Base Hit [OFFICIAL]

Tehran's powerful Revolutionary Guards said they struck two US military air bases with ballistic missiles in Washington-allied Jordan, causing "heavy damage." Jordan's army said it had intercepted eight missiles, without stating their origin.

In a statement reported by Iranian news agencies on Sunday, the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) said the combined missile and drone operation, which it called "Punishment of the Aggressor", hit the King Hussein and Al Azraq bases in Jordan.

Hours later, the IRGC said it had launched a combined ballistic missile and drone attack on two US air bases in Jordan, claiming "heavy damage" to technical and maintenance infrastructure and areas where fighter jets were stationed at Muwaffaq Salti Air Base in Azraq and King Hussein Air Base. There was no immediate independent confirmation of the damage claimed by Iran.

**Assessment:** [OFFICIAL] Iran's retaliation was geographically dispersed but largely intercepted. Jordan's interception of eight missiles and UAE's denial of base damage suggests Iranian strikes were either successfully defended against or calibrated for limited impact. This pattern — retaliation that demonstrates reach without US casualties — could enable more frequent tit-for-tat exchanges because both sides can claim resolve without major costs.

### 3. Trump's AI Kharg Island Video — Psychological Warfare [VERIFIED]

President Donald Trump published what appears to be an AI-generated video showing several explosions on Iran's Kharg Island, although Iranian sources did not indicate the island had been targeted by the U.S. military in renewed strikes over the weekend. "Kharg Island being blown to smithereens!!!" Trump said in a post to his Truth Social platform late Sunday.

Speaking to reporters at Joint Base Andrews on Monday, US Vice President JD Vance addressed questions regarding the video, stating that while Trump occasionally varies his approach on social media, he does not announce military decisions through those channels.

The National Iranian Oil Company denied any attack on Kharg, stating that operations at the island's oil facilities are continuing normally. The company's CEO characterized claims of Kharg's destruction as "false." Kharg Island serves as Iran's primary crude oil export terminal, handling approximately 90 percent of the country's oil exports before the outbreak of war.

**Assessment:** [VERIFIED] The AI video adds a psychological warfare dimension — suggesting future targeting of Iran's economic lifeline while not actually striking it. Vance's explanation that Trump "likes to switch it up on social media" provides deniability while maintaining implicit threat. The video's existence will factor into Iranian calculations about escalation risks.

"We know you admitted this during your 'top secret' emergency meeting yesterday," he added.

**Assessment:** [REPORTED] Iran is framing its retaliation as successful deterrence while the US has now expanded its strikes. The Iranian claim about a "top secret" US emergency meeting suggests intelligence claims about US decision-making, though unverified. Both sides are talking past each other on what deterrence means.

### 6. G20 Summit — Bessent Claims Multilateral Backing [OFFICIAL]

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent arrived at the Group of 20 finance ministers summit in Asheville, North Carolina, with a message that left little room for diplomatic ambiguity: Washington is prepared to inflict what he called "financial violence" to enforce compliance with its Iran sanctions regime, and any country — including China — that continues purchasing Iranian crude does so with full awareness that every option remains on the table.

The EU's posture at Asheville provided Bessent with a degree of multilateral cover he has been actively cultivating. European partners made known, Bessent said, "in the strongest terms" their support for Operation Economic Outcast — a level of alignment that, if reflected in the final communiqué expected when the summit concludes Tuesday, would represent a meaningful shift from Europe's historically cautious approach to extraterritorial U.S. sanctions.

The US Treasury Secretary intends to use bilateral meetings to build support for squeezing Tehran, and stated that Washington will sanction another bank this week, though he declined to name it.

**On China:** Bessent told the AP that "all options are on the table" in terms of sanctioning Beijing for its continued purchases. But he rejected the idea that the administration was reluctant to confront China, calling it "a completely false narrative." He insisted that China and the U.S. agree on the need to reopen the Strait of Hormuz and prevent Iran from developing a nuclear weapon.

Russia's finance ministry said Finance Minister Anton Siluanov met with Bessent on Monday on the sidelines of the meeting. Asked why the Russian official had been invited to the meeting despite invading Ukraine more than four years ago, President Donald Trump said, "We like getting along with everybody."

**Assessment:** [OFFICIAL] Bessent's G20 diplomacy is proceeding in parallel with military escalation. EU support claim, if substantive, represents meaningful coalition building. The Bessent-Siluanov meeting signals Trump's broader diplomatic engagement with Russia. The Xi summit on September 24 remains protected — Bessent's "all options" rhetoric on China is hedged.

### 7. Mine Warfare — Disputed Claims Continue [DISPUTED]

US Central Command (Centcom) has denied claims by Iran that a supertanker transiting the Strait of Hormuz was brought to a halt after hitting two mines. "No ships have hit mines in the Strait of Hormuz. This is yet another IRGC attempt to intimidate regional commercial shipping through disinformation," Centcom says in a post on X.

However, a supertanker caught fire in the strait after hitting two naval mines, underscoring the persistent risks facing Hormuz shipping. (Note: This claim from Trading Economics reflects Iranian media reports; CENTCOM denies.)

The latest Joint Maritime Information Center advisory, issued through the United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations office on Aug. 25, keeps the Strait of Hormuz at a "SEVERE" threat level and says deliberate hostile action remains highly likely. The advisory says mine-danger areas remain active and warns of a continued risk from drifting or uncharted mines in and near the Traffic Separation Scheme.

**Assessment:** [DISPUTED] Iran and the US directly contradict each other on mine incidents. The IRGC uses claimed mine strikes as warnings to commercial vessels, while CENTCOM characterizes this as "disinformation." The UK's "SEVERE" threat level remaining in place despite CENTCOM's clearance announcement suggests international shipping authorities remain cautious.
