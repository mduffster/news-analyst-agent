## Key Developments Since Prior Report (August 31)

### 1. US Launches Second Round of Strikes on Iran — MAJOR ESCALATION

**[OFFICIAL]** The US military carried out additional strikes against Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps targets in Iran on Tuesday. "Today at 12 p.m. ET, U.S. forces began striking Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) targets in Iran," CENTCOM wrote in a post on X. "The strikes follow recent attempted attacks by the IRGC against commercial shipping in the Strait of Hormuz and against American service members deployed to the region."

**[REPORTED]** The US launched a fresh wave of strikes against targets in Iran, extending a back-and-forth of fighting with neither side prepared to cede control over the Strait of Hormuz. US Central Command said it began strikes on Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps targets following Iran's attacks on commercial shipping and American forces in the region. Explosions were heard in Bandar Abbas and Chabahar in southern Iran, state-run Nour news reported Tuesday.

**[REPORTED]** Unconfirmed reports placed explosions near Qeshm Airport, while blasts were heard in parts of Bandar Abbas and Sirik. Four projectiles struck areas in the southeastern Iranian cities of Chabahar and Konarak on Tuesday, state-run IRNA news agency reported. Iranian state media reported explosions in Bandar Abbas, Chabahar, Konarak, Qeshm, and Sirik.

**[OFFICIAL]** The United States carried out "precise defensive strikes" on sites in Iran's southern port city of Bandar Abbas, Al Arabiya reported citing a US official.

*Assessment: This is the second round of US strikes within 48 hours, following Sunday's Larak Island attack. The expanded target set — hitting Bandar Abbas, Chabahar, Konarak, Qeshm, and potentially Sirik — represents a broader response than the initial preemptive strike. The month-long military pause is decisively over, with both sides now in active combat.*

### 2. Iran Response — Claiming Deterrence Success

**[OFFICIAL]** Iran's response to the US strike on Larak Island on Sunday marked an evolution in Tehran's deterrence doctrine, a senior lawmaker said on Tuesday, after Iranian attacks targeting US forces in Jordan and the UAE. "The message is clear: the might of its armed forces will ensure enemy regret," Ebrahim Azizi, chairman of the Iranian parliament's National Security and Foreign Policy Committee, said on X. "We know you admitted this during your 'top secret' emergency meeting yesterday," he added.

**[VERIFIED]** Iran issued joint statements warning: "As we have demonstrated in practice so far, while respecting the national sovereignty of our neighbors, we will never tolerate any form of aggression and will deliver a much heavier response. We warn you: you have repeatedly tested the strong resolve of our armed forces and our courageous and resilient people. Do not test it again." Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi reiterated that the only way for the current situation to be resolved is for the U.S. to live up to its commitments under the Islamabad Memorandum of Understanding, signed by both nations in June.

*Assessment: Iran is framing its retaliation as successful deterrence while demanding US compliance with the June MOU. The Iranian claim about a "top secret" US meeting suggests intelligence gathering on US decision-making, though this is unverified.*

### 3. Trump Threatens Further Retaliation — Escalatory Rhetoric

**[OFFICIAL]** President Donald Trump acknowledged Monday that more strikes might be necessary.

**[VERIFIED]** The earlier exchange of strikes sent global oil prices almost 3% higher and drove US stock indexes lower Monday. The attacks occurred days after Trump declared the Strait of Hormuz free from mines and marks a shift from Washington's recent focus on maximizing economic pressure on Iran rather than using military might.

*Assessment: Tuesday's strikes confirm Trump's warning. The administration has pivoted from the economic pressure campaign back to kinetic operations, abandoning the month-long pause. This represents a significant strategic shift.*

---

### 4. Tanker Mine Incident — IRGC Claims Vessel Struck Mines

**[REPORTED]** The Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) Navy claimed on Monday morning, August 31, that what it termed a "violating supertanker" suffered "severe fires" and came to a complete halt after striking two naval mines while transiting the southern lane of the Strait of Hormuz. The IRGC statement did not specify what violation the tanker had allegedly committed while passing through the Strait of Hormuz, an international waterway. The name of the vessel and its flag state were also omitted from the official announcement.

**[VERIFIED]** Iranian media also reported that a tanker had struck two mines and that authorities seized a bulk carrier near Bandar Abbas, although US Central Command said no vessels had hit mines in Hormuz.

*Assessment: Iran and the US directly contradict each other on this incident. The IRGC is using the claimed mine strike as a warning to commercial vessels, while CENTCOM denies any mine strike occurred. This information warfare complicates understanding of actual conditions in the strait.*

### 5. Oil Prices Continue Rising — $94+ and Climbing

**[VERIFIED]** As of 8 a.m. Eastern Time today, oil is trading at $94.11 per barrel, based on the Brent benchmark. That's $1.08 above yesterday morning's level and about $25.40 higher than where it stood a year ago.

**[VERIFIED]** Brent rose to 91.28 USD/Bbl on September 1, 2026, up 0.87% from the previous day. Over the past month, Brent's price has risen 8.96%, and is up 32.02% compared to the same time last year.

**[VERIFIED]** The developments have heightened fears that further military escalation could disrupt shipping and tighten global energy supplies. Brent is now around 3% higher in August, following a 20.5% surge in July.

*Assessment: Oil is now firmly above $94 and approaching $95, up from ~$89 at the beginning of the weekend. The escalation has reversed weeks of gradual price decline. $100/barrel is increasingly plausible if hostilities continue.*

### 6. G20 Summit — Bessent Claims EU Support, Meets with Russia

**[OFFICIAL]** Bessent said the European Union had backed his sanctions efforts with "fulsome support." On Sunday, the EU said in a statement it welcomes added economic pressure on Iran and will work with the U.S. and other partners to keep the squeeze on Iran.

**[OFFICIAL]** The summit opened with Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent claiming European Union backing for Washington's Iran sanctions campaign and disclosing direct talks with Russia's finance minister on Trump's Ukraine peace plan. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent opened the Group of 20 finance ministers and central bank governors summit in Asheville, North Carolina, on Monday by claiming broad European backing for Washington's Iran sanctions campaign. Bessent told reporters upon arriving at the summit that the European Union had made known "in the strongest terms" its support for Operation Economic Outcast.

**[VERIFIED]** Russia's finance ministry said Finance Minister Anton Siluanov met with Bessent on Monday on the sidelines of the meeting. Asked why the Russian official had been invited back to the G20 despite the ongoing war in Ukraine — and criticism from Europeans — President Donald Trump said, "We like getting along with everybody."

**[OFFICIAL]** Bessent has said publicly, at least 38 times by various counts, that a deal is close. He said Monday that the Iranian economy does not need to collapse for the strategy to work — that the goal is a change in Tehran's calculus, which he believes could come within weeks or months.

**[OFFICIAL]** Both meetings serve as groundwork for the leaders' summit at Trump National Doral in Miami on 14 and 15 December, and come weeks before Xi Jinping is expected in Washington on 24 September. The US Treasury Secretary intends to use bilateral meetings to build support for squeezing Tehran, and stated that Washington will sanction another bank this week, though he declined to name it.

*Assessment: Bessent is successfully building a diplomatic narrative of multilateral support for Iran sanctions at the G20. The EU backing, if genuine, represents a significant alignment. The Bessent-Siluanov meeting is a notable signal of Trump's broader Russia engagement. The Xi summit on Sept 24 remains protected — no Chinese banks targeted.*

### 7. Diplomatic Channels — Qatari Mediation Efforts Continuing

**[VERIFIED]** Qatar's PM visit to Tehran follows a week of intense diplomacy in Iran involving separate meetings with Oman's Foreign Minister Badr Albusaidi and senior Pakistani officials seeking ways to end the US-Israel war on Iran and reopen the strategic waterway through which about 20 percent of the world's oil passes in peacetime. Alongside mediator Pakistan, Qatar played a key role in securing an April ceasefire and the June memorandum of understanding between Iran and the US, but those deals have since collapsed.

**[REPORTED]** Qatar's latest diplomatic effort comes after recent visits to Tehran by Pakistan's Field Marshal Asim Munir and Omani Foreign Minister Badr Al-Busaidi, as regional efforts intensify to reopen a path towards negotiations between Washington and Tehran. The IRGC had said the two sides reached an understanding on sharing control of the strait and its revenues, but a senior Iranian source later said negotiations were still underway.

*Assessment: Qatar, Oman, and Pakistan continue active mediation despite renewed hostilities. However, the military escalation directly undermines these efforts. The claimed "understanding on sharing control" remains unverified and contradicted by ongoing combat.*
