### Critical New Developments (May 11-12)

#### Trump Administration Considers Resuming Combat Operations
**[REPORTED]** Some Trump aides said he is now more seriously considering a resumption of major combat operations in Iran, according to sources. President Donald Trump has grown increasingly frustrated with how the Iranians are handling negotiations to end the war.

**[REPORTED]** Trump has grown impatient with the continued closure of the Strait of Hormuz, as well as what he perceives as division in Iranian leadership that is preventing them from making substantial concessions on nuclear talks. The latest response from Iran, which Trump has deemed both "totally unacceptable" and "stupid," has led several officials to question whether Tehran is willing to take on a serious negotiating position.

**[REPORTED]** There are different camps within the administration that are recommending alternating paths for how to proceed. Some, including officials in the Pentagon, have argued for a more aggressive approach to pressuring the Iranians to the table — including targeted strikes that further weaken Tehran's position. Others, however, are still pushing to give diplomacy a fair shot.

**Assessment:** This is the most significant new development since yesterday. The shift from rhetoric ("massive life support") to active consideration of military options represents a meaningful escalation in administration posture. The internal debate between Pentagon hawks and diplomacy advocates will likely shape decisions in the coming days.

---

#### Iran Issues Counter-Threat
**[OFFICIAL]** Iran's top negotiator, Parliament Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf, said Monday that his country is "prepared for every option" as negotiations with the United States remain at an impasse. "Our armed forces are prepared to deliver a lesson-giving response to any aggression," Ghalibaf said on social media. "We are prepared for every option. They will be surprised." Later Monday, Ghalibaf said in another post that "there is no alternative but to accept the rights of the Iranian people as laid out in the 14-point proposal" submitted by Iran earlier this month.

**[REPORTED]** Iran's new supreme leader, Mojtaba Khamenei, who has not appeared publicly since the war began, also issued "new and decisive directives" for military operations, a state broadcaster reported, without elaborating.

**Assessment:** Iran is signaling it will not capitulate under pressure and is preparing for the possibility of resumed hostilities. The vague "new directives" from the supreme leader suggest internal preparations for escalation.

---

#### Qatar Publicly Criticizes Iran
**[OFFICIAL]** Qatar accuses Iran of "weaponising" the Strait of Hormuz and "blackmailing" Gulf states as energy supplies and other crucial goods remain locked in the region.

**[OFFICIAL]** Qatar's top diplomat and premier said Iran should not use the Strait of Hormuz as a means to blackmail Gulf states. "Iran should not use this strait as a weapon to pressure or to blackmail the Gulf countries," the Qatari minister said. Turkey's Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan also said the strait must not be "used as a weapon." Gulf countries have borne the brunt of Iran's attacks during the war, with Tehran targeting US assets but also civilian infrastructure, airports and energy facilities.

**Assessment:** Qatar's public criticism of Iran marks a significant shift. As a key mediator alongside Pakistan and Turkey, Qatar's frustration signals growing Gulf state impatience with Iran's Hormuz strategy and could complicate Tehran's regional positioning.

---

#### New US Sanctions on Iranian Nuclear Program
**[OFFICIAL]** On May 12, the US imposed new sanctions targeting Iranian nuclear research with possible military applications, followed days later by sanctions on individuals and entities in China and Iran over support for Iran's ballistic missile program.

**Assessment:** The sanctions signal the US is maintaining "maximum pressure" even as it publicly considers resuming combat operations. The timing — on the eve of Trump's China visit — may be intended to establish leverage.

#### Oil Prices Surge to $110+
**[VERIFIED]** As of 9 a.m. Eastern Time today, oil is trading at $110.43 per barrel, based on the Brent benchmark. That's $2.76 above yesterday morning's level and about $45 higher than where it stood a year ago.

**[VERIFIED]** Brent rose to 104.97 USD/Bbl on May 12, 2026, up 0.73% from the previous day. Over the past month, Brent's price has risen 5.65%, and is up 57.54% compared to the same time last year.

**[REPORTED]** Reports suggested President Trump is set to meet with his national security team to weigh a potential return to military operations, alongside renewed discussions about escorting commercial vessels through Hormuz.

**Assessment:** Oil prices have jumped ~$3/barrel since yesterday ($107.67 → $110.43), reflecting market alarm at the potential for resumed combat operations. The $110 threshold is psychologically significant.

#### Trump's China Visit Tomorrow
**[OFFICIAL]** "The most pressing agenda item is the Iran-Hormuz crisis," according to Nomura's Chief China Economist. Trump is due to arrive in Beijing on Wednesday evening. The following morning, he will participate in a welcome ceremony and hold a bilateral meeting with Xi, before they tour the historic Temple of Heaven.

**[REPORTED]** "I do think that this war will dominate the summit," said Lyle Goldstein, director of the China Initiative at Brown University. "Let's face it, it will push a lot of other things off the agenda." He could imagine Trump asking Xi to help pressure Iran to re-open the Strait of Hormuz and make a deal to end the war.

**[REPORTED]** With the war dragging on far longer than the Trump administration's previous prediction of a four-to-six-week endeavor, some see China gaining a positional advantage. "It provides China a degree of leverage," one expert noted, citing that Beijing is Iran's largest trade partner and the top buyer of its oil.

**[REPORTED]** The source pointed out that it was "very likely" that Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi will be in Delhi for the BRICS Foreign Ministers' meeting on Thursday and Friday - the same days that Trump is in Beijing. Egypt and Saudi Arabia are among the countries facilitating backchannel dialogue between the US and Iran that is being mediated by Pakistan. "China is going to be an important player in ensuring that the dialogue continues."

**Assessment:** Trump's China visit (May 13-15) represents a critical diplomatic window. Both the Trump-Xi summit and the BRICS meeting (with Iran's foreign minister present) will occur simultaneously, creating potential for parallel diplomatic tracks or cross-talk. China may seek to extract concessions on Taiwan or trade in exchange for any assistance on Iran.

#### Lebanon: Violence Continues Ahead of Talks
**[VERIFIED]** Israel's military has killed six people in an air raid on a house in southern Lebanon, the latest violation of a United States-brokered truce that has only ever existed on paper. The Israeli attack on Monday night targeted a house in the Kfar Dounin municipality, some 100km (60 miles) south of Beirut. The air raid is the latest in a near-daily series of Israeli attacks despite an April 16 ceasefire, during which Hezbollah has also exchanged fire.

**[VERIFIED]** Israel's air force says it has targeted more than 1,100 sites in Lebanon since the so-called truce began. Lebanon's Ministry of Public Health reports that at least 380 people have been killed during the truce, bringing the total death toll since Israel's invasion and bombardment of Lebanon began.

**[VERIFIED]** Israel showed no signs of slowing its military operations on Tuesday, issuing a series of new forced displacement orders and threats of further attacks. The Israeli military's Arabic-language spokesman told residents to flee from the town of Sohmor in the Bekaa valley, and the towns of Arzoun, Tayr Debba, Bazouriyeh and al-Haush in southern Lebanon. The Israeli military also blew up a water pumping station in the town of Deir Mimas.

**[OFFICIAL]** Lebanese leaders have appealed to the US to press Israel to halt attacks before a third meeting between Lebanese and Israeli officials set to take place in Washington, DC later this week. Lebanese Prime Minister Nawaf Salam said he asked US Ambassador to Lebanon Michel Issa to "exert pressure on Israel to stop the ongoing attacks and violations, in order to consolidate the ceasefire". The upcoming Israel-Lebanon meeting in Washington will "essentially determine the next phase of this ceasefire, which is really hanging on in name only."

**[REPORTED]** Hezbollah said that if fighting continues, it threatens to turn Lebanon into "hell" for Israel. "We will respond to the aggression and violations, and we will not return to the pre-March 2nd status quo."

**[REPORTED]** Nearly 100,000 civilian buildings are destroyed or damaged, including 27,000 homes, 182 public buildings, 35 schools and nine historical landmarks, according to the Southern Council. More than 27,000 homes, 182 public buildings, 35 schools and nine historical landmarks have been completely flattened since March 2. In total, nearly 100,000 civilian buildings have been affected to some extent, either destroyed or partially damaged.

**Assessment:** The May 14-15 Lebanon-Israel talks face severe headwinds. With 380+ deaths during the "ceasefire" and massive infrastructure destruction, the gap between nominal diplomacy and ground reality has never been wider. Hezbollah's threat to make Lebanon "hell" for Israel signals no willingness to de-escalate.

#### First Qatari LNG Transit Since War Began
**[VERIFIED]** A Qatari liquefied natural gas tanker crossed the strait on Sunday for the first time since the war began, a passage that was reportedly approved by Iran to build confidence with Qatar and Pakistan, though the symbolic opening did little to ease broader market concerns.

**[REPORTED]** Pakistan held talks with Iran to allow a limited number of Qatari liquefied natural gas cargoes to transit the Strait of Hormuz, as Qatar sent through its first shipment since the war began. The Al Kharaitiyat, which loaded at the Ras Laffan export plant earlier this month, exited the strait and was in the Gulf of Oman on Sunday. The vessel appears to have navigated the Tehran-approved northern route that hugs the Iranian coast through the strait.

**Assessment:** The selective passage of a single LNG tanker demonstrates Iran's continued control over Hormuz traffic while offering a symbolic concession to mediators. This selective opening underscores that Iran is using Hormuz strategically rather than maintaining a blanket closure.
