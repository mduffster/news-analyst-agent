## Daily incremental update — US–Israel–Iran conflict  
**As of Tue, May 12, 2026 (ET)**  
**What changed since Mon, May 11, 2026 (ET)**

### 1) U.S. escalation planning becomes more explicit (Project Freedom restart + renewed strikes discussed)
- **Axios reports** Trump convened/engaged his national security team to discuss “the way forward,” with officials saying he’s **leaning toward some form of military action** to increase pressure; options discussed include **resuming “Project Freedom” ship-guidance/escort operations through Hormuz** (paused last week) and **resuming the bombing campaign** against remaining target sets. **[REPORTED]** ([axios.com](https://www.axios.com/2026/05/11/trump-iran-ceasefile-military-action))  
- The same Axios piece also claims Trump said Iran had earlier agreed (then “reversed course”) regarding **handing over its enriched uranium stockpile**, framing nuclear issues as the core sticking point. **[REPORTED]** ([axios.com](https://www.axios.com/2026/05/11/trump-iran-ceasefile-military-action))  

**What it means:** This is a meaningful shift from “public rejection” to **concrete menu-of-options signaling**. Restarting Project Freedom would be a **direct challenge to Iran’s enforcement posture in/near the Strait**, increasing near-term risk of ship/escort engagements. Renewed strikes (especially if tied to nuclear leverage) raise the odds of a **near-term spike** even if the broader war remains “air/sea-centric.”

---

### 2) Domestic U.S. constraint sharpens: munitions/stocks + war costs now a louder bipartisan issue
- Defense Secretary **Pete Hegseth faced bipartisan questioning** about the war’s **endgame, costs, and U.S. weapons stockpile drawdowns**, in hearings tied to a **$1.5T FY2027 defense budget request**. **[OFFICIAL]** ([apnews.com](https://apnews.com/article/7e9173700a2cf1ea8d5c4b1a85a6bce3?utm_source=openai))  

**What it means:** The political center of gravity is moving from “can we win/open Hormuz?” to “**how long can we sustain this tempo** and at what readiness cost?” That can push toward:
- **more coercive, time-bound escalation** (to force concessions faster), *or*
- **more deal-seeking** if stockpile/readiness concerns become binding.  
Today’s evidence mainly supports **pressure to define an endgame**, not necessarily immediate de-escalation.

---

### 3) Regional spillover signal: Lebanon track faces internal pushback as fighting continues despite ceasefire
- Hezbollah leader **Naim Kassem urged Lebanon’s government to withdraw from planned direct talks with Israel** and revert to indirect negotiations; AP also reports **continued strikes and casualties despite the April 17 ceasefire**, and reiterates the **two-day Washington talks are scheduled to start Thursday (May 14)**. **[VERIFIED]** ([apnews.com](https://apnews.com/article/462af0a3095db4b5a95f2898d1c5a3f4))  

**What it means:** The Lebanon compartment may be **harder to stabilize** right as the Iran track is hardening. Even if Washington wants the May 14–15 talks to “ringfence” Lebanon, Hezbollah’s stance increases the chance the talks **fail to launch cleanly**, become politically untenable in Beirut, or get undercut by fresh violence.

---

### 4) Gulf partner friction/expansion risk: Kuwait alleges an Iranian attack tied to a China-backed port project
- **Kuwait accused Iran** of a **failed attack earlier this month** on an island where **China is helping build a port**; Iran did not immediately acknowledge the allegation, per AP. **[OFFICIAL (Kuwaiti allegation) / REPORTED]** ([apnews.com](https://apnews.com/article/f3d5738853111cfc80985c157edab7c3?utm_source=openai))  

**What it means:** If substantiated, this widens the conflict’s political footprint: it pulls in **Gulf sovereignty + Chinese equities** (infrastructure investment) into the active dispute set, potentially complicating Trump’s China trip diplomacy and increasing pressure for **collective Gulf air/maritime defense measures**.
