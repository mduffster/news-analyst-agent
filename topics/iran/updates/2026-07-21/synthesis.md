## Critical Developments (July 21)

### 1. CRITICAL: Strait of Hormuz "Deserted" — Only 0-3 Vessels Transiting [VERIFIED]

The attack on the Kuwaiti tanker follows strikes in recent days on vessels owned by Dynacom Tankers Management Ltd. Hormuz was deserted on Tuesday with no ships observed transiting, data shows.

Lloyd's List Intelligence recorded just 53 vessel transits in the week through July 20, down 66% from 157 the previous week. Tanker and gas carrier movements, which are the ships responsible for transporting most Gulf crude oil and liquefied natural gas, dropped to 30 crossings from 90.

"The latest escalation shows how expectations of a rapid opening of the Strait were premature," said Saul Kavonic, head of energy research at MST Marquee. "The hostilities and reimposed blockade set the conflict back on an escalatory trajectory," he said, adding that flows through Hormuz had dropped to around 15% of pre-war levels.

*Assessment: [VERIFIED] The Strait is effectively closed despite 10 consecutive nights of US strikes. Traffic has collapsed from a pre-war baseline of ~88-130 vessels/day to near zero. The US campaign has not compelled Iran to loosen its grip on the waterway.*

### 2. CRITICAL: Kuwaiti Tanker Kaifan Attacked — Crew Abandons Ship [VERIFIED/OFFICIAL]

Another tanker was attacked in the Strait of Hormuz as renewed hostilities empty the waterway, while a threat by Houthi rebels to blockade Saudi Arabia in the Red Sea heightened regional maritime risks. The Kaifan, an oil products tanker owned by Kuwait Oil Tanker Co., was the vessel attacked in the strait, according to security consultancy EOS Risk Group. The ship was struck by an unknown projectile northeast of Oman's Limah, UK Maritime Trade Operations said earlier Tuesday, without naming the tanker.

Kuwait summoned Iran's ambassador to protest an attack by the Islamic Republic on the Kuwaiti tanker Kaifan while it was transiting the Strait of Hormuz on Monday, the foreign ministry said. The attack injured several crew members, the ministry added.

The UKMTO said that a tanker had been struck by an unknown projectile in the Strait of Hormuz and that according to the company CSO the crew had abandoned the vessel and were currently in a lifeboat. There were no details of damage to the tanker from the attack. The attack took place 8nm northeast of Limah, Oman and was reported at 20:10 hrs UTC on 20 July.

*Assessment: [VERIFIED] This represents at least the eighth commercial vessel struck since the ceasefire collapsed around July 7. Crew abandonment demonstrates Iran's continued ability to interdict shipping despite the sustained US strike campaign. Kuwait's formal diplomatic protest marks an escalation in Gulf Arab responses.*

## Emerging Signals

**Dual-Chokepoint Crisis Operational:** With competing U.S. and Iranian restrictions on shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, the Houthi blockade targeting Saudi Arabia—a major oil exporter in the region—threatens to block another critical waterway for energy markets, which could further destabilize the global economy. The feared scenario is now reality — both chokepoints are contested.

**Shipping Behavior Change Locking In:** Sinokor Group, the world's largest owner of supertankers, offered crews six months of additional pay to make a round trip through the Strait of Hormuz to load oil in Saudi Arabia or Iraq and unload it in the Gulf of Oman. These incentive structures suggest operators expect prolonged disruption.

**"Middle Corridor" Concept Under Discussion:** Mediators have proposed a shipping lane through Omani-controlled waters that bypasses Iranian control. This represents a potential face-saving compromise but requires US-Iran agreement that remains elusive.

**Force Buildup for Major Campaign:** Washington is simultaneously preparing for the talks to fail. The U.S. military has sent dozens of fighter jets and refueling aircraft to the region in recent days, assembling the forces needed for a major escalation.

**Iranian Persistence Despite Bombardment:** Tehran continues attacking commercial shipping, launching strikes at Jordan/Bahrain/Kuwait, and maintaining Hormuz closure despite 10 consecutive days of US strikes — demonstrating resilience that informs Trump's "massive campaign" consideration.
