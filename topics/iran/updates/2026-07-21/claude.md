### Key Developments Since Prior Report (July 20)

#### 1. US Strikes Iran for 10th Consecutive Night; Iran Attacks Tanker

**[VERIFIED]** The United States conducted its 10th consecutive night of strikes on Iran. U.S. Central Command said today that it had targeted "Iranian military command centers, maritime capabilities, missile and drone launch sites and air defense systems."

**[VERIFIED]** Iran attacked a tanker in the Strait of Hormuz early on Tuesday, forcing its crew to abandon the ship even as the United States conducted yet another round of air strikes targeting the Islamic Republic over control of the key waterway.

**[VERIFIED]** The Kaifan, an oil products tanker owned by Kuwait Oil Tanker Co., was the vessel attacked in the strait, according to security consultancy EOS Risk Group. The ship was struck by an unknown projectile northeast of Oman's Limah.

**[VERIFIED]** The British military's United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations Centre said a tanker came under attack early on Tuesday in the strait off Oman, forcing the crew to abandon the vessel. Iran's paramilitary Revolutionary Guard claimed the attack, as well as two other attacks on ships on Monday in the waterway.

**[VERIFIED]** The 10 consecutive nights of US air strikes have not compelled Tehran to loosen its grip on the strait, through which about a fifth of all crude oil and natural gas traded once passed in peacetime.

*Assessment: US strike campaign has now reached 10 days with no apparent degradation in Iran's ability to interdict shipping. The attack on the Kuwaiti tanker and crew abandonment demonstrates Iran's continued control over the strait.*

---

#### 2. Strait of Hormuz "Deserted" — Only 3-9 Vessels Per Day

**[VERIFIED]** Hormuz was deserted on Tuesday with no vessels observed transiting, ship-tracking data shows.

**[VERIFIED]** Lloyd's List Intelligence said only three ships transited the strait on Sunday.

**[VERIFIED]** Daily shipping traffic in the Strait of Hormuz has consistently dropped since US-Iranian negotiations collapsed. In the past 24 hours, just nine vessels passed through, according to MarineTraffic data. This limited movement through the strait is a stark contrast to five months ago, before the US-Iran conflict, when an average of 130 ships passed through daily.

*Assessment: Traffic has collapsed to 2-7% of pre-war levels. The strait is effectively closed.*

---

#### 3. Houthi Blockade Producing Immediate Effects — Saudi Tankers Reverse Course

**[VERIFIED]** Two oil tankers that loaded Saudi crude for China and India made U-turns in the Red Sea on Tuesday and headed toward the Suez, according to shipping data on LSEG, following a warning from the Houthis. Shipping companies should not load or discharge cargo at Saudi Arabian ports, and such activity may result in being targeted "in any location" by Yemen's Iran-aligned Houthis, according to an email sent to companies by the terror group.

**[OFFICIAL]** The Saudi Foreign Ministry said in a statement that it would take "all necessary measures" to protect its ships.

**[REPORTED]** A Houthi closure of the Bab Al Mandeb strait could halt Saudi oil exports to Asia and severely disrupt regional trade routes. A Saudi-led coalition spokesman said claims of a Saudi blockade of ports and airports were "disinformation."

**[VERIFIED]** Closing the strait would cut down the world's oil supply by 7 percent and leave most Saudi oil exports unable to exit the Middle East. It would add to the disrupted flow of oil out of the Persian Gulf, which has seen a 10 percent reduction since the war began.

*Assessment: The Houthi blockade is already operational — ships are responding to warnings. This validates the dual-chokepoint scenario feared yesterday.*

#### 4. US Death Toll Rises to 18; Nearly 100 Wounded in Two Weeks

**[VERIFIED]** At least 18 U.S. service members have been killed supporting the United States during the now months-long war in Iran, including three killed since combat resumed a week ago.

**[VERIFIED]** The Pentagon said yesterday that nearly 100 U.S. troops have been injured in the war with Iran over the last two weeks. The "vast majority" of those injuries, it said, were "mild concussions," adding that 96% have returned to duty.

**[VERIFIED]** The Iranian ballistic missile attack on a Jordanian air base last week that killed two U.S. servicemembers struck prefabricated housing units, according to U.S. officials. Iran has circulated satellite imagery on social-media accounts showing what it says is damage to the base. The attack on Muwaffaq Salti Air Base was one of three separate missile strikes on the base in 24 hours.

**[VERIFIED]** "Sgt. Michael Emmanuel Swinton, 30, of Fayetteville, North Carolina, was killed in action during a controlled detonation of a downed One-Way Unmanned Aerial System on July 19, 2026, at Erbil Air Base, Iraq."

**[VERIFIED]** Jordan's military said today that Iran targeted it with five drones and three missiles, all of which were shot down.

*Assessment: US casualties rising significantly faster than during the initial war phase. 100 wounded in 2 weeks is a notable acceleration. The vulnerability of US bases in Jordan remains concerning.*

#### 5. Washington "Downplays" Ceasefire — Trump Focused on Retaliation

**[REPORTED]** Mediators have floated a 10-day ceasefire between the US and Iran, but Washington has downplayed the prospect of Trump accepting it.

**[OFFICIAL]** In a post on Truth Social on Monday, President Donald Trump said, "Every time Iran kills an American Soldier they will pay for that killing many times over!" He added that this directive had been passed on to every leader in the military.

**[REPORTED]** Washington insists on reaching at least partial understandings regarding freedom of navigation through the Strait of Hormuz before any ceasefire takes effect. One of the sources said that at least some officials within the Trump administration have described the Iranian proposal as "absurd" and "illogical."

**[REPORTED]** The president has voiced deep frustration with Tehran since the ceasefire collapsed, calling Iran's leaders "sick people" and dismissing further negotiations as a "waste of time." One regional source said the White House may continue bombing Iran for several more days to avenge the killing of the U.S. service members in Jordan before seriously considering the proposal.

**[REPORTED]** Option 1: Pursue a new 10-day ceasefire aimed at reopening the Strait of Hormuz. Option 2: Launch a massive joint military campaign with Israel to force Tehran's capitulation. Trump hasn't made a decision yet. But officials believe the next few days will determine which path prevails.

**[REPORTED]** Qatar, Egypt, Pakistan and other regional mediators have presented the U.S. and Iran with a proposal for a 10-day ceasefire. The Trump administration is exploring the proposal and has urged Israel to avoid steps that could close the diplomatic window.

**[REPORTED]** The mediators, including senior Qatari, Egyptian, Omani, and Pakistani officials, have presented the proposal to the United States and have added further elements during discussions with both Washington and Tehran. One such idea is the creation of a "middle corridor" through the Strait of Hormuz, located between Omani-controlled and Iranian-controlled waters.

*Assessment: Ceasefire prospects have dimmed since yesterday. Trump's focus on retaliation, combined with the "absurd" characterization of Iran's proposal, suggests no imminent deal. The next few days are critical.*

#### 6. ASEAN Issues Statement — Regional Alarm Growing

**[OFFICIAL]** "We are deeply concerned that these developments severely undermine the ongoing efforts of key mediators and diminish the prospects for a peaceful resolution through dialogue and diplomacy," foreign ministers of the Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN) said in a joint statement.

**[VERIFIED]** Countries in Asia have been the hardest hit by Iran's effective closure of the strait, a critical route for about 60% of the region's energy supply.
