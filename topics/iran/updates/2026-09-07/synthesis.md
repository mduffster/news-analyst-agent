### 4. Iran Announces Expanded "Restricted Zone" Beyond Hormuz [OFFICIAL]

Iran will establish a new "restricted" zone outside the Strait of Hormuz. Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council, said the zone would start at the line of the U.S. Navy's blockade and stretch into parts of the Gulf. The measure will take effect in the coming days.

Ships that enter the zone without coordination with Iran would face sanctions affecting their insurance coverage and future passage through the waterway.

Rezaei forcefully rejected statements by US President Donald Trump claiming that the vital energy corridor remains accessible to global trade. He dismissed Washington's account as a "big lie" and confirmed that the Strait of Hormuz is "completely closed and under the control of the armed forces".

Rezaei said some vessels had attempted to cross the strait through a route near Oman, with some switching off their navigation systems to avoid detection. He said most of the vessels were being hit, but added that Iran had no intention of sinking them for now because of environmental concerns.

**Assessment:** [OFFICIAL] Iran's restricted zone announcement extends its maritime control claims beyond the existing Hormuz closure into the broader Persian Gulf. The reference to not sinking ships "for now because of environmental concerns" combines restraint with implied threat of escalation.

### 6. OPEC+ Holds Output Steady — Acknowledges Limited Market Influence [OFFICIAL]

The Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries decided to keep oil production the same for October during a meeting Sunday, a shift from the monthly increases seen in the last six months. Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, Kuwait, Kazakhstan, Algeria and Oman, the main OPEC+ countries, "will continue to hold monthly meetings to review market conditions."

The Iran war continues to disrupt oil exports through the Strait of Hormuz, limiting OPEC+'s influence over prices and market share.

"OPEC+ kept October production policy unchanged rather than introducing a fresh supply increase", which "leaves the market more exposed to disruptions in existing Gulf exports". "The latest available global assessment expects 2026 oil supply to fall by 4.3 million barrels per day to around 102 million barrels per day."

The seven countries will hold their next meeting on Oct. 4.

**Assessment:** [OFFICIAL] OPEC+'s decision to pause output increases acknowledges the reality that war disruptions have rendered quota decisions largely symbolic. The explicit admission that the Iran war limits OPEC+'s influence over the market represents an extraordinary acknowledgment of how fundamentally the conflict has altered global energy dynamics.

Iranian defense officials have warned that any direct attack on the Pickaxe complex would trigger heavy retaliatory measures.

**Assessment:** [VERIFIED] Trump's renewed threats against Pickaxe Mountain — combined with intelligence suggesting Iran has moved centrifuges to the fortified site — brings the nuclear dimension back into focus. The facility's extreme depth (300+ feet) makes destruction uncertain with conventional weapons, but the threats signal the administration's willingness to escalate.

Iran firing ballistic missiles at a US aircraft carrier — the first such attack ever — represents a qualitative shift even though missiles were evaded. The US response of destroying three Iranian tankers, including one at Kharg Island (handling 90% of Iran's oil exports), signals both sides have escalated to deliberate economic warfare at sea.

The fundamental contradiction between administration messaging and battlefield reality has widened dramatically.
