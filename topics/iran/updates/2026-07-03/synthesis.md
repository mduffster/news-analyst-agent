## Critical Developments (July 3)

### 1. CRITICAL: Mojtaba Khamenei Confirms He Will NOT Attend Father's Funeral [VERIFIED]

Iran's current supreme leader and son of Khamenei, Mojtaba Khamenei, will not be attending the funeral, his representative to India said on Thursday. This is due to security reasons following Israel's latest threat to assassinate Mojtaba Khamenei.

Mojtaba Khamenei is unlikely to appear publicly at the funeral of his father, Iran's late Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei, because authorities cannot guarantee his safety, the Supreme Leader's representative in India, Ayatollah Hakim Elahi said. Speaking exclusively to India Today before departing for Tehran from New Delhi's Indira Gandhi International Airport, Elahi said the decision was being driven entirely by security concerns.

Elahi said "I was in Iran last week and met some of my friends who met him. They said he wants to come out. He wants to meet people. But the security doesn't allow him to come."

Mojtaba, who was dangerously wounded in the strike that killed his father, has not been seen in any new image since the war began.

**Assessment:** This is the single most significant signal about Iranian regime stability since the war began. The confirmation that Mojtaba will skip his father's funeral — officially attributed to Israeli threats — could indicate either genuine security concerns or that his injuries are more serious than acknowledged. Elahi's claim that "he wants to come out" but security prevents it suggests the regime is aware of the propaganda cost of his absence but cannot mitigate the risk. Either way, Mojtaba's non-appearance at his father's funeral will fuel speculation about his fitness to govern.

### 2. MAJOR: IRGC Commander Vahidi Makes First Public Appearance Since War Began [VERIFIED]

Ahmad Vahidi, the caretaker commander of Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps, has made his first public appearance since the start of the US-Israel war on Iran, according to Al Jazeera. The appearance comes as Iran prepares for the funeral of former Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei, who was killed in an air strike early in the conflict. Khamenei's coffin arrived at the ceremony site near the Imam Khomeini Hussainiya mosque in southern Tehran on Thursday evening, where senior Iranian officials gathered to pay their respects.

Vahidi, who has not been seen publicly since February 8, is widely regarded by analysts as a key figure shaping Tehran's hardline negotiating stance over any permanent end to the recent conflict with the United States.

He has become one of the principal architects of Iran's hardline approach to negotiations over a possible permanent end to the war with the United States. He took charge of the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps in the time of war with US-Israel, which killed many of the country's senior military leadership, succeeding Mohammad Pakpour, the IRGC's commander-in-chief, who was killed in the joint US-Israeli strikes on February 28.

Sanctioned by the US and wanted by Interpol, Vahidi has been helping to craft Tehran's next moves during the war and is known to be a prominent voice in the country's decision-making circles.

**Assessment:** Vahidi's emergence is a carefully calibrated signal. His appearance demonstrates that Iran's military leadership is confident enough in security measures to show themselves publicly — even while Mojtaba remains hidden. The appearance of multiple officials, such as IRGC chief Brig. Gen. Ahmad Vahidi, may indicate that they are confident in their security measures despite knowing the risks. Vahidi's prominence as the architect of Iran's hardline negotiating stance suggests IRGC dominance in the decision-making process during Mojtaba's absence.

### 3. Funeral Ceremonies Begin — Massive Mobilization [VERIFIED]

The multi-day public farewell, scheduled to take place on July 4 and 5 in the capital city, is anticipated to draw a record-breaking crowd of up to 20 million participants, according to the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC).

Representatives from more than 100 countries are expected to attend the funeral of former Iranian Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei, which starts on Friday, according to Iranian state-linked news broadcaster IRIB.

Airspace over Tehran will be "completely closed" on Monday for the late Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei's funeral procession, Iran's Civil Aviation Organization said on Friday. "On Monday, coinciding with the funeral procession for the late revolutionary leader, Tehran's airspace will be completely closed, and no flights will be permitted."

However, reports indicate the regime is not leaving turnout to chance:

What the directive campaign reveals is that the regime is not confident that grief alone will produce a crowd of that magnitude. The pattern is not new – the Islamic Republic has routinely mobilized state employees for politically important public gatherings – but the scope of the current effort reflects the particular stakes.

Basij members visited shops and threatened to seal those that opened.

**Assessment:** The funeral is designed as a domestic legitimacy test — matching or exceeding the 10 million who attended Khomeini's 1989 funeral. The mobilization effort (workplace directives, Basij pressure) suggests the regime is uncertain about organic turnout. The security apparatus is fully deployed, with Tehran airspace closure and massive presence.

### 4. Hormuz Traffic Recovery: Independent Confirmation [VERIFIED]

Crude oil held steady around $69 per barrel on Friday, hovering near levels last seen before the Middle East conflict erupted in late February as commercial shipping through the Strait of Hormuz continued to recover amid progress in US-Iran talks. Saudi Arabia's crude exports have rebounded to about 90% of their pre-war levels as more tankers successfully transit the key waterway, signaling that regional oil supply is gradually normalizing.

The United Arab Emirates restored its exports to over 3.9 million barrels daily, pushing total daily Hormuz flows past 10 million barrels. This surge, combined with ongoing emergency reserve releases and ad hoc Saudi sales to Asia, has created a market surplus.

Commercial activity in the Strait of Hormuz is recovering despite uncertainty over the crucial waterway's future, according to data from the maritime risk consultancy Marisks.

However, the system remains operationally fragile:

Convoys are moving under naval escort. 27 ships transited vs ~84/day normal. (Note: This appears to be older data from June 28; the weekly Marisks figure of 335 vessels suggests higher current throughput.)

Following an attack on a vessel in the Gulf of Oman, IMO has decided to temporarily pause its evacuation operation pending further clarity. IMO Secretary-General Mr. Arsenio Dominguez stated: "Following the launch of the IMO's evacuation plan, through which several vessels have already been successfully evacuated, I have decided to temporarily pause its implementation in order to reconfirm that the necessary safety guarantees continue to be in place."

**Assessment:** The traffic recovery is real but incomplete. The US official claim of 10+ million barrels/day through Hormuz is supported by Saudi/UAE export data. However, the IMO evacuation pause (since June 25) confirms that operational safety guarantees remain fragile. The emerging dual-corridor system (southern Omani route vs. northern Iran-controlled route) creates ongoing friction points.

## Emerging Signals

**Mojtaba's absence creates negotiating uncertainty:** The Supreme Leader's representative claims he "wants to come out" but security prevents it. Whether the constraint is genuine Israeli threats or his medical condition, the effect is the same: Iran's top constitutional authority is absent from decision-making visibility. Vahidi and other IRGC commanders are filling the void.

**IRGC dominance solidifying:** Vahidi's public appearance — sanctioned by the US, wanted by Interpol — signals the military is confident enough to show itself while the political leader hides. Unlike his two predecessors, Vahidi is not solely a military figure. Alongside his career in the IRGC, he has held senior political posts, serving as defence minister under former President Mahmoud Ahmadinejad and later as interior minister under the late President Ebrahim Raisi until 2024. His political background positions him to bridge military-civilian decision-making.

**Dual-corridor system crystallizing:** The pattern emerging in Hormuz is a divided regime — southern corridor (Omani/IMO) vs. northern corridor (Iran-controlled). On Wednesday, Iran's military warned vessels to not use the southern route approved by the IMO and said any new transit route through the Strait of Hormuz established without its approval is "unacceptable and dangerous." At least two vessels performed U-turns on their way out of the Middle East Gulf, according to Lloyd's, after Iran insisted that vessels use the routes approved by Tehran. This creates persistent friction and incident risk.

**IMO pause as tripwire:** The IMO's evacuation plan remains paused following the June 25 attack. "I have always reiterated that the safety of the seafarers remains paramount. Therefore, to ensure a coordinated approach and navigational safety, the evacuation plan will be paused until further clarity is obtained." The continued pause demonstrates that safety guarantees have not been restored.

**Oil market complacency:** Markets have returned to pre-war levels despite unresolved governance disputes, Iran's explicit military threats, and the IMO evacuation pause. Any setback — an enforcement incident, a failed negotiation round, or an attack during/after the funeral — could trigger sharp repricing.
