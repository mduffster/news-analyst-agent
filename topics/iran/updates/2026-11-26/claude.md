## Key Developments (Nov 25-26)

### **HOUTHI ATTACK KILLS WORKER AT JAZAN REFINERY** [VERIFIED]

Some operations at Saudi Aramco's Jazan refinery were temporarily halted after a hostile aerial projectile struck the facility on Thursday, killing a Nepalese worker and wounding several others, Saudi Arabia's Energy Ministry said in a statement.

The refinery in southwestern Saudi Arabia, near the Yemeni border, is designed to process up to 400,000 barrels of crude oil per day.

The strike sparked a fire and temporarily suspended some operations at the facility. The attack came a day after the Houthis claimed to have struck Saudi Arabia's biggest oil refinery at Ras Tanura.

**[ANALYSIS]** This is the first confirmed fatality from a Houthi strike on Saudi energy infrastructure during the current escalation. Combined with yesterday's Ras Tanura attack, the Houthis have now struck 950,000 bpd of Saudi refining capacity in 48 hours. The targeting of two separate refineries demonstrates sustained multi-axis capability.

### **IRAN DENIES KUWAIT ATTACK — TWO ITALIAN TECHNICIANS KILLED** [VERIFIED]

Iran denied responsibility for a drone attack in Kuwait that killed two Italian technicians, while ADNOC confirmed the death of a missing Jordanian crew member following an earlier attack on its vessel in the Strait of Hormuz.

The two were carrying out maintenance work on an air defence system when the explosion occurred.

The Gulf News live feed confirmed the victims "worked for Rheinmetall" and the EU chief urged "pressure" on Iran following the deaths.

**[ANALYSIS]** This is a significant escalation — the first Western civilian deaths in a GCC state since the war began. The victims' connection to Rheinmetall (a German defense contractor) working on air defense systems suggests the strike targeted the defensive infrastructure itself. Iran's denial does not preclude IRGC or proxy responsibility. This could increase European pressure on Iran and complicate EU efforts to maintain diplomatic channels.

### **KUSHNER CONFIRMS DIRECT TALKS WITH IRAN** [OFFICIAL]

Kushner revealed that he had met Iranian representatives on more than one occasion. "We met with the Iranians a couple times," he said.

He also confirmed that he had worked under US Vice President JD Vance as part of the Trump administration's efforts to address the conflict with Iran.

Previous reporting described indirect communications between US and Iranian representatives mediated by regional governments, so Kushner's account of direct meetings marks a departure from that pattern.

Kushner did not say when or where the meetings took place, which officials were involved, or whether they produced any agreement.

"President Trump always keeps all his options on the table," Kushner replied when asked about further military action.

**[ANALYSIS]** This is the first explicit confirmation of direct US-Iran talks since the war began. The acknowledgment that Kushner worked "under Vice President JD Vance" suggests institutional back-channel structure. However, the vague framing and lack of details suggests these may have been exploratory rather than substantive negotiations. Combined with Trump's January funding deadline, this could indicate both sides are positioning for post-January negotiations.

Military spokesperson Effie Defrin says Israel is ready to attack Iran with "force at any given moment, even independently, without the Americans." Israel's military spokesperson Effie Defrin said the country is prepared to attack Iran independently and at any time, amid continuing threats between the two countries since the war began on 28 February.

Tehran continues to pose an existential threat to Israel, Al Jazeera reports.

This comes alongside Iran-backed armed groups in Iraq are preparing to strike US military assets in the Middle East if Washington starts new attacks on Iran.

**[ANALYSIS]** This is a more explicit threat than previous Israeli statements. Coming one day after Lieberman rejected the need for immediate action, Defrin's statement appears designed to maintain pressure and preserve Netanyahu's options. The juxtaposition with Iraqi militia warnings suggests both sides are positioning for possible escalation. However, the statement may be more signaling than operational intent given the coalition dynamics.

### **EISENKOT COALITION TALKS ACCELERATE** [VERIFIED]

Eisenkot, tapped by Israel's president to form a new government, has so far struggled to cobble together a ruling coalition. "I call on all parties to come together as soon as possible to finalize agreements and sign them, form a government, and lead Israel in a new direction."

Ra'am chairman MK Mansour Abbas claimed on Thursday that a government headed by Yashar chairman Gadi Eisenkot would include both Ra'am and United Torah Judaism (UTJ), with the Joint List providing parliamentary support from outside the coalition.

"There will be a government with a Jewish majority," Lieberman said.

Eisenkot has until December 6 to try to form a government, with the possibility of a 14-day extension.

**[ANALYSIS]** The Ra'am-UTJ scenario would represent an unprecedented coalition bridging Arab Islamist and ultra-Orthodox parties. Lieberman's insistence on a "Jewish majority" creates tension with this path, but his participation alongside implicit Joint List support could thread the needle. If Abbas's claim is accurate, Eisenkot's coalition is closer to reality than previously understood.
