# US-Israel-Iran Conflict: First Fatalities at Saudi Refinery and in Kuwait as Kushner Confirms Direct Iran Talks; IDF Signals Independent Strike Readiness

## November 26, 2026 — Day 272

**Assessment:** The conflict reached a significant inflection point on Day 272 with the first confirmed fatalities from attacks on both Saudi energy infrastructure and in a GCC state. A Houthi strike on Saudi's Jazan refinery killed a Nepalese worker—the first energy sector death in this escalation phase—while two Italian technicians died in an Iranian drone attack on a Kuwaiti air defense facility, marking the first Western civilian deaths in a GCC country since the war began. Meanwhile, Jared Kushner's public confirmation of direct meetings with Iranian representatives reveals a diplomatic back-channel previously hidden from public view, even as IDF spokesperson Effie Defrin declared Israel "ready to strike with force at any given moment, even independently, without the Americans." The divergence between rhetorical escalation and covert diplomacy defines the current phase: both sides are positioning for the January political transition while maintaining operational pressure.

## 1. First Fatality at Saudi Energy Infrastructure — Jazan Refinery Strike

An aerial projectile hit an oil refinery in Saudi Arabia's city of Jazan, killing one worker and injuring several other people, according to the country's Ministry of Energy. The Houthi attack on Saudi Arabia's Jazan refinery on Thursday killed a Nepali worker and injured several others. [VERIFIED — multiple sources]

Thursday's attack on the facility on the kingdom's Red Sea coast caused a fire and temporarily halted some operations, the ministry said. The refinery in southwestern Saudi Arabia, near the Yemeni border, is designed to process up to 400,000 barrels of crude oil per day.

The attack came a day after the Houthis claimed to have struck Saudi Arabia's biggest oil refinery at Ras Tanura. Satellite imagery taken Wednesday showed a huge plume of black smoke rising from the coastal peninsula following the attack.

Later on Thursday, the coalition claimed its forces had destroyed a Houthi ballistic missile launch platform in Yemen's Hodeidah governorate used by the armed group to stage the attack on the Jazan refinery.

**Combined Impact:** The Houthis have now struck 950,000 bpd of Saudi refining capacity (Ras Tanura: 550,000; Jazan: 400,000) in 48 hours. The first confirmed fatality at a Saudi refinery during this escalation phase marks a qualitative shift—demonstrating Houthi capability to cause human casualties, not just material damage.

## 2. Two Italian Technicians Killed in Kuwait — First Western Civilian Deaths in GCC

The Italian technicians were working for German defense company Rheinmetall AG and died in an explosion caused by the strike involving a number of drones of Iranian origin, Italy's Ministry of Foreign Affairs said in a statement. An Iranian drone attack on Kuwait on Thursday, the second this month, killed two Italian civilians and injured two others during an assault on what the Gulf nation's army said was a "vital" facility. [VERIFIED — multiple sources]

The Italian technicians were in Kuwait to maintain "detection antennas" for an air defense system used by the country's armed forces, Italy's foreign ministry said. The men were employed by Rheinmetall Italia, the Italian arm of German defense contractor Rheinmetall, and were maintaining radar equipment for Skyguard, Kuwait's short-range air defense system.

Kuwait's Defense Ministry condemned the attack as "heinous Iranian aggression against the country." Iran denied carrying out the attack. Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi also denied any Iranian involvement during a phone call with his Italian counterpart.

**Significance:** These are the first Western civilian fatalities in a GCC state since the war began February 28. The targeting of air defense radar equipment suggests deliberate attacks on defensive infrastructure rather than collateral damage. This could increase European pressure on Iran and complicate EU mediation efforts. This marks the first fatal Iranian attack on Kuwaiti territory since July.

## 3. Kushner Confirms Direct Meetings with Iran — Diplomatic Channel Revealed

US envoy Jared Kushner has confirmed several meetings with Iranian representatives, revealing ongoing contacts between Washington and Tehran. [OFFICIAL — Kushner public statement]

Speaking on the MD Meets podcast, Kushner discussed his previous interactions with Iranian representatives. He confirmed that both sides had held meetings on multiple occasions. Kushner acknowledged that the meetings had taken place several times.

However, Kushner did not provide specific details about these discussions. He also did not identify the Iranian representatives involved. Kushner offered no details about their timing, participants or specific outcomes.

Asked whether Washington could consider additional military action against Iran, Kushner said Trump had not ruled out any option. Iranian officials have rejected claims of direct negotiations with US representatives.

**Context:** This confirmation follows previously reported indirect channels. In September, Trump revealed that Kushner and Witkoff held three hours of talks with members of an Iranian delegation in New York. However, Kushner's latest remarks provide no further clarification on whether the meetings he described were direct negotiations or contacts conducted through intermediaries.

**Significance:** Kushner's public acknowledgment—combined with Trump's January funding deadline—suggests the administration is preparing for post-January negotiations rather than decisive escalation. The lack of concrete outcomes indicates exploratory rather than substantive diplomacy.

## 4. IDF: "Ready to Strike Iran Without the Americans"

Israel's military is ready to strike Iran on its own, military spokesman Effie Defrin told Israeli public broadcaster Kan on Thursday. "We are monitoring the situation and are ready to strike with force at any moment, including on our own, without the Americans," Defrin said. [OFFICIAL — IDF statement]

Defrin claimed that Iran still poses a threat, saying that its "vision of destroying" Israel has not "expired".

Israeli Defense Minister Israel Katz alleged Tuesday that Iran is developing chemical weapons and floated a preemptive strike, a claim Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said was aimed at deceiving the U.S. Gadi Eisenkot, leader of the Yashar party tasked with forming a government, and his allies have said there is no threat requiring an immediate attack.

Despite this stated readiness, The Jerusalem Post reported that the Israeli defense establishment believes a joint operation with the United States is the preferred course of action, and it is considered unlikely that Israel would launch an attack without U.S. participation or approval.

Israel's readiness to act alone follows nine months of conflict with Iran that has already reshaped regional energy markets. The divergence between Israeli military posture and Washington's push for economic diplomacy marks a notable strain in the US-Israel partnership during this war.

**Analysis:** Defrin's statement juxtaposed against Lieberman's rejection of immediate strikes (November 25) reflects the Netanyahu government's efforts to maintain escalatory options before Eisenkot potentially forms a new government. The statement appears designed more for political signaling than operational intent.

## 5. Eisenkot Coalition Progress — UTJ Rabbis Greenlight Negotiations

Ra'am chairman MK Mansour Abbas claimed on Thursday that a government headed by Yashar chairman Gadi Eisenkot would include both Ra'am and United Torah Judaism (UTJ), with the Joint List providing parliamentary support from outside the coalition. [REPORTED — Jerusalem Post]

UTJ rabbis are said to have given the go-ahead to launching official coalition talks with Eisenkot. Kan reported that the two parties are expected to move from informal contacts to official coalition negotiations next week, indicating that the main points of contention between them have largely been resolved.

Even with UTJ, the Eisenkot-led bloc would only have 55 seats. The inclusion of both UTJ and Ra'am, however, would increase the total to 63, two more than the minimum 61 needed for a majority government.

But UTJ on Thursday denied negotiating possible cabinet posts with Eisenkot.

**Coalition Mathematics:** With 21 seats (Yashar) + 7 seats (UTJ) + 8 seats (Ra'am) + 27 seats (other bloc parties including Yisrael Beiteinu, Democrats) = 63 potential seats. Joint List's 11 seats could provide outside support. Eisenkot has until December 6 to form a government, with possibility of a 14-day extension.

## 6. Strait of Hormuz — Competing Narratives on Attack Mechanism

An outbound tanker was struck by an unknown projectile off Oman during a transit of the Strait of Hormuz on 26 November, UKMTO reported. [OFFICIAL — UKMTO Warning]

UK Maritime Trade Operations (UKMTO) published Warning 223-26 on Thursday, classifying the incident as an attack. It said local authorities had reported that an outbound tanker was struck by an unknown projectile 18 nautical miles east of Khasab, Oman.

UKMTO said the crew was accounted for after the incident, but Vanguard went into more detail, claiming that one crew member was severely injured during the attack. The unknown projectile hit the vessel's engine room, said Vanguard, causing a fire onboard which has been extinguished.

The latest JMIC report, covering the period 22-25 November, covers the details of seven attacks on merchant ships, not including today's Kun Peng attack. On November 22, three tankers in the Strait of Hormuz were struck by unknown projectiles within the space of 17 minutes.

**Attribution Contest:** Iran's Revolutionary Guards said shortly afterwards that an oil tanker had caught fire after hitting a mine while attempting to transit the Strait of Hormuz near Khasab, Iranian state media reported. UKMTO consistently reports "projectiles" while IRGC claims "mines in unauthorized zones"—a narrative that could serve as quasi-legal justification for area-denial operations.

## 7. Oil Markets — Third Consecutive Session of Gains

Brent rose to 96.33 USD/Bbl on November 26, 2026, up 1.82% from the previous day. [VERIFIED — Trading Economics]

Over the past month, Brent's price has fallen 11.95%, but it is still 54.03% higher than a year ago.

Brent crude oil could return to $100 per barrel in early 2027 if supply disruptions occur through the Strait of Hormuz, according to leading analyst Natalya Milchakova. The upcoming OPEC+ meeting on November 29, the last this year, is the nearest market event, with the alliance likely to maintain January production quotas.

**Three-Day Trajectory:** Oil has risen for three consecutive sessions following the Ras Tanura attack (Nov 25) and Jazan strike (Nov 26), recovering from recent lows of ~$87 to current levels of $94-96.

## 8. December 11 Funding Deadline — Bipartisan Deal Taking Shape

With a December 11 government funding deadline looming, Senate Republicans and Democrats are reportedly discussing a deal that would prevent a government shutdown and fund certain Pentagon priorities but cut off money for the Iran war. [REPORTED — multiple sources]

Sen. Tim Kaine (D-VA) told reporters on Friday that key Senate Democrats had finalized a proposal to trade other military funding for a fast wind-down of the war and were in serious negotiations with leading Republicans. Senate Majority Leader John Thune (R-SD) confirmed that such discussions were going forward.

Collins confirmed to reporters that she is receptive to a bipartisan deal that would restock munitions at the Pentagon while denying funds for continuing the Iran war.

Democrats, fresh off sweeping midterm victories that included flipping nine Senate seats, are divided over how aggressively to use the funding deadline to force an end to the war.

According to the most recent polling from Reuters/Ipsos, 71 percent of Americans say they want the new Congress to end the Iran war.

**Key Date:** December 1 — Senate Democrats hold leadership elections (Schumer vs. Van Hollen), which will shape their December 11 posture.

## 9. Iraqi Militias Positioning for Escalation

Iran-backed Kataib Hezbollah has moved military equipment and established new positions in western Iraq for a possible renewed US-Iran war, while retaining its strongholds despite a government withdrawal order, according to Iraqi media and US think tanks. [REPORTED — ISW/CTP, Iran International]

More than 2,000 Kataib Hezbollah members have been incorporated into the militia-affiliated 72nd and 79th brigades of Iraq's Popular Mobilization Forces (PMF).

The group is among those that have claimed attacks on US forces in Gulf states since the start of the US-Israeli war on Iran.
