## Key Developments (Past 24 Hours)

### **TRUMP PLEDGES NO IRAN STRIKES BEFORE MIDTERMS** [OFFICIAL]

"We will not be attacking Iran at any time prior to the Midterm Elections to be held in the United States on November 3rd," Trump wrote on his social media site, adding that the U.S. was having "productive discussions with the Islamic Republic of Iran."

President Donald Trump's surprise pledge that the United States would not resume airstrikes against Iran before the midterm elections failed to reverse a sharp jump in oil prices Thursday. But Thursday, after a brief price dip, oil markets largely shrugged off his announcement.

However, Trump had been considering for several days resuming major combat operations before the midterms. However, his past statements about his intentions in Iran haven't always matched what he ultimately did.

**[ANALYSIS]** The Axios report notes Trump's credibility gap—In June 2025, before Operation Midnight Hammer, the White House said Trump would decide "within two weeks" whether the U.S. would join Israel's war against Iran. When he made that statement, he had already decided to attack Iran's nuclear sites. On Feb. 27, less than 24 hours before Israel and the U.S. launched the war against Iran, Trump claimed he hadn't made a decision on going to war. In reality, Trump had already decided to authorize the attacks.

### **NYT DETAILS THREE-DAY STRIKE PLAN** [REPORTED]

The US military is preparing options for a three-day campaign of intense strikes on Iran, The New York Times reported on Thursday, citing US officials.

Senior national security officials, led by Vice President JD Vance, discussed the plans at Camp David last Friday, with the options centering on a short, intensive campaign lasting about three days. The strikes would focus on Iran's missile and drone arsenal, much of which has been rebuilt by the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) since the early phase of the war.

Other potential targets include Iranian energy facilities as well as IRGC command posts and headquarters.

Notably, Trump has rejected five proposals for major operations against Iran or the Houthis in recent months.

### **HOUTHI ATTACK KILLS SAUDIA PILOT; MAJOR AIRLINES SUSPEND RIYADH SERVICE** [VERIFIED]

Saudi Arabia has reopened Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport following attacks by the Houthis that killed three people and injured several. In a statement on social media on Friday, the General Authority of Civil Aviation (GACA) said attacks the previous day had hit airport facilities and an aircraft belonging to state carrier Saudia.

Saudia Airlines said one of its pilots was killed and an aircraft was damaged. "It is with profound sadness that Saudia announces the passing of Captain Hamoud Ali Alkalthami," the flag-carrier said in a statement.

By Friday afternoon, at least 124 flights to and from Riyadh had been canceled after hundreds more were canceled a day earlier, according to flight-tracking site FlightAware.

The Indian Embassy said Air India had suspended operations through to Saturday, while the Lufthansa Group said its airlines would suspend flights to Riyadh through to Oct. 16.

The leader of the Iran-backed Houthis in Yemen has warned Saudi Arabia to prepare for a long conflict, as his group intensified its attacks on the kingdom.

### **OPERATION YEMEN DAWN: BAB AL-MANDAB SECURED** [VERIFIED]

In a sign of the widening scope of military operations, Colonel Majed Al-Nuzaili, spokesman for the Yemeni Armed Forces, announced on Thursday that 1,729 precision strikes had been carried out against Houthi military targets across various fronts.

Al-Nuzaili said the operations resulted in the neutralization of 1,370 Houthi fighters and the destruction or disabling of 300 military vehicles and pieces of equipment.

The PLC said that it captured the Kahbub front near the Bab al-Mandab. It added that 1,860 Houthis were "neutralized" as part of Operation Dawn of Yemen.

**Cumulative casualties (since Oct 5):** 838 killed in Yemen, 3,000+ injured in Yemen, 5 killed in Saudi Arabia, 126+ injured in Saudi Arabia, 201,217 displaced in Yemen.

### **NEW US SANCTIONS TARGET IRAN'S SHADOW FLEET** [VERIFIED]

The U.S. Department of the Treasury imposed sanctions on six individuals and dozens of companies and vessels linked to the Islamic Republic on Thursday, October 8. According to an official statement issued by the department, five Indian nationals and one Turkish citizen were added to the new sanctions list for their cooperation with the Iranian regime.

Additionally, 27 entities and 22 vessels, identified by the U.S. Treasury Department as the "remnants of the Islamic Republic's shadow fleet," were designated.

"Treasury is starving the tyrannical regime in Tehran of the money it uses to wage war in the region, and we will continue exposing those who enable the regime's oil sales," said Secretary of the Treasury Scott Bessent.
