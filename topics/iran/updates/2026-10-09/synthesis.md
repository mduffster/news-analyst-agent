# US-Israel-Iran Conflict: IRGC Strikes Tanker in Hormuz as Trump Announces Russian Diesel Deal

## October 9, 2026 — Day 224

**Assessment:** The conflict entered a new phase today as Iran's Revolutionary Guard Corps escalated its Hormuz campaign by striking a tanker and threatening to pursue vessels "anywhere in the region," while Trump pivoted to Russia for diesel relief ahead of midterms. The IRGC claimed responsibility for setting an oil tanker ablaze in the Strait of Hormuz and threatened to pursue vessels it deems in violation beyond the strait and across the wider region. This represents a significant expansion of Iran's declared targeting envelope. Meanwhile, Trump announced that Putin would "immediately" release hundreds of thousands of tons of diesel fuel onto the global market—a move that signals desperation over record fuel prices 25 days before midterms. The Yemen front saw continued coalition strikes against Houthis while Riyadh airport resumed operations after Thursday's deadly attacks. EASA expanded its Saudi airspace warnings, formalizing a regulatory posture that will outlast any single attack.

## IRGC Strikes Tanker, Expands Maritime Threat Zone

The most significant escalation came from Iranian naval forces, who demonstrated both capability and intent to expand operations beyond Hormuz.

Iran's Revolutionary Guard claimed responsibility on Friday for setting an oil tanker ablaze in the Strait of Hormuz. The IRGC Navy said it targeted the engine room and propulsion system of the MV Sun Shine, a tanker belonging to the company Natwit, after the vessel attempted to use what it described as an unauthorised route through the strait's southern section. [OFFICIAL - IRGC claim]

In a statement, the IRGC warned that action against "violating" vessels would no longer be confined to the Strait of Hormuz and that any ship using what it deems unauthorised routes would be pursued and "punished" anywhere in the region. Companies it accuses of co-operating with the US would be sanctioned and all their vessels targeted. [OFFICIAL]

Eleven tankers came under attack while transiting Hormuz during the week that ended Oct. 4, the most strikes on commercial ships since the war began. Twelve sailors were injured Monday when a projectile struck their tanker as it transited Hormuz. The surge of attacks came as crude oil exports from the Middle East rebounded in September to prewar levels, aided by the U.S. military. [VERIFIED]

The strait is effectively closed to commercial shipping as of 9 October 2026, with only 4 ships transiting on October 4 versus ~85/day normal. [VERIFIED]

**Significance:** Iran's explicit threat to pursue vessels "anywhere in the region" represents a doctrinal expansion that could extend attack risk to ships transiting outside Hormuz—including the UAE coast, Gulf of Oman, and potentially the Arabian Sea. This directly challenges the U.S. escort operation model that has enabled limited oil flows through alternative routes.

## Trump-Putin Diesel Deal: Desperation Before Midterms

With diesel prices at record highs, Trump turned to an unexpected source for relief—Russia.

Trump said Russia "will immediately supply over 300,000 Tons of Diesel Fuel to the American and global marketplace" with much more to be supplied afterward, he posted to Truth Social while explaining that he had a "highly successful discussion" with Russian President Vladimir Putin on Friday. [OFFICIAL]

"Additionally, based on the condition of their Diesel Refineries, Russia will then deliver, within a short period of time, 3,000,000 Tons of Diesel Fuel," he wrote. "Between our TOTAL CONTROL of the Strait of Hormuz, and this great announcement on Russian Energy, Diesel Prices for Americans and, indeed, the World, will be COMING DOWN, IN RECORD NUMBERS, AND FAST!" [OFFICIAL]

Diesel prices are up 70% since the US-Israeli war on Iran began despite two high-profile recent moves by Trump to boost supplies of the fuel: pressuring allies to release emergency reserves and expanding access to tax-exempt red-dyed diesel. The wars in Iran and Ukraine have triggered an unprecedented global fuel supply crunch, pushing average US diesel prices to $6.28 a gallon on Thursday according to AAA. [VERIFIED]

After multiple Ukrainian attacks on Russian energy assets, the Kremlin in July imposed a diesel fuel export ban. It has extended that through the end of October. [REPORTED]

**Analysis:** Trump's claim of "TOTAL CONTROL of the Strait of Hormuz" is contradicted by today's IRGC tanker attack and the 4-vessel daily transit count. The Russia deal—if implemented—would require lifting or circumventing portions of the existing sanctions regime, which could create friction with European allies and complicate the Ukraine negotiations happening simultaneously. The political urgency is clear: diesel prices directly affect farmers and truckers in swing states 25 days before the election.

## Riyadh Airport Resumes Operations After Deadly Attacks

Saudi civil aviation authorities confirmed the full toll from Thursday's attacks while announcing resumed operations.

An airline pilot was among three Saudi citizens killed in attacks targeting Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport and a Saudia aircraft. Several people "of different nationalities" were wounded. [OFFICIAL]

The pilot was identified as Captain Hamoud Ali al-Kalthami. "It is with profound sadness that Saudia announces the passing of Captain Hamoud Ali al-Kalthami, who lost his life on Thursday, October 8, 2026, in the incident at King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh," the airline said. [OFFICIAL]

The Saudi civil aviation authority said air traffic at Riyadh airport had returned to normal and flight tracking site flightradar24 showed a gradual resumption of flights, including from some Middle Eastern airlines. [VERIFIED]

The Indian Embassy said Air India had suspended operations through to Saturday, while the Lufthansa Group said its airlines would suspend flights to Riyadh through to Oct. 16. Pakistan International Airlines has also suspended flights to Riyadh because of the evolving situation in the Middle East. [OFFICIAL]

**Cumulative Saudi casualties since October 5:** 6 killed, 40+ injured across airport attacks. [VERIFIED]

## EASA Expands Saudi Airspace Advisory

The EU aviation regulator formalized the increased threat level with expanded recommendations.

The European Union Aviation Safety Agency (EASA) said on Friday it recommended airlines not to operate in additional portions of Saudi airspace after Yemen's Iran-aligned Houthis attacked Riyadh airport. In an updated conflict zone information bulletin for Saudi Arabia airspace, initially issued on September 30, the EU regulator said recent attacks had demonstrated the ability and intent of the Houthis to strike targets deeper inside Saudi territory, including the capital. [OFFICIAL]

Europe's safety regulator has tightened recommended airspace restrictions for Saudi Arabia following intensified paramilitary activity from Houthi forces. The new restriction, which took effect on 9 October, features a new region of concern encompassing Riyadh international airport. [OFFICIAL]

Saudi airspace remains open and is still one of the main routes for traffic avoiding the wider Middle East conflict, with heavy overflight demand through the OEJD/Jeddah FIR. However, the risk picture has worsened significantly, with Houthi attacks now reaching deeper into Saudi Arabia, including Riyadh. Four airports (OERK/Riyadh, OEAB/Abha, OEGN/Jazan and OENG/Najran) have been targeted in recent days. [VERIFIED]

**Why this matters:** Regulatory advisories create durable compliance pressure on airlines that outlasts individual attack incidents. Insurance, liability, and reputational considerations mean these warnings often lead to sustained operational changes even if daily attack tempo fluctuates.

## Operation Dawn of Yemen: Coalition Claims Continue

The Saudi-backed counter-offensive entered its fifth day with mixed results.

The spokesperson for the internationally recognised government's military said in a social media post early on Thursday it had carried out 2,103 "precise targeting operations" against Houthi military positions across several fronts as part of the ongoing "Dawn of Yemen" campaign. The Yemeni government, backed by Saudi Arabia, also says its forces have captured strategic mountain positions in western Taiz overlooking the coast. [OFFICIAL - PLC claim]

However, PLC claims of liberating Mokha proved to be false, and the Yemeni government failed to sufficiently pull away Houthi reserves and relieve the siege of Taiz. Though the offensive lost momentum and Houthi forces continued to make advances in Taiz Governorate, government forces took the Umm Rish Military Camp on 7 October. [REPORTED - contested claims]

Saudi airstrikes hit Houthi targets in the Yemeni capital Sanaa overnight, Houthi officials and affiliated media said, part of an intensifying air war against the Iran-allied group aimed at reversing its recent territorial gains, threats to international shipping, and attacks on the kingdom. [REPORTED]

In a separate statement, the Saudi-led coalition fighting the Houthis said on Friday that it would respond firmly to the militants' recent attacks, after confirming the destruction of three missile launchers used by the group. [OFFICIAL]
