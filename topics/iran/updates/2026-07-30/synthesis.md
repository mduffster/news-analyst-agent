## Critical Developments (July 29-30)

### 1. CRITICAL: US Completes "Heavy Wave" of Strikes on Iran — Pause Definitively Ends [VERIFIED]

The US struck dozens of Iranian targets in a two-hour operation after Tehran fired ballistic missiles at an American base in Jordan. The US military said it struck dozens of Islamic Revolutionary Guard targets in Iran, including military command centres and drone facilities, in a two-hour operation launched after Tehran fired ballistic missiles at US forces in the Middle East. The operation began at 0000 GMT and ended at 0200 GMT on Thursday.

The US launched new strikes against Iran, hitting dozens of military targets. It described the attack as a "heavy wave" but there were no indications it targeted key bridges or other civilian infrastructure that would signal a major escalation.

The US military said it completed a "heavy wave of strikes" against Iran, with explosions heard across southern Iran, including Bandar Abbas and the Persian Gulf island of Kish, Iranian state media reported.

Iranian media reported that three civilians were killed in the latest strikes. Iran's Revolutionary Guard said four of its members, who serve as military advisors with the militias, were killed in those strikes.

*Assessment: [VERIFIED] The five-day strike pause is definitively over. The US strikes were retaliatory but deliberately calibrated — targeting military infrastructure rather than civilian bridges or critical infrastructure. This suggests measured escalation. The proximity of strikes to various Iranian provinces indicates comprehensive targeting of IRGC assets.*

### 2. CRITICAL: Iran Vows "Punishment Today" — IRGC Claims Full Control of Hormuz [OFFICIAL]

Iran vowed to "punish" the United States on Thursday after the American military said it had completed a "heavy wave of strikes" overnight, the latest retaliation for an attack by Tehran on US forces in the Middle East.

The IRGC said on Thursday that it "will punish the aggressor today," adding that countries aiding the US in the conflict would also "receive a harsh response." The IRGC also said it maintains "full control" of the Strait of Hormuz, and that "a stranger who has come from thousands of kilometres away will not be allowed to interfere."

The Iranian military in the early hours of Thursday hit the Ali Al Salem air base in Kuwait housing US forces, destroying two drone hangars and a fuel storage facility, in response to US attacks on Iran. "Today, as part of Operation Nasr-2 and the punishment of the aggressor, IRGC forces attacked the US Ali Al Salem air base, destroying two drone hangars and a fuel storage facility for aircraft and military helicopters," the IRGC said.

In an earlier statement, the IRGC said two oil tankers under US escort attempted to exit the Strait of Hormuz via an "unsafe" southern route on Wednesday evening but turned back. It said a severe fire broke out on one of the ships, forcing both to retreat. It added the tankers were operating with US aircraft support.

*Assessment: [OFFICIAL] Iran's explicit threat of same-day retaliation and its attack on US facilities in Kuwait mark a significant escalation in rhetoric and action. The IRGC's claim of "full control" over Hormuz and its assertion that tankers attempting the southern route were forced to retreat underscores Tehran's continued defiance despite Qatar's successful transit.*

### 3. CRITICAL: Conflict Expands Geographically — Egypt, Kuwait, Jordan Hit [VERIFIED]

**Egypt (First Attack on Egyptian Soil):**
A drone struck a US-owned liquefied natural gas vessel at Egypt's Mediterranean port of Damietta on Wednesday, triggering a fire that spread to a second ship, in what would be the first attack on Egyptian soil since the war between Iran and the US began in February. Egypt's cabinet confirmed on Thursday morning that "preliminary investigations by the relevant authorities found that the incident was caused by a drone."

The two affected vessels — a storage tanker and a regasification unit — were moved offshore as authorities assessed the damage. Security company Ambrey confirmed the US-owned Energos Winter, a floating storage and regasification unit, was struck by a drone.

No party has claimed responsibility. There are three candidate perpetrators: Hezbollah, firing at a range of 300 miles, Iraqi Iranian-controlled PMF forces firing from Iraq at 750 miles range, and the Houthis firing from launch sites in Hajjah or Amran Governorates at a range of 1,250 miles.

**Kuwait (Fatal Strike):**
An Iranian strike on a Chinese company's building in northern Kuwait killed one worker and caused significant damage.

**Jordan (Second Day of Interceptions):**
The Jordanian Armed Forces said air defenses intercepted five missiles launched from Iran toward the kingdom. The military said the attempt to target Jordanian territory was stopped.

*Assessment: [VERIFIED] The conflict's geographic footprint has expanded significantly. The Damietta attack is particularly concerning as it threatens the SUMED pipeline route — one of Saudi Arabia's critical alternative export paths while Hormuz remains disrupted. The Kuwait fatality and Jordan interceptions demonstrate Iran's willingness to strike regional US allies.*

### 4. SIGNIFICANT: Qatar LNG Tanker Successfully Transits Hormuz — First in Three Weeks [VERIFIED]

Qatar has sent its first shipment of liquefied natural gas through the Strait of Hormuz since one of its tankers was attacked in the waterway more than three weeks ago.

The Al Areesh, which had been idling in the Persian Gulf since picking up a shipment from the Ras Laffan export facility in early July, sailed through Hormuz with its transponder turned on and was entering the Gulf of Oman on Thursday morning local time.

The departure is the first recorded exit by a QatarEnergy-controlled LNG carrier through the Strait of Hormuz since the Al Rekayyat was struck in early July amid escalating regional tensions.

Twelve commodity ships passed through the Strait of Hormuz on Wednesday, with six entering and six exiting. The number increased from the previous two days.

More than a dozen tankers are currently idling near Ras Laffan, according to ship-tracking data, indicating that they may be preparing to pick up shipments from the Qatari plant.

*Assessment: [VERIFIED] This is a significant positive development — a major LNG shipper successfully transited Hormuz for the first time in weeks with transponder on. However, the IRGC's simultaneous assertion of "full control" and claim that US-escorted tankers were forced to retreat suggest this may be opportunistic timing rather than a policy shift.*

*Assessment: [VERIFIED] Oil has spiked ~$8 in 48 hours (from ~$84 to $92.65). The Egypt attack threatening Mediterranean routes, renewed US-Iran strikes, and IRGC defiance are all pushing prices toward the psychologically significant $100 level.*

## Emerging Signals

**Egypt Enters the Conflict Zone:** The Damietta port attack is the first on Egyptian soil since the war began. The Saudis can redirect some oil through a pipeline that stretches across Egypt. But Riyadh would have to unload some oil at the pipeline and then retrieve the cargo on the Mediterranean Coast because the Suez Canal is too shallow for fully loaded supertankers. This attack threatens a critical alternative shipping route.

**Trump's Strategic Paralysis:** The same official warned the administration has "had a series of tactical victories," but is "facing a strategic defeat without clear policy guidance or a decision about where this is going." This increases the risk of reactive, escalatory decisions.

**IRGC "Operation Nasr-2":** The IRGC's naming of its Kuwait attack as part of "Operation Nasr-2" suggests a coordinated campaign of retaliation rather than ad hoc responses.

**Hormuz Contradictions:** Qatar's successful transit and the IRGC's simultaneous claim that US-escorted tankers were "forced to retreat" suggest selective enforcement — possibly allowing some traffic while blocking others.

**Conflict Velocity:** The pace of events — strike pause ending, IRGC threatening same-day retaliation, conflict spreading to Egypt/Kuwait/Jordan all within 24 hours — indicates the situation is accelerating.
