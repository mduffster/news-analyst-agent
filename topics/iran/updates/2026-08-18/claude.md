## Key Developments Since Prior Report (August 17)

### 1. Trump Posts Map of Hormuz as "New US Territory" — Declares No Talks Scheduled

**[OFFICIAL]** US President Donald Trump on Tuesday posted a map labeling the Strait of Hormuz "New US Territory," days after saying he planned to declare the strategic waterway part of the United States and threatening military action against Oman if it obstructed US efforts there.

**[OFFICIAL]** Trump said there were no conversations taking place with Iran, and none were scheduled, adding that the Strait of Hormuz was open. "There are no talks or conversations going on, or scheduled, with the Islamic Republic of Iran. The Naval Blockade remains in full force and effect. The Hormuz Strait is open and operating. All water mines have been removed or detonated," Trump said in a post on Truth Social.

**[OFFICIAL]** A White House official tells Shelby Talcott that Trump's Truth Social post is him calling off talks with Iran for the foreseeable future.

*Assessment: This represents a hardening of the US position. The "New US Territory" map is provocative symbolism, but the confirmation of no scheduled talks is the more significant development. Combined with Iran's statement (below), the diplomatic track has effectively collapsed.*

### 2. Ghalibaf Declares Strait Closed Until US Meets All MOU Conditions

**[OFFICIAL]** The Strait of Hormuz will remain shuttered until the US honors the terms of a now-expired framework ceasefire deal, according to Iran's parliament speaker and top negotiator, Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf. In a statement cited by Al Jazeera, Ghalibaf demanded that Washington lift a naval blockade of Iranian ports, unfreeze assets, remove oil sanctions, and halt "military operations on all fronts." Until these and other conditions are implemented, "the strait will not be opened," Ghalibaf said.

**[OFFICIAL]** Addressing the 180th open session of Iran's 12th Parliament, Ghalibaf said the time had come for Iran's military and diplomatic tracks to work together to respond to what he called the US "breach of covenant." "As we predicted, the enemy - who had accepted the ceasefire agreement out of desperation - quickly reneged on its commitments to compensate for its heavy political defeat in practice. Therefore, the time has come for the military field to once again stand alongside the diplomatic field and provide a practical response to the enemy's breach of covenant."

*Assessment: Ghalibaf's statement is maximalist — demanding full US compliance with all MOU conditions (sanctions lifted, blockade ended, assets unfrozen) before any Hormuz reopening. This is essentially a restatement of Iran's opening position, suggesting they see no path forward through negotiation.*

### 3. Ship Attacked in Strait of Hormuz — Crew Casualty Reported

**[VERIFIED]** A projectile has hit a ship sailing out of the Strait of Hormuz, according to the British military's UK Maritime Trade Operations center.

**[VERIFIED]** The UKMTO noted it received a report of a ship being hit with "an unknown projectile" as it was leaving the strait. The vessel's engine room was damaged, and a crew member was killed.

**[REPORTED]** The unnamed vessel was sailing eastbound out from the Persian Gulf when it was hit, amid claims by Tehran's top negotiator that the critically important sea route would remain closed pending the lifting of the US naval blockade and the unfreezing of Iranian assets.

*Assessment: This is the first reported ship attack since the MOU expiration. The timing — coinciding with Ghalibaf's hardline statement — suggests Iran may be signaling willingness to enforce its closure by force. A crew fatality raises the stakes.*

### 4. Iran Signals Shift to Offensive Military Doctrine

**[OFFICIAL]** IRGC Political Deputy Brigadier General Yadollah Javani told Iranian state media that Iran's actions so far had been defensive but could assume an offensive character in the future. Iran's military doctrine is signaling a potential shift from defensive to offensive actions. Javani warned that while current actions remain defensive, they could take on an offensive character in the future, with adversaries told to anticipate strategic surprises. The shift aligns with new commander-in-chief Major-General Ahmad Vahidi's emphasis on maximum deterrence.

**[REPORTED]** A senior Iranian official told Reuters that Tehran would escalate tensions in the Strait of Hormuz and the wider region if diplomacy with Washington failed. The official said Iran had decided to shift from a defensive posture to a "fully offensive" one and had given the United States "a few weeks" to fully implement the memorandum of understanding. The official noted that relying on pressure or mediators to secure a lasting agreement was no longer considered realistic.

*Assessment: Combined with the ship attack and Ghalibaf's statement, Iran is signaling it is prepared to escalate if the US doesn't meet its demands. The "few weeks" timeline suggests a potential flashpoint in early September.*

### 6. Congressional Response to Oman Threat

**[OFFICIAL]** Senator Tim Kaine said: "President Trump is increasingly deranged in his one-man campaign to threaten and initiate wars all over the globe. His wars are illegal since they are not approved by Congress, but he keeps on despite the massive toll on our troops, their families, the pocketbooks of everyday Americans, our stock of munitions and our alliances around the globe. His threat to waste even more of our dwindling weapons on Oman—a country the United States has a strong diplomatic relationship and a free trade agreement with—makes real adversaries like China and Russia rub their hands with glee."

**[OFFICIAL]** Kaine said that he would introduce the resolution as soon as Congress returns from the August recess. Senators are not scheduled to return to Washington until mid-September.

**[REPORTED]** War powers resolutions, though largely symbolic, have become Democrats' go-to tool for trying to rein in what they see as a far too trigger-happy administration. "At this point we may just need to file war powers resolutions for the 192 other countries," Rep. Ro Khanna (D-Calif.) told Axios.

*Assessment: The Kaine resolution is unlikely to pass (Trump would veto), but it signals domestic political pushback is growing. The Senate won't return until mid-September, limiting immediate impact.*

### 7. Iran Rejects Trump's Hormuz Map as "Delusion"

**[OFFICIAL]** "The Strait of Hormuz has been Iranian, is Iranian, and will remain Iranian," Iranian Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi said in a post on X. "This strait will only be closed and opened under Iran's command." Gharibabadi added that as long as the US does "not accept the reality of defeat" and indulges in its "delusions," Iran will continue to enforce its blockade of the crucial waterway.

*Assessment: Iran's response to the "New US Territory" map is defiant, reinforcing their position that they control Hormuz regardless of US claims.*
