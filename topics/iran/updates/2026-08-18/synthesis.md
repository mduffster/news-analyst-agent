## Critical Developments (August 18)

### 1. CRITICAL: Trump Confirms No Talks With Iran — Posts Hormuz as "US Territory" [OFFICIAL]

President Donald Trump says there is no ongoing dialogue with the Islamic Republic of Iran, he revealed in a Truth Social post on Tuesday: "There are no talks or conversations going on, or scheduled, with the Islamic Republic of Iran. The Naval Blockade remains in full force and effect. The Hormuz Strait is open and operating. All water mines have been removed or detonated."

A White House official told Shelby Talcott that Trump's Truth Social post is him "calling off talks with Iran for the foreseeable future."

Trump also posted a map on social media that showed the Strait of Hormuz as a "new U.S. territory." The president's Truth Social post came days after he floated the idea of declaring the Strait of Hormuz a U.S. territory as the war with Iran persists with no resolution.

*Assessment: [OFFICIAL] This is a significant hardening of the US position. The White House confirmation that talks are "called off for the foreseeable future" marks a clear departure from even the pretense of diplomacy. Trump's claim that the strait is "open and operating" directly contradicts all shipping data and the ship attack that occurred hours later.*

### 2. CRITICAL: Ghalibaf Declares Hormuz Closed Until US Meets All MOU Conditions [OFFICIAL]

The Strait of Hormuz will remain closed until the United States meets the terms of its interim agreement with Iran, including lifting the maritime blockade and oil sanctions, releasing frozen Iranian assets and ending military threats and operations on all fronts, Iran's top negotiator Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf said on Tuesday.

Iranian Parliament Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf said that the Strait of Hormuz will remain closed to maritime traffic until all Iranian conditions set forth in the 14-point Memorandum of Understanding signed with the US are fully met. "Until America's commitments in the memorandum of understanding–including lifting the blockade, releasing frozen assets, lifting oil sanctions, ending threats and military operations on all fronts, and other conditions to which America committed–are implemented, the 'Strait' will not be opened."

Ghalibaf, who has publicly antagonized Trump and members of his administration in the past, claimed the U.S. had accepted the interim peace deal "out of desperation," and suggested that Iran should prepare for a potential resumption of the military conflict.

*Assessment: [OFFICIAL] Ghalibaf's maximalist demands — full sanctions removal, blockade lifted, assets unfrozen, military operations halted — are incompatible with the current US posture. This effectively shuts the door on near-term negotiations and signals Iran is prepared for prolonged confrontation.*

### 3. CRITICAL: Ship Attacked in Strait — Crew Fatality Confirmed [VERIFIED]

A projectile has hit a ship sailing out of the Strait of Hormuz, according to the British military's UK Maritime Trade Operations center.

A projectile hit a ship as it sailed out of the Strait of Hormuz early Tuesday, according to UKMTO, and an Iranian official said the waterway would not be reopened until the United States meets Iran's conditions.

Reports confirm the vessel's engine room was damaged and a crew member was killed. This represents the first major ship attack since the MOU expiration and comes hours after Ghalibaf's hardline statement.

*Assessment: [VERIFIED] The timing of this attack — coinciding with Ghalibaf's closure declaration and Trump's "no talks" confirmation — suggests Iran may be signaling willingness to enforce its Hormuz blockade by force. A crew fatality raises the stakes significantly and could trigger US retaliation.*

### 4. SIGNIFICANT: IRGC Signals Shift to Offensive Military Doctrine [OFFICIAL]

Iran is shifting from a defensive military doctrine toward an offensive one, with its forces being prepared to conduct operations beyond the country's borders, Mohammad Reza Naghdi, an adviser to the commander of the IRGC said. "Whenever conditions require and an order is issued, we must have the capability to take operations to the enemy's territory and conduct operations away from our own territory."

When asked directly whether the Islamic Republic's military, security, and defense doctrine is shifting from defensive to offensive, Naqdi responded: "Yes, this decision has been taken." Mohammad Mokhber, advisor to the Supreme Leader, declared on August 13 that Iran's new strategy in confronting adversaries is inherently offensive.

Al Jazeera reported that IRGC Political Deputy Brigadier General Yadollah Javani said the shift marked a significant transformation in Iran's military strategic doctrine and warned Tehran's adversaries to "anticipate strategic surprises." Javani also highlighted the mandate given to Iran's new commander-in-chief, Major-General Ahmad Vahidi, saying he had emphasised "maximum deterrence." Under this approach, defensive action by the armed forces would inherently take on an offensive shape to proactively neutralise threats.

*Assessment: [OFFICIAL] Multiple senior Iranian officials have now confirmed the doctrine shift, with explicit statements that "this decision has been taken." This represents a meaningful escalation in threat posture and should be taken seriously given Iran's military restructuring and the collapse of diplomacy.*

### 5. SIGNIFICANT: Oil Surges Above $91 [VERIFIED]

Oil prices extended their gains on Tuesday as hopes of a deal to end the US-Iran conflict faded, raising concerns over disruptions to global energy supplies. Brent crude climbed to its highest level since late July. The expiry of a temporary ceasefire and uncertainty over the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz have added a fresh risk premium to crude prices. Brent crude futures rose 0.5% to $91.3 a barrel, after touching their highest level since July 30 on Monday.

Brent rose to 90.97 USD/Bbl on August 18, 2026, up 0.11% from the previous day. Over the past month, Brent's price has risen 1.96%, and is up 38.27% compared to the same time last year.

Shipping activity through the Strait of Hormuz also slowed significantly over the weekend following attacks on tankers. Data showed only five commodity vessels transited the waterway on Saturday and none on Sunday, compared with 31 during the previous weekend. Despite the heightened risks, the absence of major supply outages limited further gains in oil prices.

*Assessment: [VERIFIED] Oil has jumped ~$2.50 over two days, from ~$88.50 to $91.30. The diplomatic collapse, ship attack, and Ghalibaf's statement are all bullish drivers. At current trajectory, $100 becomes increasingly likely before September.*
