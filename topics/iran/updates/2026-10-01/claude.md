## October 1, 2026 — Day 216 Update

### Key Developments (Last 24 Hours)

**UK-IRANIAN DUAL NATIONAL ARRESTED IN RAF FAIRFORD TERROR PROBE** [OFFICIAL]

A U.K. Iranian dual national was arrested on suspicion of preparing to carry out terrorist acts in connection with an incident at an English air base used by the U.S., British counterterrorism police said Thursday. The 27-year-old man was arrested in Westminster, London, on Thursday afternoon on suspicion of preparation of terrorist acts under Section 5 of the Terrorism Act 2006. Police also questioned a 26-year-old British man and searched two properties in the area. The five men in their 20s arrested on Sunday remain on police bail.

Senior National Coordinator for Counter Terrorism Policing Vicki Evans described the investigation as "hugely complex," with specialist teams "interrogating multiple lines of enquiry."

**[ANALYSIS]** [OFFICIAL] The arrest of a UK-Iranian national represents a significant escalation in the investigation and appears to strengthen the Iran attribution. This arrest, separate from the original five suspects, suggests a broader network may be under investigation.

**ISRAEL CLAIMS IT PROVIDED UK WITH ADVANCE INTELLIGENCE ON FAIRFORD PLOT** [OFFICIAL]

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has claimed that Israel provided the UK with intelligence ahead of the suspected RAF Fairford plot. Five British nationals were arrested last weekend on suspicion of terrorism and explosives offences.

In an interview with Fox News, Netanyahu said: "We gave the Brits the intel that there was going to be an attack and that it's an Iranian-sponsored attack, and pretty much the next day they slapped sanctions on us."

Israeli President Isaac Herzog told BBC Radio 4 that he agreed with Netanyahu's assessment, saying "I confirm that I agree with PM Netanyahu on this issue" when asked about Iranian involvement.

However, Whitehall officials said the incident was not an "intelligence-led operation." Such operations involve Counter Terrorism Policing and MI5 tracking suspects and arresting them before they commit a crime.

**[ANALYSIS]** [OFFICIAL] Netanyahu's claim creates a political dimension — linking the intelligence sharing to UK sanctions on Israel. The UK denial that it was "intelligence-led" suggests either the warning was general rather than specific, or there are sensitivities about revealing sources.

**IRAN CONFIRMS RECEIPT OF US RESPONSE; PEZESHKIAN CALLS FOR "WIN-WIN" DEAL** [OFFICIAL]

Iranian officials indicated Wednesday they have received an official US response to Tehran's latest proposal to end the seven-month war, which US President Donald Trump had publicly rejected just days earlier.

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi presented the US response to President Masoud Pezeshkian at a Cabinet meeting, government spokesperson Fatemeh Mohajerani told the state-run IRNA news agency. The Cabinet meeting followed Araghchi's return to Iran after travelling to New York for last week's UN General Assembly.

Pezeshkian said: "We will make every effort to bring the agreement to fruition and will firmly stand up for the rights of the Iranian people," adding that any agreement with the US must be based on "a win-win strategy."

Araghchi last week said he floated the proposal to reopen the strait in a week if the US agreed to lift its blockade of Iranian ports, release frozen Iranian assets and waive sanctions on Iranian oil sales. Trump swiftly rejected the proposal, saying Iran wanted to make a deal because it was "losing so badly" but that its offer "would not be acceptable."

**[ANALYSIS]** [OFFICIAL] Despite Trump's public rejection, the formal response via mediators suggests the diplomatic channel remains active. Pezeshkian's "win-win" framing signals Iran is seeking face-saving language, but his emphasis on "the rights of the Iranian people" suggests domestic pressure to show results.

**TRUMP THREATENS POST-MIDTERM ATTACKS, SAYS "PEACE" WITH IRAN IMPOSSIBLE** [OFFICIAL]

Trump told TIME magazine that U.S. military action against Iran could resume after the midterm elections if Tehran and Washington fail to reach a satisfactory agreement, and that he doesn't think "you could ever have peace" with Iran.

Trump said "we lost 18" U.S. servicemembers in the war. He added: "But we have a nation that's been virtually destroyed — their economy is no good, they have 300%-plus inflation. Their Navy is gone, their Air force is gone, their anti-aircraft equipment is gone."

He claimed the U.S. has "virtually total control of the Hormuz Strait."

**[ANALYSIS]** [OFFICIAL] Trump is explicitly linking timing to the November midterms. The rhetoric about Iran being "destroyed" while simultaneously engaging via mediators suggests a "maximum pressure" negotiating posture — threaten annihilation while keeping the diplomatic door open.

**FLYDUBAI HIJACKING ATTEMPT: CO-PILOT STABBED CAPTAIN, TRIED TO CRASH ISRAEL-BOUND PLANE** [VERIFIED]

A FlyDubai flight from Dubai to Israel was forced to make an emergency landing in Saudi Arabia on Wednesday after a violent attempt by the co-pilot to seize control of the aircraft and crash it, passengers and Israeli officials said.

174 Israeli passengers were returned to Ben-Gurion Airport after multiple passengers helped restrain an Omani hijacker-pilot who attempted to crash the airplane.

Trump suggested the flydubai pilot who tried to crash the plane may be linked to Iran. Asked by reporters outside the White House whether the pilot is linked to Iran, Trump responded: "We're working on it. They're being very open with us. I would say the answer based on what I'm hearing is yes."

However, there are no indications that Iran directed or was involved in today's attack, a senior U.S. official briefed on the Israeli investigation told NBC News. Multiple sources told the Jerusalem Post that the Mossad, Shin Bet, IDF, and Transportation Ministry believe the hijacker was a lone wolf, not under orders from Iran or another terror group.

Netanyahu said "we've had signs that Iran, in particular through its proxies, was intending to heighten terrorist attacks against Israel and Israeli nationals abroad. But I think it's too early to say whether there was Iranian collusion here."

**[ANALYSIS]** [UNCONFIRMED/CONFLICTING] Israeli intelligence currently assesses this as a lone-wolf attack, contradicting Trump's suggestion of Iranian involvement. However, Trump's comments may reflect broader suspicion or political messaging rather than specific intelligence.

**IRANIAN RIAL HITS NEW RECORD LOW: 2.5 MILLION TO THE DOLLAR** [VERIFIED]

Iran's currency fell to a new record low on Tuesday, with traders in Tehran exchanging more than 2.5 million rials to the U.S. dollar in a stark reflection of how the war in the Middle East has steadily eroded the Iranian economy.

The latest decline came just 27 days after the rial hit its previous record low of 2.2 million to the dollar on Sept. 2. The currency has repeatedly reached new lows since the war began in February.

This marks a massive fall in the Iranian currency's value since the Memorandum of Understanding collapsed in mid-July, when the rial stood at about 1.79 million rials per one U.S. dollar.

Analysts noted the drastic effects of the blockade as early as Sept. 1. Iran has now gone nearly two and a half months without any new oil tankers reaching Chinese ports, its last main oil customer, stripping it of much-needed export revenue.

**[ANALYSIS]** [VERIFIED] The rial has lost ~40% since the MoU collapsed in July. Combined with the oil export cutoff, this represents severe economic pressure on Tehran. This may be forcing Iran's hand toward negotiations, but also risks internal instability.

**OIL PRICES DECLINE AMID DIPLOMACY AND SUPPLY RECOVERY** [VERIFIED]

Brent fell to 96.76 USD/Bbl on October 1, 2026, down 1.30% from the previous day.

Crude oil climbed toward $90.50 a barrel on Wednesday after dipping in the previous session, as reports that President Trump has no intention of easing sanctions on Iran raised concerns over exports. Iranian government spokesperson Fatemeh Mohajerani said Tehran had received a US proposal on reopening the Strait of Hormuz.

Despite the diplomatic uncertainty, Middle East crude supplies have recovered close to pre-war levels.

Crude supply worries have eased following the resumption of oil tanker loadings by Saudi Arabia from its Red Sea port of Yanbu after the kingdom restarted operations on its East-West Pipeline. "The pipeline has done much of the heavy lifting when it came to moving crude out of the Gulf."
