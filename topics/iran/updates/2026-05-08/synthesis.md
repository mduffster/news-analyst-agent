## Critical Developments Since May 7

### 1. US-Iran Exchange Fire in Hormuz — Both Sides Claim Ceasefire Intact

**[OFFICIAL]** Earlier, U.S. Central Command said three U.S. Navy destroyers were crossing the Strait of Hormuz when they came under fire from Iranian missiles and drones, and the U.S. launched "self-defense" strikes in response.

**[OFFICIAL]** Iran has accused the United States of violating their ceasefire by targeting two ships near the Strait of Hormuz and attacking civilian areas along the country's southern coast. US Central Command (CENTCOM) said its forces intercepted "unprovoked Iranian attacks" and responded with "self-defence strikes", while Iranian state media reported explosions on Qeshm Island and said air defence systems had intercepted several drones over the area.

**[OFFICIAL]** President Donald Trump, in a call with an ABC News reporter later Thursday, insisted that the ceasefire remains in effect, saying the strikes were "just a love tap."

**[OFFICIAL]** President Donald Trump said Thursday that U.S. forces "blew Iran away" after an exchange of fire in the Persian Gulf, accusing Tehran of provoking the clash. The president was referring to Iran's attacks on three U.S. Navy destroyers, which prompted an American counterstrike. "They trifled with us today. We blew them away," Trump told reporters before shifting to questions about the ceasefire. "I'll let you know when there's no ceasefire. Otherwise, you'd be seeing a big glow coming out of Iran," Trump said.

**[OFFICIAL]** Iranian foreign minister Seyed Abbas Araghchi accused the U.S. of launching a "reckless military adventure" following the recent exchange of fire in the Strait of Hormuz. "Every time a diplomatic solution is on the table, the U.S. opts for a reckless military adventure. Is it a crude pressure tactic? Or the result of a spoiler once again duping POTUS into another quagmire?" Araghchi said Friday on X.

**[VERIFIED]** U.S. Central Command (CENTCOM) said Thursday that American forces "responded with self-defense strikes" on Iranian targets after three Navy destroyers came under missile and drone fire from Iranian forces but were not struck. The U.S. targeted three Iranian ports along the Strait of Hormuz: Bandar Abbas, Qeshm and Bandar Kargan.

**Assessment:** This represents the most significant military exchange since the ceasefire began on April 8. Both sides engaged in considerable kinetic activity while rhetorically maintaining the ceasefire is intact. Trump's rhetorical minimization ("love tap," "trifle") appears designed to avoid formal escalation while demonstrating willingness to respond forcefully. This creates dangerous ambiguity where substantial combat can occur without diplomatic consequences, but risks miscalculation.

### 2. Rubio: US Expects Iranian Response Today

**[OFFICIAL]** Secretary of State Marco Rubio said Friday that Washington anticipates a response from Iran on the proposed U.S. peace deal by the end of the day. Speaking to reporters in Italy, Mr. Rubio was scant on details but said the public should know something more about Iran's response by the end of the day.

**[OFFICIAL]** Secretary of State Marco Rubio told reporters the administration expected a response from the Iranian regime on Friday, but that "only stupid countries" wouldn't respond to fire with fire when facing attacks like the U.S. did in the Strait of Hormuz.

**[OFFICIAL]** Secretary of State Marco Rubio said Friday that a clear red line has been conveyed to the Iranian regime: "If they threaten Americans, they're going to get blown up." Rubio made the remark after U.S. Central Command announced that "U.S. forces intercepted unprovoked Iranian attacks and responded with self-defense strikes." "If you were a missile launching guy, whatever they call that job, and you're sitting there and you fire a missile at the United States and we saw you fire it, we're going to hit you. Of course we are. Who doesn't do that?" Rubio said. "Unless you want to get your ship sunk."

**[REPORTED]** Iran has not formally responded, but officials indicate a large divide remains between the two sides.

**[REPORTED]** Iran's foreign ministry spokesperson said Tehran had not yet reached a conclusion on the emerging plan, which Reuters said centred on a short-term memorandum rather than a comprehensive peace deal, underscoring deep divisions between the two sides. The spokesperson said once a decision was made, it would be conveyed to the Pakistani mediators, and based on the results of this exchange of messages, the next steps would be determined.

**Assessment:** This is the clearest US statement yet on timeline. If Iran delivers its response today as anticipated, there will be significant clarity on the deal's trajectory. Rubio's comments justifying the US military response suggest Washington wants to demonstrate it won't be deterred by Iranian pressure while awaiting an answer. The parallel messaging — readiness for military action alongside readiness for diplomacy — reflects the administration's dual-track approach.

### 3. Iran Seizes Sanctioned Oil Tanker in Gulf of Oman

**[VERIFIED]** Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) claims to have captured an oil tanker, Ocean Koi, in a "special operation" in the Gulf of Oman, saying the vessel was attempting to "disrupt oil exports and the interests of the Iranian nation", according to state media. In a statement carried by the Fars news agency, a spokesperson from the army said the Iranian Navy's "rangers and marines directed the offending tanker to the southern coast of the country".

**[VERIFIED]** It said the Barbados-flagged tanker was carrying Iranian oil and "was trying to harm and disrupt oil exports... by exploiting regional conditions". The oil tanker - which has been under U.S. sanctions since February - was escorted to the southern coast of Iran and handed over to the judicial authorities, according to state media.

**[VERIFIED]** Iranian media reported the vessel involved as being the Ocean Koi, which maritime security company Vanguard Tech identified as the Barbados-flag, 72,000 dwt, Jin Li, which was previously named Ocean Koi. The Ocean Koi was sanctioned by OFAC as part of the shadow fleet transporting Iranian petroleum.

**[REPORTED]** In February, the US treasury department placed sanctions on the Barbados-flagged tanker, which it said has since 2020 been part of Iran's so-called "shadow fleet" – a network of vessels used to transport oil while evading sanctions. The tanker "has transported millions of barrels of Iranian high-sulphur fuel oil (HSFO) and condensate since May 2025."

**Assessment:** This is an unusual seizure. Iran appears to have captured a vessel carrying its own sanctioned oil, suggesting it may have been operating outside Tehran's direct control as part of the "shadow fleet." This demonstrates Iran's determination to control all maritime activity in the region — even vessels nominally serving Iranian interests — and signals Tehran's assertive posture on maritime enforcement regardless of diplomatic progress.

### 4. Israel-Lebanon Talks Confirmed for May 14-15

**[OFFICIAL]** Lebanon and Israel are expected to hold a new round of negotiations in Washington on May 14-15 aimed at reaching a peace agreement, a US official said, despite new Israeli strikes.

**[VERIFIED]** Netanyahu pledges continued strikes on Hezbollah: Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu claimed responsibility for the killing of a senior Hezbollah commander in Beirut the previous day, saying "no terrorist is immune" from Israeli attacks. Israeli air attacks on the towns of Doueir, Harouf and Habboush in the Nabatieh district killed 11 people, including two children, and wounded 36.

**[VERIFIED]** Israeli strikes in Lebanon have killed more than 2,700 people since March 2, including dozens since the April 17 ceasefire brokered by Washington between Israeli and Lebanese representatives.

**Assessment:** The diplomatic track continues despite Israeli military actions. The scheduled talks suggest Washington believes progress is possible, but Israel's continued strikes create friction with Iran's negotiating position, which has demanded a halt to Israeli operations in Lebanon as part of any deal.

### 5. Oil Prices Rebound on Renewed Tensions — Physical Market Stress Severe

**[VERIFIED]** At 9 a.m. Eastern Time today, oil was priced at $104.07 per barrel with Brent serving as the benchmark. That's a gain of $3.62 compared with yesterday morning and around $40 higher than the price one year ago.

**[VERIFIED]** Despite the rally in crude oil, Friday was set to end the week in the red. For the week both Brent and WTI were both down by about 6% after earlier hopes of an impending ceasefire eased off supply disruption fears.

**[REPORTED]** Recently, the spread between Dated Brent and the front-month contract has widened substantially, "reflecting emerging shortages in physical supply". "Demand for immediate crude delivery has helped push Dated Brent prices to well above $130 per barrel, while other Middle Eastern grades also achieved prices above $135 per barrel, reflecting the shortage of available crude for refining." The BMI analysts said the recent widening highlights the severe stress crude buyers are facing.

**[REPORTED]** Global oil supply is expected to remain muted even if restrictions in the Strait of Hormuz are lifted. Resumption of production capacity in GCC states is due to be slow due to damage and insurers are reluctant to service tankers crossing the Strait following reports of strikes in the region.

**[REPORTED]** Separately, the IEA warned that the war was disrupting roughly 14 million barrels per day of global oil supply and noted that any post-conflict production recovery would likely proceed gradually.

**Assessment:** Markets are whipsawing between deal optimism and military escalation concerns. The gap between futures prices (~$100-104) and physical crude prices ($130+) indicates severe physical market stress that will persist regardless of diplomatic progress. Even a deal will take months to normalize shipping, and GCC infrastructure damage means supply recovery will be gradual.

## Emerging Analysis: The "Ceasefire-Plus-Combat" Paradox

**Today's developments reveal a new operational framework: significant military exchanges occurring within a nominally-intact ceasefire.** This creates several implications:

1. **Rhetorical flexibility** — Trump's "love tap" framing allows substantial kinetic activity to be dismissed as non-escalatory, preserving diplomatic space but risking miscalculation

2. **Enforcement as negotiation** — Both sides are using tactical military actions (US blockade enforcement, Iranian tanker seizure) as leverage rather than combat

3. **Divergent timelines** — Diplomatic track (days) operates independently of military track (ongoing), creating potential for disconnect

4. **Physical vs. paper market divergence** — "Recent price action in Brent futures continues to reflect tension between physical tightness and shifting geopolitical expectations," with volatility reinforcing the view "that futures prices are struggling to provide a stable signal of underlying market conditions."

**This pattern may be sustainable short-term but carries inherent fragility.** The question is whether today's Iranian response creates a pathway toward formalization — or whether continued tactical friction erodes the diplomatic foundation.
