### Critical New Developments (May 20-21)

#### 1. Senate Advances War Powers Resolution — First GOP Defection Wave

**[VERIFIED]** The Senate voted 50-47 on Tuesday to move forward with a resolution to force President Donald Trump to end the war in Iran, a breakthrough for the Democratic-led effort.

**[VERIFIED]** Four Republicans — Sens. Susan Collins of Maine, Lisa Murkowski of Alaska, Rand Paul of Kentucky and Bill Cassidy of Louisiana — joined the bulk of Democrats in favor of advancing the resolution. Sen. John Fetterman of Pennsylvania was the sole Democrat to oppose.

**[VERIFIED]** Sen. Bill Cassidy, R-La., who just lost his primary for renomination over the weekend after he faced opposition from Trump, voted "yes" to advance the measure, the first time he has done so. Cassidy stated: "While I support the administration's efforts to dismantle Iran's nuclear program, the White House and Pentagon have left Congress in the dark on Operation Epic Fury."

**[VERIFIED]** The resolution, led by Democratic Sen. Tim Kaine of Virginia, would direct the president to "remove the United States Armed Forces from hostilities within or against Iran, unless explicitly authorized by a declaration of war or a specific authorization for use of military force." The vote marked only a first step in the Senate.

**[VERIFIED]** Three Republicans — Sens. John Cornyn of Texas, Tommy Tuberville of Alabama and Thom Tillis of North Carolina — did not vote, tipping the scales in Democrats' favor for the first time since they began bringing war powers resolutions.

**Assessment:** This is a significant political development. While the resolution faces further votes and a near-certain Trump veto, the breakthrough signals eroding GOP unity on the war. Cassidy's flip after his primary loss demonstrates the political cost of challenging Trump but also shows defeated members may become freer actors. Combined with Massie's defeat yesterday, the picture is mixed: Trump can eliminate war critics in primaries, but those he defeats may then vote against him.

#### 2. Supreme Leader Khamenei: Enriched Uranium Cannot Leave Iran

**[REPORTED]** Iran's Supreme Leader has issued a directive that the country's near-weapons-grade uranium should not be sent abroad, two senior Iranian sources said, hardening Tehran's stance on one of the main US demands at peace talks. Ayatollah Mojtaba Khamenei's order could further frustrate US President Donald Trump and complicate talks on ending the US-Israeli war on Iran.

**[REPORTED]** "The Supreme Leader's directive, and the consensus within the establishment, is that the stockpile of enriched uranium should not leave the country," said one of the two Iranian sources. Iran's top officials believe that sending the material abroad would leave the country more vulnerable to future attacks by the United States and Israel.

**[REPORTED]** Israeli officials have told Reuters that Trump has assured Israel that Iran's stockpile of highly enriched uranium will be sent out of Iran and that any peace deal must include a clause on this.

**[REPORTED]** Reuters, citing two unnamed senior Iranian sources, reported Iranian Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei has issued a directive that the country's near-weapons-grade uranium stockpile must not be sent abroad. But Iranian state and state-affiliated media have yet to report on such a demand, and a US official said the directive had not been communicated to the White House as of Thursday morning.

**[REPORTED]** Before the war, Iran signalled willingness to ship out half of its stockpile of uranium, which has been enriched to 60%, a level far higher than what is needed for civilian uses.

**Assessment:** If accurate, this directive is a major obstacle to any deal. Trump has assured Israel the uranium will leave Iran; Khamenei has reportedly ordered it cannot. This represents a fundamental, non-bridgeable gap unless one side retreats. The timing—just as negotiations enter a critical phase—may be deliberate positioning or may reflect genuine hardening as war-damaged Iran calculates its long-term security needs.

#### 3. Trump-Netanyahu "Tense" Phone Call — Clear Divergence on Strategy

**[REPORTED]** President Trump and Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu discussed a new effort to reach a deal with Iran in a difficult call on Tuesday, three sources said, with one source saying Netanyahu's "hair was on fire" after the call. A revised peace memo was drafted by Qatar and Pakistan with input from the other regional mediators to try to bridge the gaps between the U.S. and Iran.

**[REPORTED]** Netanyahu is highly skeptical about the negotiations and wants to resume the war to further degrade Iran's military capabilities and weaken the regime by destroying its critical infrastructure.

**[REPORTED]** During the hour-long conversation, Netanyahu pushed for a resumption of military action, an Israeli source familiar with the matter said. The divergence was clear: Trump wants to see if a deal can be reached, but Netanyahu was expecting something else, an Israeli official said.

**[REPORTED]** Trump told Netanyahu that the mediators were working on a "letter of intent" that both the U.S. and Iran would sign to formally end the war and launch a 30-day period of negotiations on issues like Iran's nuclear program and opening of the Strait of Hormuz. Two Israeli sources said the two leaders were in disagreement about the way forward.

**[OFFICIAL]** "He's fine. He'll do whatever I want him to do," Trump said when asked about Netanyahu. "He's a very good man."

**[REPORTED]** Netanyahu has argued that a delay only benefits the Iranians, according to Trump officials and Israeli sources. Netanyahu made his disappointment known on Tuesday, telling Trump that he believed delaying the expected attacks was a mistake and that the president should continue as planned. An Israeli source said Netanyahu is skeptical talks will produce an agreement, particularly because, to date, Iran has refused to give up its enriched uranium.

**Assessment:** The US-Israel coordination that characterized Operation Epic Fury is fraying. Netanyahu wants war resumed; Trump wants a deal. Trump's claim Netanyahu will "do whatever I want" downplays real tension. The Axios report of Netanyahu's "hair on fire" and the Khamenei uranium directive together suggest that even if Trump and Iran reach a framework, Israel may view it as inadequate and act independently—consistent with yesterday's CNN reporting.

#### 4. IRGC Threatens War "Beyond the Region"

**[OFFICIAL]** The Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) issued a statement on Wednesday, May 20, threatening the United States and Israel that the scope of the war would expand beyond the region should they launch another attack on Iran. In the statement, the IRGC claimed that despite deploying "all of their capabilities," the United States and Israel have failed to achieve their objectives, while the Islamic Republic has yet to fully bring its own capacities into the battlefield.

**[OFFICIAL]** "If the aggression against Iran is repeated, the promised regional war will this time spread far beyond the region, and our devastating blows will crush you," the Guards said in a statement on their website Sepah News.

**[OFFICIAL]** The IRGC also warned that if the attacks are repeated, "crushing blows" would be delivered at locations that, according to the military entity, the opposing side "cannot even imagine."

**Assessment:** The IRGC threat of "extra-regional" war is new escalatory rhetoric. While partly posturing, it signals Iran may be planning asymmetric responses outside the Middle East if hostilities resume—potentially targeting US interests globally through proxies or cyber operations. This raises the stakes for any resumed US strikes.

#### 5. Oil Plunges on Hormuz Transit, Deal Hopes

**[VERIFIED]** By 9 a.m. Eastern Time today, oil had reached $108.76 per barrel, measured using the Brent benchmark. That's $1.58 less than it cost yesterday morning and about $44 above its price a year earlier.

**[VERIFIED]** Brent fell to 105.54 USD/Bbl on May 20, 2026, down 5.16% from the previous day. Over the past month, Brent's price has risen 7.17%, and is up 62.60% compared to the same time last year.

**[VERIFIED]** Brent crude oil futures fell more than 5% to the $105 per barrel mark on Wednesday on fresh hope that supply from the Middle East could be gradually restored. An agreement would likely end each country's naval blockade of commercial vessels crossing the Strait of Hormuz. Optimism on supply was magnified by satellite data showing three supertankers crossing the Strait.

**[REPORTED]** Abu Dhabi National Oil Co.'s CEO said full recovery in Middle Eastern oil flows is unlikely before late 2027.

**Assessment:** Oil's sharp decline—from $112.93 on Monday to the $105-108 range—represents significant relief rally on combined optimism: tanker transits, Trump's delayed attack, and deal speculation. However, the fundamental impasses (uranium, Netanyahu's war aims) suggest this relief may be premature. The ADNOC CEO's warning about late 2027 recovery indicates the market damage is already substantial regardless of diplomatic outcome.

### Key Emerging Issues

1. **Khamenei's Uranium Directive** — If the Supreme Leader has truly ordered that enriched uranium cannot leave Iran, this is a structural obstacle to any deal Trump can sell to Netanyahu and Congress. The timing suggests Iran may be positioning for long-term threshold status rather than complete denuclearization.

2. **Senate War Powers Breakthrough** — The 50-47 vote shows erosion in GOP support for the war. While a veto is certain, continued votes could shift public perception and constrain Trump's freedom of action. Watch for whether more Republicans defect on subsequent votes.

3. **Trump-Netanyahu Rift** — The divergence between Trump's deal-seeking and Netanyahu's war-resumption preference creates a new dynamic. Netanyahu may face a choice: accept a deal he considers inadequate or act independently and risk alienating the US.

4. **IRGC "Extra-Regional" Threats** — The explicit threat to expand conflict beyond the Middle East is new. This could indicate planning for asymmetric responses through proxies, cyber operations, or attacks on US interests globally.

5. **Oil Market as Peace Indicator** — The sharp Brent decline (now near $105) reflects market assessment that some resolution is emerging. Tanker transits through Hormuz suggest de facto normalization even without formal agreement.

### Bottom Line

**The diplomatic picture has clarified—and the news is not good for a comprehensive deal.**

Khamenei's reported directive that Iran's enriched uranium must remain in the country directly contradicts Trump's assurances to Israel. This gap is fundamental and may be unbridgeable absent regime change or capitulation by one side.

**The Trump-Netanyahu coordination has visibly frayed.** Netanyahu pushed for resumed attacks; Trump delayed them. Netanyahu's "hair was on fire" after their call. Trump publicly claimed Netanyahu "will do whatever I want"—a statement that reads as wishful thinking given CNN's reports of Israeli independent strike preparations.

**Congressional restraint is emerging, tentatively.** The Senate's first successful vote to advance war powers legislation shows the political cost of prolonged conflict is mounting. Cassidy's defection after his primary loss demonstrates a potential pattern: Trump can defeat war critics, but defeated critics may then vote against him.

**Oil markets are pricing in resolution prematurely.** The $5+ decline from Monday's peak reflects hope, not reality. The Khamenei directive, IRGC threats, and Trump-Netanyahu tensions all suggest the underlying conflict remains unresolved.

**The most likely near-term outcome is continued limbo**—neither war nor peace, but an extended ceasefire with partial Hormuz normalization, no comprehensive deal, and periodic crisis cycles as deadlines approach and pass.
