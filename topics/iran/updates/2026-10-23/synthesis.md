# US-Israel-Iran Conflict: Barrack Signals Strike Possible "This Weekend" as Istanbul Summit Urges Restraint; UAE Oil Spill Closes Beaches

## October 23, 2026 — Day 238

**Assessment:** The conflict entered a critical phase today as U.S. Ambassador Tom Barrack publicly stated strikes on Iran could happen "this weekend" — directly contradicting President Trump's pledge to hold off until after November 3. US Ambassador to Turkey Tom Barrack says there "absolutely" could be a US strike on Iran as early as this weekend. Tom Barrack, the U.S. ambassador to Turkey and point man on Syria, who does not have a direct role on Iran policy, told Israeli army radio in response to a question. The comment moved oil to $116 — a six-month high — and arrived hours before an unprecedented eight-nation summit in Istanbul issued a collective warning against escalation. Iran's detailed plans to target undersea cables if attacked represent a new dimension of infrastructure risk. Meanwhile, environmental consequences of the maritime conflict are becoming visible: a 160km² oil slick from a war-damaged tanker forced UAE beach closures. Israel's election in four days adds further uncertainty, with Netanyahu's regime-change directive and a West Bank shooting attack dominating the final campaign days.

## 1. Barrack's "This Weekend" Comment: Policy Shift or Loose Talk? [OFFICIAL/DISPUTED]

US Ambassador to Turkey Tom Barrack says there "absolutely" could be a US strike on Iran as early as this weekend. The comment, made in an interview with commentator Mario Nawfal, immediately moved markets.

Barrack stated: "The president is very clear. He's open and available and accessible to a deal. And we've been at the table for months and months and months," adding that "the nuclear proliferation and where we go with the enrichment and all those things are, are details, the punctuation in the chapter that needs to be finished."

On the scope of any action, Barrack said: "The consequence won't be regime change. The United States, the president of the United States has said he's not interested in regime change. He's interested in rebalancing so that there's no hostages, there's no threats, and the world can calm down."

**Key limitation:** Barrack does not have a direct role on Iran policy. Analysts note this comment ranges from "deliberate signalling to loose talk that was walked back" in past episodes.

**Market interpretation:** "Strike warnings directed at Iran have historically repriced crude and safe havens in two distinct stages: the initial risk premium on the threat itself, which tends to fade if no kinetic event follows." The "crude transmission channel runs through supply-disruption probability rather than current flows."

**Analysis:** Barrack's statement creates immediate uncertainty about whether Trump's pre-midterm hold remains firm. The White House has not publicly walked back the comment. Whether this represents deliberate pressure on Tehran or an ambassador speaking beyond his brief, the market and regional actors are treating it as credible signal of compressed timelines.

## 2. Eight-Nation Istanbul Summit Warns Against Escalation [VERIFIED]

Turkish Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan cautioned against any renewed attack on Iran, saying such a move will exacerbate tensions and further expand the conflict there, adding that diplomacy and dialogue are the only way to end the war.

"We are all in agreement that a new attack on Iran would expand the war in the region," Fidan told a press conference after the meeting. "A military intervention on Iran is not in any way a solution. Nobody approves this at the moment."

Fidan met his counterparts from Egypt, Pakistan, Saudi Arabia, Germany, France, Britain and Italy in Istanbul. It was the first collective meeting between the countries, comprising the Regional Four (R4) and EU 3 (E3), alongside Italy.

French Foreign Minister Jean-Noel Barrot said renewed escalation between Iran and the United States would leave only losers and push oil prices higher.

German Foreign Minister Johann Wadephul said Germany, France, and Britain were ready to negotiate with Iran to rule out its acquisition of nuclear weapons, while calling for support for Saudi Arabia and Yemen.

The Istanbul meeting could mark a shift from regional consultations toward broader security coordination. It follows months of efforts by regional states to build cooperation against threats from the Islamic Republic.

The three European powers joined the talks with energy concerns. The platform could lead to a form of division of labor in the Iran war, operating in parallel with US and Israeli military pressure and focused primarily on containing the Iran-backed Houthis.

**Analysis:** The unprecedented R4+E3+Italy format — eight foreign ministers in one room specifically to address the Iran war — represents significant diplomatic mobilization. The unified warning against strikes arrives as Washington signals compressed timelines, creating a potential collision between U.S.-Israeli planning and international opposition.

## 3. Iran Details Cable Attack Plans; UKMTO Confirms Fresh Tanker Strike [REPORTED/VERIFIED]

Tehran has drawn up detailed plans to target undersea communications cables in the Gulf if the United States and Israel attack Iran again, two senior Iranian security officials told Reuters.

Yadollah Javani, the deputy head of political affairs for Iran's Revolutionary Guards (IRGC), said on Wednesday that Iran could target seven major internet cables beneath the Strait of Hormuz if Iranian infrastructure came under attack.

Undersea cables carry around 99% of global internet traffic, according to the International Telecommunication Union. But the global impact of such attacks by Iran could be limited as less than 1% of international bandwidth passes through the Strait of Hormuz, said Alan Mauldin, research director at TeleGeography.

These cables primarily serve countries in the Persian Gulf, meaning an attack would pose a more immediate threat to regional connectivity than to the wider global internet. Damage to submarine cables in the Strait of Hormuz could disrupt international connectivity in Bahrain, Qatar, Kuwait and the United Arab Emirates, although all four countries have alternative terrestrial connections.

**Fresh tanker incident confirmed:** A tanker was hit by an unknown projectile while transiting outbound through the Strait of Hormuz, the UK Maritime Trade Operations (UKMTO) said Friday. The incident occurred at 1715 GMT on Thursday, Oct. 22, approximately 7 nautical miles northeast of Limah, Oman.

"The tanker was able to continue its voyage to the next port of call."

Since July 6, 57 of 79 projectile strike incidents were reported along the southern route through the Strait of Hormuz that is along the coastline of Oman, according to UKMTO.

**Analysis:** The cable threat represents Iran's expansion of escalation options into digital infrastructure. While global internet impact would be minimal, Gulf states hosting U.S. military assets and financial centers would face significant disruption. The threat is calibrated to raise costs for Gulf capitals supporting U.S. operations.

## 4. Oil Spill Forces UAE Beach Closures as Environmental Toll Mounts [VERIFIED]

The United Arab Emirates reported an oil spill from a tanker off its coast on Friday. The authorities "detected a tanker oil spill affecting Umm Al Quwain's beaches," the Ministry of Climate Change and Environment said.

Martin Kelly, CEO of Orbital Risk Intelligence, told Reuters that the spill was coming from a tanker called the Gem No.2. "The slick is considerably larger, covering approximately 160 km2, and looks to be coming from the ship," said Leon Moreland, researcher at the Conflict and Environment Observatory.

Shipping sources said on October 9 that the Gem No.2 was struck by an unidentified projectile 13 nautical miles west of Al Jazeera in the UAE, causing a fire that was later extinguished.

People in the UAE have in recent days reported an acrid and strong fuel smell in the air. The ministry said there was no health risk from the odour, but advised the public to stay away from any oil that washes ashore.

**Analysis:** The Gem No.2 incident demonstrates how maritime attacks create cascading environmental damage weeks later. A 160km² slick affecting multiple emirates' coastlines and potentially reaching desalination intakes represents a new category of collateral damage from the Hormuz conflict. This may increase pressure on Gulf states to seek diplomatic solutions.

## 5. Two IDF Soldiers Killed in West Bank; Netanyahu Orders "Full Lockdown" [VERIFIED]

The army said two soldiers, Staff Sgt. Ron Yonatan Roytman, 23, and Sgt. Yuval Shai Svilem, 20, were killed and three others were lightly wounded during an attack by Palestinian gunmen. The attack took place near the Yehuda Junction outside the West Bank city of Hebron.

Israel ordered a "full lockdown" of the occupied West Bank Friday. A cordon would be imposed around the cities of Hebron and Halhul and Israel would consider "extending this model to other cities as well," Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said.

Israel began demolishing buildings and forcing residents to evacuate a neighborhood in the occupied West Bank on Friday, hours after the attack.

The incident comes just days before a crucial election that will decide whether Netanyahu can hold on to power.

**Analysis:** The attack four days before Israel's election creates immediate political dynamics. Netanyahu's muscular response — full lockdown, demolitions, promised "most intense" operation — serves dual purposes as both security response and campaign messaging. The timing will test whether security incidents benefit or harm the incumbent.

## 6. Netanyahu's Regime Change Directive and Election Context [OFFICIAL]

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said he instructed his country's military and intelligence leaders to pursue overthrowing the Iranian regime in Tehran. "Therefore, there is only one way to remove this threat once and for all: to topple the regime in Iran, and that is my directive to the IDF, the Mossad and the security agencies."

Speaking to cadets at an Israel Defense Forces graduation ceremony, Netanyahu said Iran is continuing to develop its nuclear arsenal, which Israel considers an existential threat.

Defense Minister Israel Katz warned: "We identify your attempts, we are prepared to thwart them, and ready to strike you with full force."

**Election context:** An October 21 polling comparison found Eisenkot's Yashar averaging 22.5 seats, compared with 19.75 for Likud. But the larger blocs remained close: approximately 50 seats for Netanyahu's allies and 53 for the Zionist opposition. Neither was close to a majority.

Polls open in Israel at 7am local time on Tuesday, October 27. Polls close at 10pm local time that night.

**Contrast with Barrack:** Barrack stated the U.S. is "not interested in regime change," focused instead on "rebalancing." This creates an apparent gap between Israeli and American objectives, though both support renewed strikes.

## 7. Oil Markets: Brent Hits $116, Six-Month High [VERIFIED]

At 7:15 a.m. Eastern Time today, oil was priced at $116.03 per barrel with Brent serving as the benchmark. That's an increase of $2.82 compared with yesterday morning and around $50 higher than the price one year ago.

Oil prices rose more than 1% as renewed tensions in the Middle East raised concerns about the security of global energy supplies. Brent crude futures reached $114.47 a barrel, while US West Texas Intermediate climbed to $100.84.

The latest gains followed a sharp rally on Thursday, when Brent settled at its highest level since May 4. Attacks targeting Saudi Arabia, maritime security concerns and reduced output at two Libyan oilfields added to the market's uncertainty.

Over the past month, Brent's price has risen 6.57%, and is up 74.24% compared to the same time last year.

**Analysis:** The market is now pricing both immediate strike risk (Barrack's comments) and structural supply uncertainty (ongoing tanker attacks, Saudi vulnerability). At $116, Brent creates acute political pressure on Trump ahead of midterms while straining global consumers.

## 8. Egypt Vote on Saudi Military Support Remains October 26 [REPORTED]

Egypt has called for renewed diplomatic efforts to resolve the Yemen conflict as its parliament prepares to vote on October 26 on a proposal to authorise military support for Saudi Arabia against Iran-aligned Houthi rebels.

President Sisi said Egypt's support would be limited to defending Saudi territory and its borders, rather than participating in attacks on Yemen.

Yahya Ali al-Rai, speaker of the Houthi-run House of Representatives, warned on Saturday against Egyptian involvement, urging parliament to reject the proposal: "Should Egypt slide down this path, it would pose a grave threat to its own national security and internal stability."

**Analysis:** The Egyptian vote — now one day before Israel's election — represents a potential expansion of the anti-Houthi coalition. Parliamentary delay suggests Cairo is weighing intervention costs carefully, but approval remains likely given Saudi Arabia's strategic importance to Egypt.
