### **ARAMCO EVACUATES DHAHRAN HEADQUARTERS — FIRST SINCE WAR BEGAN** [VERIFIED]

Saudi state oil company Aramco has told staff to evacuate its headquarters in Dhahran on the Gulf, six people with knowledge of the matter told Reuters on Thursday, as fighting between the world's largest crude exporter and Yemen's Iran-backed Houthis intensifies.

Aramco has ordered an immediate evacuation of its headquarters in Dhahran, eastern Saudi Arabia, instructing all remaining essential employees to work from home until further notice amid escalating attacks by Yemen's Houthi group, AFP reported on Thursday.

It is the first full headquarters evacuation since the U.S.-Israeli war with Iran began in late February 2026.

Three sources familiar with the matter, speaking on condition of anonymity for security reasons, said that most employees had already been working remotely since an attack targeted the company's headquarters last week. Only essential personnel had continued working on site before the latest directive ordered the complete evacuation.

**[ANALYSIS]** The first full Aramco HQ evacuation in the war signals a new level of threat perception in Saudi Arabia. This follows the October Houthi airport campaign (now 22 dead, 383+ injured) and claimed strikes on the Defense Ministry and Yanbu facilities.

### **IRAN HAS DETAILED PLANS TO ATTACK UNDERSEA CABLES** [REPORTED]

Tehran has drawn up detailed plans to target undersea communications cables in the Gulf if the United States and Israel attack Iran again, two senior Iranian security officials told Reuters.

One of them said Iran would aim to prevent repairs after any attacks to prolong disruptions to communications, including internet connections.

"If Iran is attacked, there will be no more red lines … from undersea cables to military bases, including covert ones," one of the officials said. The official warned Washington not to "play with fire", and said Iran had full access to the undersea cables and could easily destroy them.

These cables primarily serve countries in the Persian Gulf, meaning an attack would pose a more immediate threat to regional connectivity than to the wider global internet. Damage to submarine cables in the Strait of Hormuz could disrupt international connectivity in Bahrain, Qatar, Kuwait and the United Arab Emirates, although all four countries have alternative terrestrial connections.

**[ANALYSIS]** This escalates Iran's deterrence posture beyond energy to communications infrastructure. While the global impact would be limited (<1% of international bandwidth passes through Hormuz), Gulf states would face serious disruption. This threat is aimed at deterring both Washington and Gulf capitals.

### **EIGHT-NATION DIPLOMACY SUMMIT IN ISTANBUL** [VERIFIED]

Turkish Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan cautioned on Friday against any renewed attack on Iran, saying such a move would exacerbate tensions and further expand the conflict there, adding that diplomacy and dialogue were the only way to end the war. Fidan met his counterparts from Egypt, Pakistan, Saudi Arabia, Germany, France, Britain and Italy in Istanbul to discuss developments in the Iran war, attacks on Saudi Arabia by Iran-backed Houthi militants, and Russia's war in Ukraine.

It was the first collective meeting between the countries, comprising the Regional Four (R4) and EU 3 (E3), alongside Italy.

**[ANALYSIS]** This unprecedented eight-nation format reflects growing international concern about escalation. The gathering brings together key regional powers (Turkey, Egypt, Pakistan, Saudi Arabia) with European powers (Germany, France, UK, Italy) to push for de-escalation before the post-election window.

Iran will not reopen the Strait of Hormuz or return to negotiations until seven new conditions presented through intermediaries are met, Anadolu Ajansi reported, citing Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of the Supreme National Security Council.
