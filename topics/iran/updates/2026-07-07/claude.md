### Key Developments Since Yesterday

#### 1. CRITICAL: Iran Attacks Ships in Strait of Hormuz — Qatar Formally Blames Tehran

**[VERIFIED]** Two ships were struck by projectiles Tuesday in the Strait of Hormuz, the British military said. One of the ships was a tanker traveling off the coast of Oman that caught fire, according to the United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations center.

**[VERIFIED]** Iranian state television said the liquefied natural gas tanker came under attack after ignoring warnings but did not directly claim the assault. The second ship was believed to have sustained structural damage, but no one was injured.

**[OFFICIAL]** "The targeting of the Qatari tanker 'Al Rekayyat' as it passed near the Strait of Hormuz constitutes an unacceptable assault on the security and safety of international navigation and global energy supplies, and a grave and blatant violation of international law," Qatari foreign ministry spokesman Majed Al Ansari wrote on X.

**[OFFICIAL]** "We hold Iran fully legally responsible for this assault and any resulting damages or consequences."

**[REPORTED]** Axios reported that Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) fired at least two missiles at commercial ships transiting through the strait on Monday night. The report added that two ships suffered significant damage but there were no casualties.

*Assessment: This is the most significant escalation since the MOU was signed. Qatar has been a key mediator between the US and Iran, making this attack particularly damaging to diplomatic progress. The fact that Iran hit a Qatari vessel — not just a vessel using the Omani route — signals Iran is escalating pressure even on its regional partners.*

#### 2. Iran FM Warns Final Deal Talks "Will Not Commence if Threats Continue"

**[OFFICIAL]** Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi stated: "Para 13 of the MoU is clear: Negotiations on final Deal will not commence if threats continue. Honor your signature."

**[REPORTED]** The Iranian Foreign Minister said that final peace negotiations would stall if geopolitical threats persist.

*Assessment: Araghchi's statement is a direct response to Trump's renewed threat yesterday to "finish the job." This adds another layer of uncertainty to the 60-day window. Iran is publicly signaling it won't move to final deal negotiations while Trump continues threatening military action.*

#### 3. Oil Prices Spike on Tanker Attack — Reversing Days of Decline

**[VERIFIED]** Brent rose to 72.89 USD/Bbl on July 7, 2026, up 1.26% from the previous day.

**[VERIFIED]** Crude Oil rose to 68.99 USD/Bbl on July 7, 2026, up 0.64% from the previous day.

**[VERIFIED]** Brent crude rose toward $73 per barrel on Tuesday, reaching a one-week high after a fully laden LNG carrier owned by a Qatar state shipping company was struck by a projectile near the Omani coast. The incident renewed concerns among shipowners and raised questions over the durability of the US-Iran agreement aimed at preventing attacks in the strategic waterway.

**[VERIFIED]** Even so, oil prices remained close to their lowest levels since late February as growing supply expectations continued to weigh on the market.

**[REPORTED]** Saudi Aramco also cut the price of its Arab Light crude for Asian buyers next month by $11 per barrel, widening the discount to $1.50 below the regional benchmark amid softer market conditions.

*Assessment: The price spike demonstrates that markets haven't fully priced in Hormuz risk — any attack immediately moves prices. However, the fact that Brent is still only at ~$73 (vs. $117+ at war's peak) shows supply normalization has already taken hold. Saudi's dramatic price cut signals producers expect oversupply despite the attack.*

#### 4. Mojtaba Khamenei Still Absent as Funeral Moves to Qom

**[VERIFIED]** Khamenei's son and successor Mojtaba has still not appeared publicly since his father was killed in an airstrike as the US and Israel launched their war with Iran on February 28.

**[VERIFIED]** On 7 July 2026, funeral ceremonies for Ali Khamenei were held in the city of Qom. The ceremony began at 6:00 a.m. at the Jamkaran Mosque, where funeral prayers were led by Ayatollah Javadi Amoli.

**[VERIFIED]** Aerial footage broadcast by Iranian media showed the streets of Qom filled with large crowds of mourners, with reports describing the participation of millions of people in the farewell ceremony.

**[REPORTED]** On July 6, a flood of conflicting reports emerged from various individuals and media outlets regarding the presence or absence of Mojtaba Khamenei at his father's funeral procession. Mojtaba Khamenei was not present at the ceremony, yet his name was repeatedly invoked through slogans, narratives, and official as well as unofficial remarks.

**[UNCONFIRMED]** That morning, a pro-Islamic Republic journalist claimed that Mojtaba Khamenei was indeed present at the funeral. He argued that, contrary to popular belief, Mojtaba had not gone into hiding but was instead positioned in a spot hidden from view under special security arrangements.

*Assessment: The "hidden presence" claims are likely regime damage control. If he were physically capable, appearing at his father's funeral would be the single most important legitimacy-building moment for the new Supreme Leader. His continued absence through multiple ceremonies — now Day 5 — strongly suggests physical incapacity rather than purely security concerns.*

#### 5. Tehran Procession Completed with Massive Turnout

**[VERIFIED]** Tehran's streets were flooded by huge crowds today, as hundreds of thousands of mourners came out to watch the funeral procession of Iran's slain Supreme Leader Ali Hosseini Khamenei.

**[VERIFIED]** Helicopter images aired on Iranian state television showed a massive crowd stretching from Tehran's Azadi, or Freedom, Square for miles down a multilane street. The crowd appeared to be larger than the one that turned out for the 2020 procession for the late Revolutionary Guard Gen. Qassem Soleimani, which drew over 1 million people.

**[VERIFIED]** Monday, the climax of the funeral events in Tehran, saw the largest crowds yet on the streets of Iran's capital city. The nearly 20-kilometer-long (12 miles) procession route, stretching through the center of Tehran, was packed full of mourners.

#### 6. NATO Summit Opens in Ankara — Iran/Hormuz on Agenda

**[VERIFIED]** The 2026 Ankara Summit is a meeting of heads of state and heads of government of NATO member states, held in Ankara, Turkey, on 7–8 July 2026. It will be the 36th NATO summit and the second hosted by Turkey.

**[VERIFIED]** President Donald Trump said he's considering selling F-35 fighter jets to Turkey and plans to lift sanctions on the country. Ukraine and the Strait of Hormuz will also be up for discussion at the summit.

**[REPORTED]** According to Reuters, leaders are expected to say in a summit declaration that "Iran must never have a nuclear weapon and call on Iran to fully respect freedom of navigation in the Strait of Hormuz."

**[VERIFIED]** Leaders from all 32 member states of NATO will gather on July 7-8 in the Turkish capital of Ankara, including United States President Donald Trump. Friction around Europe's refusal to materially support the US in the Iran War, and Trump's threats to annex Greenland have also tested the group's unity.

#### 7. Israel Strikes Lebanon Continue Despite Ceasefire

**[VERIFIED]** An Israeli attack on a vehicle in southern Lebanon killed at least four people, according to Lebanese state media. The official National News Agency (NNA) said on Monday that a school principal, her mother, a foreign domestic worker and a Syrian citizen were killed when an Israeli drone targeted the car they were travelling in.

**[VERIFIED]** Israel has continued to launch intermittent strikes on southern Lebanon, particularly in the Nabatieh area, despite a two-week-old ceasefire agreement, saying it is targeting Hezbollah sites and fighters. Both sides have accused each other of violations.

### Emerging Signals

**Qatar-Iran relations strained:** This is the first time Qatar has formally blamed Iran for an attack since the MOU was signed. Qatar has been essential to the mediation process — attacking their vessel damages Iran's diplomatic infrastructure even as it asserts Hormuz control.

**Mojtaba's absence pattern now clear:** He missed his wife's funeral, the Tehran ceremonies, and now Qom. Tomorrow's Iraq ceremonies (Najaf/Karbala) and Thursday's burial in Mashhad are the last opportunities. If he doesn't appear at his father's burial, speculation about his physical condition will become the dominant narrative.

**Saudi Aramco's $11/barrel price cut:** Saudi Aramco cut the price of its Arab Light crude for Asian buyers next month by $11 a barrel to a $1.50 discount against the regional benchmark. The last two times the company offered the grade at a discount were during oil price wars in 2020 and 2015. This suggests Saudi expects oversupply despite Hormuz tensions — they're prioritizing market share.

**Iran's mine-clearing explanation:** A Tehran-based analyst told Al Jazeera that the Qatari tanker might have been targeted because it strayed into an area where Iranian teams were performing mine-clearing operations. "The area near Oman is likely full of mines." This raises the alarming possibility that Iranian-laid mines remain active in the strait, creating unpredictable hazards beyond intentional attacks.
