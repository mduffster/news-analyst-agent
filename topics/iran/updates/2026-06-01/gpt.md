## Daily incremental update — US–Israel–Iran conflict  
**As of Mon, June 1, 2026 (ET)**  
**New since Fri, May 29, 2026 (ET)**

### 1) The 60‑day MOU is still not finalized publicly; Trump held the decision meeting but did **not** announce approval
- AP reports Trump **held** the Situation Room meeting on **Friday, May 29**, but **had not yet made a decision** afterward on whether to proceed with the ceasefire-extension/Hormuz package. **[OFFICIAL]** ([apnews.com](https://apnews.com/article/cac5206df0f0c7b79fe9321c08d63096?utm_source=openai))  

**What changed / what it means:** The “binary” window that opened May 29 did **not** resolve cleanly into an announced deal. That increases near-term risk of drift back into coercive pressure/retaliation dynamics (because both sides can claim “talks continue” while preparing contingency actions), and it suggests either (a) unresolved U.S. internal redlines (notably on uranium disposition and mine clearance), (b) lack of confidence Iran will execute, or (c) a desire to keep leverage and avoid looking like a concession.

---

### 2) Lebanon channel: the Pentagon “security track” is now explicitly launched (DoD statement)
- A Defense Department statement (reported by Anadolu) says Under Secretary of Defense for Policy **Elbridge Colby hosted Israeli and Lebanese military delegations at the Pentagon** to “launch the security track” supporting talks. **[OFFICIAL]** ([aa.com.tr](https://www.aa.com.tr/en/americas/us-hosts-israeli-lebanese-military-delegations-at-pentagon-for-security-talks/3951593?utm_source=openai))  

**What changed / what it means:** This moves the Israel–Lebanon line from “talks reported” to a more concrete, U.S.-managed process. It doesn’t imply de-escalation on the ground by itself, but it modestly improves odds of **localized guardrails** (procedures, communications, potential delineation of zones) that can reduce accidental escalation—especially important if U.S.–Iran talks stall and regional fronts heat up.

---

### 3) Hormuz operational picture: “unrestricted shipping” still not visible; trackers/industry press still describe **effective closure / limited transits**
- An industry/live tracker claims Hormuz is still “effectively closed” as of **May 31** verification time. **[REPORTED]** ([straits.live](https://straits.live/?utm_source=openai))  
- ICIS (May 29) describes transits as still **limited**, even as ceasefire-extension talk buoyed sentiment. **[REPORTED]** ([icis.com](https://www.icis.com/explore/resources/news/2026/05/29/11212542/strait-of-hormuz-transits-remain-limited-as-us-iran-ceasefire-extended?utm_source=openai))  

**What changed / what it means:** The market/political narrative (“MOU will reopen Hormuz”) still appears **ahead of on-the-water reality**. Practically, that means: even if an MOU is signed later, normalization will likely be **phased** (mine-clearance milestones, insurance repricing, convoying practices), leaving a high vulnerability to single incidents resetting flows and prices.

### 4) “Nuclear dust” / IAEA element: still more commentary than confirmation; no clear IAEA operational buy-in surfaced in the new reporting reviewed
- Recent coverage continues to frame Trump’s uranium-handling concept as a major sticking point, with technical feasibility discussed in science-focused press—without confirming Tehran’s acceptance or an IAEA-agreed mechanism. **[REPORTED]** ([scientificamerican.com](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/trump-wants-irans-nuclear-dust-heres-how-the-u-s-could-remove-the-uranium/?utm_source=openai))  

**What changed / what it means:** The lack of a crisp, attributable IAEA/Tehran confirmation keeps this as a primary **implementation risk**: uranium custody/verification is exactly where “ceasefire extension” can fail even if both sides want time.
