### Executive Summary

**MAJOR DEVELOPMENT: Diplomatic setback as Iran suspends talks, then claims of resumption create confusion**

Today marks a significant deterioration followed by rapid claims of recovery. Iran on Monday suspended high-stakes negotiations with the United States to protest Israel's expanding military offensive in Lebanon, according to government-aligned media, complicating efforts to end the three-month war. However, the situation evolved rapidly throughout the day.

**Key developments:**

1. **[OFFICIAL] Iran suspends talks** — Iranian negotiators will stop exchanging messages with the U.S. through intermediaries in retaliation for ongoing ceasefire violations, Iran's state-affiliated news outlet Tasnim said. 2. **[OFFICIAL] Trump claims talks continue** — In another post, Trump wrote, "Talks are continuing, at a rapid pace, with the Islamic Republic of Iran." Trump later posted that after speaking with Israel and representatives from Hezbollah about easing tensions, talks between the U.S. and Iran were back on "at a rapid pace."

3. **[VERIFIED] Oil prices spike** — Oil prices leapt more than 7% higher following Tasnim's report. WTI crude oil futures surged more than 7% to above $94 per barrel on Monday after reports that Iran would suspend exchanges of messages with the US in response to Israel's escalating military operations in Lebanon.

4. **[OFFICIAL] Israel escalates in Lebanon** — Israel's government ordered strikes on the southern suburbs of Beirut on Monday, a day after its ground forces reached their deepest point in Lebanon in 26 years and as Hezbollah fired rockets at northern Israel including the outskirts of coastal city of Haifa.

5. **[REPORTED] Iran threatens Bab al-Mandeb activation** — According to Reuters, the agency also said Iran and the Resistance Front, which includes its allies in Yemen, Lebanon and Iraq, have set an agenda to completely block the Strait of Hormuz and activate other fronts, including the Bab El Mandeb Strait.

### Critical Developments (May 30-June 1)

#### 1. Iran Suspends Negotiations — Then Claims of Resumption

**[OFFICIAL]** "Given the continuing crimes of the Zionist regime (Israel) in Lebanon and considering that Lebanon was one of the preconditions for the ceasefire and that this ceasefire has now been violated on all fronts, including Lebanon, the Iranian negotiating team is suspending dialogues and exchange of texts through mediators," Tasnim reported.

**[OFFICIAL]** Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu on Monday ordered attacks on Hezbollah-controlled suburbs in Beirut, Reuters reported. Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi in an X post Monday morning said, "The ceasefire between Iran and the US is unequivocally a ceasefire on all fronts, including in Lebanon."

**[OFFICIAL] Trump's conflicting responses:**
- "It's an appropriate thing to say, because they're better negotiators than they are fighters," he said in a brief phone call. "But they haven't informed us of that." "It doesn't mean we're going to go and start dropping bombs all over there," added Trump. "We'll keep the blockade."
- "If they're over, they're over ... frankly, I thought they started to get very boring."

**Assessment:** The diplomatic situation is genuinely confused. Iran's Tasnim (IRGC-linked) announced suspension; Trump then claimed talks continue "at a rapid pace." This could reflect: (a) different channels remaining open, (b) Iranian posturing to extract leverage, or (c) competing Iranian factions signaling different things. The link between Lebanon operations and Iran negotiations is now explicit — Iran is demanding the US restrain Israel as a precondition for continued talks.

---

#### 2. Trump's Weekend Deliberations — No Final Decision

**[VERIFIED]** There still appeared to be no decision from President Trump as of Sunday afternoon on whether he would sign off on a potential peace agreement with Iran.

**[REPORTED]** A source with knowledge of the negotiations said Mr. Trump had made somewhat significant changes to the U.S-Iran memorandum of understanding, with those edits focusing on the Strait of Hormuz and the removal of highly enriched uranium.

**[VERIFIED]** Talks between Iran and the US have so far largely focused on the Strait of Hormuz, which has been largely closed to international shipping since the war began, Iran's enriched uranium stockpiles, sanctions relief and the terms of a lasting settlement.

**Assessment:** Trump's Friday Situation Room meeting did not produce a decision. He then sent amendments to the draft deal over the weekend, focusing on tougher terms for Hormuz and uranium. This delay, combined with Israel's Lebanon escalation, appears to have triggered Iran's suspension announcement.

#### 3. Israel's Deepest Lebanon Incursion in 26 Years

**[VERIFIED]** A joint statement by Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and Defense Minister Israel Katz said that following what they called repeated violations of the ceasefire by Hezbollah and the "attacks against our cities and citizens," they have ordered the Israeli military to attack targets in Beirut's southern suburbs known in Arabic as Dahiyeh.

**[VERIFIED]** The Alma center reported that Hezbollah launched 105 attack waves on Israel on this day, making it the most intense day of attacks on Israel since the beginning of the 2026 Iran war.

**[VERIFIED]** More than 3,400 people in Lebanon have been killed in the latest conflict, according to Lebanon's Health Ministry. Its figures do not distinguish between combatants and civilians. More than 1.2 million people have been displaced.

**[OFFICIAL]** The president said "all shooting will stop" between Israel and Hezbollah, the Iranian-backed group in Lebanon, after Mr. Trump said he spoke with officials from both sides of the conflict.

**Assessment:** The Lebanon front has become the key obstacle to US-Iran negotiations. Iran is now explicitly conditioning talks on Israeli restraint in Lebanon, while Netanyahu has ordered the deepest incursion in over two decades. Trump's claim that "all shooting will stop" between Israel and Hezbollah is unconfirmed by either side and may be premature.

---

#### 4. Strait of Hormuz: Still Effectively Closed, Now Threatened Again

**[VERIFIED]** Per IMF PortWatch, 4 vessels transited the Strait of Hormuz on 2026-05-24 at 4% of typical, effectively closed to commercial shipping.

**[REPORTED]** Twenty-nine of the 109 bigger vessels, those capable of hauling 700,000 barrels or more, which were stranded when the strait was effectively shut after the conflict started on February 28, have now crossed the chokepoint, shipping data compiled by Bloomberg shows.

**[VERIFIED]** Analysts note that even a near-term agreement would require weeks for demining, insurance normalization, and clearance of over 1,500 stranded vessels, with full recovery potentially extending into September. Market-implied odds near 75% for no normalization by June 30 reflect trader assessments that these structural and security barriers will outweigh incremental diplomatic progress in the near term.

**Assessment:** Despite some vessels escaping with US help, the Strait remains at ~4% of normal capacity. Iran's threat to pursue "complete closure" raises the prospect of conditions deteriorating further rather than improving.
