### 1. US Strike Kills Civilians at Wedding — MAJOR POLITICAL FALLOUT

**[VERIFIED]** Iran has accused the United States of bombing a house during a wedding celebration in the southern county of Sirik, killing at least five people, including a child. Esmaeil Baghaei, the spokesman for Iran's Ministry of Foreign Affairs, said the "brutal" attack hit a residence in the city of Kuhestak early on Wednesday.

**[REPORTED]** The Hormozgan governor's office on Wednesday identified four victims as four-year-old Amir Ali Karimi, 16-year-old Mohammad Malahi, 43-year-old Kolsoum Malahi and 43-year-old Zarkhatoun Taheri. More than 60 others were wounded, some seriously.

**[OFFICIAL]** In a post on X, US Central Command said it was carrying out strikes against targets of Iran's Revolutionary Guards following their "recent attempted attacks … against commercial shipping in the Strait of Hormuz and against American service members deployed to the region." A CENTCOM spokesman, when asked about reports of civilian casualties, insisted "the US military never targets civilians."

**[REPORTED]** Some Iranian media outlets estimated the tower to be around 140 metres from the wedding site. On Wednesday morning, the Red Crescent shared a video apparently filmed from above after daybreak, showing a hole in the roof of a house at the wedding site, with wreckage and rubble strewn across nearby alleyways.

*Assessment: This is the most politically damaging civilian casualty incident for the US since the war began. The killing of a 4-year-old child and wedding guests provides Iran powerful propaganda ammunition. CENTCOM's denial and proximity of the communications tower (~140m) to the wedding site suggests the strike hit its intended target but caused significant collateral damage.*

### 2. Iran Retaliates Across Four Countries — Most Expansive Strike Since February

**[VERIFIED]** Iran's military said it launched attacks on US bases and assets in Jordan, Bahrain, Kuwait, and Iraq's Erbil. Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) said its forces carried out a combined missile and drone attack on US facilities in Erbil province in northern Iraq in retaliation for the US strike. Iran claimed it hit the Sheikh Isa Airbase, which hosts US forces, in Bahrain. The IRGC said it launched a "heavy" ballistic missile attack on Prince Hassan airbase and a US Marine base in Jordan.

**[VERIFIED]** Jordan's Ministry of Defense confirmed that 13 missiles entered its airspace from Iran, targeting the King Hussein and Al-Azraq air bases. Three impacts were detected, but Jordanian authorities said the missiles crashed in remote areas and caused no damage. Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps announced it attacked a Marine Corps training facility in Jordan known as Camp Titin, killing several U.S. service members. Jordanian and U.S. authorities denied the IRGC's claim, asserting that there were no American casualties.

**[VERIFIED]** Sirens sounded in Jordan as air defenses intercepted 10 of the 13 Iranian missiles that entered the country's airspace, the Jordanian Armed Forces confirmed in a statement on Wednesday. The three impacts were in remoted areas, away from population centers, the statement added. There were no American casualties from Iranian attacks on US military bases in Jordan, two US officials told Reuters.

**[REPORTED]** Tehran said its attack on Kuwait set fire to a drone hangar and a ramp through which UAVs participated in attacks on Iran, adding that several US drones were also destroyed, and claimed to have killed a number of US soldiers. Kuwait reported intercepting drones and missiles but did not report any casualties.

*Assessment: This is the most geographically dispersed Iranian retaliation since February 28. Iran claims US casualties; US and regional allies deny them. The pattern of claims vs. denials continues. However, the scale of retaliation — four countries simultaneously — represents an escalation in Iranian operational tempo.*

### 5. G20 Concludes with Hormuz Statement — China Objects

**[VERIFIED]** The G20 chair's statement said the global economy remained resilient despite recent shocks but warned that disruptions to energy trade could threaten economic stability. The statement called for "free, safe and predictable navigation" through the Strait of Hormuz and said resolving wars and conflicts is essential to durable economic growth. All G20 members present agreed to the statement except China, which objected to four paragraphs of the document.

**[OFFICIAL]** U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said on Tuesday the United States is likely to announce sanctions on a bank this week as part of its economic campaign against Iran. "We are probably going to announce a bank sanction this week, and we will announce one the week after. We are speaking to our allies here, who have all come forward, and we have had a great show of support," Bessent said.

**[OFFICIAL]** In a message directed at the IRGC, Bessent said on Tuesday: "We know where in the British Virgin Islands your accounts are at these trust companies. We know the 100 million dollar houses you have around the world, and we are going to freeze those."

*Assessment: G20 produced a statement supporting free navigation through Hormuz — diplomatic win for US. China's objection to four paragraphs signals continued resistance to US framing. Bessent's public threat to IRGC personal assets marks escalation in economic warfare rhetoric.*

### 6. US Claims Record Hormuz Oil Transit — 17 Million Barrels

**[OFFICIAL]** More than 17 million barrels of oil transited the Strait of Hormuz by ship on Monday, a record since the Iran war began in late February, U.S. Energy Secretary Chris Wright told CNBC in an interview Wednesday. Oil exports from the region on Monday were higher than pre-war levels when Saudi Arabia's and the United Arab Emirate's pipelines that bypass Hormuz are included, Wright said.

**[OFFICIAL]** Iran is losing its ability to "hold the world economic hostage" as the U.S. military helps oil tankers sail through Hormuz, Wright said. Tehran is "causing some disruption but they are losing that card," he said.

**[REPORTED]** The U.S. government has provided data on oil exports through Hormuz that are higher than those reported by independent ship-tracking firms. Wright has said the U.S. military and Department of Energy have the best data as private companies often miss covert transits.

*Assessment: The US is claiming significant success in reopening Hormuz — 17 million bpd vs. pre-war 20 million bpd. However, private tracking firms report lower numbers. The discrepancy suggests covert transits are occurring. Regardless, this represents substantial progress from the 4.9 million bpd average in Q2 2026.*

## Bottom Line

**The conflict has entered its most dangerous phase since the initial strikes:**

The U.S. military hit targets in Iran on Tuesday and Tehran fired missiles and drones at various sites across the region in response as hostilities flare once again in an intermittent war that has lasted more than six months.

**Key dynamics:**

- **Wedding strike creates political vulnerability** — The killing of a 4-year-old child provides Iran its strongest propaganda win of the war. CENTCOM denials are unlikely to overcome the imagery.

- **Four-country retaliation is unprecedented scope** — Iran struck Jordan, Bahrain, Kuwait, and Iraq simultaneously. While largely intercepted, this represents operational escalation.

- **Trump's rhetoric is at maximum** — The "biggest attack waiting in the wings" threat and refusal to seek negotiation suggests either brinkmanship or genuine preparation for major escalation.

**The fundamental tension:** The US claims success in reopening Hormuz while simultaneously escalating military operations that make sustained commerce impossible. These objectives are in tension. Something will have to give.
