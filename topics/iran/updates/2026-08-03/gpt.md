## Daily incremental update — US–Israel–Iran conflict

### 1) Washington signals another “pause” in new strikes tied to claimed diplomacy; markets react
- President Trump said (late **Sat, Aug 1** / reiterated **Sun, Aug 2**) he would **halt/hold off on new planned U.S. strikes on Iran** to pursue negotiations aimed at winding down the war and reopening traffic through the Strait of Hormuz. **[REPORTED]** ([apnews.com](https://apnews.com/article/e61e88c14d8aca48c1c56368650baea5?utm_source=openai))  
- AP also published a deeper look arguing that **Trump has repeatedly announced halts while strikes later continue**, highlighting a pattern of stop–start signaling rather than a clean strategic shift. **[REPORTED]** ([apnews.com](https://apnews.com/article/670f077d2cf5de7614cfce4ad40e4aaa?utm_source=openai))  
- Oil prices reportedly **fell after the halt order**, underscoring how tightly energy markets are trading on perceived U.S. escalation/stand-down cues. **[REPORTED]** ([apnews.com](https://apnews.com/article/194ab3d3130bb44de445cf3d05954beb?utm_source=openai))  

**What changed / what it means:** Since July 31’s “no strikes overnight” window, this is more explicit: **a White House political directive to pause new major strikes** (at least temporarily) and frame it as diplomacy-driven. The AP “they keep happening” framing matters because it implies the pause may be **conditional, reversible, and not fully controlling operational realities**, which keeps escalation risk elevated even during “talks.”

### 2) Kuwait reports a new drone wave + a tanker strike in/near Hormuz (continued maritime pressure)
- AP reports that on **Sat, Aug 1**, Kuwait responded to **another round of Iranian drone attacks** that struck “vital facilities,” and **a tanker was struck** in the strategic waterway. **[REPORTED]** ([apnews.com](https://apnews.com/article/e61e88c14d8aca48c1c56368650baea5?utm_source=openai))  

**What changed / what it means:** This reinforces the direction already visible on July 31 (maritime/economic coercion + pressure on Gulf hosts). Even with U.S. strike restraint signaling, Iran (or Iran-aligned forces) appears to be **sustaining a parallel escalation ladder**: shipping disruption + host-nation stress. That combination makes a durable, messy “entanglement” outcome more likely than a clean taper.

Practically, the most plausible near-term “deal” looks like **a shipping/security understanding** (Hormuz) rather than a grand settlement—if it materializes at all.
