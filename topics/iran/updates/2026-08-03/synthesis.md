## Critical Developments (August 1-3)

### 1. CRITICAL: Trump Cancels "Biggest Attack Since WWII," Claims Deal Perimeters Agreed [OFFICIAL]

US President Donald Trump said he called off the "biggest attack since World War II" against Iran following a plea from Tehran and requests from US Gulf allies, adding that peace negotiations would resume on August 3, with a deal on the crucial Strait of Hormuz "imminent."

"We have just been asked by Iran, and other Middle Eastern Countries, to hold off any attack in that the perimeters of a deal has been agreed to," Trump said. The president said that the agreement would include the "Immediate, Complete, and Total OPENING OF THE HORMUZ STRAIT, and an end to Iran's nuclear threat."

Trump said leaders from the Gulf countries "much prefer a deal" as opposed to a potential refugee crisis. Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman was among the leaders requesting that Trump give diplomacy a chance, speaking on Saturday before Trump said the allies reached the "perimeters" of a deal.

The president said there is no deadline for the "denuclearization of Iran," as he described Iran eliminating its nuclear program.

*Assessment: Trump's pattern of threatening maximum escalation then pulling back has repeated multiple times during this conflict. The "biggest attack since WWII" language and "locked and loaded" rhetoric followed by cancellation creates strategic uncertainty. Saudi pressure via MBS appears to have been influential in the decision.*

### 2. CRITICAL: Iran Denies US Talks — But Confirms Oman Negotiations in "Final Stages" [OFFICIAL]

The U.S. president said late Sunday that he had called off "the biggest attack since WWII" so that talks could resume. Iran said Monday that no talks were planned.

At a news conference on Monday, an Iranian Ministry of Foreign Affairs spokesman said: "We are not going to host a delegation or be guests of a country during these days."

However, Iran simultaneously confirmed significant progress on a separate track:

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said negotiations with Oman concerning the Strait of Hormuz are in their "final stages" and are "on the way to being finalized," according to Iranian state media.

Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmail Baghaei said that Tehran and Muscat were close to reaching a deal on a new maritime route through Hormuz, separate from existing corridors.

Iran has reported progress in talks with Oman over a temporary safe shipping route through the Strait of Hormuz, but says an agreement between Tehran and Muscat alone would not be enough to reopen the strategic waterway. "The Strait of Hormuz was not closed because of differences between Iran and Oman," Baghaei said, blaming what he described as military aggression by the United States and Israel.

"Our proposal is for one route to lie entirely within the territorial waters of the Islamic Republic of Iran, with part of the other route also passing through Iranian territorial waters, so that Iran can effectively exercise oversight over both inbound and outbound traffic," Iran's Deputy Foreign Minister Gharibabadi said.

*Assessment: [OFFICIAL] Iran is pursuing a two-track strategy — denying US talks while advancing Oman negotiations. This suggests Tehran wants to establish Hormuz management on its own terms (with oversight/tolls) rather than as an American concession. The Iran-Oman track could deliver partial opening regardless of US-Iran talks.*

As of today, just 33 percent of Americans support the conflict, while about 59 percent oppose it. Just 28 percent of Americans said the war was worth fighting in the latest Washington Post/Ipsos poll.

Just 28 percent of Americans said the war was worth fighting. That's less support than the wars in Afghanistan and Iraq got in WaPo's historical polling.

Nearly eight in ten expect U.S. military involvement in Iran to continue for extended period. On average across 31 countries, 81% think their country should avoid getting involved in the Iran war.

*Assessment: [VERIFIED] Public support has collapsed to historic lows — lower than initial support for Afghanistan or Iraq wars. This creates significant domestic political pressure for either resolution or decisive victory, not indefinite stalemate.*

### 6. MODERATE: Saudi Red Sea Coalition — Houthi Standoff Continues [OFFICIAL]

The Saudis proposed leading the coalition, which would address threats in the Red Sea, its southern entrance, the Bab al-Mandab Strait, and the Gulf of Aden. Thirteen countries expressed support for the initiative, namely Kuwait, Bahrain, Qatar, Pakistan, Turkey, Egypt, Jordan, Yemen, Bangladesh, Nigeria, Sudan, Djibouti and Somalia.

Yemen's Houthi leader Abdul-Malik al-Houthi threatened Saudi Arabia with "full escalation," hours before Riyadh announced the coalition. "The indicators show that the Saudi side is heading towards full escalation, and we will respond to its full escalation with full escalation," al-Houthi said.

Neither the United Arab Emirates nor Oman have signed up, despite having similar security objectives amid the conflict centred on Iran.

*Assessment: [OFFICIAL] The 14-nation coalition is now formalized but has not yet operationalized. Houthi threats of "full escalation" create risk of Red Sea conflict intensification. UAE and Oman absence signals continued hedging.*
