# Daily Update: US-Israel-Iran Conflict
## May 22, 2026 — Day 84 of the Conflict

---

## Executive Summary

**Rubio reports "slight progress" in talks, but three structural obstacles harden: Khamenei's uranium directive remains in force, Iran pushes permanent Hormuz toll system with Oman, and House Republicans had to cancel a war powers vote they were about to lose; oil drops sharply to $104-105 as markets price in hope that may be premature.**

**Bottom line:** Day 84 reveals a paradox—incremental diplomatic momentum coexists with entrenching structural obstacles. US Secretary of State Marco Rubio said there was "slight progress" in mediated negotiations with Iran, as the prospect of a deal to turn a fragile ceasefire into a lasting peace agreement remains uncertain. Yet Iran is simultaneously attempting to institutionalize its wartime gains: Iran is discussing with Oman how to set up some form of a permanent toll system that will formalize its control of maritime traffic through the Strait of Hormuz. The Khamenei uranium directive reported yesterday remains in effect with no denial from Tehran. Meanwhile, congressional opposition is crystallizing—the House would have passed a war powers resolution if Republicans hadn't pulled the vote at the last minute. The most likely near-term trajectory remains extended stalemate: neither comprehensive peace nor resumed full-scale war, but an uneasy equilibrium with unresolved core issues.

## Critical Developments Since May 21

### 1. Rubio: "Slight Progress" in Talks — But No Breakthrough

**[OFFICIAL]** U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said Friday there was "slight progress" during talks with Iran amid uncertainty about whether a deal will be reached or war will resume. He spoke days after U.S. President Donald Trump said he was holding off on a military strike against the Islamic Republic because "serious negotiations" were underway.

**[OFFICIAL]** "I don't want to exaggerate it, but there's been a little bit of movement, and that's good," Rubio said at a meeting of NATO foreign ministers in Sweden, on Friday.

**[OFFICIAL]** Rubio said he did not want to exaggerate the progress, saying there had been "a little bit of movement and that's good." He said the conversations were ongoing. In recent weeks there have been repeated claims of progress, but a deal has stayed out of reach.

**[OFFICIAL]** Rubio rejected Iran's Hormuz toll proposal outright. "No one is in favor of a tolling system, it can't happen, it would be unacceptable and it would make a diplomatic deal unfeasible," Rubio says.

**Assessment:** Rubio's carefully hedged language—emphasizing he didn't want to "exaggerate"—signals that while communication channels remain active, no breakthrough is imminent. The pattern of repeated claims of progress without actual deals continues.

### 2. Iran-Oman Hormuz Toll System — Attempt to Institutionalize Control

**[REPORTED]** "Iran and Oman must mobilize all their resources both to provide security services and to manage navigation in the most appropriate manner," the Iranian ambassador to France, Mohammad Amin-Nejad, said in an interview with Bloomberg in Paris. "This will entail costs, and it goes without saying that those who wish to benefit from this traffic must also pay their share."

**[REPORTED]** The U.S., Europe and Arab Gulf countries warn that allowing Iran to charge tolls or assert lasting control would set a dangerous precedent.

**[REPORTED]** UAE's state oil company chief underscored regional concern: "Once you accept that a single country can hold the world's most important waterway hostage, freedom of navigation as we know it is finished," Sultan Al Jaber said on May 20. "If we do not defend this principle today, we will spend the next decade defending against the consequences."

**[VERIFIED]** Iran claims limited transit is occurring: Iran has claimed, without giving evidence, that 26 tankers and other ships transited between Tuesday and Wednesday with the help of the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps. That would be an unusually high number for recent weeks, but still far below pre-war levels of roughly 135 ships a day.

**Assessment:** This is a major strategic development. Iran is attempting to convert temporary wartime leverage into permanent institutional control over the world's most critical oil chokepoint. The involvement of Oman—traditionally a neutral mediator—suggests this is more than posturing. If successful, this would fundamentally restructure global energy security regardless of whether a formal peace deal is reached.

### 3. House GOP Cancels War Powers Vote — Would Have Passed

**[VERIFIED]** House GOP leaders abruptly canceled a vote Thursday on a resolution to limit President Donald Trump's war powers in Iran just as Republicans were on the verge of losing the vote due to absences.

**[VERIFIED]** House GOP leadership pulled a scheduled vote Thursday to rein in President Trump's military campaign in Iran after it became clear they did not have the votes to defeat it. It would have been Congress' first successful rebuke of Trump's Iran war effort after multiple Democratic-led war powers attempts had failed. Rep. Jared Golden (D-Maine), the one Democrat who has consistently voted against Iran war powers resolutions, was planning to flip his vote to yes.

**[VERIFIED]** Republicans struggled Thursday to find the votes to dismiss legislation that would compel President Donald Trump to withdraw from the war with Iran, delaying planned votes on the matter into June. But as it became clear that Republicans would not have the numbers to defeat the bill, GOP leaders declined to hold a vote on it. It was the latest sign of the slipping support in Congress for a war that Trump launched more than two months ago without congressional approval.

**[REPORTED]** Trump attacked Rep. Brian Fitzpatrick (R-Pa.) for his position: "He likes voting against Trump. You know what happens with that? It doesn't work out well." Fitzpatrick told Axios on Wednesday that he still intended to vote for the measure despite the president's threats. "We don't report to any party or any person here in D.C.," he said.

**[VERIFIED]** GOP leaders plan to bring the measure back up when the chamber returns from its week-long Memorial Day recess.

**Assessment:** This is a significant political development. Combined with Tuesday's Senate breakthrough (50-47 to advance), both chambers are now showing real momentum toward war powers action. The vote delay to June merely postpones the reckoning. Congressional constraint remains unlikely to directly force Trump's hand—he would veto any resolution—but the political costs of the war are mounting visibly.

### 4. Pakistan Mediation — Mixed Signals on Munir Visit

**[REPORTED]** Reports conflicted on whether Pakistan Army Chief Asim Munir traveled to Tehran on Friday. Initial reports indicated plans for a visit: Pakistan's Army Chief Asim Munir will travel to Tehran today as part of ongoing Pakistani mediation in talks and consultations between Tehran and Washington over the Iran war, Iran's ISNA news agency says.

**[REPORTED]** However, later reports indicated the visit was postponed: Pakistan Army Chief Asim Munir, who is acting as a key mediator between the US and Iran, would travel to Tehran only if the final formula for the framework sought by both sides is reached, according to reports. Munir's travel plans have been a topic of discussion since US Secretary of State Marco Rubio hinted on Thursday that there were "good signs" for a deal to end the war with Iran. However, reports later emerged that Munir called off his plans to visit Iran.

**[REPORTED]** According to Al Jazeera, the visit was called off at the last minute after Iran decided it needed additional time to review Washington's latest proposal aimed at ending the regional conflict and addressing nuclear tensions.

**[REPORTED]** Competing reports suggest the visit did occur: Pakistan's army chief Asim Munir has traveled to Tehran for meetings with senior Iranian officials, IRNA reported on Friday. A Qatari negotiating team arrived in Tehran on Friday in coordination with the United States to help secure a deal aimed at ending the war.

**Assessment:** The conflicting reports on Munir's travel status reflect the fluid and uncertain nature of the negotiations. Whether or not Munir made it to Tehran today, Pakistan's intensive mediation efforts continue. The reported postponement tied to Iran needing more time to review US proposals suggests the core impasses remain unbridged.

### 5. NATO Considering Hormuz Mission — July Deadline Forming

**[REPORTED]** NATO is discussing the possibility of helping ships pass through the blocked Strait of Hormuz if the waterway isn't reopened by early July, according to a senior official in the military alliance.

**[REPORTED]** The idea has support from several members of the North Atlantic Treaty Organization, but doesn't yet have the necessary unanimous support, said a diplomat from a NATO country.

**[VERIFIED]** Leaders from NATO countries will meet in Ankara July 7-8.

**[REPORTED]** The passageway has since become a source of tension between the US and its European allies in NATO, who refused to heed President Donald Trump's demands that they help reopen the strait. Trump has repeatedly fumed about the reaction and Washington recently announced it would withdraw 5,000 troops from Germany.

**[REPORTED]** A coalition led by France and the UK is also developing a plan to help secure navigation in the Strait of Hormuz once the fighting abates.

**Assessment:** NATO's July deadline creates a new potential escalation pathway. If diplomacy fails and NATO authorizes an intervention to forcibly open Hormuz, this would transform a US-Iran conflict into a broader Western-Iran confrontation. However, the lack of unanimous NATO support and European reluctance suggests any mission would be defensive/escort rather than combat operations.

### 6. Oil Drops Sharply — Record SPR Release Continues

**[VERIFIED]** At 9 a.m. Eastern Time today, oil was priced at $104.68 per barrel with Brent serving as the benchmark. That's a drop of $4.08 compared with yesterday morning and around $40.46 higher than the price one year ago.

**[VERIFIED]** Brent rose to 104.52 USD/Bbl on May 22, 2026, up 1.89% from the previous day. Over the past month, Brent's price has fallen 0.52%, but it is still 61.35% higher than a year ago.

**[VERIFIED]** Despite today's pullback, oil prices remain nearly 50% above pre-war levels, supported by ongoing supply tightness. In the United States, nearly 10 million barrels were withdrawn from the Strategic Petroleum Reserve last week, marking the largest release on record.

**[VERIFIED]** Despite Friday's gains, Brent futures were still down more than 4% for the week amid optimism that the conflicting parties could eventually reach an agreement.

**Assessment:** Oil's sharp decline from ~$110 on Tuesday to ~$104-105 today represents a significant relief rally driven by deal optimism. However, the record SPR draw—10 million barrels in a single week—signals how seriously the administration views the supply crisis. At current depletion rates, this is unsustainable. Markets may be pricing in resolution prematurely given the structural obstacles.

### 7. Khamenei Uranium Directive — No Official Denial

**[REPORTED]** The directive reported yesterday that Iran's near-weapons-grade uranium should not be sent abroad has not been denied by Tehran. Earlier, Reuters reported that Iran's Supreme Leader had directed that the country's near-weapons-grade uranium should not be sent abroad, hardening Tehran's stance on one of Washington's key demands in the negotiations.

**[OFFICIAL]** Trump reiterated US position on Thursday: President Donald Trump told reporters the United States will "get the highly enriched" uranium stockpiles from Iran.

**Assessment:** The silence from both sides on this reported directive is notable. Neither Iran has denied it nor has the US acknowledged it publicly. This fundamental gap—Trump insisting the uranium will leave, Khamenei reportedly ordering it cannot—remains the core obstacle to any comprehensive deal.

## Emerging Analysis: The Institutionalization Problem

**Day 84 reveals that Iran may be pursuing parallel tracks:**

1. **Formal negotiations:** Engaging with US proposals via Pakistani mediators, generating "slight progress"

2. **Facts on the ground:** Simultaneously institutionalizing Hormuz control through Oman partnership, consolidating nuclear threshold status through Khamenei directive

**This "facts on the ground" strategy could make a comprehensive deal impossible even if both sides want one.** If Iran succeeds in establishing a permanent toll system and retaining its enriched uranium, it will have converted wartime leverage into permanent institutional gains—regardless of what any peace agreement says.

**The fundamental question is whether Washington recognizes this strategy and how it responds.**
