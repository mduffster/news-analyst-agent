### Critical New Developments (May 21-22)

#### 1. House GOP Cancels War Powers Vote — Would Have Passed

**[VERIFIED]** House GOP leaders abruptly canceled a vote Thursday on a resolution to limit President Donald Trump's war powers in Iran just as Republicans were on the verge of losing the vote due to absences.

**[VERIFIED]** It would have been Congress' first successful rebuke of Trump's Iran war effort after multiple Democratic-led war powers attempts had failed. Rep. Jared Golden (D-Maine), the one Democrat who has consistently voted against Iran war powers resolutions, was planning to flip his vote to yes.

**[VERIFIED]** The previous war powers resolution failed in a tied vote last week. But eight Republicans — only one of whom was likely to vote for the resolution — were absent for Thursday's votes, with only two Democrats not present.

**[REPORTED]** Trump turned his fire on Rep. Brian Fitzpatrick (R-Pa.) on Wednesday, telling reporters: "He likes voting against Trump. You know what happens with that? It doesn't work out well." Fitzpatrick told Axios on Wednesday that he still intended to vote for the measure despite the president's threats. "We don't report to any party or any person here in D.C.," he said.

**[VERIFIED]** GOP leaders plan to bring the measure back up when the chamber returns from its week-long Memorial Day recess.

**Assessment:** This is a significant near-miss for the administration. Had the vote occurred, Democrats would have won their first congressional rebuke of the war. The delay to June merely postpones the reckoning. Combined with Tuesday's Senate breakthrough, bipartisan pressure on Trump's war authority is intensifying, even if veto-proof majorities remain unlikely.

---

#### 2. Rubio: "Slight Progress" in Talks — But Rejects Hormuz Toll

**[OFFICIAL]** U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said Friday there was "slight progress" during talks with Iran amid uncertainty about whether a deal will be reached or war will resume.

**[OFFICIAL]** Rubio spoke ahead of a meeting of NATO foreign ministers in Helsingborg, Sweden, where the military alliance is expected to discuss what role it could play in helping police the Strait of Hormuz once the war is over.

**[OFFICIAL]** Speaking at a Nato meeting in the southern Swedish city of Helsingborg, Mr Rubio said tolls "can't happen" and that he did not want to overstate any momentum in the talks. "There's been some slight progress, I don't want to exaggerate it, but there's been a little bit of movement, and that's good," Mr Rubio said alongside Nato Secretary General Mark Rutte.

**[OFFICIAL]** "We've always said a tolling system in the strait would be unacceptable," US Secretary of State Marco Rubio told reporters on Thursday. "No one in the world is in favor of a tolling system. It can't happen. It would make a diplomatic deal unfeasible."

**[OFFICIAL]** Mr Rubio said a draft resolution at the UN Security Council that demands Iran stops obstructing shipping in the strait, removes sea mines and halts "illegal tolls" now has more co-sponsors than any other resolution in history. Its fate remains unclear, however, as veto holders Russia and China have expressed serious concerns.

**Assessment:** Rubio's carefully hedged optimism masks a fundamental impasse. The "slight progress" contrasts sharply with Iran's new push for permanent Hormuz control. The UN Security Council resolution is symbolic—Russia and China will veto it—but it signals the US coalition is holding. The real question is whether Iran's toll proposal is a negotiating tactic or a genuine attempt to institutionalize its wartime gains.

---

#### 3. Iran-Oman Talks on Permanent Hormuz Toll System — Major Development

**[OFFICIAL]** Iran is discussing with Oman how to set up some form of a permanent toll system that will formalize its control of maritime traffic through the Strait of Hormuz. "Iran and Oman must mobilize all their resources both to provide security services and to manage navigation in the most appropriate manner," the Iranian ambassador to France, Mohammad Amin-Nejad, said in an interview with Bloomberg in Paris on Wednesday.

**[OFFICIAL]** "This will entail costs, and it goes without saying that those who wish to benefit from this traffic must also pay their share," he said in Farsi, through an interpreter, adding that the system will be transparent.

**[VERIFIED]** Iran is also reportedly working with Oman on a framework for a permanent toll system that would formalize its control over maritime traffic through the Strait of Hormuz.

**[OFFICIAL]** However, President Donald Trump rejected the proposal, insisting the waterway should remain open, free, and without toll charges.

**[REPORTED]** Iran has claimed, without giving evidence, that 26 tankers and other ships transited between Tuesday and Wednesday with the help of the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps.

**[REPORTED]** That would be an unusually high number for recent weeks, but still far below pre-war levels of roughly 135 ships a day.

**Assessment:** This is a major new development. Iran is attempting to convert its wartime leverage into permanent institutional control over the world's most critical oil chokepoint. The involvement of Oman—a traditional mediator between Iran and the US/Gulf—suggests this is more than posturing. If Iran succeeds in establishing a toll system, it would represent a fundamental shift in the global energy order, regardless of whether a formal peace deal is reached.

---

#### 4. NATO Weighing Hormuz Mission — July Deadline Emerging

**[REPORTED]** NATO is discussing the possibility of helping ships pass through the blocked Strait of Hormuz if the waterway isn't reopened by early July, according to a senior official in the military alliance. The idea has support from several members of the North Atlantic Treaty Organization, but doesn't yet have the necessary unanimous support, said a diplomat from a NATO country.

**[VERIFIED]** Leaders from NATO countries will meet in Ankara July 7-8.

**[OFFICIAL]** Secretary of State Marco Rubio promotes the Maritime Freedom Construct to address Hormuz via information-sharing and diplomatic-economic pressure, not offensive operations.

**[REPORTED]** The passageway has become a source of tension between the U.S. and its European allies in NATO, who refused to heed President Donald Trump's demands that they help reopen the strait.

**[REPORTED]** A coalition led by France and the U.K. is also developing a plan to help secure navigation in the Strait of Hormuz once the fighting abates.

**Assessment:** NATO's July deadline creates a new potential trigger for escalation. If diplomacy fails and NATO intervenes to forcibly open Hormuz, this could transform a US-Iran conflict into a broader Western-Iran confrontation. However, the lack of unanimous NATO support and European reluctance to join offensive operations suggests any mission would be defensive/escort rather than combat.

#### 5. Oil Plunges to $104 — Record SPR Release

**[VERIFIED]** At 9 a.m. Eastern Time today, oil was priced at $104.68 per barrel with Brent serving as the benchmark. That's a drop of $4.08 compared with yesterday morning and around $40.46 higher than the price one year ago.

**[VERIFIED]** Despite today's pullback, oil prices remain nearly 50% above pre-war levels, supported by ongoing supply tightness. In the United States, nearly 10 million barrels were withdrawn from the Strategic Petroleum Reserve last week, marking the largest release on record.

**[VERIFIED]** Despite Friday's gains, Brent futures were still down more than 4% for the week amid optimism that the conflicting parties could eventually reach an agreement.

**[VERIFIED]** SPR levels now at 374.18M barrels for week of May 15, 2026.

**[VERIFIED]** In mid-March 2026, the Trump administration announced the largest single-country release of emergency oil reserves in history — 172 million barrels — as part of a coordinated 400-million-barrel release by 32 nations in response to the closure of the Strait of Hormuz.

**Assessment:** Oil's continued decline to ~$104 reflects market hope that some resolution is emerging, but the SPR depletion is concerning. At current draw rates, the US is burning through strategic reserves at an unsustainable pace. The largest-ever SPR release signals how seriously the administration views the supply crisis, but it's a Band-Aid, not a solution.

---

#### 6. Khamenei Uranium Directive: No Official Denial

**[REPORTED]** Iran's Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei has issued a directive stating that near-weapons-grade uranium must not be sent abroad, hardening Tehran's stance on one of the main U.S. demands in peace talks, Reuters reports. The order from Ayatollah Mojtaba Khamenei could further frustrate U.S. President Donald Trump and complicate negotiations to end the U.S.-Israeli war against Iran.

**[OFFICIAL]** On Thursday, Trump vowed that the United States would not allow Iran to keep its stockpiles of highly enriched uranium. "We're going to get it. We don't need it, we don't want it. We'll probably destroy it after we get it, but we're not going to let them have it," Trump told reporters at the White House.

**[REPORTED]** The same insiders revealed that Iran's top administrative and military echelons are convinced that relinquishing control of the material and sending it overseas would make the country more vulnerable to future attacks by the U.S. and Israel.

**Assessment:** The Khamenei directive, first reported yesterday, has not been denied by Tehran or confirmed by the White House. The silence is telling—neither side wants to admit the impasse publicly while talks continue. Iran's position appears to be hardening around retaining its nuclear leverage.

### Key Emerging Issues

1. **Iran-Oman Toll System** — This is the most significant new development. If Iran formalizes permanent control of Hormuz through an Oman partnership, it would represent a fundamental restructuring of the global energy order—even without a formal peace deal.

2. **NATO July Deadline** — The alliance is setting up a potential intervention trigger. If Hormuz remains closed by July 7-8 (Ankara summit), NATO could authorize an escort mission that would internationalize the conflict.

3. **Congressional Pressure Building** — Both chambers nearly passed war powers resolutions this week. The pattern of GOP absences saving Trump can't continue indefinitely. June votes could produce the first congressional rebuke.

4. **SPR Depletion** — The record 10 million barrel weekly release signals emergency mode. At this rate, the strategic buffer is depleting rapidly with no clear end to the crisis.

5. **Institutional vs. Negotiated Outcomes** — Iran appears to be pursuing parallel tracks: formal negotiations with the US, while simultaneously institutionalizing its Hormuz control through Oman. This "facts on the ground" strategy could make a comprehensive deal impossible even if both sides want one.

### Bottom Line

**The structural obstacles to a comprehensive deal are hardening, not softening.**

Iran's Iran-Oman toll discussions represent a pivot from "negotiating leverage" to "permanent institutional control." Combined with the Khamenei uranium directive, Tehran appears to be pursuing facts on the ground rather than negotiated concessions. Rubio's carefully hedged "slight progress" masks this reality.

**Congressional restraint is becoming real.** Both chambers were on the verge of passing war powers resolutions this week. The House vote was pulled only because Republicans knew they would lose. This buys time but doesn't resolve the underlying political pressure.

**The NATO July deadline is the next escalation trigger.** If diplomacy fails and Hormuz remains closed, a NATO escort mission could transform this from a US-Iran conflict into a broader Western-Iran confrontation.

**Oil markets are pricing in resolution that isn't materializing.** The drop to $104 reflects hope, but the SPR is being depleted at record rates to maintain that price. The underlying supply crisis remains unresolved.

**The most likely near-term outcome remains extended stalemate**—with Iran institutionalizing its Hormuz control, the US burning through strategic reserves, and Congress slowly building toward a potential constitutional showdown over war powers.
