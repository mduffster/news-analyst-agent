## Key Developments (Past 24 Hours)

### **TRUMP: "WE WILL NOT ALLOW" DIRTY BOMB** [OFFICIAL]

President Donald Trump said Friday, "We won't allow that," when asked about a reported plot by Iran to potentially detonate an explosive laced with nuclear material, or a 'dirty bomb,' in the United States.

"There is a rumor that they're building a dirty bomb," he said during this afternoon's news conference. "We know a lot about what's happening in Iran. We know everything they say. We know who they are, where they are. We'll find out about it."

"There is a rumor that they are building a dirty bomb. If they do, we're going to fight," Trump said.

Secretary of State Marco Rubio downplayed concerns about the plot during the trip in Asia, telling reporters there is no "imminent threat" to the U.S. homeland.

Trump accused Iran of failing to make a good-faith effort to end the war, complaining that "they've been bullshitting people for 51 years."

**[ANALYSIS]** This is the first time Trump has publicly threatened military action specifically over the dirty bomb intelligence. While Rubio downplayed the threat, Trump's statement — "if they do, we're going to fight" — adds a potential new trigger for escalation beyond the existing nuclear/chemical weapons redlines.

### **HOUTHIS STRIKE RIYADH'S PIF TOWER** [VERIFIED]

Houthi militants have struck the tallest skyscraper in Riyadh, causing damage to the building housing the Saudi sovereign wealth fund, amid the escalating conflict between the Middle Eastern country and the Iran-backed militia.

Though no injuries were reported, the attack resulted in damage to several floors of the tower and nearby buildings.

It was the second time in less than two weeks that a Houthi attack damaged a landmark in Riyadh's financial district, with state oil company Aramco's building hit in a Nov 8 attack.

Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree said the Houthis also attacked the Al-Sadaf Petrochemical company in Jubail, the Khamis Mushait Air Base and Khalid International Airport in Riyadh.

The kingdom's foreign ministry said it reserved the right to take "all necessary measures" to defend its sovereignty and protect its citizens and national assets following the Houthi attack.

**[ANALYSIS]** Two strikes on major Saudi financial landmarks in 12 days represents a significant escalation. The Houthis are demonstrating penetration capability against high-profile targets, which could increase pressure on Saudi Arabia to either negotiate or escalate its Yemen response.

### **FOURTH TANKER STRUCK IN 96 HOURS** [VERIFIED]

Another tanker was struck by a projectile and caught fire in the Strait of Hormuz on Friday, extending a wave of attacks on commercial shipping that has pushed maritime security incidents to their highest weekly level since mid-October.

The latest incident occurred at approximately 1500 UTC, eight nautical miles north of Khasab, Oman. The tanker was transiting outbound through the strategic waterway when an unknown projectile struck its port bow, sparking a fire onboard.

In the Persian Gulf, Strait of Hormuz and Gulf of Oman, Kelly recorded seven strikes involving six vessels. Four tankers were hit within 13 nautical miles of Khasab over three days.

Iran's Revolutionary Guards said shortly afterwards that an oil tanker had caught fire after hitting a mine while attempting to transit the Strait of Hormuz near Khasab.

The attack was reported as Iranian media said that a missile had hit Iran's Qeshm Island on the northern side of the strait. There was no immediate information available on possible damage.

**[ANALYSIS]** The tempo of Hormuz attacks has sharply increased — four strikes in four days is well above the weekly average. Iran appears to be intensifying pressure despite the economic damage this causes to its own shipping interests.

Combined with the Aramco Tower strike 12 days earlier, the Houthis are demonstrating they can strike at the heart of Saudi economic confidence. This could force Saudi Arabia into a response cycle that draws the US deeper in.

**Track 4: Israeli Political Wildcard**
Opposition concerns that Netanyahu could launch strikes to derail coalition talks remain live. With Likud-Yashar negotiations resuming Saturday and UTJ talks advancing, Netanyahu's window for remaining in power through political means is narrowing. The Dec 6 deadline creates a potential intersection point with US decision-making in January.

His APEC comments about Xi and Putin wanting the war to end suggest diplomatic channels remain active, but the dirty bomb rhetoric provides cover for rapid reversal.
