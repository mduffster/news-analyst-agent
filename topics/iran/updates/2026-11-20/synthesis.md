## 1. Qatari Mediation: Witkoff-Kushner Meet Senior Qatari Officials in Berlin

President Trump's envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner met in Berlin on Thursday with senior Qatari officials to discuss mediation efforts between the U.S. and Iran, according to two sources with knowledge of the meeting.

Qatar, the key mediator between the U.S. and Iran, has been trying for weeks to reach a deal to resume the ceasefire, restart nuclear negotiations and prevent a return to war. Qatari Prime Minister Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al Thani and his envoy, Ali al-Thawadi, attended the Berlin meeting.

Qatar has served as a key interlocutor between Washington and Tehran. Top Qatari officials, including Prime Minister Al Thani, have been in regular contact with US officials during negotiations. Al Thani visited Washington, DC, last week and met with President Donald Trump and Vice President JD Vance.

The meeting comes after a low-level delegation of Iranian negotiators met with Qatari counterparts in Doha earlier this week. Despite the meetings, no major progress has been made, sources tell Axios.

Trump said at a press conference on Friday: "Iran wants to make a deal, but they don't want to make a deal that's acceptable to me. We want to make a real deal, not a fake deal that they've been doing for 51 years."

**Significance:** This is the first confirmed high-level diplomatic engagement in days, signaling that the administration is actively working a negotiation track in parallel with military posturing. [REPORTED — Axios, confirmed by CNN] However, Trump's dismissive rhetoric and the lack of reported progress suggest the diplomatic track remains far from breakthrough. Germany's public backing of Qatar's mediation—with Chancellor Merz calling the opening of Hormuz and an end to Iran's nuclear program essential—adds European weight to diplomatic efforts.

## 2. Strike Readiness: US Forces Can Attack Within 2–4 Days

Israel and the United States are considering launching another military offensive against Iran in the near future, CBS News reported Friday, citing sources familiar with the discussions. Prime Minister Netanyahu's government is increasingly concerned about Iran's efforts to restore its ballistic missile production capabilities and move sensitive military and nuclear facilities underground.

Netanyahu reportedly favors conducting the operation jointly with the United States. According to the sources, American forces could begin an offensive within two to four days of receiving an order.

The military could begin an offensive operation as soon as 2–4 days after receiving the relevant order. According to the sources, the Israeli army is ready for a new confrontation and is maintaining the highest level of defensive readiness in case of a first strike by Iran. American and Israeli forces already have sufficient resources in the region to launch an offensive within 2–4 days of receiving the order.

Trump told Fox News on Wednesday, "I'm going to finish it off," referring to the conflict. However, speaking to Israel's Channel 12 on Thursday, Trump said he was "not rushing into any decision."

**Significance:** The 2–4 day readiness window confirms that military option remains immediately available despite Trump's stated delay. The dual messaging—"finish it off" vs. "not rushing"—creates strategic ambiguity but also indicates Trump has not foreclosed the escalation option. [REPORTED — CBS News, multiple sources]

## 4. Houthis Strike PIF Tower — Second Major Riyadh Landmark in 12 Days

Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund (PIF) headquarters in Riyadh was damaged in a Houthi attack on Thursday, November 19, with several floors of the skyscraper and neighbouring buildings affected, according to Saudi authorities and Reuters. The authority did not identify the building by name, but an accompanying photograph published by the Saudi Press Agency (SPA) showed PIF Tower, the headquarters of the Kingdom's sovereign wealth fund and Riyadh's tallest skyscraper.

The attack on the Public Investment Fund Tower, which is 80 stories tall, destroyed windows across up to eight floors of the building and bent portions of its metal facade.

The incident marks the second time in less than two weeks that a Houthi attack has damaged a major building in Riyadh's financial district. On November 8, an attack struck a building belonging to Saudi Aramco, the Kingdom's state-owned energy company.

The strike destroyed windows across seven to eight floors of the tower, Riyadh's tallest skyscraper, and bent part of its metal facade. The attack is the second time this month that the Houthis have struck at the heart of Saudi Arabia's image as a safe investment destination. PIF says it has around $1 trillion in assets.

PIF Tower is the headquarters of the Kingdom's sovereign wealth fund, which plays a central role in financing the Kingdom's economic diversification and major development projects. Authorities have not reported any injuries from Thursday's attack.

**Significance:** Two strikes on major Saudi financial landmarks in 12 days demonstrates Houthi capability to penetrate Saudi defenses and strike prestige targets. The PIF is central to Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman's Vision 2030 diversification plan—making this a symbolic as well as physical attack on Saudi economic confidence. [VERIFIED — Saudi authorities confirmed]

## 5. Fifth Tanker Attack in Four Days; Qeshm Island Also Struck

Another tanker was struck by a projectile and caught fire in the Strait of Hormuz on Friday, extending a wave of attacks on commercial shipping that has pushed maritime security incidents to their highest weekly level since mid-October.

A tanker sailing outbound through the Strait of Hormuz was struck by an unknown projectile, the UK Maritime Trade Operations (UKMTO) said on Friday. The ship was hit 8 nautical miles north of Khasab in Oman's Musandam region. No casualties or environmental impact were reported.

In the Persian Gulf, Strait of Hormuz and Gulf of Oman, Kelly recorded seven strikes involving six vessels. Four tankers were hit within 13 nautical miles of Khasab over three days, including the LPG tanker TARA GAS, which was attacked twice.

Iran's Revolutionary Guards said shortly afterwards that an oil tanker had caught fire after hitting a mine while attempting to transit the Strait of Hormuz near Khasab. The attack was reported as Iranian media said that a missile had hit Iran's Qeshm Island on the northern side of the strait. There was no immediate information available on possible damage.

**Weekly Attack Summary:**
- Nov 16: TARA GAS struck (first attack), one seafarer missing
- Nov 17–18: TARA GAS struck again; ADNOC vessel (Navig8 Messi) hit, one seafarer missing, 11 injured
- Nov 18 (evening): Tanker struck near Khasab
- Nov 20: Fifth tanker struck, fire onboard
- Nov 20: Qeshm Island struck (tit-for-tat)

**Significance:** The pace of Hormuz attacks has sharply intensified—seven strikes involving six vessels this week is the highest since mid-October. This suggests Iran is testing whether increased harassment will drive up oil prices or force negotiations. The simultaneous strike on Qeshm Island indicates ongoing tit-for-tat targeting. [VERIFIED — UKMTO confirmed]

## 6. Iraq Deploys to Jurf al-Sakhar Under Compromise with Kataib Hezbollah

Iraqi forces began deploying on Friday in Jurf al-Sakhar, a town south of Baghdad held for more than a decade by Iran's most powerful militia ally in Iraq, under a deal brokered by Shiite leaders days after the head of Iran's Quds Force arrived in Baghdad. A convoy flying Iraqi flags drove toward Jurf al-Sakhar on Friday, and hundreds of soldiers took up positions on the outskirts. Under the deal, Kataib Hezbollah fighters are to withdraw to other parts of Iraq.

The standoff began on Monday, when Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi ordered troops to prepare to enter Jurf al-Sakhar after an investigation accused Kataib Hezbollah of running a network that siphoned off hundreds of millions of dollars' worth of state oil through tampered pipelines. Jurf al-Sakhar, a Sunni farming town on the Euphrates about 50 kilometers south of Baghdad, was retaken from Islamic State in 2014 by the army and Shiite militias.

Iraqi security forces began to enter Jurf Al Sakhar on Friday under a government plan aimed at restoring state power in an area long controlled by the Iran-backed Kataib Hezbollah militia. "If it's enforced as planned, it will be break the last stronghold of Kataib Hezbollah since the war on ISIS," Lahib Hagel, an Iraq expert at Crisis Group, told The National.

Kataib Hezbollah has denied the allegations of stealing oil. Nine senior security and oil officials have been dismissed following the investigation into alleged oil smuggling in Jurf al-Sakhar.

**Significance:** The compromise avoids direct confrontation between Baghdad and its most powerful Iran-aligned militia. However, the deal includes PMF participation and a gradual withdrawal timeline—meaning this is stabilization-by-compromise rather than a clean assertion of state authority. Near-term risk of Iraq becoming an escalation flashpoint is reduced, but the underlying militia-state tension persists. [VERIFIED — AP, Al Jazeera]

## 7. Trump-Vance Tensions: Publicly Downplayed

President Donald Trump denied a report that he voiced unhappiness with Vice President JD Vance for blaming the Republicans' midterm wipeout on the war with Iran.

During a Friday news conference, Trump said Vance's post-election social media post, in which he said the war angered voters, was a "very accurate statement." But Trump reiterated that, if given the chance to make the decision all over again, he would "absolutely" still have started the conflict in order to prevent Iran from acquiring nuclear weapons.

He maintained that the midterm blue wave was because he was not on the ballot and denied that he was angry that Vance blamed the GOP's midterm losses on the Iran war.

"The reason we lost is the war," Vance said of the Trump administration's ongoing conflict with Iran.

**Significance:** Trump's public support for Vance while reaffirming the war decision is an attempt to manage internal GOP divisions. However, the underlying tension remains: Vance's criticism resonates with the party's populist base, and the vice president's break with Trump on the war's political wisdom creates 2028 positioning dynamics.
