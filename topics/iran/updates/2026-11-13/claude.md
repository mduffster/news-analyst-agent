### **MOSSAD CHIEF GOFMAN IN WASHINGTON — HIGH-LEVEL TALKS** [VERIFIED]

Roman Gofman, director of the Mossad, has arrived in Washington, DC, to meet with senior officials from the Trump administration to discuss the war with Iran, sources told *The Jerusalem Post*. Gofman's visit comes as Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu is pushing US President Donald Trump to return to fighting against Iran. Gofman is meeting with CIA Director John Ratcliffe and will then travel to Miami to meet United States Special Envoy to the Middle East, Steve Witkoff.

The visit by Roman Gofman, one of Benjamin Netanyahu's closest confidants, comes as the Israeli prime minister is pressing President Trump to resume the war against Iran.

Trump has so far rejected that pressure. Instead, Trump has favored continuing a naval blockade and economic pressure campaign against Iran while exploring the possibility of a deal. He has aligned with the preferences of Saudi Arabia, Qatar and the United Arab Emirates.

**[ANALYSIS]** This is a critical development. Netanyahu is deploying his top intelligence asset to Washington to circumvent diplomatic channels and press directly for war resumption. The fact that Gofman is meeting both CIA Director Ratcliffe and envoy Witkoff suggests a full-court press. However, Trump's continued resistance and alignment with Gulf states indicates this push is likely to fail.

### **ISRAELI SECURITY CABINET MEETING SUNDAY — FIRST SINCE OCT 5** [VERIFIED]

Israel's security cabinet is expected to meet Sunday amid opposition concerns that Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu could launch a military strike on Iran to remain in power.

The security cabinet is due to convene for the first time since early October, after it reportedly instructed the IDF to raise its readiness for a possible strike. Eisenkot wrote to Netanyahu this week about "extraneous considerations being introduced into the holiest of holies, Israel's security." He urged him to "show restraint and caution, and refrain from dangerous moves that can wait."

Opposition circles in Israel fear Netanyahu could launch a military strike on Iran to place the country under a state of emergency, potentially paving the way for a unity government involving both Netanyahu and Eisenkot. The latter opposes such an arrangement. However, any such action would require approval from the security cabinet, which is headed by Netanyahu and includes ministers from his government.

**[ANALYSIS]** Sunday's security cabinet meeting is the most critical near-term flashpoint. Netanyahu controls this cabinet with his appointees — if he can secure cabinet approval for strikes, it would create a direct confrontation with Zamir and potentially drag Trump back into combat operations.

### **IAEA: IRAN EXTENDS INSPECTOR BLACKOUT — "INCREASINGLY CONCERNED"** [VERIFIED]

Iran extended its shutout of United Nations nuclear inspectors, deepening the lack of international knowledge of the country's uranium stockpile with no sign of fresh talks with the US. International Atomic Energy Agency inspectors still can't verify the location or status of Iran's stockpile of near-bomb grade uranium, according to a 13-page restricted document circulated Friday in Vienna.

The global nuclear watchdog is "increasingly concerned" about a uranium-enrichment plant in Iran that may contain significant quantities of near weapons-grade material. A confidential document sent by the IAEA said the body was urgently seeking to establish how much enriched uranium there was at the Isfahan complex, which was bombed in June 2025. The IAEA believes Iran has about 440kg of uranium enriched to 60 per cent, which is just short of weapons grade, and enough fissile material for about 10 bombs.

**[ANALYSIS]** This IAEA report adds urgency to the nuclear dimension. The lack of verification creates significant uncertainty about Iran's actual capabilities, which could fuel both Israeli arguments for preemptive strikes and broader international concern.

### **IRAN: "NOT A SINGLE ATOM" OF ENRICHED URANIUM LEAVES** [VERIFIED]

Mohsen Rezaei, Secretary of the Supreme National Security Council of the Islamic Republic, announced in an interview with Al-Mayadeen that no portion of Iran's enriched nuclear material will leave the country.

"We need the enriched material to design ships and submarines that work on nuclear energy," Rezaei said, adding that Tehran had informed Moscow that its enriched nuclear material would remain in the country and "we will use it ourselves."

There's also discussion on diluting and moving Iran's enriched uranium abroad. However, the head of Iran's Supreme National Security Council, Mohsen Rezaei, told Iran's state broadcaster Wednesday: "We will not remove even a shred of enriched nuclear material from Iran."

**[ANALYSIS]** Combined with yesterday's Ghalibaf statement on Hormuz "never returning" to pre-war status, Iran is systematically rejecting every major US negotiating demand. This makes diplomatic resolution extremely difficult and validates the "indefinite stalemate" scenario.

### **TRUMP: "STATUS QUO" IS WHAT GULF ALLIES WANT** [VERIFIED]

Negotiations with Iran are stuck with mediator Qatar working to bridge the divide. Trump met Qatar's prime minister at the White House this week, and said afterward that Gulf Arab allies prefer "the status quo" of continued economic pressure on Iran. He wrote that while the U.S. remains "locked and loaded", an attack at this point "seems to be quite unlikely."

Trump offered no timeline for ending the conflict but said several Gulf countries had urged him not to escalate military operations against Iran. Trump said Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Kuwait, the United Arab Emirates and Bahrain had asked him to maintain the current approach toward Tehran for the time being. He claimed he could have "completely destroyed Iran" but had taken a different course at the request of other countries.

Meanwhile, on Thursday, Trump said Washington's allies in the Middle East had asked him to hold off on renewed large-scale attacks against Iran.

**[ANALYSIS]** Trump is explicitly citing Gulf allies as the reason for restraint. This gives him political cover to resist Netanyahu's pressure while maintaining the blockade strategy. The "status quo" framing signals extended stalemate as the preferred option.
