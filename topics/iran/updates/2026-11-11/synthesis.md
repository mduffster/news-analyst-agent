# US-Israel-Iran Conflict: Iran Expands Asymmetric Pressure as US Settles on Economic Strangulation Strategy

## November 11, 2026 — Day 257

**Assessment:** Day 257 reveals an increasingly defined conflict posture: the United States has formally committed to prolonged economic pressure rather than resumed combat operations, while Iran demonstrates expanding asymmetric reach — targeting navigation infrastructure in neutral Oman, threatening European bases, and driving three Cabinet secretaries onto military bases. The Hegseth impeachment was blocked by Republican procedural maneuver, though ironically Democratic absences proved decisive. Oil rebounded modestly (~2%) after Iran rejected key nuclear demands, but remains well below pre-election highs. The Israeli political transition is becoming a flashpoint, with Eisenkot accusing Netanyahu of denying security briefings and potentially seeking unauthorized military action during the caretaker period.

## 1. Treasury Secretary Bessent Relocated to Military Base — Third Cabinet Official Under Protection [OFFICIAL]

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has moved from his home in Washington, DC, to an undisclosed US military base in the wake of "Iranian threats" to his life.

A senior official told Fox News that threats against Bessent had reached "more than an advanced stage." Security concerns increased when The Wall Street Journal published an aerial view of the Georgetown mansion that he bought for $12.5m shortly after his confirmation, which allowed Bessent's home to be geo-located.

Other Cabinet officials including Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth and Secretary of State Marco Rubio are currently living on military bases while in Washington. Bessent becomes the latest in a growing number of senior administration officials who have moved to military bases for their safety.

Bessent has led Operation Economic Outcast, a Treasury campaign launched in August to isolate Iran economically through sanctions and other financial measures. The Cabinet secretary "remains committed to executing Operation Economic Outcast and maintaining the blockade, which together are collapsing Iran's economy," according to a Treasury spokesperson.

**Significance:** Three senior Cabinet members now reside on military bases due to Iranian threats — an unprecedented security posture for a U.S. administration. Iran is specifically targeting Bessent because of his leadership of the economic pressure campaign, signaling that Tehran views the sanctions offensive as a primary threat requiring direct intimidation of its architect.

## 2. Rubio at G7: Economic Pressure Eliminates "Need for Major Combat Operations" [OFFICIAL]

Secretary of State Marco Rubio said on Tuesday that he told G7 allies that the US economic sanctions and naval blockade are working so well that President Donald Trump does not need to conduct major combat operations in Iran. "The economic pain that is being inflicted on Iran right now is extraordinary and it compounds," Rubio said.

"We don't need to conduct military strikes if we're achieving the same objective through economic means and economic pressure. Never has Iran ever faced the inability to sell energy in the global market like they are today."

He said the economic pressure on Tehran would intensify over time and that Washington remained open to reaching a "real deal," but described Iran's conditions for an agreement as "absurd."

Inside the administration, Rubio, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and the new White House chief of staff, Stephen Miller, favor keeping up heavy economic pressure, one diplomat said.

**Significance:** This represents the clearest articulation yet of the administration's post-midterm strategy: sustained economic strangulation rather than resumed kinetic operations. The "no need" framing establishes a policy baseline while preserving escalation options. However, the absence of any diplomatic progress — combined with Iran's continued attacks — suggests this approach may produce prolonged stalemate rather than resolution.

## 3. Iran Threatens US Bases in Europe — Rezaei Warning Extends Conflict Geography [OFFICIAL]

Iranian security chief Mohsen Rezaei has threatened to strike U.S. bases beyond the Middle East, including in Europe, if they are used for attacks against the Islamic Republic. "If American aircraft take off from an American military base, even one located in a European country or some other distant region, to bomb Iran, we will strike those bases as well," Rezaei told Lebanon-based Al Mayadeen TV in an interview broadcast on Wednesday.

Rezaei said Iran might also strike "some islands they think our missiles cannot reach." He added that U.S. bases in European countries could be hit if American aircraft took off from them to bomb Iran.

**Significance:** This threat directly follows the evacuations of U.S. aircraft from RAF Fairford (October) and RAF Mildenhall (November 10). Iran is explicitly signaling capability and intent to extend the conflict beyond the Middle East theater, placing European allies in a difficult position regarding hosting U.S. military assets.

## 4. DOJ Charges Three Iranian Operatives in RAF Fairford Terror Plot [OFFICIAL]

The charges announced on Tuesday centre on RAF Fairford in southwest England, where US forces and bombers had been stationed. "These defendants helped orchestrate a campaign of terror on behalf of terrorist organisations affiliated with Iran that targeted Jewish communities in Europe and the United States, and the British military base, RAF Fairford, housing American service members," said Manhattan US Attorney Jamie McDonald.

"This eight-count indictment shows the determination of Iran to commit terrorist attacks in Europe and the U.S., including one plot that was recently prevented at RAF Fairford, a British Royal Air Force Base where American forces were stationed," said FBI Director Kash Patel.

The charges involved nearly 20 attacks and attempted attacks, with those in Europe carried out in the name of a purported new terrorist group, Harakat Ashab al-Yamin al-Islamiya (HAYI), which the DOJ asserted was actually a front for Kata'ib Hizballah and other designated terrorist organizations.

**Significance:** The indictment confirms Iranian direction of the September RAF Fairford plot, corroborating Rezaei's explicit threat to European bases. Combined with the UK base evacuations and European cyberattacks, this establishes a pattern of Iranian asymmetric operations extending well beyond the regional theater.

## 5. Iran Attacks Navigation Systems on Omani Island — Targets Third-Party Infrastructure [VERIFIED]

Iran has attacked ship navigation systems on the Omani island of Didamar in the Strait of Hormuz, three sources with knowledge of the matter confirmed, showing Tehran's ability to target infrastructure used to restore Gulf oil flows.

Iranian state media said on Tuesday that the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps had carried out strikes destroying "enemy navigation systems" on Didamar island.

Oman confirmed that a loud explosion was detected at the base on the island. Didamar, also known as Little Quoin Island, is located off Oman's Musandam Peninsula next to the southern shipping corridor.

According to estimates by two representatives of the shipping industry and a source in the maritime security sector, the attack is likely to have a limited impact on commercial shipping in the Strait of Hormuz. This is because alternative navigation systems are available elsewhere.

**Significance:** Iran attacked Omani territory — a neutral party with historically close ties to Tehran — to degrade shipping infrastructure. While immediate operational impact appears limited, this demonstrates willingness to expand targeting beyond belligerents and ability to complicate efforts to restore Gulf traffic.

## 6. Hegseth Impeachment Blocked — Democratic Absences Proved Decisive [VERIFIED]

The House voted 211 to 204 to refer the articles of impeachment filed against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth to committee, effectively killing the impeachment effort spearheaded by Rep. Thomas Massie, R-Ky.

Democrats in part gave House GOP leaders a window to sideline the impeachment effort due to absences. Eleven House Democrats did not vote on the measure, more than the seven-vote margin.

Former House Speaker Nancy Pelosi was in San Francisco for a Veterans Day event.

But the reprieve is probably short-lived: Democrats are all but guaranteed to force the issue again when they retake majorities in the House and Senate in January.

Former Pentagon spokesman John Ullyot tells CNN that Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth is "going to get impeached," despite House Republicans blocking an immediate vote.

**Significance:** Republican attendance discipline succeeded — but only because 11 Democrats failed to vote. This underscores the razor-thin margins controlling Washington. Hegseth survives the lame-duck session, but faces near-certain House impeachment when Democrats take control in January.

## 7. Eisenkot-Netanyahu Clash Intensifies — PM Accused of Blocking Security Briefings [VERIFIED]

Yashar party leader Gadi Eisenkot asks Attorney General Gali Baharav-Miara to intervene so he can receive regular security briefings from caretaker Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, alleging that his team's attempts to arrange them with Netanyahu's staff have failed.

Without mentioning Iran by name, Eisenkot said in a letter to Netanyahu that "the use of security forces in the absence of a direct and immediate threat... could have disastrous consequences for Israeli democracy and for citizens' trust."

Eisenkot also referred to recent reports suggesting that considerations unrelated to national security had influenced security decision-making. "In recent weeks, deeply troubling reports have emerged concerning the introduction of ulterior considerations into the most sacred of responsibilities: Israel's security," he wrote.

The Prime Minister's Office called Eisenkot's letter "an irresponsible move that publicly discusses sensitive security matters. It is a transparent attempt to politicize issues concerning Israel's national security."

**Significance:** This represents a significant constitutional clash during Israel's political transition. Eisenkot's warning against military action and accusation that Netanyahu is denying security briefings suggests genuine concern about unauthorized operations during the caretaker period. The implications for any Israeli military action against Iran during this transition are substantial.

## 8. Maritime Attacks Continue — November Pace Remains High [VERIFIED]

**ADNOC Vessel Attack (November 10):** A vessel operated by UAE's ADNOC Logistics and Services sustained damage following an attack in the country's territorial waters in the Arabian Gulf on Tuesday afternoon, November 10. ADNOC Logistics & Services confirmed that one of its vessels sustained damage in an attack in the Persian Gulf. The company provided no details about the vessel's identity, the extent of the damage or whether any crew members were injured.

**New Mine Strike Claim (November 11):** An oil tanker caught fire on Wednesday in the eastern part of the Strait of Hormuz after hitting a naval mine in a "restricted area," Iran's Army said in a statement carried by state media.

**Prior Incidents:** Two oil tankers burst into flames after running into naval mines in the Strait of Hormuz early on November 8, 2026, according to Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC). There is no independent confirmation.

**Significance:** Maritime incidents continue at elevated pace despite de-escalation rhetoric. October's 37 attacks were the highest monthly total since the war began, and November is tracking similarly. The pattern of Iranian mine claims serves both deterrent and informational warfare purposes, regardless of independent verification.

## 9. Houthi-Saudi Exchange Continues — Second Day of Riyadh Interceptions [VERIFIED]

Saudi air defences intercepted Houthi missiles and drones launched towards Riyadh, Mecca and other regions on Wednesday, as hostilities between the Iran-aligned group and the kingdom worsen.

The Saudi-led military coalition fighting the Yemeni group said it had intercepted and destroyed three cruise missiles headed to the Riyadh region, two drones aimed at Mecca and southern areas and two missiles pointed towards the west.

Houthi spokesman Yahya Saree also said the Houthis had attacked a pumping station in Saudi Arabia's Yanbu, a key Saudi oil hub that allows exports to avoid the blocked Strait of Hormuz.

Wednesday's interceptions marked the second consecutive day that Saudi air defences had intercepted Houthi missiles launched towards Riyadh.

**Significance:** Houthi targeting of Yanbu — the Red Sea terminal for Saudi Arabia's critical East-West pipeline bypass — demonstrates continued proxy pressure on the infrastructure that Gulf states use to circumvent the Hormuz closure. This is the second attack on the pipeline system in recent days.

## 10. Oil Rebounds Modestly on Iran Rejection of Nuclear Demands [VERIFIED]

Brent rose to 92.40 USD/Bbl on November 11, 2026, up 0.99% from the previous day. Over the past month, Brent's price has fallen 13.36%, but it is still 47.34% higher than a year ago.

Brent crude is advancing to US$92.77 per barrel, up US$1.28, or 1.40%, from Tuesday's settlement of US$91.49. The international benchmark is rebounding as geopolitical supply risks return to the forefront.

Historical data shows significant decline from pre-midterm highs: Oct 30: $117.64; Nov 2: $104.77; Nov 9: $92.57; Nov 11: $92.84.

**Significance:** Oil has fallen ~21% from pre-election highs of ~$118 to ~$93. Markets continue to price in de-escalation that operational reality does not support. The modest rebound reflects Iran's rejection of nuclear material transfer demands and continued maritime incidents, but the overall trajectory suggests markets remain confident in eventual resolution.
