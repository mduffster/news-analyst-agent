### Key Developments (Last 24 Hours)

**UK PM BURNHAM: "STRONG INDICATIONS" IRAN BEHIND RAF FAIRFORD INCIDENT** [OFFICIAL]

UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham has confirmed there are "strong indications" that Iran played a part in a suspected terror plot near an RAF base. Speaking to ITV News, Burnham stated: "I can say that there are strong indications that Iran played a part in what happened over the weekend at RAF Fairford. So we can confirm our belief that they did play a part, but it remains a live police investigation."

Burnham revealed he chaired a COBRA meeting on Monday regarding the incident, saying it "has taken up quite a bit of my time here in Liverpool this week." Police confirmed "no improvised explosive devices were found" in vehicles used by the men, but "a quantity of petrol was recovered."

The UK earlier this year allowed the U.S. Air Force to use RAF Fairford for limited defensive strikes against Iranian missile capabilities. Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps named RAF Fairford in July when it said that any British base used for attacks on Iranian territory would be considered a "legitimate target."

**[ANALYSIS]** [OFFICIAL] This is a significant escalation from Rubio's "foreign actor" statement yesterday. The UK government is now publicly attributing the incident to Iran, even while the investigation remains ongoing. This could have major diplomatic consequences.

**US COMPLETES IRAQ WITHDRAWAL** [VERIFIED]

The United States announced it had completed its withdrawal from Iraq on Wednesday, more than 20 years after invading the country to topple Saddam Hussein, in a move that was celebrated by the Iraqi government and the Pentagon – as well as Iran and its allies, who claim years of pressure forced the American departure.

President Trump called the departure "a victory for the United States" and said it "marks the end of an excursion from hell." Speaking at a White House event, Trump said "it's a victory for Iraq and a decisive victory over ISIS, which has been completely decimated." He added: "We've been in that war for 23 years, lost more than 4,500 great American soldiers and 31,000 injured, some very badly."

CENTCOM announced it completed the orderly departure of U.S. forces and equipment from Erbil Air Base, marking the end of the Operation Inherent Resolve mission in Iraq.

Baghdad is marking the occasion with four "Sovereignty Days," a national holiday from September 30 to October 3 that underscores the deep symbolism of the US exit.

Iran-allied groups in Iraq have celebrated the US withdrawal. Kataib Hezbollah declared: "The expulsion of the occupation forces, defeated, from the land of Iraq is the beginning of full sovereignty."

**[ANALYSIS]** [VERIFIED] The Iraq withdrawal was pre-planned (agreed in 2024) but lands amid the Iran war with significant symbolic weight. Iran and its proxies are framing it as a victory, which may strengthen Tehran's domestic messaging that it is winning the broader regional contest.

**IRAN RECEIVES FORMAL US RESPONSE ON 7-DAY PROPOSAL** [VERIFIED]

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araqchi received US feedback via Qatari mediators in Doha on Tuesday on a seven-day plan aimed at building trust between Washington and Tehran, ‌with sequencing the main sticking ‌point. Araqchi is expected to discuss the US response in Tehran on Wednesday.

According to sources briefed on the talks: "The core disagreement between the two sides is the sequence of implementing the plan," adding that "the plan aims to build trust and return to a strengthened version of the cease-fire memorandum of understanding signed in June."

The document being circulated outlines a seven-day period of trust-building aimed at returning the two sides to an enhanced version of the memorandum of understanding agreed in June, including concrete steps on Iran's nuclear programme. The disagreement centres on the sequencing of the steps rather than the components of the plan.

**[ANALYSIS]** This is notable progress. Despite Trump's public rejection, a formal response was transmitted through mediators. The dispute being over "sequencing rather than components" suggests the substantive framework has some overlap — the question is who moves first.

**HORMUZ CRUDE FLOWS HIT PREWAR LEVELS** [VERIFIED]

Crude oil exports from the Strait of Hormuz have basically returned to levels normal before the Iran war, as U.S. military escorts have boosted shipments and pipelines have redirected flows. Crude transiting Hormuz reached a seven-day average of 13.5 million barrels per day as of Monday, which matches a prewar baseline for shipments through the strait, according to Kpler data.

Crude exports from the Middle East reached an estimated 16.328 million barrels per day (bpd) in September – their highest level since the war began in late February. Kpler said Middle East crude exports have recovered to just less than 80 percent of their pre-war level.

However, the recovery is uneven. JPMorgan's head of global commodities strategy said the "crude market has largely normalized even as refined product supplies remain constrained." Refined products shipped through Hormuz are at a seven-day average of 677,000 bpd as of Monday compared to 3.6 million bpd before the war.

**[ANALYSIS]** [VERIFIED] This is a major development that significantly erodes Iran's primary leverage. If crude flows are at prewar levels, Tehran's ability to threaten global energy markets through Hormuz disruption has been substantially diminished. However, the refined products shortfall continues to drive fuel price pressures.

**[ANALYSIS]** Oil prices are responding to the Hormuz flow recovery. The $8-10 drop from Monday's highs represents the market pricing in reduced disruption risk. If crude flows are truly normalized, prices could continue declining toward $90 or below.

### Assessment

**[ANALYSIS]** Today marks a potentially significant inflection point in the conflict:

1. **Iran's Hormuz leverage is eroding rapidly.** The recovery of crude flows to prewar levels — if sustained — fundamentally changes Tehran's negotiating position. Its primary coercive tool was the ability to disrupt global energy markets. That leverage is now substantially diminished. This may explain Iran's willingness to engage on the 7-day proposal despite Trump's public rejection.

2. **The RAF Fairford attribution is diplomatically significant.** A Five Eyes ally publicly attributing a terror plot to Iran during an active war creates pressure for response. The UK allowing US strikes from Fairford makes this both a symbolic and operational flashpoint. Iran's July declaration that such bases are "legitimate targets" now looks prescient — and the alleged plot validates that threat.

3. **The Iraq withdrawal is symbolic but real.** Iran and its proxies are framing it as a strategic victory, which bolsters domestic narratives that the "axis of resistance" is prevailing. The timing — coinciding with the Iran war — amplifies this messaging. For the US, it removes a pressure point (base vulnerabilities) but also reduces regional presence.

4. **Negotiations have moved from posturing to specifics.** The dispute being about "sequencing rather than components" suggests the broad framework of a deal exists. Both sides agree on what needs to happen; they disagree on who goes first. This is closer to a deal than the public rhetoric suggests.
