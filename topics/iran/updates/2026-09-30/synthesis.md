# US-Israel-Iran Conflict: Major Inflection Point as Hormuz Leverage Erodes and UK Attributes Fairford to Iran

## September 30, 2026 — Day 215

**Assessment:** Today marks a potential inflection point in the seven-month war. Three developments converge to reshape the strategic landscape: Gulf oil producers, with considerable support from the US Navy, are now jamming crude through the Strait of Hormuz right under Iran's nose, with oil and petroleum product flows averaging 13.1 million barrels per day last week—just under 80% of the 17.1 million barrels that had traveled through the strait before the war. This substantially erodes Tehran's primary coercive leverage. Meanwhile, UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham confirmed the government believes the Iranian state was behind the RAF Fairford security incident—the first official Five Eyes attribution to Iran. And the United States completed its withdrawal from Iraq, more than 20 years after invading the country, a move celebrated by Iran and its allies who claim years of pressure forced the American departure. Despite these shifts, diplomatic engagement continues: Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi presented the US response to President Masoud Pezeshkian at a cabinet meeting, with the dispute now characterized as one of sequencing rather than components. The combination of restored oil flows, diplomatic specifics emerging, and symbolic victories for Iran's "axis of resistance" narrative creates cross-pressures that could accelerate either settlement or escalation.

## Hormuz Oil Flows Near Prewar Levels: Iran's Leverage Eroding

The most significant structural development of the past week is the recovery of Middle Eastern crude exports. According to JP Morgan, September daily oil flows averaged 17.5 million barrels daily, or 98% of pre-war levels, with fuel exports at 3 million barrels daily, or 58% of pre-war daily average levels. [VERIFIED]

Total Middle Eastern crude flows—through Hormuz and around it—are even more impressive: back to 98% of prewar levels, according to JPMorgan. "Given such a strong volume passing through the strait, it is clear Iran is losing its influence over it," said Matt Smith, director of commodity research at Kpler. [VERIFIED]

The milestone is the product of a complex operation involving military-escorted shuttles engaging in surreptitious "dark" transits that has helped restore significant flows for Middle East oil producers. Adding to the recent strait traffic is a return of oil from Saudi Arabia that had been primarily diverted to the Red Sea until Iran-allied Houthis attacked a major Saudi oil pipeline earlier this month. [REPORTED]

Satellite imagery has confirmed a "major operational recovery" at Saudi Arabia's Yanbu export terminal, according to Kpler. Exports from Yanbu were disrupted by the drone attack on the East-West pipeline earlier this month. Saudi Arabia has restored flows through the pipeline to around 3.5 million barrels per day. [VERIFIED]

However, the recovery is uneven. The JP Morgan note suggests that crude oil flows from the Middle East have normalized at about 98% of prewar volumes, but refined products (58% of prewar) have not, supporting higher finished product prices. [REPORTED]

**Significance:** This development fundamentally changes Iran's negotiating position. Tehran's primary leverage throughout the war has been its ability to disrupt global energy markets through Hormuz. With crude flows substantially restored, that leverage is diminished—which may explain Iran's continued engagement on the 7-day proposal despite Trump's public rejection.

## UK Prime Minister Attributes Fairford Incident to Iran

Speaking to the BBC on Wednesday, UK Prime Minister Burnham said: "There are strong indications that Iran played a part in what happened over the weekend at RAF Fairford, but of course it remains an ongoing, complex, and serious police investigation." He added: "We will say more in due course, but we can confirm our belief that we believe Iran to have played a part." [OFFICIAL]

Three vans were discovered blocking a road near RAF Fairford in Gloucestershire, which has been used in recent months by US bombers to launch airstrikes against Iran, in the early hours of September 27. Five British nationals, all in their mid-20s, were subsequently arrested on suspicion of explosives offences and terrorism before being released on bail on Monday September 28. [VERIFIED]

Police say they found a quantity of an accelerant—gasoline—in the vans, but no explosives. [OFFICIAL]

Burnham revealed he chaired a meeting of COBRA on Monday to review the incident with cabinet colleagues. [OFFICIAL]

The UK earlier this year allowed the US Air Force to use RAF Fairford for limited defensive strikes against Iranian missile capabilities. Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps named RAF Fairford in July when it said that any British base used for attacks on Iranian territory would be considered a "legitimate target." [VERIFIED]

Rubio told Fox News Monday that the incident "ties us to the reality here, and that is we are dealing with elements in the world—and in the case of Iran, for example, who have openly threatened to attack American interests globally." [OFFICIAL]

**Significance:** This is a significant escalation from Rubio's earlier "foreign actor" statement. The UK—a Five Eyes ally—is now publicly attributing the incident to Iran while the investigation remains open. This creates potential diplomatic consequences and pressure for response. The finding of petrol rather than explosives suggests a possible arson plot rather than the bombing originally feared, which somewhat reduces severity but does not eliminate the gravity of alleged state-sponsored terrorism on allied soil.

## US Completes Iraq Withdrawal After 20+ Years

The U.S. military announced on Wednesday that it has completed the withdrawal of all American troops from Iraq, ending a 12-year mission to fight the Islamic State group. [VERIFIED]

The long-planned withdrawal was completed after US troops left their last remaining bases in Erbil, northern Iraq, and comes amid the ongoing war between the US and neighboring Iran. [VERIFIED]

Iraqi Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi presided over the handover ceremony and described the U.S. withdrawal as the beginning of a "new phase, one defined by Iraq's sovereignty, the strength of its institutions, the readiness of its forces, the unity of its decision-making, and balanced partnerships with its friends." [OFFICIAL]

US President Donald Trump hailed the withdrawal of US-led coalition forces as a "victory" for Washington. "This Day is a victory for the United States, a victory for Iraq, and a decisive victory over ISIS," Trump said. [OFFICIAL]

Iraq is set to hold four "Sovereignty Days", a national holiday marking the complete withdrawal of US troops from the country and underscoring the deep symbolism of the exit after more than two decades of foreign intervention. [VERIFIED]

**Iran-allied militias celebrating:** "The expulsion of the occupation forces, defeated, from the land of Iraq is the beginning of full sovereignty," Abu Mujahid al-Assaf, a security official with the powerful Kataib Hezbollah militia, said in a statement. [REPORTED]

One Iranian-supported militia, As'hab Al-Kahf, cheered the US exit, congratulating Iraqis "on the humiliating withdrawal of the American occupier under the strikes of the Resistance's drones and missiles." [REPORTED]

Iran's allies are celebrating today after Washington withdrew the last of its forces from neighboring Iraq. Tehran views the pullout as a victory for its "axis of resistance" of allied armed groups, and a step toward its aim of driving US forces out of the region. [REPORTED]

**Kurdish concerns:** The Kurdish regional government's Peshmerga Ministry said the Kurdish region had faced more than 1,000 drone and ballistic-missile attacks during the recent U.S.-Iran conflict. "The departure of the United States from the Kurdistan Region under these complex regional circumstances, without the provision of a defense system, is a matter of concern," the statement said. [OFFICIAL]

**Significance:** The withdrawal was pre-planned (agreed in 2024) but lands amid the Iran war with substantial symbolic weight. Iran and its proxies are framing it as a strategic victory, which strengthens Tehran's domestic narrative that it is winning the broader regional contest. For the US, it removes base vulnerabilities but also reduces regional presence at a time of ongoing conflict.

## Iran Receives Formal US Response; Sequencing Remains Core Dispute

Iranian government spokesperson Fatemeh Mohajerani confirmed to the state-run IRNA news agency that the U.S. response had been received, saying that at the cabinet meeting Foreign Minister Araghchi reported the U.S. proposal to President Masoud Pezeshkian. [OFFICIAL]

Iranian foreign minister Abbas Araqchi received US feedback via Qatari mediators in Doha on Tuesday on a seven-day plan aimed at building trust between Washington and Tehran, with sequencing the main sticking point, an official briefed on the talks told Reuters. [REPORTED]

"The core disagreement between the two sides is the sequence of implementing the plan," the source said, adding, "The plan aims to build trust and return to a strengthened version of the cease-fire memorandum of understanding signed in June." [REPORTED]

Qatari officials met Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi in Doha on September 29 after returning from Washington, and Araghchi was expected to review the response with officials in Tehran on September 30. An official briefed on the talks said the plan provides for "a seven-day period of trust-building aimed at returning the two sides to an enhanced version of the memorandum of understanding agreed in June, including concrete steps on Iran's nuclear program." [REPORTED]

"We will make every effort to bring the agreement to fruition and will firmly stand up for the rights of the Iranian people," Pezeshkian said. [OFFICIAL]

**Significance:** Despite Trump's public rejection, a formal response was transmitted through mediators. The dispute being characterized as about "sequencing rather than components" suggests the substantive framework has overlap—the question is who moves first. This is closer to a deal than public rhetoric suggests, though substantial obstacles remain.

## Oil Prices Drop Sharply on Flow Recovery

Brent rose to 97.09 USD/Bbl on September 30, 2026, up 0.97% from the previous day. Over the past month, Brent's price has risen 7.30%, and is up 48.57% compared to the same time last year. [VERIFIED]

This represents a significant decline from the ~$105/barrel level on September 29. The September 30 close was $97.46, down from $100.28 on September 29. [VERIFIED]

Fortune reported Brent at $102.56 per barrel at 9:25 a.m. Eastern Time, representing an increase of $2.37 since yesterday's report—showing considerable intraday volatility. [REPORTED]

**Significance:** Oil prices are responding to the Hormuz flow recovery. The drop from Monday's highs of ~$105 to the mid-$90s represents the market pricing in reduced disruption risk. If crude flows remain normalized, prices could continue declining—a significant political development ahead of the November 3 midterms.

## NPT Withdrawal Bill: Political Positioning Continues

Iran's parliament has drafted a bill on withdrawing from the Treaty on the Non-Proliferation of Nuclear Weapons (NPT), Iranian Deputy Parliament Speaker Ali Nikzad announced. The proposal is currently under review, including an assessment of its potential international repercussions. [OFFICIAL]

Once finalised, the bill is expected to be brought before a plenary session under an exceptional-urgency procedure, which would require lawmakers to consider it on the same day. [REPORTED]

"Leaving the NPT during wartime would effectively mean pursuing a nuclear weapon, something Iran does not intend to do," Mohammad Mehdi Shahriari told Jamaran. "Withdrawal would only increase costs and further escalate the situation," he added. [REPORTED]

The prior synthesis noted that the bill is unlikely to pass, with potentially fewer than 70 votes of the 290-seat parliament. The bill functions as hardliner positioning to constrain Pezeshkian's diplomatic flexibility rather than imminent policy shift.

## Yemen Theater: Structural Escalation Continues

The fighting coincides with the ongoing 2026 Iran war, which has marked the deadliest fighting in Yemen since the collapse of the 2022 ceasefire. [VERIFIED]

The Yemeni government lost areas around the Bab al-Mandab Strait in Yemen's southwest after a swift battle over Mokha, resulting in the entire west coast of Yemen falling under control of the Houthi movement. [VERIFIED]

The Iranian Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) reportedly guided and armed the Houthi offensive. Saudi-backed government sources claimed the offensive was led by IRGC Quds Force officer Abdolreza Shahlaei. There are reportedly hundreds of IRGC officers stationed in Yemen to support Houthi operations, aiming to close the Strait of Bab al-Mandab. [REPORTED]

Houthi strikes on Saudi Arabia's Abha Airport in July 2026 now threaten to reignite the civil war. [VERIFIED]

The Yemen theater remains structurally expanded. Iran's 7-day proposal notably omits Yemen, leaving the Bab al-Mandab chokepoint outside any prospective deal.

**Key Drivers of Change:**

JPMorgan calling it "a remarkable recovery for a region still at war" captures the strategic significance.

2. **Diplomatic specifics emerging.** The formal US response and characterization of the dispute as "sequencing rather than components" indicates both sides agree on what needs to happen—the disagreement is who moves first. This is a negotiable gap, not a fundamental impasse.

3. **Symbolic victories for Iran offset by material losses.** The Iraq withdrawal provides narrative ammunition for the "axis of resistance," but Iran's economic position continues to deteriorate with oil exports blocked and frozen assets inaccessible.

4. **Fairford attribution creates pressure but limited escalation risk.** The UK public statement creates diplomatic consequences, but the finding of petrol rather than explosives, and the release on bail, suggests the incident will not become a major flashpoint.
