# US-Israel-Iran Conflict: US Strikes Qeshm Island as Action-Reaction Cycle Accelerates; Third Bahrain Attack in Five Days

## November 17, 2026 — Day 263

**Assessment:** The conflict entered a new phase of escalation today with US fighter jets striking an IRGC missile launch site on Iran's Qeshm Island — the two sides intensified tit-for-tat attacks in the ongoing war. This came hours after Iran's third drone attack on Bahrain in five days, breaking a two-month lull in strikes on US regional basing. The LPG tanker Tara Gas was struck twice in the Strait of Hormuz, forcing crew abandonment with one Indian seafarer still missing. Despite the spike in kinetic activity, Brent crude fell to ~$92/barrel — "the surplus story is in charge, and Brent is back at the bottom of its November range." The pattern reveals a conflict locked in sustained mutual attrition: both sides are escalating within defined limits while markets price the stalemate as economically manageable.

---

## 1. US Strikes IRGC Missile Site on Qeshm Island — First Confirmed Strike in Two Weeks

American warplanes hit an Iranian missile launch site on Qeshm Island in the Persian Gulf on Tuesday, a US military official said. [VERIFIED]

"Earlier today, US fighter aircraft used precision munitions to strike an Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) missile launch site on Qeshm Island in self-defense," a US military official told *The Jerusalem Post*. "The strike was in response to IRGC aggression targeting US personnel and assets."

Iranian news agency IRNA reported that "the sound of five to six explosions was heard in the city of Qeshm." Earlier on Tuesday, Iran's semi-official Mehr News Agency reported that US forces had struck several locations on Qeshm Island, adding the assault caused no casualties or damage to critical and public-service infrastructure.

The latest operation is separate from earlier US strikes on Qeshm Island reported on November 2, which American officials had also described as defensive actions. Qeshm lies off Iran's southern coast in the Strait of Hormuz — the critical transit point for oil and gas that has long been the focal point of the conflict.

**Significance:** This marks the return of direct US strikes on Iranian territory after a two-week pause. The increase in strikes suggests neither the US nor Iran is keen to compromise on key issues that could resolve the war, such as an end to the US naval blockade or major concessions from Iran on its nuclear programme. Both sides continue characterizing their actions as "defensive" while systematically targeting the other's military infrastructure — a pattern that sustains the conflict without triggering strategic escalation.

## 2. Iran Attacks Bahrain for Third Time in Five Days — Two-Month Lull Broken

Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps said it launched drones at a U.S. radar system at Bahrain's Sheikh Isa Air Base on Tuesday, the third such attack on the Persian Gulf kingdom in five days after a roughly two-month lull. [VERIFIED]

U.S. Central Command, or CENTCOM, said the multiple one-way attack drones were intercepted without damage to U.S. equipment or injury to American personnel. Bahrain's armed forces said they intercepted and destroyed a number of Iranian aerial attacks before they could achieve their objectives.

The IRGC said its drones carried out a "precision operation" in response to repeated violations of Iranian airspace by U.S. military drones. Iran's attacks could signal a shift in its strategy after months during which it waited for an attack and then retaliated to target the operational capability of bases, Tehran-based security analyst Mostafa Najafi told The Associated Press.

Tuesday's attack on Bahrain, a Shiite-majority country ruled by a Sunni Muslim family, followed the sentencing there of 64 people, mostly Shiite clerics, to life in prison on charges of colluding with Iran to undermine the state.

**Regional Condemnation:** Egypt, Qatar, Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates each condemned Iran's attack on Bahrain, calling it a violation of Bahrain's sovereignty and a threat to its security and stability. Jasem Al Budaiwi, the head of the Gulf Co-operation Council, called it a "blatant challenge" to regional peace efforts. Iran's "persistence in repeating its assaults" was a "direct threat" to the region, he said. Qatar said Iran's "continued attacks" on Bahrain "undermine diplomatic efforts" and warned that any escalation in fighting posed an "extremely grave danger" to the prospects of reaching an agreement.

**Significance:** Iran has lowered its threshold for retaliation — now striking in response to US drone surveillance flights, not just direct attacks. The resumption after a two-month lull represents a deliberate policy shift. Regional condemnation, including from Qatar (a key mediator), increases diplomatic isolation for Tehran but has not yet translated to operational consequences.

## 3. Tanker Tara Gas Double-Struck — One Indian Seafarer Missing

The crew of the Panama-flagged LPG carrier Tara Gas have abandoned ship after the vessel was attacked twice in the Strait of Hormuz. The Tara Gas was first struck while transiting the Strait of Hormuz on 16 November according to maritime security firm Vanguard Tech. [VERIFIED]

The incident took place 7nm north Khasab, Oman and the impact disabled the vessel resulting in an engine room fire. Indian Minister of Ports, Shipping & Waterways, Sarbananda Sonowal, said that in a "distressing development" the Tara Gas was subject to a subsequent attack and the crew were forced to abandon ship. "While 24 crew members have been safely accounted for, one Indian seafarer remains missing."

The Indian Embassy in Oman said 24 crew members were evacuated, while search operations were continuing for the missing Indian seafarer. Oman's Maritime Security Center later confirmed that 24 crew members had been evacuated from the Tara Gas after a fire broke out aboard the vessel two nautical miles off Musandam.

There were a total of 24 Indian seafarers aboard the tanker, MT Tara Gas. Two of them were hospitalised with injuries while others were safely evacuated.

**Significance:** The double-strike pattern — targeting a vessel already disabled from an initial attack — represents an escalation in Iran's maritime campaign. The missing seafarer is the first confirmed missing crew member in weeks, adding a human casualty dimension that could complicate India's neutrality and prompt stronger diplomatic responses. At least 22 Indian nationals have been killed since the war began.

## 4. Iran Claims 18th US Drone Shootdown — US Has Not Confirmed

Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) claimed on Tuesday that it shot down a US MQ-1 Predator reconnaissance and combat drone in the southern waters of Iran, near Qeshm Island. According to the Guard, this would be the eighteenth drone of this type that its forces have destroyed. [REPORTED — Iranian claim, unconfirmed by US]

The army's public relations office said the drone was "hit and destroyed" by domestically developed air defense systems operating under Iran's integrated air defense network. Separately, Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps Navy said earlier that it had brought down a US MQM-170 drone intact south of Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz.

There has been no independent confirmation of Iran's claim from US authorities.

**Significance:** If accurate, Iran's cumulative drone shootdown claims suggest it is successfully contesting US ISR dominance over the Strait. However, Iranian claims frequently exceed verifiable losses. The US typically confirms significant asset losses, and silence may indicate either ongoing operations security or exaggerated Iranian claims.

## 5. Oil Markets Price Surplus Despite Escalation — Brent Falls to ~$92

Brent fell to 91.80 USD/Bbl on November 17, 2026, down 0.70% from the previous day. Brent crude futures traded at $91.66 at 04:30 GMT on Tuesday, down 78 cents or 0.8 percent, after a 3.55 percent fall on Monday to $92.44. [VERIFIED]

Over the past month, Brent's price has fallen 16.21%, but it is still 41.46% higher than a year ago.

Oil prices declined on Tuesday, as expectations of a global supply surplus and recovering exports from the Middle East outweighed concerns over continued attacks on oil tankers and energy infrastructure in the Gulf region.

Bear scenario: exports keep recovering, inventories build and the market starts to price next year's surplus now. Base scenario at $91-$93: attacks on shipping continue without cutting supply, and Brent holds near the November low.

**Significance:** The ~$5 drop from $96-97 on November 15-16 to ~$92 on November 17 — coinciding with the most concentrated escalation in weeks — reveals a decisive market judgment. Traders have concluded that Iran cannot close Hormuz, the US Navy's escort operation is succeeding, and global supply recovery is outpacing disruption. This reduces Iran's economic leverage substantially.

## 6. IAEA Board Meeting Underway — Iran Rejects Inspections

The IAEA Board of Governors is meeting November 16-20 in Vienna. [OFFICIAL]

Iran's Mission to the UN Office in Vienna criticized the IAEA Board of Governors' resolutions on Iran as legally flawed, saying normal implementation of safeguards cannot resume under the abnormal circumstances created by attacks on the country's safeguarded nuclear facilities.

"As stated in my reports to the IAEA Board of Governors, as of today the IAEA has not been able to verify that there has been no diversion of nuclear material required to be safeguarded under Iran's CSA." (Director General Grossi to UN Security Council, October 2026)

The European Union accused Iran of suspending its cooperation with the IAEA and refusing to allow the agency to inspect its nuclear facilities. Since the US and Israel launched attacks on Iran in June last year, IAEA staff have not been allowed to continue inspecting Iran's three nuclear facilities.

**Significance:** The ongoing Board meeting could produce additional resolutions increasing diplomatic pressure on Iran, though it is unlikely the UN Security Council will take any action, as Iran's allies Russia and China are veto-holding members. The nuclear verification gap remains a persistent source of uncertainty and potential escalation trigger.

## 7. Israeli Coalition Talks — Stalemate Continues

Representatives of Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's Likud, Gadi Eisenkot's Yashar and Naftali Bennett's B'Yachad (Together) parties met on Sunday for initial coalition negotiations and agreed to continue discussions on forming a government. [VERIFIED]

Ahead of the talks, Yashar denied an Army Radio report that it was considering offering Likud a rotation agreement contingent on Eisenkot serving first as prime minister, telling The Times of Israel that the only proposal on the table was for Likud to join a government led by Eisenkot.

Representatives of Israel's largest parties met for talks seen largely as a statement of intent. Likud wants to show it is reaching out, while Eisenkot continues pursuing options for a government without Netanyahu. Looming over the talks: the battle for Knesset speaker and reliance on Arab parties' votes.

President Isaac Herzog has awarded the mandate to Eisenkot, presenting him with the task of forming the next government. Eisenkot will initially have 28 days, with a possible 14-day extension, to form a coalition. (Deadline: December 6)

**Significance:** Coalition negotiations remain deadlocked with no substantive progress. The Netanyahu-Zamir alignment reported yesterday suggests the defense establishment is not currently pressing for immediate action on Iran, reducing the risk of an Israeli unilateral strike during this political transition. Eisenkot has 19 days remaining on his initial mandate.
