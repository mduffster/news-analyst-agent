## B-1B Bombers Withdrawn From RAF Fairford After Iranian Threats

The most consequential operational development of the weekend is the complete withdrawal of US strategic bombers from the United Kingdom. The United States has made the abrupt decision to withdraw its bombers from the Royal Air Force (RAF) base Fairford in the western part of the United Kingdom, citing concerns about a potential attack.

The United States withdrew all twelve B-1B Lancer strategic bombers from RAF Fairford in the United Kingdom on October 4, 2026, returning the aircraft to their home stations after months of supporting U.S. operations against Iran.

President Trump explicitly linked the withdrawal to Iranian threats. When asked if there was a specific threat that led to the bombers being moved, Trump told reporters: "Yeah, I think we can say that. They didn't fly out by accident. There were some threats, and rather than tempting the enemy… why should we leave them there? We moved them around a little bit, not very far."

He added that Washington "actually knows the people that made the threat." When asked if they are linked to Iran, Trump responded: "They would be linked to Iran."

The specific nature of the threat was reportedly a drone attack. ITV News has now learnt from a source that the United States was concerned about a multiple drone attack—prompting the withdrawal of US bombers from RAF Fairford.

The Pentagon emphasized continued strike capability. "The Airmen that operate America's bomber force, including B-1s, B-2s, and B-52s, remain ready to deliver precision global strike capabilities – including through operations that launch and recover from the continental United States – anywhere, anytime," the official added.

**Significance:** [VERIFIED] This represents the first major operational concession to Iranian pressure during the conflict. While CONUS-based bombers can still strike Iran, the withdrawal removes the forward-deployment advantage that enabled faster response times. The explicit acknowledgment that specific, known threats drove the decision—rather than routine rotation—validates Iranian asymmetric strategy. The pattern of warnings extending to German bases suggests a broader Iranian campaign to raise European costs of participation.

## Camp David War Council Signals Major Decisions Ahead

President Trump's top cabinet members huddled for hours at Camp David on Friday to discuss next steps in the Iran war and the Saudi-Houthi conflict in Yemen, three U.S. officials told Axios.

The highly unusual meeting was not announced by the Trump administration. The last time a similar meeting took place was in June 2025, just days before Israel went to war against Iran.

The meeting was comprehensive. Vice President Vance chaired the meeting. Among the other participants were Secretary of State Marco Rubio, Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth, White House envoy Steve Witkoff, CIA director John Ratcliffe, chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff Gen. Dan Caine and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent.

"Things were decided or at least deeply discussed," one of the officials said, referring to the crisis in the Middle East.

With negotiations stalled, President Trump is considering resuming major combat operations in Iran.

Secretary Rubio subsequently attempted to downplay the meeting's significance. Secretary of State Marco Rubio confirmed the Camp David meeting in a gaggle with reporters on Monday before heading to Europe. "We constantly are meeting on long-term strategic planning and things of this nature. It is not unusual for us as a team to get together. That was just an opportunity to do it in a setting where there were less distractions," he said.

Iran was not the only issue discussed at Camp David. US officials told Axios that the Saudi-Houthi conflict in Yemen was also on the agenda as Washington considers how far it is prepared to become involved. That makes Friday's meeting potentially about more than the next phase of the Iran war. Trump is weighing decisions that could affect two interconnected conflicts across the region.

**Significance:** [REPORTED] The June 2025 precedent—when a similar meeting preceded Israel's strikes on Iran—makes this gathering highly significant. Combined with the carrier surge and Trump's ultimatum rhetoric, the administration appears to be positioning for major post-midterm decisions. The Yemen component suggests potential US involvement in the new Saudi offensive.

## Operation Yemen Dawn: Saudi-Backed Forces Claim Bab el-Mandeb Control

The Yemen conflict expanded dramatically with a major Saudi-backed offensive launched October 5. "Yemen will march towards a new dawn of freedom, peace and stability," said Rashad Al Alimi, head of Yemen's Presidential Leadership Council, announcing Operation Yemen Dawn. Yemeni government forces claimed on Monday they had seized key positions around the Bab Al Mandeb strait and the Dhubab area overlooking the sea lane, as a Saudi-led coalition said 100 fighter jets were taking part in an anti-Houthi offensive.

Coalition spokesman Maj Gen Turki Al Malki said the alliance was providing round-the-clock air support to Yemeni armed forces on the ground and air cover for the Bab Al Mandeb strait, with operations against moving targets continuing. He said 324 "high-value" Houthi targets had been destroyed in the offensive, with 100 fighter jets involved.

Saudi-backed Yemeni government forces seized key positions on the Bab el-Mandeb Strait on Monday, military and government sources said, as they fought to reverse last month's advances by the Iran-backed Houthis and drive them from the vital waterway. The government forces have taken large parts of Dhubab district, which overlooks Bab el-Mandeb, and are asserting full control over the area with heavy air support from a Saudi-led coalition.

The Houthis responded with attacks on Saudi territory. Yemen's Iran-aligned Houthis said on Saturday they had targeted an Aramco facility in the Saudi capital, Riyadh, with ballistic missiles and drones, saying the attack was in retaliation for Saudi attacks on Yemen's Sanaa and other provinces.

The Houthis' claims that the Iran-allied armed group has targeted Saudi Arabia's capital Riyadh are "misleading", according to the Saudi-led coalition supporting Yemen's internationally recognized government. Coalition spokesman Turki al-Maliki's remarks came on Saturday after the Houthis claimed ballistic missile and drone attacks on an Aramco oil facility near Riyadh. [DISPUTED]

**Significance:** [REPORTED/VERIFIED] The Yemen offensive opens a second major theater in the conflict, directly addressing Houthi control of Bab el-Mandeb—which they seized in September. This dual-chokepoint dynamic (Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb) was explicitly discussed at the Camp David meeting. The claims of rapid gains cannot be independently verified, but the scale of the Saudi air commitment (100 jets, 324 targets) indicates serious intent.

## Hormuz Shipping Attacks Intensify: Seven Incidents in Five Days

Maritime attacks in the Strait of Hormuz accelerated significantly. This is the fourth incident reported to UKMTO in the last 24 hours and marks another major attack on commercial shipping in the Strait of Hormuz, with at least seven tanker attacks reported during the first week of October.

Another oil tanker has been struck by an unidentified projectile in the Strait of Hormuz, the United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations (UKMTO) said on October 4 (local time). This marks the fourth time this month that the UKMTO has reported a vessel being attacked in the Strait of Hormuz.

The UKMTO stated on the same day, "A report of an incident has been received in the Strait of Hormuz," adding that "an oil tanker was struck by an unknown projectile, resulting in damage to the engine room." It added that the crew members are safe, no environmental damage has been reported, and authorities are investigating the incident.

A new escalation emerged with direct IRGC intervention. Iran's IRGC orders tanker to turn back in Hormuz. The tanker was inbound through the Strait of Hormuz when it was hailed by the IRGC and instructed to turn back or face being targeted. The vessel's master confirmed that the tanker complied with the instruction, UKMTO said.

The cumulative attack toll remains severe. In its weekly Voluntary Reporting Area Overview released on 2 October, UKMTO said 91 incidents of damage to vessels have been reported since February this year.

Iran reiterated its position. Iran says the Strait of Hormuz will remain "closed" until the US meets seven conditions, while Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi says there is "no military solution" to the conflict. [OFFICIAL]

**Significance:** [VERIFIED] The attack tempo is accelerating—seven incidents in five days represents a marked increase. The IRGC's direct order for a tanker to turn back represents a qualitative escalation from projectile attacks to overt enforcement of closure. Despite continued attacks, Middle East oil exports excluding Iran rose above pre-war levels last week despite attacks on ships in the Strait of Hormuz, according to maritime tracking firm Kpler.

## FlyDubai Investigation: Pre-Planned Attack, But No State Link Found

The investigation into the September 30 cockpit attack produced significant new findings. The flydubai co-pilot who stabbed his captain in an alleged attempt to crash the plane last week told UAE interrogators he planned the attack before being hired by the airline, according to two Israeli officials with knowledge of the probe. A third source briefed on the UAE probe said the co-pilot, an Omani national, had planned to crash Wednesday's flight into Ben Gurion airport near Tel Aviv. He had also operated a flight to Tel Aviv earlier this year and intended to hijack and crash the plane then, but opted not to, the source added.

The two Israeli officials said the co-pilot told interrogators that he had applied for a job at Dubai state-owned flydubai after searching for airlines that fly to Tel Aviv.

The UAE formally designated the incident as terrorism. The co-pilot of flydubai flight FZ1073, which was bound for Israel from Dubai last Wednesday, attacked the pilot with an emergency crash axe, the United Arab Emirates prosecutor general has said, calling the incident a "terrorist attack". [OFFICIAL]

However, no state or organizational link has been established. The third source said that Israel was being continuously updated about the investigation from the UAE. Israeli sources maintain they have found no evidence of state direction, though Trump previously suggested Iranian involvement without providing evidence.

**Significance:** [REPORTED] The emerging profile is of a lone actor who deliberately sought employment at an airline serving Tel Aviv and planned the attack over an extended period—consistent with inspired terrorism rather than directed state action. This reduces the incident's utility as a potential casus belli, though the revelations about pre-employment planning raise serious questions about aviation security screening.

## Israeli Warnings Extend Threat Assessment to German Bases

The threat picture expanded beyond RAF Fairford to continental Europe. Israel warned Germany of an increased risk of attacks against US military bases on its territory, particularly Ramstein Air Base and Spangdahlem Air Base, The New York Times reported on Saturday, citing a senior US official.

Across Europe, concern among officials was growing rapidly. According to a senior US official, Israel warned Germany that US bases there were at heightened risk, noting Spangdahlem and Ramstein in particular.

On September 23, media outlets affiliated with Iran published remarks by a former senior Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps official who explicitly mentioned Ramstein Air Base as one of the facilities used by the US in its war against Iran.

According to Site Intelligence Group, which tracks the online activity of jihadi organisations, Safavi condemned Europe as a "logistics hub" for the US war on Iran, naming airbases that US forces have used to conduct the war, including Ramstein in Germany, Aviano in Italy and Fairford.

**Significance:** [REPORTED] The extension of threat warnings to German bases—and the explicit IRGC-linked media identification of these facilities—suggests a coordinated Iranian pressure campaign against European participation in the conflict. If threats materialize, European governments will face difficult decisions about continued support.

## Third Carrier Strike Group Deploys; Three-Carrier Presence Possible

The US military buildup continues. The Navy is sending a third aircraft carrier strike group to the Middle East for what is expected to be an extended deployment amid rising tensions with Iran. The USS Theodore Roosevelt left its San Diego home port without fanfare Sunday.

It is deploying to the U.S. Central Command region where it is expected to replace the USS George Washington. The Japan-based Washington arrived in the Middle East in August to relieve the USS Abraham Lincoln, which spent more than 250 days at sea supporting operations related to the U.S. conflict with Iran.

A three-carrier presence remains possible. Separate reports indicate the United States may keep the George Washington in the region, creating a three-carrier force capable of delivering overwhelming air and naval power.

According to the Wall Street Journal, the deployment of USS Theodore Roosevelt, USS Makin Island and other ships adds 9,000 to 10,000 US troops to the more than 50,000 already in the Middle East, with arrivals expected by the end of November 2026.

USS Theodore Roosevelt deployed with more supplies as the Navy applies lessons learned from extended carrier operations during the Iran war. The USS Theodore Roosevelt is sailing with "significantly more" food and supplies than the USS Abraham Lincoln carried, in part due to damage to a logistics base in Bahrain.

**Significance:** [VERIFIED] The Roosevelt deployment timeline—arrival by late November—aligns precisely with Trump's stated post-midterm escalation window. The extended supply load suggests the Navy is preparing for sustained operations without port access. A three-carrier presence would represent unprecedented concentration in the region during this conflict.
