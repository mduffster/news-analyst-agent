## Key Developments (Past 24 Hours)

### **SENATE REPUBLICANS BLOCK WAR POWERS RESOLUTION** [VERIFIED]

The Senate voted 51-48, largely along party lines, to defeat a war powers resolution previously passed by the House of Representatives.

Only three Republicans – Susan Collins of Maine, Lisa Murkowski of Alaska and Rand Paul of Kentucky – voted with Democrats in favor of the resolution. One Democrat, John Fetterman of Pennsylvania, voted with Republicans against it.

The vote marked the first time the Senate had considered a major Iran war-related measure since Democratic victories in the midterm elections on Nov 3. Trump's fellow Republicans, who rarely break with the president, stood behind him in support of the US-Israel war on Iran that started on Feb 28.

**[ANALYSIS]** This is a significant political test. Despite the midterm losses attributed to the war, Senate Republicans held firm. However, this was the *lame-duck* GOP majority — the new Democratic-controlled Senate will have far more leverage starting in January.

### **NETANYAHU PRESSING FOR 4-7 DAY AIR CAMPAIGN** [REPORTED]

President Donald Trump is weighing a new round of military strikes against Iran amid pressure from Israel over Tehran's rebuilt missile and drone capabilities, though internal administration disagreements remain over the intelligence and justification for an attack.

Despite these concerns, Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu is pushing the US to join Israel in a four- to seven-day airstrike campaign against Iran, the report said.

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth and Joint Chiefs of Staff Chairman General Dan Caine briefed Trump on strike plans crafted by US Central Command chief Admiral Brad Cooper, while Washington withdrew regional personnel and sent Israel another Patriot battery.

However, a senior Israeli official disputed the characterization that Netanyahu had pressured Trump to act. A spokesman for the prime minister said Netanyahu had always worked to "safeguard the security" of Israel.

### **TRUMP DELAYS DECISION TO 2027** [VERIFIED]

US President Donald Trump said he was looking to next year before deciding on renewed strikes against Iran, pushing back a timeline he had previously tied to this month's midterm elections. Asked about the possibility of new Iran strikes, Mr Trump told Reuters in an interview: "Well, I'll talk to you about it after the beginning of the year."

"I am not rushing into any decision [on Iran]. We will see what happens after the beginning of the year," Trump told Axios from Air Force One.

US President Donald Trump backed out multiple times in recent weeks from strikes ready to be launched against Iran, Channel 12 says in an unsourced report that it says was approved by Israel's military censor.

Trump's stated timing could mean he's giving more time for the naval blockade and the economic pressure campaign to play out... But it could also indicate that if Iran doesn't make concessions by the end of the year, Trump wants to leave himself enough time for military action before the new Democratic-controlled Congress settles in.

**[ANALYSIS]** This is a significant shift. Trump appears to be rejecting Netanyahu's urgent timeline, but the Channel 12 report suggests this isn't the first time he's pulled back — and he could change course again. The January deadline creates a lame-duck window for action.

### **PENTAGON: WAR COST $48.3 BILLION** [VERIFIED]

The war against Iran has cost the U.S. military more than $48 billion since fighting began in late February, a Pentagon official told lawmakers Thursday. "That number includes munitions, equipment replacement costs, and operational cost," Arthur Miller, the Pentagon's deputy comptroller, said during a hearing on his nomination.

The tally excludes damage to U.S. bases in the Middle East and the war's broader economic fallout. Miller's testimony provided the first update since mid-October, when U.S. Central Command put the cost at $47.1 billion.

That figure is an increase of roughly 26% from one previously provided by the Congressional Budget Office, which put the cost of the war at US$38 billion as of Aug 1.

**[ANALYSIS]** At $48.3B, the war has consumed more than 5% of the annual defense budget in less than 9 months. The cost is accelerating — up $1.2B in just one month since the October estimate.

### **USS GEORGE WASHINGTON DEPARTS — TWO CARRIERS REMAIN** [VERIFIED]

The U.S. military is back to two aircraft carriers supporting operations against Iran after the USS George Washington left the Middle East, the Associated Press reported on Wednesday.

The Japan-based carrier strike group, originally out on a western Pacific deployment, headed to the Middle East to relieve USS Abraham Lincoln and some of its escorts. Similarly, the Theodore Roosevelt Carrier Strike Group arrived in the Middle East on Sunday prior to George Washington's departure.

The carrier's departure leaves at least 15 Navy warships in the Arabian Sea.

Its move to the Middle East left the US without a carrier in the Indo-Pacific.

**[ANALYSIS]** This was a planned rotation, not a drawdown. Two carriers (USS Theodore Roosevelt and USS George H.W. Bush) remain. The departure finally restores US carrier presence in the Indo-Pacific, which had been absent for three months.

### **THIRD TANKER ATTACKED THIS WEEK** [VERIFIED]

A tanker was struck by an unknown projectile in its engine room while sailing outbound through the Strait of Hormuz near Oman's Khasab, the United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations (UKMTO) said on Thursday, in the latest attack on commercial shipping in one of the world's most strategic waterways.

Another tanker has been struck in the Strait of Hormuz, the latest in an escalating series of attacks on merchant shipping that has left two seafarers missing and more than a dozen injured this week.

UKMTO reported that attack had occurred about 13 nautical miles north of Khasab and involved an "unknown projectile." A fire broke out aboard the vessel but was later extinguished.

### **EISENKOT: WILL FORCE GOVERNMENT VOTE** [REPORTED]

Yashar chairman Gadi Eisenkot, who has been tasked with forming Israel's next government, intends to bring a proposed government to a Knesset vote before his mandate expires, even if coalition negotiations led by his bloc fail, Haaretz has learned.

Likud announces fresh talks with Yashar, accuses Eisenkot-led bloc of obstructing unity.

As Gadi Eisenkot continues his attempts to form a government, coalition talks with the ultra-Orthodox parties – particularly between Yisrael Beiteinu chairman Avigdor Lieberman and the Degel Hatorah faction – are continuing.

Haredi analyst Yisrael Cohen tells Arutz Sheva about UTJ-Eisenkot bloc talks: Conscription demands remain far from opposition positions, but a Liberman plan could enable a deal, amid fears UTJ could be left out of government.

**[ANALYSIS]** Eisenkot is signaling he's prepared to form a minority government with Arab party support if negotiations fail. This aggressive timeline puts pressure on UTJ to decide quickly whether to join or risk exclusion.

Brent rose to 92.69 USD/Bbl on November 19, 2026, up 0.80% from the previous day.

Brent crude prices held around $92 per barrel on Thursday, trading near their lowest levels since late August as expectations of abundant global supplies outweighed renewed geopolitical tensions in the Middle East.
