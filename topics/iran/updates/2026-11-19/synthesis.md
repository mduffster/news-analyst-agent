# US-Israel-Iran Conflict: Senate Republicans Block War Powers Vote as Trump Delays Strike Decision to 2027; Netanyahu Pushes for 4-7 Day Air Campaign

## November 19, 2026 — Day 265

**Assessment:** The Trump administration's posture shifted decisively today toward delay rather than escalation. Trump confirmed he will wait until "after the beginning of the year" to decide on renewed major strikes, while Senate Republicans blocked a war powers resolution 51-48 despite midterm losses blamed partly on the war. These developments came as reporting revealed Netanyahu is pushing for a specific 4-7 day joint air campaign — a more concrete ask than previously known. The conflict's operational tempo remains intense: Iran attacked three tankers in Hormuz this week, the USS George Washington departed (reducing carriers to two), and Pentagon officials disclosed war costs have reached $48.3 billion. Oil markets largely shrugged off the violence, with Brent hovering near $92. The emerging picture is of a conflict settling into sustained attrition through year-end, with the January transition to Democratic congressional control creating a critical decision window.

---

## 1. Trump Delays Strike Decision to 2027

US President Donald Trump said he was looking to next year before deciding on renewed strikes against Iran, pushing back a timeline he had previously tied to this month's midterm elections. Asked about the possibility of new Iran strikes, Mr Trump told Reuters in an interview: "Well, I'll talk to you about it after the beginning of the year." [VERIFIED — direct quote]

Mr Trump has acknowledged Iran played a role in the losses but has said he would not have changed course, adding that he would hold off on fresh strikes for now.

This marks a significant shift from early November, when Trump suggested a decision was imminent. The discussions come after Trump had ruled out further major strikes before the elections, with his administration now weighing a renewed large-scale military campaign, continued efforts to strangle Iran's regime economically through sanctions and the naval blockade, or a negotiated settlement with Tehran.

**Significance:** The delay creates a critical window: either Trump acts during the lame-duck period before Democrats take full control, or the new Congress will have leverage over war funding and authorization. The explicit timeline — "after the beginning of the year" — suggests Trump is giving the blockade and economic pressure more time to work while preserving optionality.

## 2. Netanyahu Pushing for Specific 4-7 Day Joint Air Campaign

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has been pressing Washington to join Israel in an air campaign that would last four to seven days. US President Donald Trump is weighing another round of major military strikes against Iran as Israel urges Washington to act quickly, The New York Times reported, citing American and Israeli officials familiar with the discussions. [REPORTED — multiple sources]

Trump has yet to make a decision, the officials said. He is weighing Israeli assessments that Iran is rebuilding its missile and drone capabilities and advancing efforts to develop chemical and biological weapons.

The report said administration officials are divided over the reliability of the intelligence and whether another round of strikes is warranted. Skepticism has grown since the midterm elections, which the Times described as reflecting voter frustration over a war that drove up prices and dragged on beyond the swift victory Trump had promised.

According to the report, Netanyahu's push comes as he heads a caretaker government following last month's election and seeks to prevent opposition parties from forming a coalition. The Times noted that renewed fighting could delay or derail those coalition efforts and create pressure for a unity government that includes Netanyahu.

A senior Israeli official rejected the characterization that Netanyahu was pressuring Trump, while a spokesperson for the Prime Minister said Netanyahu had consistently acted to "safeguard the security" of Israel.

Yair Golan, leader of Israel's Democrats party, said in a post on X, "Netanyahu, for the sake of his survival in power, is pushing Israel toward another war." He called on the country's opposition to "set aside every political or partisan consideration, or any dispute over portfolios" and "establish an emergency government without delay."

**Significance:** This represents a more concrete Israeli ask than the general "lobbying for strikes" reported earlier this week. The 4-7 day timeframe suggests a concentrated campaign targeting Iran's rebuilt missile/drone capacity and potentially CBW facilities — not an open-ended escalation. However, administration skepticism post-midterms and Trump's explicit delay suggest this push has not yet succeeded.

## 3. Senate Republicans Block War Powers Resolution 51-48

US Senate Republicans on Thursday rejected legislation directing president Donald Trump to end the war in Iran or obtain Congress' approval to continue it, backing the president weeks after sweeping defeats in midterm elections largely tied to Americans' frustration with the conflict. [VERIFIED]

Those three Republicans – Sens. Susan Collins of Maine, Lisa Murkowski of Alaska and Rand Paul of Kentucky – had supported similar war powers resolutions before the election.

John Fetterman (D-Pa.), an ardent supporter of Israel, was the only Democrat to vote against it.

Thursday's vote marked the first time either chamber has weighed in on the war since the midterm elections, which dealt Republicans sweeping losses. Voters cited the war in Iran, and its toll on the economy, as among their top concerns.

Senator Chris Murphy did not vote.

**Significance:** Despite electoral losses blamed on the war, Senate Republicans held firm. This was the outgoing GOP majority's last major test on Iran — the new Democratic-controlled Senate in January will have far more leverage. Fetterman's break with Democrats continues his pattern of supporting the war since February, as he has distanced himself from the Democratic Party and has expressed support for Israel and the war on Iran.

## 4. Pentagon: War Has Cost $48.3 Billion

The war against Iran has cost the U.S. military more than $48 billion since fighting began in late February, a Pentagon official told lawmakers Thursday. "That number includes munitions, equipment replacement costs, and operational cost," Arthur Miller, the Pentagon's deputy comptroller, said during a hearing on his nomination to become assistant secretary of the Navy for financial management. [OFFICIAL]

The tally excludes damage to U.S. bases in the Middle East and the war's broader economic fallout. Miller's testimony provided the first update since mid-October, when U.S. Central Command put the cost at $47.1 billion.

That figure is an increase of roughly 26% from one previously provided by the Congressional Budget Office, which put the cost of the war at US$38 billion as of Aug 1.

The U.S. military campaign against Iran since the end of February 2026 has cost $48.3 billion. This is more than 5% of the country's annual defense budget.

**Cost Trajectory:**
- February 28 – March 5: $11.3 billion (first 6 days)
- August 1: $38 billion (CBO estimate)
- October: $47.1 billion (CENTCOM)
- November 13: $48.3 billion (Pentagon)

**Significance:** The war is consuming approximately $5.5 billion monthly — accelerating from earlier rates. Congressional pressure to disclose base damage costs is mounting, which could add substantially to the total. This provides ammunition for Democrats' efforts to constrain the war when they take control in January.

## 5. USS George Washington Departs — Two Carriers Remain

Aircraft carrier USS George Washington has sailed out of the Middle East after a three-month stay, leaving only two U.S. carriers in the region. [VERIFIED]

The aircraft carrier USS Theodore Roosevelt arrived in the Middle East on November 15, shortly before the George Washington departed.

The USS George H.W. Bush, which arrived in the region in April, and the Roosevelt are now the two carriers there. They are joined by more than a dozen destroyers, cruisers and a littoral combat ship.

The carrier's departure leaves at least 15 Navy warships in the Arabian Sea. Washington, the Navy's only forward-deployed aircraft carrier, entered the Middle East in August to relieve USS Abraham Lincoln amid concerns about the well-being and mental health of its service members.

**Significance:** This was a planned rotation, not a drawdown. The departure finally restores US carrier presence in the Indo-Pacific, which had been absent for three months. The carriers have served as the linchpin of a U.S. flotilla that has blockaded all merchant shipping to and from the Islamic Republic. The Trump administration is looking to keep squeezing Tehran's economy by preventing its oil from reaching global markets.

## 6. Third Tanker Attack This Week in Hormuz

Another tanker has been struck in the Strait of Hormuz, the latest in an escalating series of attacks on merchant shipping that has left two seafarers missing and more than a dozen injured this week. [VERIFIED — UKMTO confirmed]

A tanker was struck by an unknown projectile in its engine room while sailing outbound through the Strait of Hormuz, prompting a maritime security warning, according to the United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations (UKMTO). The incident occurred approximately 6 nautical miles north of Khasab, Oman, at 1645 UTC (8:45pm UAE time) on November 18.

After a period of low-grade clashes, attacks increased in October and continued at the higher tempo this month, according to data compiled by Bloomberg. In a sign of the mounting severity, at least two seafarers are missing after attacks this week.

**Weekly Attack Summary:**
- November 16: Tara Gas struck, one seafarer missing (Indian national)
- November 18 (early): ADNOC vessel (Navig8 Messi) struck, one seafarer missing, 11 injured
- November 18 (evening): Unidentified tanker struck near Khasab

**Significance:** The pace of attacks has intensified — four tanker attacks in 96 hours according to Gulf News. Despite this, oil markets remain largely unmoved, suggesting traders believe supply routes remain viable.

## 7. Eisenkot to Force Government Vote Even Without Majority

Yashar chairman Gadi Eisenkot, who has been tasked with forming Israel's next government, intends to bring a proposed government to a Knesset vote before his mandate expires, even if coalition negotiations led by his bloc fail, Haaretz has learned. [REPORTED]

A vote could come as soon as November 28 or 29, Haaretz reports.

Such a government could be seated if it wins a vote in the Knesset, even without holding an absolute majority of the body. Eisenkot is reportedly counting on the Arab-led Ra'am party to vote in favor of such a government, even without a coalition agreement, while the Arab-led Joint List would abstain.

Eisenkot has until December 6, with a possible 14-day extension, to form a government.

**Coalition Math:**
- Eisenkot bloc (Yashar, B'Yachad, Yisrael Beiteinu, Democrats): 48 seats
- Arab parties (Joint List + Ra'am): 19 seats
- Netanyahu bloc: 53 seats
- Need: 61 for majority

**Significance:** Eisenkot's willingness to force a vote — even without securing full coalition agreements — signals confidence he can win with Arab party support. The speaker election (66-52 for Eisenkot ally Kahana) demonstrated this arithmetic works. If Eisenkot forms government, Netanyahu's lobbying for joint strikes becomes moot.

## 8. Oil Markets Unmoved Despite Violence

Brent rose to 92.69 USD/Bbl on November 19, 2026, up 0.80% from the previous day. [VERIFIED]

Brent crude prices held around $92 per barrel on Thursday, trading near their lowest levels since late August as expectations of abundant global supplies outweighed renewed geopolitical tensions in the Middle East.

The prospect of an expanding global oil surplus has emerged as a key headwind for crude prices, with improving flows through the Strait of Hormuz easing concerns over the availability of supplies. The recovery in supply flows has strengthened expectations that the global market could face an excess of crude, particularly if demand growth fails to keep pace with rising availability.

**Price Context:**
- November 4: ~$104 (pre-midterm)
- November 9: ~$92 (post-midterm crash)
- November 19: ~$92-93 (stable near lows)

**Significance:** Markets have concluded the conflict is economically sustainable at current intensity. The 15% decline from early November reflects confidence that supply routes remain viable despite attacks. This paradoxically reduces pressure on both sides to seek resolution.
