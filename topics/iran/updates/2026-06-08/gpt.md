## Daily incremental update — US–Israel–Iran conflict  
**As of Mon, June 8, 2026 (ET)**  
**New since Fri, June 5, 2026 (ET)**

### 1) Fragile ceasefire framework hit hard: Israel struck Beirut (Dahieh), Iran responded with direct missiles at Israel
- **Israel conducted strikes in Beirut’s southern suburbs (Dahieh)** after Hezbollah attacks on northern Israel, per multiple reports. **[REPORTED]** ([axios.com](https://www.axios.com/2026/06/07/israel-strikes-lebanon-beirut-iran-response-trump?utm_source=openai))  
- **Iran launched missiles toward Israel on Sunday, June 7**—described as the first direct Iranian missile attack since the **April 8 ceasefire**, explicitly framed as retaliation for the Beirut strike. **[REPORTED]** ([axios.com](https://www.axios.com/2026/06/07/iran-israel-missiles-us-tehran-negotiations-ceasefire-risk?utm_source=openai))  
- Israel’s messaging (via spokespeople cited by outlets) signaled **planning for further action** and rejection of an Iran-driven “new equation” where Tehran strikes Israel in response to Beirut operations. **[REPORTED]** ([axios.com](https://www.axios.com/2026/06/07/trump-israel-iran-missile-attack?utm_source=openai))  

**What changed / what it means:**  
- This is the most important change since the last report: the conflict appears to be **re-coupling the Lebanon theater to direct Iran–Israel exchanges**, undermining the earlier “containment” logic.  
- It also increases the likelihood that escalation decisions now hinge on **Beirut strike frequency/scale** and **whether Iran chooses to normalize direct launches** (vs. reverting to proxies). That’s a major risk inflection for the next 0–60 days.

### 2) U.S. posture: Trump pushed for restraint to prevent an Israel–Iran tit-for-tat spiral
- President Trump said he would (and then reportedly did) **urge Netanyahu not to retaliate** after Iran’s missile attack, with Axios reporting the call occurred and was aimed at buying time for diplomacy. **[REPORTED]** ([axios.com](https://www.axios.com/2026/06/07/trump-israel-iran-missile-attack?utm_source=openai))  
- Other coverage similarly frames the U.S. line as **de-escalatory** (reduce immediate Israeli response) because escalation would damage negotiations and widen the war. **[REPORTED]** ([washingtonpost.com](https://www.washingtonpost.com/politics/2026/06/07/trump-says-us-open-unfreezing-iranian-funds-easing-sanctions-if-they-behave/?utm_source=openai))  

**What changed / what it means:**  
- Compared with June 5, this is a clearer signal that Washington is prioritizing **ceasefire salvage / escalation control** over permissive latitude for Israeli retaliation—at least publicly.  
- The gap to watch is **compliance**: if Israel acts anyway (or expands Beirut strikes), it will reveal limits of U.S. leverage and raise the odds of sustained, structured campaigning.

**What changed / what it means:**  
- Even if the main kinetic exchange re-centers on Israel–Lebanon–Iran, the **risk of U.S.–Iran incidents at sea** is rising because “warning” actions compress decision time and increase miscalculation odds (especially if paired with air-defense nerves after the Kuwait episode).
