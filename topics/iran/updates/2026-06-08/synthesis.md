## Executive Summary

**KEY DEVELOPMENT: War's 100th day marked by direct Israel-Iran missile exchange — most serious escalation since April 8 ceasefire — followed by mutual pause at US urging**

Israel and Iran traded fire on Monday, their war's 100th day, putting its already fragile truce in serious jeopardy and threatening to reignite all-out regional war. The new attacks came hours after President Trump reportedly called on Israel to refrain from retaliating against Tehran's missiles. Israel launched airstrikes at Beirut's southern suburbs Sunday, which led to Iran retaliating with its own strike on Israel, then to Monday's attacks and counterattacks.

The escalation sequence was clear: Iran launched missiles into Israel after the IDF carried out strikes against Hezbollah in Lebanon this weekend. Israel retaliated with its own strikes on Tehran, targeting military infrastructure and missile-production facilities.

By mid-day Monday, both sides appeared to pause: Iran said it would halt its military offensive against Israel after the two countries exchanged missile fire. However, Iran warned that its military would launch harsher attacks if Israel engages in further "hostile acts." Netanyahu said Israel has halted attacks on Iran, stopping short of acknowledging a ceasefire: "At the moment, the fire has ceased, because after we struck the terror regime in Tehran, it stopped attacking us. If the terror regime in Iran makes the mistake of attacking us again, we will respond with force."

**Critically, a new front has opened:** The Houthis, an Iran-backed militant group that controls much of Yemen, declared a ban on Israeli ships in the Red Sea, threatening a key bypass route to the Strait of Hormuz.

**The bottom line:** The April 8 ceasefire effectively collapsed on Day 100, though both sides have paused at US urging. The conflict has established a dangerous new pattern: Israeli strikes on Hezbollah in Lebanon now directly trigger Iranian retaliation on Israel. Trump's leverage over Netanyahu appears limited, with the Israeli leader striking Beirut and then Iran despite explicit US requests not to. The Houthi activation threatens a second maritime chokepoint. The conflict has entered its most volatile phase since the ceasefire began.

## Critical Developments (June 6-8)

### 1. Direct Israel-Iran Missile Exchange — MAJOR ESCALATION [VERIFIED]

**The Sequence:**
Tehran had repeatedly warned that it would intervene in support of Hezbollah if Israel targeted the Lebanese capital. The Iran-aligned armed group has rejected the US-brokered ceasefire between Israel and Lebanon.

Iran's Revolutionary Guard said its missiles late Sunday night and Monday morning targeted three military air bases in Israel, two in the center of the country and one in the north. Altogether, Iran launched nearly 30 ballistic missiles at Israel.

In the early hours of Monday, Israel unleashed missile strikes on Iran. Dozens of Israeli warplanes took part in the operation that focused mostly on Iranian air defenses that were being restored after the earlier fighting. Iranian citizens reported explosions in the capital Tehran, as well as Isfahan and Tabriz.

The Israeli military also said it hit several targets at a huge petrochemical complex in Mahshahr, in southwestern Iran. The workers at the plant were evacuated, the Iran media said.

**The Pause:**
Iran announced that it was halting attacks on Monday afternoon, but warned that it would resume hostilities if Israel carried out further acts of "aggression and hostility", including in southern Lebanon. A little later, Israeli media reported that the country would refrain from strikes on Iran at the request of United States President Donald Trump.

In his first public remarks since renewed fighting against Iran erupted last night, Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu says he will halt attacks on Iran "for now," while stressing that the fight against both Tehran and Hezbollah is "not over" and that Israel will continue to respond to any attacks on its territory. "After Iran attacked Israel" in support of Hezbollah following Israel's retaliatory strikes on the group in Beirut, "I directed the IDF to strike military and economic targets throughout Iran," Netanyahu says.

**Assessment:** This was the most serious military exchange since the April 8 ceasefire. The cycle demonstrated that Lebanon remains the critical tripwire — Israeli strikes on Beirut's southern suburbs triggered the entire escalation chain. While both sides have paused, neither has committed to a durable ceasefire, and Iran has explicitly conditioned its restraint on Israel halting Lebanon operations.

### 2. Trump-Netanyahu Tensions Erupt Publicly [VERIFIED]

In an interview with the Financial Times, Trump said the latest flare-up would not affect negotiations with Iran. "I call the shots. I call all the shots. He doesn't call the shots," Trump said, referring to Netanyahu. In a separate interview with Fox News, he said he had told Netanyahu not to retaliate against Iran.

Before the escalation, Trump told Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu to hold off on retaliating against Iran, according to a US official. Despite a reported request from Trump urging restraint, Israel then retaliated with strikes against Iran.

President Trump admits to calling Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu "crazy" during a tense and heated phone call over Israel's offensive into Lebanon.

US President Donald Trump and Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu spoke by phone for the second time in less than 24 hours on Monday. The call took place before Iran announced a suspension in its military operation against Israel. In the earlier call, on Sunday, Trump told Netanyahu to hold off on launching a retaliatory attack against Iran.

**Assessment:** Trump's public declaration that "I call the shots" contrasts sharply with Netanyahu's decisions to strike Beirut and then Iran despite explicit US requests. This suggests a deteriorating relationship that could limit Washington's ability to prevent future escalation cycles.

### 3. Houthis Declare Red Sea Ban on Israeli Shipping — NEW FRONT [VERIFIED]

The Houthis declared a ban on Israeli ships in the Red Sea, threatening a key bypass route to the Strait of Hormuz. "We declare a complete and total ban on Israeli maritime navigation," the group said Monday in a statement. "We consider all enemy movements to be legitimate military targets for our armed forces."

The Houthis also stated that they would "proceed from escalation to escalation", presumably a threat to close the Bab el-Mandeb Straits, the southern entrance to the Red Sea, if they are attacked. Several large international shipping lines that in recent months had started to return to the Red Sea and Suez Canal route have instructed their ships in the area to delay their approach to the Red Sea and to await further instructions.

The Houthis have largely sat out the US-Israeli war on Iran, but have repeatedly warned they could close the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, a narrow waterway that connects the Red Sea to the Gulf of Aden and the Indian Ocean. Renewed attacks in the area could jeopardize exports from Saudi Arabia's western port of Yanbu, where large numbers of oil tankers have pitched up in the past three months to collect Saudi crude.

Following several overnight missile barrages from Iran to Israel, the Houthis now appear to have joined the Iranian offensive in full force, with their morning missile launch triggering sirens across Israel's central and Shefela (lowland) regions.

Martin Kelly, head of advisory at EOS Risk Group, said the statement doesn't appear to alter the current risk situation much, but could mark a first step toward further escalation.

**Assessment:** The Houthis' entry represents a significant escalation risk. After largely sitting out the first 100 days, their activation alongside Iran's missile barrage suggests coordinated action. A broader Red Sea disruption would compound the Hormuz crisis and threaten Saudi oil exports through Yanbu.

### 4. Iran Attacks Kuwait and Bahrain (June 6) [VERIFIED]

There were seven missiles fired from Iran toward Kuwait and Bahrain overnight Saturday, according to U.S. Central Command. Six of them were intercepted and the seventh missed its target, the U.S. said. Kuwait did say some debris from the interceptions fell and started minor fires but it appeared no one was hurt.

The attack on the Gulf is the second since June 3. An Iranian drone strike on a passenger terminal at Kuwait's international airport killed one person and wounded 63 on Wednesday.

Saudi Arabia's Foreign Ministry denounced "Iran's aggressions against the sovereignty of Bahrain and Kuwait". Jordan decried what it called Iran's "brutal aggressions" against the two Gulf states, describing the strikes as a "dangerous escalation" that undermined efforts to restore regional security and stability. Egypt similarly condemned the attacks and reaffirmed its solidarity with both nations.

The IRGC's Aerospace Force said it targeted Kuwait's Ali al-Salem Air Base and facilities associated with the U.S. Navy's Fifth Fleet in Bahrain with ballistic missiles. Iranian officials said the attacks were retaliation for strikes on a telecommunications tower on Qeshm Island and another tower in Sirik.

**Assessment:** Iran has now struck Gulf Cooperation Council states twice in one week, drawing regional condemnation. This expands the conflict's footprint and creates pressure on Gulf states to either accommodate Tehran or seek stronger US protection.

### 5. US Military Actions — Notable Shift [REPORTED]

In a notable shift from previous rounds of conflicts, the US military didn't intercept any of the Iranian missiles fired overnight toward Israel, a US official told CNN.

The United States attacked a sanctioned vessel off the coast of Oman on Monday after it attempted to approach an Iranian port. "U.S. forces disabled an unladen oil tanker in the Gulf of Oman, June 8, after the vessel violated the ongoing blockade against Iran by attempting to sail to an Iranian port," CENTCOM said.

**Assessment:** The US decision not to intercept Iranian missiles targeting Israel is significant — it may signal Washington's displeasure with Netanyahu's defiance or an effort to avoid being drawn deeper into the escalation cycle. Meanwhile, the blockade enforcement continues.

### 6. Lebanon Death Toll and Ceasefire Violations [VERIFIED]

Lebanon's Prime Minister stated: "As for the total number of Israeli violations of the ceasefire, from April 17 to June 7, 2026, they amounted to 3,491 air raids," adding that there were 407 bombing operations and six bulldozing operations, which have left some villages entirely flattened. In total, 3,526 people were killed and 10,733 others wounded.

Israel last month invaded southern Lebanon to the deepest point in 26 years and now has a large force operating in that territory.

**Assessment:** Despite the nominal "ceasefire," Israel has conducted over 3,400 air raids on Lebanon since mid-April. This persistent level of military action explains why the Lebanon track keeps triggering broader escalation.

Brent crude futures jumped more than 4% to above $97 per barrel on Monday, rebounding from a two-session decline after Iran and Israel exchanged missile strikes, threatening to derail President Trump's efforts to secure a new 60-day ceasefire with Tehran.

Brent crude oil futures eased to $94 per barrel after having crossed $98 earlier on Monday after Iran stated it had ended its military operations against Israel.

Brent rose to 94.48 USD/Bbl on June 8, 2026, up 1.49% from the previous day. Over the past month, Brent's price has fallen 9.34%, but it is still 40.92% higher than a year ago.

Separately, OPEC+ approved another increase in July oil production quotas of 188,000 barrels per day despite persistent supply risks stemming from tensions in the Middle East.

## The Lebanon Linkage: Why It Matters

Today's escalation confirmed that **Lebanon is the critical tripwire for the broader Iran conflict**:

"If the Israelis and Americans imagine that through 'controlled tension' they can… limit the type of Iran's response, they are committing a foolish mistake," a military source told Tasnim, a state-affiliated news outlet close to Iran's Revolutionary Guards.

By striking Israeli cities in response to attacks on Hezbollah, Iran is also sending a message to its proxies that it is willing to bear the risk of escalation in their defense. "The immediate aim of (Iran's) missile response looks like enforcing linkage by signaling that the fronts can't be disaggregated," Hamidreza Azizi, a visiting fellow at the German Institute for International and Security Affairs, told CNN.

**Key implication:** Netanyahu now has the ability to collapse the US-Iran ceasefire at will by striking Beirut. This creates a fundamental tension between Israel's Lebanon campaign and Trump's desire for a broader deal.
