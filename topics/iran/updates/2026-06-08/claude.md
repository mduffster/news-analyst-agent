### June 8, 2026

### Executive Summary

**MAJOR ESCALATION: War's 100th day marked by direct Israel-Iran missile exchange — most serious confrontation since April ceasefire**

The conflict reached a critical juncture today on its 100th day, with Israel and Iran trading fire, putting its already fragile truce in serious jeopardy and threatening to reignite all-out regional war. The escalation began Sunday when Israel launched airstrikes at Beirut's southern suburbs, which led to Iran retaliating with its own strike on Israel, then to Monday's attacks and counterattacks. Iran's Revolutionary Guard launched nearly 30 ballistic missiles at Israel targeting three military air bases, and on 8 June, the IDF announced that it struck military sites in central and western Iran, with explosions being reported in Tehran, Isfahan, and Tabriz. The Houthis have now entered the fray, announcing they were banning Israeli shipping on the Red Sea, a key shipping lane, further complicating matters.

### Key Developments Since June 5

#### 1. Direct Israel-Iran Missile Exchange — MAJOR ESCALATION

**[VERIFIED]** Israel launched strikes on central and western Iran early Monday in response to missile fire from Tehran, in the most serious crossfire since an April 8 ceasefire was reached in the Iran war. Tehran warned of retaliation on Sunday after Israel struck Beirut's southern suburbs without warning in defiance of Washington's request days ago to stand down.

**[VERIFIED]** In the early hours of Monday, Israel unleashed missile strikes on Iran. Dozens of Israeli warplanes took part in the operation that focused mostly on Iranian air defenses that were being restored after the earlier fighting. Iranian citizens reported explosions in the capital Tehran, as well as Isfahan and Tabriz.

**[VERIFIED]** The Israeli military also said it hit several targets at a huge petrochemical complex in Mahshahr, in southwestern Iran. The workers at the plant were evacuated, the Iran media said.

**[OFFICIAL]** President Donald Trump has demanded that Israel and Iran cease hostilities after Iran broke the fragile ceasefire on Sunday and Israel responded in kind. "Israel and Iran must immediately stop 'shooting,'" Trump asserted.

**[OFFICIAL]** Trump then took to social media and said, "Both sides, Israel and Iran, are looking to do an immediate CEASEFIRE! Final negotiations on 'Peace' are proceeding, subject to ignorance or stupidity getting in its way."

**Assessment:** This represents the most significant military escalation since the April 8 ceasefire. The cycle of action-reaction (Israel strikes Beirut → Iran strikes Israel → Israel strikes Iran) demonstrates how Lebanon remains the critical tripwire for the broader conflict. Trump's demand for both sides to stop suggests he recognizes the ceasefire is on the verge of complete collapse.

#### 2. Trump-Netanyahu Tensions Boil Over

**[REPORTED]** US President Donald Trump says Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu "won't have any choice" but to accept whatever agreement Washington may reach with Iran, in an interview with The Financial Times. "He won't have any choice," Trump tells the outlet, adding, "I call the shots. I call all the shots. He [Netanyahu] doesn't call the shots."

**[REPORTED]** The strikes came after Iran launched missiles at Israel on Sunday and after President Trump had called Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu to urge him not to retaliate immediately for the Iranian missile attack.

**[VERIFIED]** Israel's media and many politicians have criticized Netanyahu, saying he's been too weak to stand up to Trump. They claim Trump has now forced Israel to accept three ceasefires that Israel opposed — in Gaza, Iran, and now Lebanon. Ben Caspit, a prominent columnist with the Maariv newspaper, wrote, "The truth needs to be said: Israeli policy is dictated by Trump's social media posts."

**Assessment:** The Trump-Netanyahu relationship has deteriorated significantly over the past week. Israel's decision to strike Beirut's southern suburbs despite Trump's requests — and then to retaliate against Iran despite Trump's phone call — suggests Netanyahu is no longer complying with Washington's demands. This creates a serious risk that the Lebanon track will continue to derail US-Iran negotiations.

#### 3. Houthis Declare Red Sea Ban on Israeli Shipping — New Front Opens

**[VERIFIED]** The Houthis, an Iran-backed militant group that controls much of Yemen, declared a ban on Israeli ships in the Red Sea. "We declare a complete and total ban on Israeli maritime navigation," the group said Monday in a statement. "We consider all enemy movements to be legitimate military targets for our armed forces."

**[REPORTED]** The Houthis have largely sat out the US-Israeli war on Iran, but have repeatedly warned they could close the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, a narrow waterway that connects the Red Sea to the Gulf of Aden and the Indian Ocean. Renewed attacks in the area could jeopardize exports from Saudi Arabia's western port of Yanbu, where large numbers of oil tankers have pitched up in the past three months to collect Saudi crude.

**[REPORTED]** The Houthis also stated that they would "proceed from escalation to escalation." Several large international shipping lines that in recent months had started to return to the Red Sea and Suez Canal route have instructed their ships in the area to delay their approach to the Red Sea and to await further instructions.

**Assessment:** The Houthis' entry into the conflict is a significant new development. While they have "largely sat out" the war so far, their activation threatens the Red Sea bypass route that has become essential since the Strait of Hormuz closure. This could compound the global shipping crisis and further escalate oil prices.

#### 4. War Reaches 100 Days — Key Statistics

**[VERIFIED]** Monday marked the 100th day of the Iran war, launched Feb. 28 when Israel and the United States killed Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei and other senior Iranian leaders.

**[REPORTED]** There have been more days of ceasefire than fighting in the first 100 days of the Iran war. The United States and Israel exchanged fire with Iran and its proxies for 39 days from February 28 to April 7, while June 7 marked 61 days of relative peace. Secretary of State Marco Rubio declared on May 5 that Operation Epic Fury was over.

**[REPORTED]** Iranian health authorities said at least 3,468 people have been killed in the U.S.-Israel attacks and a further 26,500 wounded. A pre-ceasefire estimate by the U.S.-based Human Rights Activists News Agency put the number at 3,636 people, nearly half of whom were civilians.

**[VERIFIED]** Israel has expanded the war on Iran to Lebanon, killing at least 3,593 people in the country, displacing more than one million and expanding its occupation in the south. More than 150 people were killed in other countries in the region, in addition to 26 in Israel and 13 US soldiers.

**Assessment:** The 100-day milestone arrives with the conflict at its most dangerous point since the April 8 ceasefire. Despite 61 days of "ceasefire," the war has continued in various forms, particularly in Lebanon.

**[VERIFIED]** Brent crude futures jumped more than 4% to above $97 per barrel on Monday, rebounding from a two-session decline after Iran and Israel exchanged missile strikes, threatening to derail President Trump's efforts to secure a new 60-day ceasefire with Tehran.

**[VERIFIED]** Israel also signaled it will hold fire for the moment. Separately, OPEC+ approved another increase in July oil production quotas of 188,000 barrels per day despite persistent supply risks stemming from tensions in the Middle East.

#### 6. Senate War Powers — Still Pending

**[VERIFIED]** The vote is mostly symbolic. Democrats, despite multiple attempts, have been unable to pass a war powers resolution through the Republican-led Senate. Even if the measure passed in Congress, it would almost certainly be vetoed by President Trump, whose administration has questioned the constitutionality of the War Powers Act.

**[VERIFIED]** The Pentagon, State Department and USAID inspectors general have launched a joint review of the US war with Iran, announcing in a press release Wednesday that they are mandated by law to probe overseas military operations that exceed 60 days. The announcement is significant because it indicates that the watchdogs believe that, legally, the war has lasted more than 60 days from its commencement on February 28.
