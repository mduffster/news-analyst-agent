# Daily Update: US-Israel-Iran Conflict
## September 24, 2026 — Day 209

### Key Developments (Last 24 Hours)

**DIPLOMATIC BREAKTHROUGH AT UN** [REPORTED]

Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi sat down with Donald Trump's special envoy Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner for a three-hour session at UN headquarters in New York. The meeting was arranged in advance and attended for most of its duration by Qatari Prime Minister and Foreign Minister Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al Thani.

President Donald Trump told reporters ⁠at the UN that US officials had a "very good" three-hour meeting with the Iranian delegation. Witkoff said the talks were conducted "through the mediators, who shuttled between the two sides throughout the day" and described them as "constructive and promising."

**IRAN'S 60-DAY CEASEFIRE ROADMAP** [REPORTED]

Iran has proposed a regionwide ceasefire of up to 60 days, a phased reopening of the Strait of Hormuz and an end to the US blockade. US and Iranian officials held talks on the sidelines of the UN General Assembly in New York, mediated by Qatar, Pakistan and Egypt.

Iran's conditions for reopening the Strait of Hormuz include the immediate lifting of the naval blockade, the release of frozen Iranian assets and an end to the war on all fronts, including Lebanon.

Supreme National Security Council secretary Mohsen Rezaei stated that Washington has four to five days to accept Tehran's terms. This deadline expires around 27 to 28 September.

**NETANYAHU'S UN ADDRESS** [VERIFIED]

In his September 24, 2026, address to the UN General Assembly, Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu defended Israel's strikes on Iran and its conduct in Gaza, describing the years since October 7 as a seven-front war. Netanyahu lambasted Tehran, dramatically claiming if he hadn't attacked Iran, "we'd all be dead."

Delegates staged a walkout as the speech began, prompting Netanyahu to invite any remaining "moral cowards" to leave. He praised President Donald Trump, rejected accusations of genocide, and sharply criticized New York Mayor Zohran Mamdani, Qatar, and Turkey.

Netanyahu also offered a timeline for regime change, with intelligence estimating the fall could come in the first half of 2027.

**SENATE REJECTS WAR POWERS RESOLUTION** [VERIFIED]

The Senate on Thursday narrowly voted down a measure demanding an end to the U.S. war with Iran. The chamber voted 49-50 on the resolution. An affirmative vote would have directed President Donald Trump to remove U.S. troops from hostilities in Iran unless Congress explicitly approves the war. The measure — a "concurrent resolution" — does not carry the force of law and is never signed by the president, making the vote mostly symbolic.

### War Status Update

**CASUALTIES** [VERIFIED]

Current casualty totals: United States military: 20–24 killed, 820 wounded (CENTCOM); Iran: 3,528 killed, 27,170 injured (Iranian sources) or 6,000+ military personnel killed (US/Israel claims); Israel: 72 killed, 7,841 injured; Lebanon: 4,383 killed, 12,406 injured (Lebanese Health Ministry).

The Pentagon has added one death to its public database of U.S. military fatalities, bringing the disclosed total to 19. An additional 66 U.S. personnel were wounded in action this month, pushing the official count of Iran war casualties above 850.

**STRAIT OF HORMUZ** [VERIFIED]

According to analytics firm Kpler, there were 27 missile strikes in the Strait of Hormuz between July 6 and early September. Kpler reports that no large ships have passed through the strait since early September.

Preliminary data showed that 10 cargo ships crossed the Strait of Hormuz on Wednesday, up from seven the previous day. The figure remained below the 10-day moving average of about 17 vessels. US Central Command said its forces had redirected 115 commercial vessels as of September 23 while enforcing what it calls America's "steel wall blockade".

**BAB AL-MANDAB CRISIS — CRITICAL DEVELOPMENT** [VERIFIED]

The Bab al-Mandab Strait crisis is an escalation that began in September 2026 as part of the wider Yemeni offensives tied to the 2026 Iran war, in which Yemen's Houthis launched an offensive to seize control of the Bab al-Mandab Strait, a global choke point.

The Houthis completed their takeover of the Bab el-Mandeb region by recapturing Perim Island on 11 September, opening a new front in the 2026 Iran war. They also completed their seizure of the entire Red Sea coast by recapturing the Hanish Islands.

The strait's strategic importance had increased dramatically because Iran's closure of the Strait of Hormuz amid the 2026 Iran war had forced Saudi Arabia to rely far more heavily on its Red Sea export route; between March and mid-July 2026, Saudi seaborne crude exports through Bab al-Mandab were eight times higher than during the same period in 2025.

United States officials met Houthi representatives at the US embassy in the Omani capital, Muscat, over the weekend.

### Iran Internal Situation

As of 21 May, Mojtaba Khamenei remains in hiding and the de facto ruler is uncertain. The IRGC is currently under command of the hardliner general Ahmad Vahidi, who is cited as the formulator of Iran's military and negotiation stance.

Iranian hard-liners are up in arms after the Araghchi-Witkoff meeting in New York, with some going as far as demanding impeachment proceedings. The Islamic Revolutionary Guards Corps (IRGC) appeared to endorse talks hours before the meeting was made public, with an IRGC spokesman telling state television that negotiating "is another facet of war."

### Assessment

**[ANALYSIS]** The diplomatic window that opened in New York is real but fragile. Iran has put forward its most detailed terms since the June MoU, including a mechanism (60-day ceasefire) and sequencing (phased Hormuz reopening). However, fundamental gaps remain: Donald Trump has ruled out lifting the blockade before Iran demonstrates sufficient goodwill. Tehran wants preconditions met before talks; Washington wants talks before preconditions.

The Rezaei deadline creates a decision point. If it passes without American engagement, the Supreme National Security Council may use it to justify a resumption of fire: a public deadline, publicly rejected, is the form in which Tehran has previously justified escalation.

The conflict appears to have become a war of attrition. On one hand, Iran may have limited abilities to continue attacks and appears to face an increasingly perilous economic situation. On the other, the United States and its partners in the Gulf and Israel may have finite capacity to counter Iranian attacks and bear associated military, social, economic, and political costs.

The Bab al-Mandab situation is the underreported variable. Saudi Arabia's Red Sea export route — its lifeline after Hormuz closed — is now at Houthi mercy. This strengthens Iran's negotiating position but also creates pressure for a wider regional deal that addresses Yemen.
