## 1. Eisenkot Secures 67-Seat Majority; Herzog Delays Mandate to Sunday [VERIFIED]

Former IDF Chief of Staff and Yashar Chairman Gadi Eisenkot is set to receive the mandate to form Israel's next government from President Isaac Herzog after securing the backing of parties representing 67 Knesset seats at the conclusion of the second round of consultations on Thursday, compared with just 46 for Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu.

Following his series of consultations, Herzog said he will hold further talks with party leaders to determine who can form a government, and intends to assign the mandate on Sunday.

The endorsements came together after both Arab-majority parties backed Eisenkot. The 67-seat backing for Eisenkot came together after the Arab-led Joint List announced its unanimous decision to recommend him, joining earlier endorsements from the opposition and the Arab Ra'am party.

Yashar representatives urge President Isaac Herzog to give party leader Gadi Eisenkot the mandate to form a government, arguing that Israel needs new leadership and rejecting a unity arrangement with Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu.

**Significance:** The 67-46 margin is decisive and represents the largest number of recommendations given to a candidate since 2015. Yashar's explicit rejection of unity with Netanyahu signals a clean break if Eisenkot forms a government. However, the recommendations do not necessarily mean that the parties backing him will ultimately join his coalition. Eisenkot will have 28 days to form a government once he receives the mandate.

## 2. Vance Privately Accused Netanyahu of Being "Wrong on Everything" [REPORTED]

An extraordinary Axios report revealed deep fissures within the Trump administration over the Iran war:

In recent months, Vance has led a faction within Trump's inner circle advising him to reach a deal with Iran to end the war. He privately accused Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu of "being wrong on everything" regarding Iran, sources said.

Vance warned that the war was highly unpopular and could cost Republicans the election, the sources said.

Most senior officials around Trump have shared Vance's views. But Trump still leaned toward resuming the war. At one point, he considered launching strikes before the midterms, as Netanyahu was pressing him to do.

Axios also reported that Netanyahu had urged Trump to resume military strikes against Iran before the elections, despite warnings from senior American officials about the potential political consequences.

**Significance:** This leak reveals that the vice president and most senior advisers opposed both the war's continuation and Netanyahu's push for escalation. Trump reportedly considered pre-election strikes at Netanyahu's urging but ultimately held off—validating Vance's political assessment. The report positions Vance as the leader of an internal restraint faction now vindicated by the midterm results.

## 3. Trump: "Finish the Job" If No Deal, But Advisers Split [OFFICIAL]

President Donald Trump warned he could "finish the job very easily" if Iran fails to agree to a binding deal preventing it from obtaining a nuclear weapon. "Certainly if Iran doesn't behave, I will," Trump said when asked about further military action.

Despite hawkish rhetoric, the administration remains divided:

CIA Director John Ratcliffe, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and other advisers want Trump to let the naval blockade and economic pressure campaign play out longer, tightening the screws on Iran.

Trump could opt to maintain the "no war, no deal" status quo.

But Trump said he'd do it again if he had to: "We had no choice. You can't let Iran have a nuclear weapon."

**Significance:** There's no tidy way for Trump to "finish the job" in Iran. The three-way split between Vance's deal faction, the Ratcliffe/Bessent blockade faction, and Trump's own hawkish instincts will determine the conflict's trajectory. The "maximum pressure without escalation" approach appears to be gaining ground.

## 4. Iran Claims Five Tankers and Tugboat Damaged in Hormuz [OFFICIAL — DISPUTED]

Iranian forces say five oil tankers and a tugboat have caught fire in several incidents in the Strait of Hormuz. At least five tankers have been damaged in the Strait of Hormuz, Iranian forces said, making clear that shipping would remain in peril as long as the United States and Israel's war on the country is unresolved.

The incidents unfolded in multiple stages Thursday:

"Last night, in two separate incidents, a large oil tanker attempting to pass through the illegal passageway south of the Strait of Hormuz suffered a fire after striking a mine and was brought to a halt," the IRGC told the state-linked Fars news agency.

The Iranian army later said three more tankers were hit by naval mines and caught fire. "Early this morning, a violating oil tanker – navigating through an unsafe area east of the Strait of Hormuz – caught fire and came to a halt after striking a naval mine," the army said. "Today at around noon, two more oil tankers struck naval mines and caught fire in the unsafe waters east of the Strait of Hormuz."

Attacks on tankers in the Gulf are still running close to a record pace.

**Significance:** Iran is maintaining maximum maritime denial regardless of US political developments. The framing around "unauthorized routes" and "unsafe areas" reinforces Tehran's claim to control strait transit. CENTCOM has not independently confirmed these incidents, but the insurance and operational impact persists regardless of verification.

## 5. Oil Rebounds ~2% After Wednesday's Crash [VERIFIED]

Brent rose to 96.30 USD/Bbl on November 5, 2026, up 1.77% from the previous day. Crude Oil rose to 90.21 USD/Bbl on November 5, 2026, up 2.37% from the previous day.

Historical data shows Brent at 97.17 on Nov 5, after falling from 103.60 on Nov 3 and 104.77 on Nov 2. The week has seen dramatic volatility, with prices down approximately 15% from their October 30 peak of $117.64.

Oil prices fell sharply Wednesday as traders weigh signs that the midterm election will add pressure on the White House to find an offramp from the Iran conflict. Wednesday's price drop — around $8 per barrel — deepened after Vance posted on X that "everyone knows" the war is why Republicans got pummeled.

"The market is saying that Congress and members of his own administration are likely to start acting as a constraint on President Trump's war in Iran," Seb Barker, a strategist with the hedge fund, Marshall Wace, tells the Financial Times.

**Significance:** Markets are pricing in political constraints on escalation but remain uncertain about actual policy direction. The partial rebound suggests traders are hedging against the possibility that rhetoric and reality diverge.

## 7. South Korea Expands Naval Mission Toward Hormuz [VERIFIED]

South Korea plans to expand the operations of its anti-piracy Cheonghae unit to waters closer to the Strait of Hormuz, the Defence Ministry said, following pressure from US President Donald Trump to provide support for the war in Iran.

The deployment will include a destroyer, a P-8 maritime patrol aircraft, an explosive ordnance disposal (EOD) team, unmanned surface vessels, unmanned underwater vehicles and remotely operated vehicles (ROVs) for underwater exploration.

South Korea will double the Cheonghae Unit's operational area near the Strait of Hormuz and add 75 personnel, but ruled out entering the strait.

Seoul emphasized this is not combat involvement: "We have upheld the principle of non-involvement and non-intervention in the war until now," a defence ministry official said. "And we will continue to uphold it going forward."

**Significance:** South Korea is threading the needle—responding to US pressure while avoiding direct conflict involvement. Tehran has warned South Korea against any such deployment. This represents the first significant allied naval expansion since Japan's deployment in June.

## 8. Iran Sends Nuclear Proposal Via Qatar; Seven Preconditions Remain [REPORTED]

Iran has sent the US a nuclear proposal via Qatar which is to be discussed only after Washington meets its seven demands, a senior Iranian official tells Reuters. Washington is reviewing Tehran's nuclear proposal, the official adds.

Iran said the United States must meet seven demands before the nuclear-related proposal can be discussed. The specific contents of Iran's seven demands and whether the United States would accept them have not been confirmed.

**Significance:** Iran's diplomatic posture remains unchanged—preconditions first, then negotiations. The seven demands reportedly include compensation for war damages, sanctions relief, asset release, and ending the blockade. These maximalist terms have been consistently rejected by Washington, suggesting the proposal is more positioning than breakthrough.

## 9. House GOP Leadership Race: Scalise vs. Jordan [VERIFIED]

Louisiana Congressman Steve Scalise will face Ohio Congressman Jim Jordan in an intraparty election to determine who will lead House Republicans when the new Congress convenes Jan. 3. Scalise and Jordan both announced their candidacies in letters to their Republican colleagues Wednesday. The internal election is set for Nov. 10.

On Wednesday Trump told reporters at the White House he intended to stay out of the coming House GOP leadership race.

Scalise has long courted backing from the House GOP's conservative wing, particularly business and energy sector lawmakers. Jordan, the brawling former wrestler who helmed the Judiciary Committee, has long been preferred by the hard right, including some from the Freedom Caucus.

**Significance:** The outcome will shape how House Republicans position themselves on Iran policy and whether any faction aligns with Democratic war-ending efforts. "President Trump should want a warrior like Jordan with impeachment experience in charge, given the Democrats will inevitably be pursuing impeachment by August recess."
