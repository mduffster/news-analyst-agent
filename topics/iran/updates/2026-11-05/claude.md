### **HERZOG: EISENKOT MANDATE ON SUNDAY** [VERIFIED]

President Isaac Herzog said Eisenkot got 67 nods as PM, but announced he will only hand over the mandate on Sunday. Following his series of consultations, Herzog said he will hold further talks with party leaders to determine who can form a government.

Eisenkot is set to receive the mandate from President Herzog after securing the backing of parties representing 67 Knesset seats at the conclusion of the second round of consultations on Thursday, compared with just 46 for Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu. Herzog is expected to formally assign Eisenkot the task of forming a government this coming Sunday. "I will leave no stone unturned in working to establish a stable government," the president said.

Yashar notes that the total for Eisenkot is the largest number of recommendations given to a candidate for forming a government since 2015.

Yashar representatives urge President Isaac Herzog to give party leader Gadi Eisenkot the mandate to form a government, arguing that Israel needs new leadership and rejecting a unity arrangement with Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu.

**[ANALYSIS]** Eisenkot's 67-46 margin is decisive. The delay until Sunday appears procedural rather than substantive. Yashar's explicit rejection of unity with Netanyahu signals a clean break if Eisenkot forms a government.

### **VANCE PRIVATELY BLASTED NETANYAHU AS "WRONG ON EVERYTHING"** [REPORTED]

Vance privately accused Netanyahu of "being wrong on everything" about Iran, the outlet said, citing sources.

President Trump remained inclined to restart the military campaign. At one point, he weighed hitting Iran before the midterms, as Prime Minister Netanyahu was urging him to do, but in the end he decided to hold off.

Axios reported that Netanyahu had urged Trump to resume military strikes against Iran before the midterm elections, despite warnings from senior US officials about the political risks. Trump considered renewed attacks but ultimately decided to wait until after the elections, according to the report.

**[ANALYSIS]** This is an extraordinary leak revealing deep administration divisions. Vance has been running interference against Netanyahu's push for escalation throughout the conflict. The report that Netanyahu pressed for pre-election strikes that Trump ultimately rejected adds another dimension to the internal rift.

### **OIL REBOUNDS ~3% AFTER WEDNESDAY'S CRASH** [VERIFIED]

Brent crude futures rose nearly $3 on Thursday as investors weighed the future course of the US-Israeli war with Iran after President Donald Trump's Republican Party suffered heavy losses in midterm elections this week. Brent futures were up $2.94, or 3.11%, at $97.56 a barrel as of 1417 GMT, while US West Texas Intermediate crude futures rose $1.73, or 1.96%, to $89.85. Both prices tumbled by more than 8% in the previous session to settle at their lowest since August 31.

Andy Lipow, president of Lipow Oil Associates, said reports of tankers striking mines in the Middle East or being targeted by drones continue to worry the oil market. Investors are also worried about Iran's unchanged position regarding negotiations with the US.

Citi said in a note there were concerns that with the midterms over, the White House could focus on its executive powers in foreign policy and potentially escalate the war with Iran. It also cut the probability of a deal to 10% from 20%, as Iran has held firm on demands that Trump is viewed as unlikely to accept. Despite the recent drop in oil prices, any escalation in the conflict could push Brent into a $110 to $125 per barrel range, Eurasia Group said in a note.

**[ANALYSIS]** The rebound signals markets remain uncertain. Citi's cut of deal probability to 10% is notable — professional analysts are skeptical that the political shift will translate to actual peace.

### **IRAN: 5 MORE TANKERS DAMAGED IN HORMUZ** [OFFICIAL — DISPUTED]

Iranian forces say five oil tankers and a tugboat have caught fire in several incidents in the Strait of Hormuz. At least five tankers have been damaged in the Strait of Hormuz, Iranian forces said, making clear that shipping would remain in peril as long as the United States and Israel's war on the country is unresolved.

Attacks on tankers in the Gulf are still running close to a record pace. Iran's Revolutionary Guards, who do not typically acknowledge firing on merchant ships but say the transit route is unsafe without Tehran's consent, said two tankers had struck naval mines and another had caught fire on Thursday.

Iran attacked at least 35 vessels in the region last month, a record high during the nine-month-long war.

Strait of Hormuz status: Effectively no, it is closed to commercial shipping as of 5 November 2026.

**[ANALYSIS]** Iran is maintaining maximum pressure on shipping despite diplomatic signals. The record pace of attacks (35 in October alone) shows Tehran has not relaxed its posture.

### **SOUTH KOREA EXPANDS NAVAL MISSION TOWARD HORMUZ** [VERIFIED]

South Korea is planning to extend the reach of its anti-piracy Cheonghae unit to waters near the Strait of Hormuz, amid pressure from US President Donald Trump to help support the war in Iran, the Defence Ministry said. The proposal would also expand its operating zone to waters near Oman's port of Duqm, around 650km (403 miles) from the Strait of Hormuz. A ministry spokesperson said the government would move a motion in parliament to prolong the unit's deployment in the Middle East, which is due to end in December 2026.

South Korea's defence ministry said Thursday it would deploy maritime patrol aircraft and an explosive ordnance disposal team to waters near the Strait of Hormuz as part of a mission to help keep the waterway open. The announcement comes as Seoul maintains it will not deploy troops to intervene militarily in the Iran war, while seeking to protect commercial shipping and energy supplies.

The Strait of Hormuz and the Gulf of Oman, however, were left out of the operational area. The government also left open the possibility of joining combined operations inside the Strait of Hormuz, such as mine clearance.

**[ANALYSIS]** South Korea is threading the needle — responding to US pressure while avoiding direct conflict involvement. This is the first significant allied naval expansion since Japan's deployment in June.

### **IRAN'S NEW NUCLEAR PROPOSAL VIA QATAR** [REPORTED]

Tehran sends nuclear proposal to Washington via Qatar, demands conditions are met. Washington has received the document and is actively reviewing Tehran's proposal. Iran is pushing a seven-point deal demanding U.S. compensation for war damages, an end to economic sanctions and the naval blockade of Iranian ports, and an end to hostilities across the region, including in Lebanon and Gaza. Iran is also demanding the release of billions of dollars in frozen Iranian assets.

Iran promised only to reopen the Strait of Hormuz to international shipping. Rezaei said Iran would not budge on reopening the Strait of Hormuz until the U.S. conditions are met. "Until our conditions are implemented, the Strait of Hormuz will not be opened, and we will not enter negotiations."

**[ANALYSIS]** Iran's demands remain maximalist. The seven-point proposal appears to be a repackaging of previous conditions — compensation, sanctions relief, asset release, end to blockade. These terms have been consistently rejected by Washington.
